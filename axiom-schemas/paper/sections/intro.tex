A mathematical community uses axioms: single sentences, such as Extensionality, and schemas, such as induction in Peano
arithmetic or Separation in set theory, each standing for infinitely many sentences. Its proofs show only instances,
often without saying which axiom they instantiate. We ask when a learner can recover from such positive instances
exactly which axioms the community uses. The aim is checking, not proving: we want a verifier that accepts a candidate
axiom step iff it is an instance of an axiom in use, and that stays sound when the candidates come from an untrusted,
possibly adversarial, prover.

This is the setting of the report \citet{claude2026whatfollows} (\emph{the parent report}) on learning to check
inferences. Its central tool is the \emph{cautious} verifier: fix a class of hypotheses and accept a sentence iff every
hypothesis in the class that contains the data contains it. If the target is a member of the class (more precisely, an
intersection of members), this verifier is sound at all times against every prover, and it is exact once the data
contain an \emph{anchor}, a finite set of instances that every covering hypothesis extends to the whole target
(\cref{sec:setting:learning}). For rules cited by name the parent report used first-order anti-unification
\citep{plotkin1970note,reynolds1970transformational}. PA induction does not fit:
$\Ind(\varphi)=(\varphi(0)\wedge\forall x(\varphi\to\varphi[Sx/x]))\to\forall x\varphi$ substitutes terms into the motive
$\varphi$. A follow-up analysis (unpublished; its record is in the accompanying repository, and we re-prove what we use)
learned induction with \emph{determinate} second-order templates. In these every metavariable has an occurrence applied
to distinct bound variables, such as $P(x)$ in $T_{\Ind}=P(0)\wedge\forall x(P(x)\to P(Sx))\to\forall x\,P(x)$, and
arguments contain no metavariables (\cref{rem:setting:nested} shows why). Matching is unique and linear, and two
instances are an anchor iff their motives have different main symbols and at least one has $x$ free. Kaarel H\"anni then
asked three questions about this method.

\subsection{The questions}\label{sec:intro:questions}

Verbatim:
\begin{quote}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item does the same thing work for learning an axiom of the form forall x phi(x) from a bunch of sentences of the form
  phi(x)?
\item does the same thing work for learning each axiom schema of ZFC from a bunch of instances?
\item anyway can you try to come up with a method that works for all these? ideally, your method would also work for
  learning many axiom( scheme)s at once, even if they do not come with labels for instances of which axiom scheme they
  are
\end{enumerate}
\end{quote}
``The same thing'' is the method for induction. We call these Questions 1--3 (not to be confused with the axioms Q1--Q7
of Robinson arithmetic $\Q$). We read the data of Question~1 as instances $\varphi(t)$ at terms $t$: since all data are
read under universal closure, a datum $\varphi(x)$ with $x$ free would already be the axiom (\cref{prop:univ:gen}).

The answers compare four classes of templates (\cref{sec:setting:templates}): first-order patterns $\FO$, whose
metavariables are $0$-ary; Miller's higher-order pattern templates $\PAT$ \citep{miller1991logic}, in which every
metavariable occurrence is applied to distinct bound variables; determinate templates $\DT$, in which every metavariable
has at least one such occurrence and the others may have any metavariable-free arguments, as $P(0)$ and $P(Sx)$; and
rigid second-order templates $\SO$ without this requirement. So $\FO\subseteq\PAT\subseteq\DT\subseteq\SO$. Data are
formulas whose free variables are parameters, read under universal closure; bound variables are de Bruijn indices, and
values of metavariables contain no free bound variable, so capture cannot occur (the $\lambda$ convention). Besides data,
a learner may use a \emph{refutation oracle}: sound but one-sided evidence that a sentence is false, such as a
$\Delta_0$ counterexample in $\N$ or a hereditarily finite counterexample to a $\Pi_1$ sentence of set theory
(\cref{sec:setting:refutation}).

\paragraph{Question 1 (\cref{sec:univ}).}
Yes for the instance schema; but the instance schema is not the axiom.
\begin{itemize}
\item The instance schema $\sigma_\varphi=\varphi(z_1,\dots,z_k)$ is a first-order pattern. Closed instances are an
  anchor in $\FO$ and in $\DT$ iff Plotkin's events hold: \evR\ the terms at each $x_i$ do not all have the same root
  symbol, and \evD\ for $i\neq i'$ some datum puts different terms at $x_i$ and $x_{i'}$
  (\cref{thm:univ:anchor,prop:univ:dt}). For one variable $\varphi(0)$ and $\varphi(S0)$ suffice, and the failure
  probability after $N$ i.i.d.\ instances is exactly $\sum_fp_f^N$, $p_f$ the probability of root $f$
  (\cref{thm:univ:rates}). In $\SO$ these events do not suffice.
\item \emph{The $\omega$-gap.} ``Every closed instance holds in $M$, hence $\forall\bar x\varphi$ does'' is valid for all
  $\varphi$ iff the closed-term substructure of $M$ is an elementary substructure (\cref{prop:univ:tv}, by the
  Tarski--Vaught criterion \citep{tarski1957arithmetical}). This holds in $\N$, but not in the ordered field $\R$, where
  $\neg(x\cdot x=1+1)$ is true at every closed term, nor in the universe of sets or in nonstandard models of $\PA$
  (\cref{ex:univ:fail}). The step is not derivable: $\Q\vdash0+\num n=\num n$ for each $n$, but
  $\Q\nvdash\forall x\,(0+x=x)$ (\cref{ex:univ:q}). When parameters are admissible values, the cautious learner takes the
  step silently after the first anchor by accepting $\varphi(\bar p)$, which is $\forall\bar x\varphi$
  (\cref{prop:univ:open}). For first-order schemas, a guard ``the value is closed'', learned from closed data, prevents
  this ($\DT$ has no guards); a datum at a parameter licenses the step as ordinary generalization
  (\cref{prop:univ:gen}).
\item \emph{Capture.} If a metavariable may take a bound variable (named variables, or de Bruijn indices treated as
  constants), the schema gains capture instances, such as $\exists y\,\neg(y=y)$ from $\exists y\,\neg(y=x)$, which can be
  false in every structure; the unguarded verifier is then unsound outright. A learned no-capture guard, or the $\lambda$
  convention, repairs this (\cref{prop:univ:capture}, \cref{tab:univ:learner}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Question 2 (\cref{sec:zf}).}
Yes, and more easily than for induction.
\begin{itemize}
\item The ZFC axioms other than Separation and Replacement are single sentences, each its own anchor
  (\cref{prop:zf:ground}). Every ZF schema, in each textbook formulation considered (\cref{tab:zf:forms}), is a Miller
  pattern: $L_\in$ has no function symbols, so an instance only renames variables inside the body
  (\cref{thm:zf:classify}). In every class between $\PAT$ and $\SO$, instances are an anchor iff \evR\ the bodies' main
  symbols are not all equal and \evN\ every argument place is used by some body (\cref{thm:zf:anchor}). One instance never
  suffices; two can. Pattern anti-unification \citep{pfenning1991unification,baumgartner2017higher} and the $\DT$ learner
  recover every schema from any anchor (\cref{cor:zf:patlgg}), at exact rates (\cref{thm:zf:rates}).
\item First-order anti-unification works iff the metavariable is applied to literally the same variables at every
  occurrence, which depends on the formulation and on how bound variables are written
  (\cref{thm:zf:classify,prop:zf:alpha}, \cref{tab:zf:classify}). Separation passes, with a freshness guard that is
  learned from the data; without it the lgg has Russell's instance (\cref{prop:zf:russell}). In the other failing cases
  with textbook names or de Bruijn indices, no finite union of guarded first-order schemas covers the schema without a
  false member (\cref{thm:zf:nounion,prop:zf:false}). The one exception, spelled-out Replacement with textbook names,
  over-generalizes truth-soundly: all guarded instances of its lgg are theorems of ZF, and over any base theory exactly
  when that theory proves Collection (\cref{prop:zf:coll}). No refutation can expose it.
\item \emph{PA induction} is not a Miller pattern. On raw instances, first-order anti-unification is unsound from two
  instances, no finite union of first-order schemas is sound, some unsound lggs are never refuted by true closed
  quantifier-free sentences, and pattern anti-unification is unsound too
  (\cref{prop:zf:indtwo,thm:zf:indunion,thm:zf:K0,prop:zf:indpat}). The $\DT$ learner is exact under \evR+\evN\
  (\cref{thm:zf:indanchor}), and $T_{\Ind}$ is the least of the 26 $\DT$ templates covering any anchor
  (\cref{prop:zf:indgen}). The obstruction is the substitution $\varphi[Sx/x]$, not induction: recording it, or Tarski's
  substitution-free form $\IndEq$ \citep{tarski1965simplified}, makes induction a first-order pattern, and $\Q$ plus the
  instances of $\IndEq$ axiomatizes $\PA$ (\cref{prop:zf:sub,prop:zf:indeq}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The general theory (\cref{sec:single}).}
These anchor conditions are cases of one theorem. For a target $T^*\in\DT$, finite data have finitely many minimal
covering templates, one of them below $T^*$ (\cref{thm:single:min}). The cautious verifier is a polynomial-time test of
``features'' of the data (\cref{thm:single:feat}), although there can be $4^n$ minimal templates
(\cref{prop:single:blowup}). Data are an anchor iff root variation at every occurrence, non-vacuity and no coincidence
hold (\cref{thm:single:anchor}); this is \evR+\evD\ for first-order targets and \evR+\evN\ for one formula metavariable
(\cref{cor:single:special}). Failure probabilities decay exponentially (\cref{thm:single:rates}), and the worst-case
number of escalations (queries passed to an oracle) is $\Theta(N^2)$ in the sentence size, which refutes an earlier
conjecture that it is linear (\cref{thm:single:esc,prop:single:quadratic}).

\paragraph{Question 3 (\cref{sec:many}).}
We propose \DTRC{} (Determinate Templates with Refutation Clustering). It works under an assumption about the world,
\emph{refutation separation}, and we show what happens without it.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Positive data need a bound.} Without a bound on the number of targets, the cautious union learner accepts
  only the data, whatever sound negatives it has (\cref{prop:many:bound}; \citealp{gold1967language}). With a bound,
  spare slots cost data diversity that no sound negative can buy, because splitting a target into sound specializations
  is never refuted (\cref{thm:many:splits,thm:many:slots,rem:many:numerals}). With the exact bound and negatives that
  refute every template covering anchor data of two targets, untagged learning is as easy as tagged learning (the
  \emph{pigeonhole theorem}, \cref{thm:many:pigeonhole}); the learner can find such negatives itself.
\item \emph{The method.} \DTRC{} needs no bound. Starting from singletons, it merges two clusters iff their union has a minimal
  covering $\DT$ template with no instance refuted within depth $d$; it verifies each final cluster cautiously against
  all its unrefuted minimal templates and asserts the union. Within-target merges are never refuted. Under refutation
  separation (every template covering data of two targets has a $d$-refuted instance) the clusters are the hidden label
  classes for every merge order, and the output is sound at all times and exact once each target's data contain an
  anchor; if separation holds for all instances, the failure probability is bounded by the tagged learner's
  (\cref{thm:many:dtrc}). Refutation does the work that citations do in tagged data.
\item \emph{Without separation} \DTRC{} is sound relative to a residue of unrefuted cross-target merges, truth-sound if
  these are, and exactness can fail by fragmentation (\cref{thm:many:residue}); on some data no learner with a
  target-blind oracle is both exact for one valid practice and target-sound for another (\cref{prop:many:ambiguity}).
  Learning $\forall x\varphi$ from its instances is the simplest sound merge (\cref{prop:many:forall,prop:univ:merge});
  with $\Delta_0$ evaluation in $\N$ and quantifier-free $\varphi$, ``never refuted'' means ``true''
  (\cref{prop:many:qf}).
\item \emph{Depth relativization is necessary}: no computable learner is both sound relative to the full-depth residue
  and eventually exact on every practice that is separated at some finite depth (\cref{thm:many:depth}, via the MRDP
  theorem \citep{matiyasevich1993hilbert}).
\item \emph{Cost}: the exact $k$-union verifier is polynomial for $k\le2$ and coNP-complete for each fixed $k\ge3$; the
  refutation test of \DTRC{} is NP-complete, and polynomial once the cluster contains an anchor
  (\cref{thm:many:complexity}; not independently refereed).
\item \emph{Natural cases}: $\Q$'s axioms with induction, ZF and ZFC are separated at small depth by $\Delta_0$
  evaluation, hereditarily finite counterexamples and logic, with Russell's paradox separating the comprehension-shaped
  axioms (proved for $\Q$ with induction and for the mixtures of \cref{sec:exp}, computed for ZF and ZFC;
  \cref{sec:many:examples}). Weak forms of Union and Power merge into a false universal-set schema that only a coherence
  refutation catches (\cref{prop:many:univset}). Minimum description length is no substitute for refutation: it tracks
  the statistics of usage, not the logical boundaries of schemas (\cref{sec:many:mdl}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Experiments (\cref{sec:exp}).}
An implementation of the learner (in the class $\DTF$, with $0$-ary term metavariables) and of \DTRC{} matches the
predicted rates on Question~1 (mean $3.27$ instances to exactness, $3.25$ predicted). It is exact on each ZF schema and on
induction in most runs from $2$ or $3$ tagged instances, with no non-instance accepted in $927$ probes
(\cref{tab:exp:e2}). On mixtures of the PA and of the ZF axioms it recovers the hidden labels in every run, and with
ambiguous data shared between clusters it matches a tagged learner target by target (\cref{tab:exp:e3}). It also shows
the limits: the evidence for separation is narrow (\cref{tab:exp:e9}); similar targets merge into valid templates
(\cref{lem:exp:valid}); and with injected mistakes and refuter budgets of $80$ and $400$, \DTRC{} accepted a false
sentence, whose merge only a budget of $2000$ refuted (\cref{prop:exp:qF}).

\subsection{Contributions}\label{sec:intro:contrib}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item A theory of learning one determinate template: finiteness of the minimal covering templates, a polynomial feature
  verifier, a general anchor theorem with counterexamples to weaker conditions, rates, tight escalation bounds and
  complexity results (\cref{sec:single}).
\item For universal axioms: anchors and exact rates for the instance schema, the $\omega$-gap and its model-theoretic
  criterion, and the roles of parameters, capture and guards (\cref{sec:univ}).
\item For ZF(C): all schemas are Miller patterns, with the anchor condition \evR+\evN\ in every class from $\PAT$ to $\SO$;
  a criterion for when they are first-order patterns; impossibility results for first-order learners and a truth-sound
  over-generalization; anchor theorems for several metavariables, which differ between $\PAT$, $\DT$ and $\SO$
  (\cref{thm:zf:multi}); and PA induction as the contrast (\cref{sec:zf}).
\item The method \DTRC{} and its theory: the need for a bound, the pigeonhole theorem, the main theorem under separation,
  the residue, sound merges, the necessity of depth relativization, noise and complexity; separation for PA, ZF and ZFC,
  and natural failures of it (\cref{sec:many}).
\item An implementation with baselines and experiments, including a reported failure (\cref{sec:exp}).
\item Corrections to earlier claims of this project, such as the refuted conjecture of linear escalation bounds
  (\cref{app:ver}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What is not achieved}\label{sec:intro:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Separation is assumed, not guaranteed.} It is proved for $\Q$ with induction and for the experimental
  mixtures, and computed for ZF and ZFC. It fails for some similar targets (\cref{lem:exp:valid,prop:many:univset}), no
  computable learner can know the separating depth (\cref{thm:many:depth}), and a characterization of separation in terms
  of the targets is open.
\item \emph{Refutation is one-sided and incomplete.} It never certifies that a template is the target
  (\cref{lem:setting:onesided}). Truth-sound over-generalizations are invisible to it (\cref{prop:zf:coll}), some unsound
  templates are never refuted by closed truths (\cref{thm:zf:K0}), and outside term-generated structures a false universal
  can have only true closed instances. Our oracles are incomplete and the implemented refuter has a budget, so ``not
  refuted'' certifies nothing.
\item \emph{Cost.} Exponentially many minimal templates, a coNP-complete union verifier for $k\ge3$, an NP-complete
  refutation test and quadratic escalations, all in the worst case. The implementation uses the restricted class $\DTF$
  and not the feature verifier, and the experiments are small and use synthetic data, not a corpus from a proof library.
\item \emph{Mistakes.} The theorems assume a valid practice and correct data. Unrefutable mistakes can be absorbed into a
  cluster or fragment it (\cref{prop:many:noise}), a frequent coherent family of mistakes cannot be told from a rule by
  positive data, and mistakes made the implementation accept a false sentence (\cref{prop:exp:qF}).
\item \emph{Rare targets.} A target seen once is never generalized, a single axiom is learned only if it occurs among the
  data, and an unrefuted singleton mistake looks like a single axiom (\cref{sec:exp:limits}).
\item \emph{Scope.} We learn which instances a community uses, not the theory they generate; from closed instances, the
  truth of $\forall x\varphi$ is certified only as far as the oracle reaches. Open problems remain in every part;
  \cref{sec:disc} collects them.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reader's guide}\label{sec:intro:guide}

\Cref{sec:setting} fixes the data, the template classes, matching, the protocol and refutation oracles. \Cref{sec:single}
is the theory of one determinate template, used by the sections after it. \Cref{sec:univ,sec:zf,sec:many} answer
Questions 1--3; each ends with a summary that can be read first
(\cref{sec:univ:summary,sec:zf:summary,sec:many:summary}). \Cref{sec:exp} reports the experiments, and \cref{sec:disc}
discusses the answers, related work and open problems. The appendices give the full proofs, one per section, and
\cref{app:ver} describes how the results were checked and what was corrected on the way. Theorem-like statements carry a
status (\textsf{proved}, \textsf{computed}, \textsf{known}, \textsf{conjecture}, or a combination) and a gray tag naming
the result in the research record of the accompanying repository, which holds the scripts, their outputs and the reports
of adversarial referees with independent code. Refuted conjectures stay labelled as refuted.
