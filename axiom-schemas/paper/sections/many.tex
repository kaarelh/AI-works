\section{Many axioms and schemas without labels}\label{sec:many}

The third question asks for one method that also learns ``many axiom( scheme)s at once, even if they do not come with
labels for instances of which axiom scheme they are''. The data now come from several targets
$\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{k'}$, and nothing says which datum is an instance of which target. This section develops the
theory of this setting and the method \DTRC{} (Determinate Templates with Refutation Clustering). The results come from
the research record of the ``untagged'' track, checked by an adversarial referee with independent code; the complexity
results of \cref{sec:many:cost} come from the ``single'' track. The record states several results under the hypothesis
that minimal covering templates are finitely many and lie below every covering template; that hypothesis is the
finitariness theorem of \cref{sec:single}, so we state the results without it. In brief:
\begin{itemize}
\item From positive data alone a bound $k$ on the number of targets is necessary, and spare slots are paid for in data
  diversity, which no sound negative evidence can replace (\cref{sec:many:bound}).
\item With the exact bound, negatives that refute every cross-target merge make untagged learning as easy as tagged
  learning, and the learner can find them itself (\cref{thm:many:pigeonhole}).
\item \DTRC{} needs no bound. Under \emph{refutation separation} it recovers the hidden labels, is sound at all times,
  and is exact once every target's data contain an anchor, with the tagged failure probability (\cref{thm:many:dtrc}).
  Without separation it is sound relative to a residue of unrefuted cross merges (\cref{thm:many:residue}).
  Learning $\forall x\varphi$ from instances is a sound cross merge (\cref{prop:many:forall}). The refutation depth
  cannot be eliminated by any computable learner (\cref{thm:many:depth}).
\item PA, ZF and ZFC are separated at small depth by $\Delta_0$ evaluation in $\N$, hereditarily finite counterexamples
  and logic; Russell's paradox separates the comprehension-shaped axioms. Weak forms of Union and Power merge into a
  false universal-set schema that only coherence refutes (\cref{sec:many:examples}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Setting}\label{sec:many:setting}

Data are formulas in closure-normal form with de Bruijn bound variables (\cref{sec:setting}), and templates are in
$\DT$. Parameters are treated as constants; the one place where sentences are identified up to renaming of parameters
(head classes, \cref{app:many:bound}) says so. Let $\manyS$ be the set of such formulas and $\manyTr\subseteq\manyS$ the
set of those whose universal closure is true in the intended structure ($\N$ or $V$).

\begin{definition}[practice, data, oracle, $k$-union learner]\label{def:many:setting}
\status{definition}\src{untagged \S1}
A \emph{practice} is a finite set $\Sigma^*=\{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{k'}\}\subseteq\DT$ of targets (ground templates,
i.e.\ single axioms, allowed), with $\manyRstar:=\bigcup_i\inst(\sigma_i)$; it is \emph{valid} if
$\manyRstar\subseteq\manyTr$, which we assume unless said otherwise. The data form a finite multiset $D\subseteq\manyRstar$
with \emph{hidden} labels $D=D_1\sqcup\dots\sqcup D_{k'}$, $D_i\subseteq\inst(\sigma_i)$ (a datum in two instance sets
carries one label). In the random model each datum
picks target $i$ with probability $\pi_i$ and then an instance $\sigma_i\theta$, $\theta\sim\Lambda_i$.
A \emph{refutation oracle} is a family $\Ref_0\subseteq\Ref_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\manyS$ with
\emph{(OS)} $\Ref_d\cap\manyTr=\emptyset$ for all $d$, and \emph{(Dec)} membership in $\Ref_d$ and the property
``$\inst(T)\cap\Ref_d\neq\emptyset$'' (``$T$ is \emph{$d$-refuted}'') are decidable uniformly in $d$, resp.\ $(T,d)$;
$\Ref_\infty:=\bigcup_d\Ref_d$. A fixed finite $N\subseteq\manyS\setminus\manyTr$, used as every $\Ref_d$, is an oracle.
For a finite set $N$ of negatives, $\manyVS_k(D,N):=\{h\in\Hk{k}:D\subseteq h,\ h\cap N=\emptyset\}$, where
$\Hk{k}=\{\bigcup_{l\le k}\inst(\tau_l):\tau_l\in\DT\}$, and the \emph{cautious $k$-union learner} accepts
$\Acc_k(D,N):=\bigcap\manyVS_k(D,N)$. For finite nonempty $X$, $\manyCov(X):=\{T\in\DT:X\subseteq\inst(T)\}$ and
$\Min(X)$ is the set of its $\gen$-minimal members up to renaming.
\end{definition}

The oracles are the refutation channels of \cref{sec:setting}, in these forms (all sound, none complete, all
\emph{target-blind}: their answers are a fixed function of the query).
\textbf{R-$\Delta_0$} at depth $d$, for sentences of size $\le d$: instantiate parameters by numerals $\le d$, evaluate
closed atoms and connectives, refute $\forall$ by a numeral counterexample $\le d$, verify $\exists$ by a witness
$\le d$, and verify $\forall$ (refute $\exists$) only by equality reasoning from the true closed equations of $\N$.
\textbf{R-HF}: hereditarily finite counterexamples to $\Pi_1$-shaped set-theoretic sentences, sound by $\Delta_0$
absoluteness. \textbf{R-logic}: a propositionally unsatisfiable set of ground instances of the Skolem form.
\textbf{R-coh} (coherence): R-logic applied together with designated true sentences.

\begin{proposition}[minimal covering templates]\label{prop:many:W}
\status{proved}\src{single Thm B}
For finite nonempty $X\subseteq\manyS$: (a) $\Min(X)$ is finite up to renaming and computable from $X$; (b) every
$T\in\manyCov(X)$ satisfies $T\gen T_0$, hence $\inst(T)\supseteq\inst(T_0)$, for some $T_0\in\Min(X)$; (c) if
$X\subseteq\inst(\sigma)$ with $\sigma\in\DT$, some $T_0\in\Min(X)$ has $\inst(T_0)\subseteq\inst(\sigma)$.
\end{proposition}

A finite $A\subseteq\inst(\sigma)$ is an \emph{anchor} for $\sigma$ if every $T\in\manyCov(A)$ has
$\inst(T)\supseteq\inst(\sigma)$. \Cref{sec:single} characterizes anchors by witness events (\evR{}+\evN{} for
$T_{\Ind}$ and the ZF schemas, Plotkin's \evR{}+\evD{} for first-order patterns); a ground $\sigma$ has the anchor
$\{\sigma\}$, and an anchor of a non-ground target has at least two elements.

\begin{lemma}[cautious soundness; finite form]\label{lem:many:cautious}
\status{proved; known in substance}\src{untagged Lemmas 1.1, 1.2}
(a) If $k\ge k'$ and $N\cap\manyRstar=\emptyset$, then $\manyRstar\in\manyVS_k(D,N)$, so $\Acc_k(D,N)\subseteq\manyRstar$;
$\Acc_k$ is monotone in $D$ and in $N$. (b) With $\Min_N(B):=\{T\in\Min(B):\inst(T)\cap N=\emptyset\}$,
$\Acc_k(D,N)$ is the intersection of the sets $\bigcup_{B\in\pi}\inst(T_B)$ over all partitions $\pi$ of $D$ into at most
$k$ nonempty blocks and all choices $T_B\in\Min_N(B)$. So for finite $N$ it is decidable (in time exponential in $|D|$).
\end{lemma}
\noindent Part (b) assigns each datum to the first slot covering it and applies \cref{prop:many:W}(b) to each block
(\cref{app:many:setting}).

\subsection{Positive data: a bound is necessary, and spare slots cost diversity}\label{sec:many:bound}

\begin{proposition}[a bound is necessary]\label{prop:many:bound}
\status{proved; known (Gold)}\src{untagged Prop 4.3}
Let $\Hk{\infty}:=\bigcup_k\Hk{k}$. For every finite $D\subseteq\manyRstar$ and every sound $N$, $\Acc_\infty(D,N)=D$.
No learner, however it uses a fixed sound oracle, identifies $\Hk{\infty}$ in the limit from text.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$D$ is a finite union of ground templates and avoids $N$. For the second claim take a true non-ground $\sigma$: the class
contains $\inst(\sigma)$ and all its finite subsets, all inside $\manyTr$, on which the oracle answers identically. So the
learner is a function of the text, and Gold's locking-sequence argument for superfinite classes applies
\citep{gold1967language}; it needs no computability.
\end{proof}

Negatives cannot replace a bound: every finite set of true data is itself a consistent hypothesis. Negatives remove
over-general \emph{lumps}; a bound forbids \emph{splits}, which replace a target by finitely many specializations that
together cover its data. Splits are sound, so one-sided refutation never removes them.

\begin{theorem}[splits; threshold]\label{thm:many:splits}
\status{proved}\src{untagged Thm 3.2}
(a) For every $N$ with $N\cap\manyRstar=\emptyset$ and $k\ge k'$, $\manyVS_k(D,N)$ contains every $h\in\Hk{k}$ with
$D\subseteq h\subseteq\manyRstar$. So if such an $h$ misses $q\in\manyRstar$, no sound negatives make the learner accept $q$.
(b) Suppose moreover that every $D_j$ is nonempty and that $N$ \emph{cross-separates} $D$: every template covering data of two targets meets
$N$. Put $m:=k-k'+1$. If every family of at most $m$ templates that covers $D_i$ and avoids $N$ has union
$\supseteq\inst(\sigma_i)$, then $\inst(\sigma_i)\subseteq\Acc_k(D,N)$. If some such family misses a $q\in\inst(\sigma_i)$
lying in no other target's instance set, then $q\notin\Acc_k(D,N)$. With pairwise disjoint instance sets this is an exact
threshold.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} (a) $h\cap N\subseteq\manyRstar\cap N=\emptyset$. (b) Cross-separation makes the slots used by
different targets disjoint, so target $i$ has at most $m$; conversely the bad family plus the other $k'-1$ targets is a
$k$-slot hypothesis (\cref{app:many:bound}).\medskip

In general a target's slots may be shared with others, provided every shared slot contains the whole target.

\begin{theorem}[slot accounting]\label{thm:many:slots}
\status{proved}\src{untagged Thm 4.1}
Fix a target $i$, a bound $k$ and a sound $N$, and let $\bar D_i:=D\setminus D_i$. Assume (Abs): every $N$-avoiding
template covering a datum of $D_i$ and one of $\bar D_i$ contains $\inst(\sigma_i)$. Let $c_i$ be the least number of
$N$-avoiding templates not containing $\inst(\sigma_i)$ that cover $\bar D_i$. (a) If every family of at most $k-c_i$
$N$-avoiding templates covering $D_i$ has union $\supseteq\inst(\sigma_i)$, then $\inst(\sigma_i)\subseteq\Acc_k(D,N)$.
(b) If some such family and some optimal cover of $\bar D_i$ both miss $q\in\inst(\sigma_i)$, then $q\notin\Acc_k(D,N)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[slot accounting for PA]\label{cor:many:paslots}
\status{proved; computed (consistency check)}\src{untagged Cor 4.2}
Let the targets be $\Q$'s axioms Q1--Q7 as ground sentences in closure-normal form ($\neg Sp=0$; $Sp=Sp'\to p=p'$;
$p+0=p$; $p+Sp'=S(p+p')$; $p\cdot0=0$; $p\cdot Sp'=p\cdot p'+p$; $\neg p=0\to\exists y\,p=Sy$) and $T_{\Ind}$, so
$k'=8$. (Abs) holds for induction for every motive: a template covering a $\Q$-axiom and an induction instance is $F_0$,
or $F_0\to F_1$ for Q2 and Q7, and both contain $\inst(T_{\Ind})$. On positive data $c=4$ (Q3--Q6 share $z_0=z_1$; Q1,
Q2, Q7 stay alone), so at $k=8$ induction gets four slots: an induction instance whose motive has $x$ free and a main
connective unseen in the data is accepted iff the induction data cannot be split into four blocks each root-homogeneous
or all-vacuous (without vacuous motives: iff at least five main connectives occur). With the three negatives $0=S0$, $(p+0)=0$, $(p\cdot0)=S0$,
$c=7$, induction gets one slot, and the tagged anchor condition \evR{}+\evN{} suffices. (In the Sub-encoded presentation
of induction analysed in the prior work of this project, $c=1$.)
\end{corollary}
The computed check of $c=4$ and $c=7$ (24/24 cases) holds by construction given (Abs) and the anchor theorem for
$T_{\Ind}$, so it is a consistency check of the implementation, not independent evidence (as the referee noted).

\begin{remark}[head classes and numerals]\label{rem:many:numerals}
\status{proved; computed}\src{untagged U17}
Because data are read under universal closure, a one-variable instance schema $\varphi(z)$ is covered by its $h=|F|+p+1$
head specializations ($F$ the function symbols, $p$ the number of parameters of $\varphi$; $5+p$ in arithmetic), one per head
class of the value of $z$, a parameter outside $\varphi$ being one class (\cref{prop:many:heads}). So with $m\ge h$
spare slots the ``failure family'' covers everything. (An earlier version of the record claimed the contrary, counting
renamed parameters as different sentences; the referee's counterexample led to this correction.) For quantifier-free,
parameter-free $\varphi$ the exact threshold is known for $m\le h$ (\cref{prop:many:onevar}) and, over a unary
signature, in closed form (\cref{prop:many:unary}); in particular \emph{numeral-only data never make the cautious
$k$-union learner accept $\forall x\varphi$ with one spare slot}: the sound split $\{\varphi(0)\}\cup\inst(\varphi(Su))$
misses $\varphi(a)$. Brute-force checks agree with all three propositions (2000/2000, 60/60, 254/254, 2509/2509 cases).
Beyond this the spare-slot threshold is open (binary symbols with $m>h$, several metavariables, templates with
parameters, $T_{\Ind}$ with $m\ge8$, where the eight root specializations already cover $\inst(T_{\Ind})$).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The pigeonhole theorem}\label{sec:many:pigeon}

\begin{theorem}[pigeonhole]\label{thm:many:pigeonhole}
\status{proved}\src{untagged Thm 2.1}
Let $k=k'$, $D\subseteq\manyRstar$, $A_i\subseteq D_i$ a nonempty anchor for $\sigma_i$, and $N\cap\manyRstar=\emptyset$ with
\emph{(X)}: for $i\neq j$, $a\in A_i$, $b\in A_j$, every $T\in\DT$ with $a,b\in\inst(T)$ meets $N$. Then
$\Acc_k(D,N)=\manyRstar$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The inclusion $\subseteq$ is \cref{lem:many:cautious}. Let $h=\bigcup_{l\le k}\inst(\tau_l)$ be in
$\manyVS_k(D,N)$, and let $L_i$ be the set of slots $l$ such that $\tau_l$ covers an element of $A_i$. The $L_i$ are nonempty and, by (X), pairwise disjoint; $k'$ of them
in $k'$ slots are singletons $\{l_i\}$, so $\tau_{l_i}$ covers $A_i$ and $\inst(\tau_{l_i})\supseteq\inst(\sigma_i)$.
\end{proof}

Only anchor data matter, and (X) forces the anchors to be disjoint. The negatives need no labels: let
$N(D,d)$ contain the first $d$-refuted instance of every $d$-refuted $T\in\Min(\{a,b\})$, over all pairs $a,b\in D$. It is
sound, and if every minimal template of every cross pair of anchor data is $d$-refuted (\emph{anchor-pair separation}),
it satisfies (X) by \cref{prop:many:W}(b). Hence $\Acc_{k'}(D,N(D,d))$ is sound at all times, decidable, and exact as soon
as $D$ contains anchors; at most $\mu\sum_{i<j}|A_i||A_j|$ negatives are needed, $\mu$ the largest $|\Min(\{a,b\})|$
(\cref{cor:many:selfneg}).
For PA (raw $\DT$ encoding), every cross pair among Q1--Q7 and $T_{\Ind}$ has exactly one minimal covering template
($F_0$, $F_0\to F_1$, $z_0=z_1$, $p+z_0=z_1$ or $p\cdot z_0=z_1$), and four sentences refute them all: $0=S0$,
$0=0\to0=S0$, $p+0=0$ and $p\cdot0=S0$ (computed; the referee's independent enumeration of all 68 covering templates of size
$\le14$ of the 49 PA cross pairs agrees). So at $k=8$ the untagged learner is exact after one occurrence of each
$\Q$-axiom and two induction instances with different main connectives, one of them non-vacuous.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\DTRC}{DTRC}: Determinate Templates with Refutation Clustering}\label{sec:many:dtrc}

\begin{definition}[coherence, \DTRC, separation]\label{def:many:dtrc}
\status{definition}\src{untagged \S\S5.1--5.2}
A finite $X\subseteq\manyS$ is \emph{$d$-coherent} if some $T\in\Min(X)$ is not $d$-refuted (equivalently, by
\cref{prop:many:W}, some covering template is not). \textbf{Algorithm $\DTRC_d$}: (1) normalize the data (closure-normal
form, canonical parameter names, de Bruijn indices); (2) starting from singleton clusters, repeatedly merge two clusters
whose union is $d$-coherent, chosen by a \emph{merge policy} (e.g.\ online: each datum joins the first cluster it is
coherent with, then pairwise merges to a fixpoint), until no pair has a coherent union; (3) for each final cluster $C$
let $\Min^+_d(C)$ be its unrefuted minimal templates and $\Acc_d(C):=\bigcap_{T\in\Min^+_d(C)}\inst(T)$ ($\emptyset$ if
there are none); (4) assert $\Acc_d:=\bigcup_C\Acc_d(C)$.
The practice is \emph{$d$-separated on $D$}, $\RS_d(D)$, if every template covering data of two different targets is
$d$-refuted, and \emph{globally} $d$-separated if every template covering instances of two different targets is.
\end{definition}

$\Acc_d(C)$ is the cautious single-template verifier of $C$ with negatives $\Ref_d$. Coherence is downward closed and
antitone in $d$ (\cref{lem:many:mono}). By \cref{prop:many:W}, $\RS_d(D)$ holds iff every minimal template of every cross
pair $(a,b)\in D_i\times D_j$ is $d$-refuted, a finite check that the implementation performs after each run.

\begin{theorem}[\DTRC]\label{thm:many:dtrc}
\status{proved}\src{untagged Thm 5.2}
Let the practice be valid and the oracle satisfy (OS), (Dec).
(i) \emph{Within-target merges are never refuted}: every $X\subseteq\inst(\sigma_i)$ is $d$-coherent for every $d$.
(ii) \emph{Separation recovers the labels}: under $\RS_d(D)$ the final clustering is $\{D_i:D_i\neq\emptyset\}$ for every
merge policy.
(iii) \emph{Soundness at all times, exactness from anchors}: under $\RS_d(D)$, $\Acc_d\subseteq\manyRstar$, and
$\Acc_d=\manyRstar$ once each $D_i$ contains an anchor of $\sigma_i$.
(iv) \emph{Rates}: under global separation and i.i.d.\ data, $\Pr[\Acc_d\neq\manyRstar]\le\sum_i\Pr[D_i\text{ contains no
anchor}]$, the failure probability of the tagged learner, bounded explicitly in \cref{sec:single}. (For $T_{\Ind}$ with $n$
induction data and i.i.d.\ motives this is $\sum_fp_f^n+(1-q)^n-\sum_fr_f^n$, with $p_f$ the probability of main
connective $f$, $q$ that of $x$ occurring free, $r_f$ that of root $f$ with $x$ not free; see \cref{sec:zf}.)
(v) \emph{Tests}: with $n$ distinct data the pairwise fixpoint makes $O(n^2)$ coherence tests; under $\RS_d(D)$ the online
policy makes at most $nk'+k'(k'-1)/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
(i) Some $T_0\in\Min(X)$ has $\inst(T_0)\subseteq\inst(\sigma_i)\subseteq\manyTr$ (\cref{prop:many:W}(c)), and (OS) never
refutes it. (ii) Pure clusters of different targets never merge, by separation; two final clusters of one target would
merge, by (i). (iii) In the cluster $D_i$ the template $T_0$ of (i) is intersected; with an anchor, every member of
$\Min(D_i)$ contains $\inst(\sigma_i)$. Details in \cref{app:many:dtrc}.
\end{proof}

Under separation, refutation does the job that citations do in tagged data: it says which instances belong together.
Within each recovered cluster the learner \emph{is} the tagged cautious learner, so tagged anchors and rates
(\cref{sec:single}) transfer verbatim, and the number of targets is discovered, not given. The inequality in (iv) can be
strict, since refuted non-target templates drop out before an anchor arrives. When a bound $k\ge k'$ is known, accepting
only what both \DTRC{} and $\Acc_k(D,N(D,d))$ accept is target-sound at all times, with or without separation, and exact
under $\RS_d(D)$ with $k=k'$ once every $D_i$ contains an anchor, because $\RS_d(D)$ implies (X) for $N(D,d)$
(\cref{rem:many:combine}).

\subsection{Without separation: residue, sound merges, fragmentation}\label{sec:many:residue}

\begin{theorem}[soundness relative to a residue]\label{thm:many:residue}
\status{proved; computed}\src{untagged Thm 5.4}
Call a final cluster \emph{mixed} if it contains data of two or more targets, and let
$\Res_d(D):=\bigcup\{\inst(T):T\in\Min(X)\text{ not }d\text{-refuted},\ X\subseteq D\text{ $d$-coherent and mixed}\}$.
(a) $\Acc_d\subseteq\manyRstar\cup\Res_d(D)$. (b) If every mixed final cluster $C$ has a truth-sound $T\in\Min_d^+(C)$
(a \emph{sound merge}), then $\Acc_d\subseteq\manyTr$: truth-sound, though not target-sound. (c) Otherwise $\Acc_d$ is sound
only relative to $\Res_d(D)$, which decreases in $d$; $\Res_\infty(D)$ consists of templates with no refutable instance.
(d) Exactness can fail even on clean data with an anchor in every $D_i$ and disjoint instance sets: a sound cross merge
can absorb part of an anchor (\emph{fragmentation}), depending on the merge policy.
\end{theorem}

For (d), let $\sigma_1=(z+0=z)$, $g=(0+S0=S0)$ a ground target and $D=\{0+0=0,\ S0+0=S0,\ g\}$; the first two data are an
anchor of $\sigma_1$. In the order $g,0+0=0,S0+0=S0$, the pair $\{g,0+0=0\}$ merges through the \emph{true} template
$0+z=z$; then $S0+0=S0$ cannot join, since the three data have the single minimal template $z_0+z_1=z_2$, refuted by
$0+0=S0$. The output rejects $p+0=p$ and accepts the true non-target $0+p=p$; in the order $0+0=0,S0+0=S0,g$ it is
exact (computed). This failure is intrinsic:

\begin{proposition}[ambiguous practices]\label{prop:many:ambiguity}
\status{proved; computed}\src{untagged Rem 5.11}
For the valid practices $P=\{z+0=z,\ 0+S0=S0\}$ and $P'=\{0+z=z,\ S0+0=S0\}$, the data $D=\{0+0=0,\ S0+0=S0,\
0+S0=S0\}$ lie in $\manyRstar_P\cap\manyRstar_{P'}$ and contain anchors for both. No learner (computable or not) with a target-blind oracle is on $D$ both
exact for $P$ and target-sound for $P'$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Anchors: $\{0+0=0,S0+0=S0\}$, $\{0+S0=S0\}$ for $P$; $\{0+0=0,0+S0=S0\}$, $\{S0+0=S0\}$ for $P'$. The learner's output on
$D$ is the same under both; exactness for $P$ needs $p+0=p$, which is not in $\manyRstar_{P'}$.
\end{proof}
Only separating data such as $SS0+0=SS0\in\manyRstar_P\setminus\manyRstar_{P'}$ resolve this; the $k'$-union learner with
self-generated negatives is not exact on this $D$ either (computed), since the true template $0+z=z$ violates (X).
Residual merges come in three kinds: \emph{target-sound} ones (a redundant axiom that is an instance of a target schema,
e.g.\ Empty Set as an instance of Separation); \emph{sound} ones (true templates below no target, as for $\forall x\varphi$
below); and \emph{unsound unrefuted} ones (the $K_0$-shaped templates of induction and the universal-set schema,
\cref{sec:many:examples}).

\subsection{Learning \texorpdfstring{$\forall x\varphi$}{forall x phi} from instances as a sound merge}\label{sec:many:forall}

Regard each instance $\varphi(t)$ as its own ground target. The universal sentence is learned when \DTRC{} merges the
instances.

\begin{lemma}[two-point anchors]\label{lem:many:twopoint}
\status{proved}\src{untagged Lem 5.5}
Let $\varphi(z)$ have a single metavariable, a 0-ary $z$ (term or formula sort) occurring in it. If closed $t_1,t_2$ have
different head symbols, $\{\varphi(t_1),\varphi(t_2)\}$ is an anchor for $\varphi(z)$ in $\DT$. So for every set of
instances of $\varphi(z)$ containing two with different heads, $\inst(\varphi(z))$ is contained in the instance set of every
covering template.
\end{lemma}
\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} If $G(z),K(z)$ contain $z$ only as a leaf and agree at two closed values with different heads,
then $G=K$. This forces every occurrence of a covering template to agree with $\varphi(z)$ (\cref{app:many:dtrc}). It is
the case of the anchor theorem of \cref{sec:single} in which the no-coincidence event \evU{} holds automatically.

\begin{proposition}[universals from instances]\label{prop:many:forall}
\status{proved; computed}\src{untagged Prop 5.6}
Let $z$ be a term metavariable and $D=\{\varphi(t_1),\dots,\varphi(t_n)\}$ closed instances, two with different heads.
(a) $D$ is $d$-coherent iff $\varphi(z)$ is not $d$-refuted. (b) $\inst(\varphi(z))$ contains $\varphi(a)$ for a
parameter $a$ fresh for $\varphi$, which under the closure reading is $\forall x\varphi(x)$ (in parameter form; the string
$\forall x\varphi$ is a different datum, \cref{sec:setting}); so $\varphi(z)$ is truth-sound iff $\forall x\varphi$ is
true. (c) If $\forall x\varphi$ is true, every subset of $D$ is coherent, \DTRC{} forms one cluster under every policy,
and $\Acc_d\ni\varphi(a)$: the axiom is learned and nothing false accepted. (Target-soundness for the $n$ ground
``targets'' fails, as it must: the instances and the axiom give the same data and oracle answers.) (d) If $\forall
x\varphi$ is false and some instance of $\varphi(z)$ is $d$-refuted, the merge is refused, though root-homogeneous subsets
may still merge into an unrefuted specialization such as $\varphi(Sz)$. (e) If the oracle refutes every false instance of
$\varphi(z)$ at some depth, ``never refuted'' means ``$\forall x\varphi$ true''; R-$\Delta_0$ does so for quantifier-free
$\varphi$ (\cref{prop:many:qf}) but not for quantified $\varphi$. At a finite budget, false universals with large least
counterexamples merge (part of $\Res_d$). An oracle that only evaluates closed instances certifies ``true at every closed
term'': in $\N$ this is the $\omega$-rule, sound but not derivable ($\Q\vdash0+\num n=\num n$ for each $n$, $\Q\nvdash\forall
x(0+x=x)$); in the field $\R$ with $0,1,+,\cdot$, where closed terms denote natural numbers, $\neg(z\cdot z=1+1)$ is an
unsound unrefuted merge.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{sec:univ} characterizes when closed instances decide universals (the Tarski--Vaught criterion). Computed: numeral
instances of Q3--Q5, as 12 ground targets, form three clusters with templates $z_0+0=z_0$, $z_0\cdot0=0$,
$z_0+Sz_1=S(z_0+z_1)$, and \DTRC{} accepts $p+0=p$; instances of the false $n\cdot n=n$ ($n=0,1$) stay apart (refuted
at $2$); $\neg(n\cdot n=2)$, $n\le2$, merge and the true $\neg(p\cdot p=2)$ is accepted.

\begin{proposition}[quantifier-free templates]\label{prop:many:qf}
\status{proved}\src{untagged Prop 5.13}
Let $T\in\DT$ be an arithmetic template with no binder in its rigid part (so all its metavariables are 0-ary; formula
metavariables may still take quantified closed formulas). If $T$ has a false instance, it has a false quantifier-free
instance, which R-$\Delta_0$ refutes at some depth. Hence every such template in $\Res_\infty$ (for R-$\Delta_0$) is
truth-sound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $T\theta$ be false at numerals $\bar n$ for its parameters. Keep the term values of $\theta$ and replace each formula
value $\theta(F)$ by $0=0$ or $0=S0$ according to the truth value of $\theta(F)[\bar n]$. The result $T\theta'$ is
quantifier-free and false at $\bar n$, since the rigid part is a Boolean combination of atoms over unchanged terms and of
metavariable occurrences with unchanged truth values. R-$\Delta_0$ at depth $\ge\max(\bar n,|T\theta'|)$ refutes it.
\end{proof}

So in the untagged setting the first question has a positive answer by a sound merge. What certifies the universal is the
absence of a counterexample, not a derivation; that is truth-sound in $\N$ for quantifier-free $\varphi$ with R-$\Delta_0$,
and otherwise only relative to the strength of the oracle. Unsound permanent residues in arithmetic need quantifiers in
the template.

\subsection{Depth relativization is necessary}\label{sec:many:depth}

\begin{theorem}[depth relativization]\label{thm:many:depth}
\status{proved}\src{untagged Thm 5.7}
There is a uniformly computable family of practices (finite sets of $\DT$ targets in arithmetic, each with a data law,
all with the oracle R-$\Delta_0$) such that no computable learner achieves, for every valid practice $\mathcal P$ of the
family and some $\delta<\frac12$, both
(a) $\Pr[\exists t:\Acc_t\not\subseteq\manyRstar_{\mathcal P}\cup\Res_\infty(\mathcal P)]\le\delta$, where
$\Res_\infty(\mathcal P)$ is the union of the instance sets of templates that cover instances (in the support of the data
law) of two different targets of $\mathcal P$ and have no refutable instance; and
(b) if $\mathcal P$ is separated at some finite depth and each target's data law yields an anchor with positive
probability, then $\Pr[\exists t\,\forall t'\ge t:\Acc_{t'}=\manyRstar_{\mathcal P}]\ge1-\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
By MRDP \citep{davis1961decision,matiyasevich1993hilbert}, $e\in K$ (the halting set) iff $\exists\bar x\,P(e,\bar x)=
Q(e,\bar x)$ for fixed polynomials. Let $T_e:=\neg\exists\bar x(z=SS(x_1+\dots+x_k)\wedge P_e(\bar x)=Q_e(\bar x))$,
$\mathcal P_e:=\{T_e\}$, and $\mathcal P'_e:=\{T_e[z:=0],T_e[z:=S0]\}$ (two true ground targets), both with data uniform on
$\mathcal P'_e$, an anchor of $T_e$. If $e\in K$, R-$\Delta_0$ refutes an instance of $T_e$ by exhibiting the witnesses, so
$\mathcal P'_e$ is separated with empty residue and (a) forbids accepting $q_e:=T_e[z:=SS0]$. If $e\notin K$, $\mathcal P_e$
is valid and separated, so (b) forces eventual acceptance of $q_e$. Then ``$q_e$ is accepted by some time with probability $>\frac12$'' would be a $\Sigma_1$
definition of $\omega\setminus K$ (\cref{app:many:dtrc}).
\end{proof}

The obstruction is the $\Pi_1$-completeness of ``this merge is never refuted'', not lack of information. \DTRC{} with a
depth schedule $d_t\to\infty$ meets (b), is sound relative to $\Res_{d_t}$ at time $t$ and to $\Res_\infty$ in the limit,
and changes its mind finitely often on every separated practice. This is the form of soundness of the parent report
\citep{claude2026whatfollows}, and the theorem shows it cannot be improved.

\subsection{Whole-cluster tests and noise}\label{sec:many:linkage}

\begin{proposition}[whole-cluster versus pairwise tests]\label{prop:many:linkage}
\status{proved; computed}\src{untagged Prop 5.8}
Coherence is downward closed but not transitive. Every cluster built by \DTRC{} is coherent at all times (except a
refutable singleton, which contributes nothing), whereas single linkage on the pairwise-coherence graph can build clusters
with no unrefuted minimal template. Under $\RS_d(D)$, the coherent subsets of $D$ are exactly the subsets of the $D_i$, and
single linkage, complete linkage and \DTRC{} agree. Without separation, \DTRC{} returns some maximal partition into
coherent blocks, which may depend on the policy.
\end{proposition}
Example: $a=(0+0=0)$, $b=(S0+0=S0)$, $c=(0+S0=S0)$. The pairs $\{a,b\}$, $\{a,c\}$ have the unrefuted templates
$z_0+0=z_0$, $0+z_0=z_0$, while $\{b,c\}$ and $\{a,b,c\}$ have only $z_0+z_1=S0$, resp.\ $z_0+z_1=z_2$, refuted by
$0+0=S0$. Single linkage lumps all three; \DTRC{} returns $\{\{a,b\},\{c\}\}$ or $\{\{a,c\},\{b\}\}$.

\paragraph{Noise.} Let $D=D_{\mathrm{clean}}\cup E$ with mistakes $E\cap\manyRstar=\emptyset$.

\begin{proposition}[noise]\label{prop:many:noise}
\status{proved; computed}\src{untagged Prop 5.9}
(a) A refutable mistake stays an incoherent singleton. (b) Under $\RS_d(D_{\mathrm{clean}})$ no coherent set contains clean
data of two targets, so mistakes never bridge targets. (c) An unrefutable mistake can be absorbed into a target's cluster
and make it unsound, and (d) it can \emph{fragment} the target: $C\cup\{m\}\cup C'$ can be incoherent although $C\cup C'$
is coherent. (e) For $|C|>e$ the \emph{trimmed verifier} $\Acc_d^e(C):=\bigcap\{\inst(T):T\text{ not }d\text{-refuted},\
|C\setminus\inst(T)|\le e\}$ is contained in $\inst(\sigma_i)$ when $C$ has at most $e$ mistakes and its clean part lies in
$D_i$, and equals $\inst(\sigma_i)$ when moreover every subset of $C$ of size $\ge|C|-e$ contains an anchor.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Robust \DTRC} asserts only clusters of multiplicity $\ge s>e$, verified with $\Acc^e_d$. If $\RS_d(D_{\mathrm{clean}})$
holds, every mistake is refutable or \emph{noise-separated} (every template covering it and a clean datum is refuted), and
every coherent set of mistakes has multiplicity $<s$, then the asserted clusters are exactly the $D_i$ of multiplicity
$\ge s$, verified soundly, and exactly once every subset of $D_i$ of size $\ge|D_i|-e$ contains an anchor. Fragmentation
is not prevented, and a frequent coherent family of mistakes is a systematic error that no positive-data method can tell
from a rule. A computed example on PA (\cref{ex:many:noise}) shows absorption of the false instance
$J^*_0=(0+0=0\wedge\forall x(0+x=0\to0+Sx=S0))\to\forall x(0+x=0)$ into a non-diverse induction cluster, its rejection by
the trimmed verifier, and order-dependent fragmentation.

\subsection{Cost: the union verifier and the refutation test}\label{sec:many:cost}

\Cref{thm:many:dtrc}(v) counts coherence tests. A test needs at most $|\Min(X)|$ refutation checks, and $|\Min(X)|$ can be
$4^n$ for two data of size $O(n)$ (\cref{sec:single}). It is $1$, and the test polynomial, when $X$ is quantifier-free
(Plotkin's lgg) or contains an anchor (under \Rich), and for a single negative $n$ the test is ``$n\notin\Acc(X)$'',
polynomial by the feature verifier of \cref{sec:single} (\cref{prop:many:testcost}). In every test of the PA and ZF runs
below, $|\Min(X)|=1$. In general both the exact union verifier and the refutation test are hard; write
$\Acc_k(D):=\Acc_k(D,\emptyset)$.

\begin{theorem}[complexity]\label{thm:many:complexity}
\status{proved; computed}\src{single Thm H}
(a) For $k\le2$, ``$q\in\Acc_k(D)$'' is decidable in polynomial time (2-SAT). (b) For every fixed $k\ge3$ it is
coNP-complete (from graph $k$-colourability). (c) With $k$ in the input it is coNP-complete already for quantifier-free
data over $\wedge,=,S,0$ (from Set Cover); for binder-free data and fixed $k$ it is polynomial. (d) The refutation test of
\DTRC{} step~2, ``does some $T\in\Min(X)$ avoid a given finite set $R$?'', is NP-complete, already for $|X|=2$ with one
binder (from CNF-SAT); it is polynomial when $X$ has a least covering template, in particular when $X$ contains an anchor
(under \Rich). $R\cap\Acc(X)\neq\emptyset$ is a polynomial sufficient condition for blocking a merge.
\end{theorem}
The proofs (\cref{app:many:cost}) describe the sets $B$ with $q\notin\Acc(B)$ by polynomially many \emph{failure types}.
These results were added to the single track's record after its referee round and were not refereed independently; they
are worst-case results. In the hardness proof of (d) one datum is false; whether the test stays hard when $X$ is
true and $R$ false in a fixed structure, as in \DTRC, is open.

\subsection{Without negatives: MDL and the size principle}\label{sec:many:mdl}

Could compression replace refutation? Determinacy makes a two-part code well defined: each datum is coded by a covering
template and its unique matcher. For $\Q$ plus induction, the question is whether MDL keeps $T_{\Ind}$ whole or splits it
by the main connective of the motive. A naive code (symbols at $\log_2$ of the alphabet size, two-part code for the
labels) prefers the split by a margin that grows linearly in the number of induction data
(\cref{prop:many:mdlnaive}). A well-specified code gains at most $O(\log n)$ from splitting
(\cref{prop:many:mdlwell}), which does not show that it selects the target partition. Computations with adaptive codes
(\cref{tab:many:mdl}) show that under a natural usage law even depth-aware codes split for $n\ge1.6\cdot10^4$, because
usage really differs across connectives, while under a law for which the code is well specified it keeps $T_{\Ind}$
whole. \textbf{MDL tracks the statistics of usage, not the logical boundaries of schemas.} For schemas it errs towards
incompleteness (a split never accepts induction on an unused connective); for rarely used ground axioms it errs towards
unsoundness (two ground axioms used $m$ times each merge into their lgg once $m$ times the body cost is below the
template saving, and $0+0=0$, $0\cdot0=0$ merge into a false template). The refutation test of \DTRC{} catches the second
error, and the anchor condition controls the first. This is the size principle \citep{tenenbaum2001generalization} and
MDL \citep{rissanen1978modeling} meeting a hypothesis space whose boundaries are logical rather than statistical.

\subsection{Natural examples: PA, ZF and ZFC}\label{sec:many:examples}

The computations use the untagged track's prototype implementation of \DTRC{} (separate from the package of
\cref{sec:exp}). After the referee found that its first version decided refutation by template-specific searches, which
is not monotone in $\gen$, it was changed to keep a global set of refuted sentences and to audit each run; a run that
passes the audit \emph{is} $\DTRC_d$ with a fixed finite oracle, so the theorems above apply to it verbatim
(\cref{prop:many:audit}). Every run passed on the first pass, and no result changed.

\paragraph{PA.} Every cross merge among Q1--Q7 and $T_{\Ind}$, and among the instance schemas of $\Q$'s axioms (the
alternative presentation of $\Q$ by closed instances), is refuted by R-$\Delta_0$ (\cref{tab:many:pacross}); for Q1--Q7 and
$T_{\Ind}$ this global separation is proved, for the instance schemas it is supported by samples. On three seeds of 40 shuffled unlabelled data, \DTRC{} returned one pure cluster per
target present (7 or 8); the induction cluster's only unrefuted minimal template was $T_{\Ind}$; 200/200 held-out
induction instances and 0 of 91 non-instances (near misses) were accepted; separation on the data was verified for all
147, 201 and 175 cross pairs. Induction is never at risk of a cross merge, because every template covering a $\Q$-axiom
and an induction instance is absorbing and refuted; it is learned incompletely only for lack of diversity in its own
data, as in the tagged case.

\paragraph{ZF and ZFC.} The targets are Ext, Pair, Union, Power, Inf, Found, Separation $\exists b\forall c(c\in
b\leftrightarrow c\in p\wedge P(c))$, Replacement with $\exists!$ spelled out, and $\in$-induction, in closure-normal
de Bruijn form; the three schemas are Miller patterns (\cref{sec:zf}). All 36 cross pairs have a unique minimal covering
template, refuted by an HF counterexample (22 pairs: $F_0$ or $F_0\to F_1$), by pure logic (14 pairs, e.g.\ $\exists
xF_0(x)$), and in three of the latter by \textbf{Russell's paradox}: Union--Power, Union--Separation and Power--Separation
all have the minimal template $\exists x\forall y(y\in x\leftrightarrow F_0(y))$, naive comprehension, whose instance
$\exists x\forall y(y\in x\leftrightarrow\neg y\in y)$ is logically false (\cref{ex:many:zfcross}; the referee's
independent enumeration of 598 covering templates agrees). On three seeds of 45 data \DTRC{} returned 9 pure clusters, each
schema cluster exact (180/180 held-out instances), and 0/30 instances of naive comprehension accepted: Separation without
its guard is not learned. The Axiom of Choice is one more ground axiom: its 12 cross templates are all refuted, and on ZFC
data (three seeds of 50) \DTRC{} returned 10 pure clusters with AC accepted, its anchor being $\{\AC\}$
(\cref{ex:many:zf}). So the ZFC axioms are separated by the two refutation channels of the setting, and Russell's paradox
does the work exactly where the axioms look alike. \Cref{sec:exp} reports an independent implementation with the same
picture on PA and ZF mixtures.

\paragraph{Where separation fails.} \emph{Sound merges}---$\forall x\varphi$ from instances, redundant targets, instances
shared by two true generalizations---cannot and should not be prevented; they are why \DTRC{} is truth-sound rather than
target-sound without separation. \emph{An unsound merge that only coherence refutes}: in bounding form,
$\mathrm{Union}_W=\exists b\forall c(\exists d(d\in p\wedge c\in d)\to c\in b)$ and
$\mathrm{Power}_W=\exists b\forall c(\forall d(d\in c\to d\in p)\to c\in b)$ have as unique minimal covering template the
\emph{universal-set schema} $U(F)=\exists x\forall y(F(y)\to y\in x)$, whose instance $\exists x\forall y(y=y\to y\in x)$
is false.

\begin{proposition}[the universal-set schema]\label{prop:many:univset}
\status{proved; computed}\src{untagged Prop 7.1}
No instance of $U$ is refuted by R-logic, by R-HF, or by any oracle sound for the structure $M_0=\HF\cup\{u\}$ with $\in$ as
in $\HF$ on $\HF$ and $y\in u$ for all $y\in M_0$. The template is refuted by coherence, together with the true $\Pi_1$
sentence $\forall x\,\neg x\in x$ or with the closure of one Separation instance.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every instance holds in $M_0$ with $x:=u$; $\HF$ is a transitive part of $M_0$, so R-HF's steps ($\Delta_0$ facts about
$\HF$, HF witnesses and counterexamples) are sound in $M_0$; R-logic refutes only logical falsehoods. A witness $b$ of
$\exists x\forall y(y=y\to y\in x)$ gives $b\in b$; or $r=\{y\in b:y\notin y\}$ gives $r\in r\leftrightarrow r\notin r$.
\end{proof}
With logic and HF only, \DTRC{} merges $\mathrm{Union}_W$ and $\mathrm{Power}_W$ and \emph{accepts the false
universal-set sentence} (computed), an unsoundness inside the residue. The learned Separation cluster accepts Russell's
instance of Separation; designating its closure as true (\emph{bootstrapped coherence}) and re-running gives 9 pure
clusters, with the universal set rejected. Bootstrapping is sound exactly when the trusted clusters are pure, and circular
otherwise. \emph{$K_0$}: the first-order lgg $K_0$ of $\Ind(0+x=x)$ and $\Ind(S0+x=Sx)$ has only instances that are
jointly consistent with the true quantifier-free sentences of $\N$ (a result of the prior work's referee), so R-$\Delta_0$
and logic refute none of them, although $J^*_0$ is one. In $\DT$ the pair has two minimal templates, the
$T_{\Ind}$-specialization with motive $\lambda x.(f(0)+x=f(x))$ and a $K_0$-shaped one (confirmed over 6258 covering
templates); because the verifier intersects both, $J^*_0$ is rejected. The $K_0$-shaped template harms only when the sound
alternative disappears (absorption of $J^*_0$, \cref{ex:many:noise}); coherence with designated or learned $\Q$ refutes
it. \emph{Permanent residue}: templates whose false instances are all consistent with the designated truths and
$\Delta_0$-correct (e.g.\ families containing $\neg\Con(\PA)$-like $\Sigma_1$ sentences) are never refuted, and by
\cref{thm:many:depth} no computable learner can tell in general whether a merge will be. In arithmetic they need
quantifiers in the template (\cref{prop:many:qf}); a natural merge of this kind was not found (open). \Cref{sec:exp}
reports a budget effect of the same type: with injected mistakes and a small refutation budget, two mistakes merged into a
false template and a false sentence was accepted; a larger budget refuted the merge.

\subsection{Summary: the answer to the third question}\label{sec:many:summary}

\DTRC{} answers the third question, with four amendments to the method first proposed, each forced by a result above:
(1) test \emph{whole clusters} and intersect \emph{all} unrefuted minimal templates (coherence is not transitive, and
$\DT$ is not unitary: a $K_0$-shaped template can sit next to the sound one); (2) keep the refutation depth as a parameter
and re-cluster as it grows (\cref{thm:many:depth}); (3) when a bound $k\ge k'$ is known, intersect with the $k$-union
learner fed with \DTRC's own negatives, which restores target-soundness without separation and, at $k=k'$ under
separation, loses no exactness once anchors are present, while spare slots cost diversity (\cref{rem:many:numerals}) and ambiguous data defeat every learner
(\cref{prop:many:ambiguity}); (4) implement refutation as a growing set of refuted sentences and audit the run
(\cref{prop:many:audit}). Under refutation separation the untagged problem is exactly as hard as the tagged one, and PA,
ZF and ZFC are separated at small depth. Open: spare-slot thresholds beyond one-variable schemas; the refutation test for
true data and false negatives; usable bounds on the separation depth for natural targets (there is no uniform computable
one); a natural permanent residue; a fragmentation-free robust merge rule (which needs more than anchors); a theorem that
some natural universal code selects the target partition; and guarantees for bootstrapped coherence.
