\providecommand{\expReplE}{\mathrm{Repl}_{\mathrm{E}}}

We implemented the determinate-template learner and \DTRC{} to check the theory of \cref{sec:single,sec:univ,sec:zf,sec:many} on data it did not choose, and to see what goes wrong when its hypotheses fail: when refutation has a budget, when targets look alike, and when the data contain mistakes. An adversarial referee re-ran every experiment with independent checks; the resulting corrections and withdrawn claims are listed in \cref{app:ver}. \emph{Computed} means that a seeded script was run; re-running reproduces every number except timings. We describe the implementation (\cref{sec:exp:impl}), its guarantees (\cref{sec:exp:theory}), the results (\cref{sec:exp:results}) and the limitations (\cref{sec:exp:limits}); proofs, further numbers and commands are in \cref{app:exp}.

In brief: on Questions~1 and~2 the implemented learner behaves as the theory predicts, and on Question~2 it accepted no non-instance in $927$ probes. On untagged mixtures of the PA and ZF axioms (Question~3), \DTRC{} recovered the hidden labels in every run, and with a sharing pass for ambiguous data it matched, target by target, a tagged learner given every label a datum deserves. For these mixtures we prove refutation separation for an ideal refuter, and the implemented refuter achieved it on every sampled merge. But with injected mistakes and a small refutation budget, \DTRC{} \emph{accepted a false sentence}; similar targets can merge into valid templates that no world-based refutation separates; and the evidence for separation beyond the mixtures is narrow.

\subsection{What was implemented}\label{sec:exp:impl}

\paragraph{Data and the class.}
Formulas over $L_A$ and $L_\in$ use de Bruijn indices and named parameters; each datum is in closure-normal form, canonicalized on its own (parameters $w_0,w_1,\dots$ by first occurrence). Leading universal quantifiers are \emph{not} stripped: the axiom Q4 is the datum $\forall x\,(x+0=x)$, and $z+0=z$ accepts $w_0+0=w_0$ but not Q4. So the PA-mixture targets below have disjoint instance sets except for $0+0=0$, and the ZF-mixture targets are pairwise disjoint (\cref{cor:exp:mixsep}; stripped quantifiers: \cref{app:exp:numbers}). Matching is up to injective renaming of the template's parameters, and $\inst$, ``covers'' and $\gen$ are read in this sense ($\inst^\sim$, $\gen^\sim$ of \cref{app:setting:align}); anchors are taken in the class $\{\inst(T):T\in\DTF\}$. The class is $\DTF$ (\cref{def:setting:template}): every target below lies in it, so the cautious verifier stays sound; positive-arity term metavariables blow up the minimal covering sets (E7); and every $\DT$ anchor is a $\DTF$ anchor, so the classes differ only before a $\DT$ anchor arrives.

\paragraph{Minimal covering templates.}
They are computed from the finitely many \emph{normal configurations} of the data's common-prefix tree (\cref{def:exp:normal,prop:exp:normal}), with parameters aligned (\cref{app:setting:align}): $\Min_F(D):=\Min^{\mathrm{al}}_{\DTF}(D)$ is the set of minimal elements of the union of $\Min^{\mathrm{lit}}_F(\alpha(D))=\Min_{\DTF}(\alpha(D))$ over all alignments $\alpha$. By \cref{prop:setting:align} it has the properties of $\Min(D)$ with respect to templates all of whose parameters occur in their skeleton, such as our parameter-free targets; the literal set fails for rigid parameters (\cref{ex:setting:align}; checks E0, E0b in \cref{app:exp:normal}). On the experiment data the aligned and literal sets almost never differ (\cref{app:exp:normal}).

\paragraph{World oracles.}
Two three-valued evaluators return True, False or unknown: for $\N$, exact on numerals, with polynomial normal forms for atoms and bounded enumeration, numeral search and case splits for quantifiers; for $V$, with hereditarily finite sets of rank $\le3$ and \emph{generic objects} standing for unknown sets outside the transitive closure of the values in scope. Both use Kleene's strong connectives with supervaluation over unknown leaves, after constant propagation (\cref{app:exp:oracle}).

\begin{proposition}[The oracles are sound]\status{proved; computed}\src{experiments Prop E2; recheck R3, R4, R11}\label{prop:exp:oracle}
If the PA evaluator returns True (False) on a datum, its universal closure is true (false) in $\N$. If $V\models\ZF$ and the ZF evaluator returns True (False) on a datum, its universal closure is true (false) in $V$.
\end{proposition}

The referee's independent evaluators found no contradiction on $2197$ PA and $592$ ZF logged verdicts and $3600$ random sentences. Neither oracle is complete: no witness is constructed from a generic or a term, so $\forall a\exists b\forall x\,(x\in b\leftrightarrow a\in x)$ is not refuted and $\forall x\,(x=0\vee\exists y\,x=Sy)$ stays undecided. Both are truth oracles, not provability oracles.

\paragraph{Template refuter and \DTRC{}.}
A template is \emph{refuted} once the oracle returns False on one of its instances. Within a budget (default $80$ instances) the refuter tries $\top/\bot$ assignments to the formula metavariables, pools and enumerations of small bodies, and bodies read off the data (\cref{app:exp:oracle}). It is sound, \emph{budgeted} and \emph{history-dependent} (it caches verdicts and accumulates data-guided tries), so it is not a fixed oracle $\Ref_d$ (\cref{def:setting:oracle}); the guarantees below are stated for it directly, and every ``refuted'' count is a lower bound for an \emph{ideal} refuter, which refutes every template with a false instance. \DTRC{} as implemented:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),nosep]
\item canonicalize the data; discard those the oracle refutes;
\item agglomerate from singletons, trying pairs by decreasing common-prefix size (ties in data order): merging $A$ and $B$ succeeds iff some member of $\Min_F(A\cup B)$ is unrefuted, and a failed pair stays failed for clusters containing its parts;
\item optionally, a \emph{sharing pass}: for each final cluster $C$ and each kept datum $d\notin C$, in data order, add $d$ to $C$ if the test on $C\cup\{d\}$ succeeds (clusters may then overlap);
\item per cluster, $\Acc(C)=\bigcap\{\inst(T):T\in\Min_F(C)\text{ not refuted}\}$, or $C$ if all are refuted;
\item assert $\bigcup_C\Acc(C)$.
\end{enumerate}
This is $\DTRC$ of \cref{def:many:dtrc} with the class $\DTF$, a fixed merge order and a budgeted refuter, with four further differences: failed pairs are inherited (lossless for an ideal refuter by \cref{prop:exp:mono}(a), a heuristic otherwise); refuted data are discarded; a cluster whose minimal templates are all refuted accepts its own data (\cref{def:many:dtrc}: nothing); and with a parameter-free datum, hence a single alignment, the literal set $\Min^{\mathrm{lit}}_F$ is used, which for parameter-free targets changes no guarantee below (\cref{app:exp:dtrc}).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
(i)~First-order $\lgg$ per tag \citep{plotkin1970note,reynolds1970transformational}, with binders named by level or as de Bruijn indices (instantiation may capture). (ii)~Higher-order pattern $\lgg$ per tag, in our reading of the merge rule of \citet{baumgartner2017higher} (their implementation was not run): the \emph{genuine} version generalizes a term disagreement containing a bound variable by a term variable $f(x,\dots)$; the \emph{formula-level} version, an earlier baseline, generalizes the whole atom. (iii)~The cautious $k$-union verifier over $\DTF$, with and without refutation negatives. (iv)~\emph{Skeleton clustering}: \DTRC{} without refutation, stopped at the true number of targets. (v)~The tagged refutation-filtered verifier with \emph{gold labels} (the generating target) or \emph{membership labels} (every target a datum instantiates).

\paragraph{Data sets and metrics.}
\emph{PA-mix}: Q1--Q7; $30$ induction instances with random motives; $8$, $6$, $4$ instances of $U_{\mathrm{add0}}=(z+0=z)$, $U_{\mathrm{mul0}}=(z\cdot0=0)$, $U_{\mathrm{0add}}=(0+z=z)$. In the main (\emph{numerals}) regime $z$ is a numeral uniform in $0..7$; in the \emph{mixed} regime it is a numeral ($0.6$), a random closed term of depth $\le2$ ($0.25$) or a parameter ($0.15$). \emph{ZF-mix}: $\Ext,\Pair,\Union,\Pow,\Inf,\Found$; $12$ instances of $T_{\Sep}$, $10$ of Replacement $\expReplE$ ($\exists!$ spelled out as in $\ReplS$, with the image consequent of $\ReplJ$; neither formulation of \cref{tab:zf:forms}), $10$ of $T_{\EInd}$, with random small bodies. Held-out sets contain schema instances only, disjoint from the training data. Metrics: the adjusted Rand index (ARI; \citealp{hubert1985comparing}) on unambiguous data; \emph{exactness}, by a sufficient syntactic test for $\Acc=\inst(T^*)$ (every unrefuted minimal template is $\gen T^*$ and one is $\preceq T^*$), reported separately for ground axioms, where it only means that the axiom stayed a singleton; held-out acceptance; and \emph{probes}: sampled accepted sentences, how many are instances of no target, and how many of those the oracle refutes, a lower bound on unsoundness (\cref{prop:exp:qF}).

\subsection{Guarantees for the implemented learner}\label{sec:exp:theory}

The theorems of \cref{sec:many} (\cref{thm:many:dtrc,thm:many:residue}) carry over to $\DTF$, $\Min_F$ and the budgeted, history-dependent refuter (world $\N$ or $V$; proofs in \cref{app:exp:dtrc}). Within-target merges always succeed, and a cluster inside the instance set of a target with true instances always intersects a never-refuted template below that target, whatever the budget (\cref{prop:exp:mono,prop:exp:budget}). So a budgeted refuter harms soundness only through clustering: a mixed cluster is truth-sound if one of its unrefuted minimal templates is valid, and may accept false sentences otherwise (\cref{prop:exp:qF}). Exactness does depend on the budget: more refutations enlarge $\Acc(C)$.

\begin{theorem}[\DTRC{} under refutation separation]\status{proved}\src{experiments Thm E4}\label{thm:exp:dtrc}
Assume \textup{(H1)} targets $T_1,\dots,T_k\in\DTF$, all of whose parameters occur in their skeletons \textup{(}e.g.\ parameter-free\textup{)}, with all instances true; \textup{(H2)} data $D=D_1\sqcup\dots\sqcup D_k$, $D_i\subseteq\inst(T_i)$, each datum an instance of exactly one target; \textup{(H3)} a sound refuter; and \textup{(H4)} every merge test on $A\cup B$ with $\emptyset\neq A\subseteq D_i$, $\emptyset\neq B\subseteq D_j$, $i\neq j$, fails. Then \textup{\DTRC{}} without sharing discards no datum, its final clusters are the nonempty $D_i$, and $\Acc(D_i)\subseteq\inst(T_i)$ (target-soundness), with equality whenever $D_i$ contains an anchor of $T_i$. If moreover \textup{(H-ref)} verdicts do not depend on the call history, $\Acc(D_i)$ equals the tagged verifier's acceptance set on $D_i$.
\end{theorem}

Exactness is stated per cluster because instance sets may overlap; the implemented refuter violates (H-ref) \src{referee F8}. The sharing pass handles ambiguous data (\cref{prop:exp:share}). If \textup{(H4$^+$)} every test on a set lying in no single instance set fails, and \textup{(H5)} every cluster of the agglomeration lies in exactly one instance set, then each such cluster grows to all data of its target, stays target-sound, is exact with an anchor, and under (H-ref) matches the tagged learner with membership labels. A target that labels no such cluster gains nothing and can be learned incompletely although its data contain an anchor (\cref{ex:exp:share}). With numerals every anchor of $U_{\mathrm{add0}}$ or $U_{\mathrm{0add}}$ contains $0+0=0$ (\cref{prop:exp:numerals}), so a partition gives this datum to at most one of them, the sharing pass to both.

For the mixtures, (H4) holds with an ideal refuter: every minimal covering template of a cross pair of mixture targets has one of five shapes, each with a false instance such as $\bot$, $\forall\bar x\,\bot$, Russell's instance or $1=0$ (\cref{prop:exp:mixsep}, \cref{tab:exp:cases}); a computed check (E10) of $9100$ random pairs found this in every case.

\begin{corollary}[Separation of the mixtures]\status{proved}\src{experiments Cors E8.2, E8.3}\label{cor:exp:mixsep}
With an ideal refuter, \textup{(H4)} holds iff every member of $\Min_F(\{a,b\})$ is refuted for all cross pairs $a\in D_i$, $b\in D_j$. Hence \textup{(H4)}, and with it \cref{thm:exp:dtrc}, holds for PA-mix and ZF-mix data in which no datum instantiates two targets; $0+0=0$ is the only sentence that does.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:exp:results}

\paragraph{Universal axioms (Question~1; E1).}
On six universal axioms ($2000$ runs each) the $\DTF$ verifier and the first-order $\lgg$ became exact at the same $N$ in every run. For the five one-variable axioms the mean $N$ was $3.27$ (predicted $3.25$, mixed regime) and $8.42$ (predicted $8.11$, numerals); these rows share seeds and form one sample, in which $8.42$ is $1.85$ standard errors off, and the referee's $200\,000$-run simulation gives $8.117$. \Cref{fig:exp:e1} shows the curves, and \cref{sec:univ:rates} discusses the numbers \src{experiments E1}.

\paragraph{Learning curves (E5).}
Streams are drawn i.i.d.\ from a mixture of all targets (weights and curves in \cref{app:exp:numbers}, \cref{fig:exp:e5}). \DTRC{}+share coincides with the membership-labelled tagged learner in every stream, per target and seed, in the means and in the first $N$ from which a target stays exact. All differences between \DTRC{} and the gold-labelled learner concern $U_{\mathrm{add0}}$, $U_{\mathrm{0add}}$ and come from $0+0=0$. $T_{\Ind}$, Separation, Replacement and $\in$-induction are exact after $5$--$20$ examples; with numerals the instance schemas are exact only after their $0$-instance appears (for \DTRC{} on $U_{\mathrm{0add}}$, beyond $120$ examples in four of five streams) \src{experiments E5; referee R9}.

\paragraph{The $k$-union verifier; why $\DTF$ (E6, E7).}
Without clustering, the cautious $k$-union verifier on $\Q$ plus induction follows the slot accounting of \cref{thm:many:slots} (counts in \cref{app:exp:numbers}): with refutation negatives, $k=8$ learns induction from the anchor pair $\Ind(x=x),\Ind(\neg x=0)$, and $k=9$ from three instances but not from these two. With third motive $\exists y\,x<y$ this matches the theory; with third motive $x+0=x$ it does not: the block $\{\Ind(x=x),\Ind(x+0=x)\}$ is root-homogeneous, and the $\DT$ template $(f(0)=0\wedge\forall x(f(x)=x\to f(Sx)=Sx))\to\forall x\,f(x)=x$ covers it, has only true instances and misses $\Ind(x\cdot0=0)$; it is not in $\DTF$, so here the implemented class accepts more than $\DT$ would. No false non-instance was accepted \src{experiments E6; prior pa-untagged}. For pairs of mixture data, full $\DT$ gives up to $64$ (PA) and $32$ (ZF) minimal templates and $3$ timeouts on ZF pairs, $\DTF$ at most $4$ and $8$ (cf.\ \cref{prop:single:blowup}) \src{experiments E7}.

\paragraph{Separation evidence (E9).}
At budget $80$ every cross merge on the mixtures is refuted ($2200$ PA and $540$ ZF pair merges; all counts in \cref{tab:exp:e9}, \cref{app:exp:numbers}), but the thousands of merges reduce to $15$ (PA) and $5$ (ZF) distinct templates, refuted by trivial instances (such as $1=0$, $1+0=0$, $\forall\bar x\,\bot$, $\top\to\bot$) or a comprehension instance. That is \cref{prop:exp:mixsep}, not evidence of separation between \emph{similar} targets \src{referee F3}. The \emph{near-miss} and \emph{hard} sets (\cref{app:exp:stress}) contain similar targets, such as $z+0=z$ and $z+1=Sz$, or $\in$-induction and $\in$-induction along $z\in y\in x$ ($\mathrm{EInd2}$). On PA, $7$ of $1080$ pair merges stay unrefuted, all valid, e.g.\ $n+z=S^nz$ from $n+0=n$ and $n+1=\num{n+1}$ (\cref{lem:exp:valid}), so no world-based refuter separates them.

Two hard ZF pairs mark the limits (\cref{prop:exp:hard}). $\mathrm{Union}_W$ and $\mathrm{Power}_W$ have the universal-set schema (with a binary metavariable) as unique minimal covering template; the ZF oracle refutes it, but no oracle sound for $\HF$ plus a universal element does (\cref{prop:many:univset}). A merge of $\in$-induction with $\mathrm{EInd2}$ is typically false and is refuted only with constant propagation ($10$ of $12$ random merges with it, $0$ without; E9b); the hard set's unrefuted merges ($1$ of $432$ pairs, $2$ of $432$ sets, all of this kind) are false and are refuted at budget $400$. Some other merges of that pair are valid, as is Separation with Separation from $\bigcup a$ on the body $x\in a$, so (H4) fails for these pairs in general.

\paragraph{Stress tests (E4).}
\emph{(a) Near-miss targets} (the near-miss sets of E9; 3 seeds). On ZF all $6$ targets are exact in every seed. On PA the three forms of induction are always separated, $z+0+0=z+0$ merges into $z+0=z$, and in seed $0$ data of $z+0=z$ and $z+1=Sz$ merge into $5+z=S^5z$ and $4+z=S^4z$; every accepted template of a mixed cluster is valid (\cref{lem:exp:valid}).

\emph{(b) Injected mistakes.} PA: $8$ mistakes (induction-like sentences with a wrong step $\varphi(x)\to\varphi(x)$, a wrong base $\varphi(S0)$, or the true conclusion $\forall x\,\varphi(Sx)$; and $n+0=0$) and $3$ true non-targets. ZF: $6$ mistakes, $5$ of them distinct (Separation with the comprehension variable free in the body, $\forall x(\varphi\to\varphi)\to\forall x\varphi$, and complement comprehension, which occurs twice). Clean targets stay exact (PA $11/11$ mixed and $9$--$10/11$ numerals, ZF $9/9$). Refuted mistakes are discarded; the others become singletons or merge. Every mixed cluster was checked with a post-hoc refuter and by hand (\cref{tab:exp:e4}).

\begin{proposition}[A false sentence is accepted]\status{proved; computed}\src{experiments Prop E10; referee F1, R15}\label{prop:exp:qF}
In the stress test with injected mistakes, PA-mix seed $0$, both regimes, the wrong-step and the $\forall x\,\varphi(Sx)$ mistakes merge at refuter budgets $80$ and $400$ into a cluster whose only accepted template is
\begin{multline*}T_F=\bigl(\exists x(P_0(x,0)\wedge\neg P_1(x,0))\wedge\forall x\bigl(\exists y(P_0(y,x)\wedge\neg P_1(y,x))\to\exists y(y<Sx\wedge\neg P_1(y,Sx))\bigr)\bigr)\\\to\forall x\,\exists y\,(y<Sx\wedge\neg P_1(y,Sx)).\end{multline*}
Its instance $q_F$ with $P_0(y,x):=(x=0)$ and $P_1(y,z):=(z=2)$ is false in $\N$ and an instance of no PA-mix target. \textup{\DTRC{}} accepts $q_F$ at budgets $80$ and $400$, not at $2000$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first premise of $q_F$, $\exists x(0=0\wedge\neg\,0=2)$, is true. The second, $\forall x(\exists y(x=0\wedge\neg\,x=2)\to\exists y(y<Sx\wedge\neg\,Sx=2))$, is true: at $x=0$ take $y=0$, since $1\neq2$; at $x\ge1$ its antecedent is false. The conclusion $\forall x\,\exists y\,(y<Sx\wedge\neg\,Sx=2)$ fails at $x=1$. The rest is computed (E4(b))\src{recheck R15}.
\end{proof}

The current oracle refutes $q_F$ by a case split on $x=0$ for the $\Pi_1$ premise (an earlier version returned unknown), but the refuter does not reach $q_F$ within $400$ tries, and the probes, which never sampled it, reported ``$0$ refuted''. So \emph{with mistakes in the data, the union asserted by \textup{\DTRC{}} is not truth-sound even in arithmetic}, and \emph{probe counts are lower bounds on unsoundness}. The other false templates met (two induction-step templates and five Separation-capture templates, all at budget $80$; \cref{app:exp:stress}) disappear at budget $400$, and at $2000$ no false merge remains; the remaining residual merges are valid (\cref{lem:exp:valid}).

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:exp:limits}

(1)~Budgeted refutation can accept false sentences (\cref{prop:exp:qF}). (2)~No world-based method separates valid residual merges; \DTRC{} is then truth-sound, not target-sound (\cref{lem:setting:onesided}(a), \cref{thm:many:residue}(b)). (3)~The sharing pass needs (H4$^+$) and (H5), and helps only targets that label a cluster of the agglomeration (\cref{ex:exp:share}). (4)~The oracles construct no witnesses. (5)~A one-instance target is never generalized (E4(c), with unequal frequencies: \cref{app:exp:numbers}), and an unrefuted singleton mistake looks like a ground axiom. (6)~$\DTF$ lacks positive-arity term metavariables, so it can accept more than $\DT$ (E6); the feature test of \cref{thm:single:feat} is not implemented. (7)~Without (H4) the partition can depend on the merge order. (8)~Separation is proved for the mixtures only, and for an ideal refuter. (9)~Scale: pure Python, about ten minutes for all experiments. Open problems: \cref{sec:disc:open}.
