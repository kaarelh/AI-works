We implemented the determinate-template learner and \DTRC{} to check the theory of \cref{sec:single,sec:univ,sec:zf,sec:many} on data it did not choose, and to see what goes wrong when its hypotheses fail: when refutation has a budget, when targets look alike, and when the data contain mistakes. The package \texttt{dtrc} (pure Python, \texttt{axiom-schemas/code/}) is independent of the prototype of \cref{sec:many}. An adversarial referee re-ran every experiment with independent checks and found one false empirical claim and one false proposition in the first version of the record (the \emph{first record} below); both are corrected here and listed in \cref{app:ver}. \emph{Computed} means that a seeded script was run; re-running reproduces every number except timings. Proofs, further numbers and commands are in \cref{app:exp}.

In brief: on Q1 and Q2 the implemented learner behaves as the theory predicts, and on Q2 it accepted no non-instance in $927$ probes. On untagged mixtures of the PA and ZF axioms, \DTRC{} recovered the hidden labels in every run, and with a sharing pass for ambiguous data it matched, target by target, a tagged learner given every label a datum deserves; for these mixtures we prove refutation separation. But with injected mistakes and a small refutation budget, \DTRC{} \emph{accepted a false sentence}; similar targets can merge into valid templates that no world-based refutation separates; and the evidence for separation beyond the mixtures is narrow.

\subsection{What was implemented}\label{sec:exp:impl}

\paragraph{Data and the class.}
Formulas over $L_A$ and $L_\in$ use de Bruijn indices and named parameters. Each datum is in closure-normal form and is canonicalized on its own (parameters renamed $w_0,w_1,\dots$ by first occurrence). Leading universal quantifiers are \emph{not} stripped: the axiom Q4 is the datum $\forall x\,(x+0=x)$, and the schema $z+0=z$ accepts $w_0+0=w_0$ but not Q4. With this choice the PA-mixture targets below have disjoint instance sets except for the sentence $0+0=0$, and the ZF-mixture targets are pairwise disjoint (\cref{prop:exp:mixsep}). (With leading quantifiers stripped, Q4 becomes an instance of $z+0=z$ and Q6 of $z\cdot0=0$; the referee re-ran \DTRC{} so and found ARI $1$ on unambiguous data, Q4 and Q6 absorbed into the clusters of these schemas, and no non-target probe \src{referee R13}.) The matcher decides membership up to an injective renaming of the template's parameters; in this section $\inst$, ``covers'' and $\gen$ are read in this sense ($\inst^\sim$, $\gen^\sim$ of \cref{app:setting}), and anchors are taken in the class $\{\inst(T):T\in\DTF\}$.

The implemented class is $\DTF$ (\cref{sec:setting}). Every target used below lies in it, so the cautious verifier stays sound; positive-arity term metavariables blow up the minimal covering sets (E7); and every $\DT$ anchor is a $\DTF$ anchor, so the classes differ only before an anchor arrives.

\paragraph{Minimal covering templates.}
Let $C(D)$ be the \emph{common-prefix tree} of a finite set $D$ of data: the positions $p$ such that all data carry the same label and arity at $p$ and above it (parameters compared by name), and the \emph{slots}, the maximal positions at which they disagree. The \emph{column} at $p$ is $(d|_p)_{d\in D}$; it is \emph{open} if some $d|_p$ contains a variable bound above $p$. In $\mathcal T(D)$, each common atom node whose subtree contains a slot with an open column is cut to a leaf slot (an \emph{F-slot}); so every term slot of $\mathcal T(D)$ has a closed column.

\begin{definition}[Normal configurations]\status{definition}\src{experiments \S1.3}\label{def:exp:normal}
For a slot $\sigma$ of $\mathcal T(D)$ let $\bar y_\sigma$ list the variables free in its column, $M_\sigma$ a fresh metavariable of the slot's sort and arity $|\bar y_\sigma|$, and $\beta_d(\sigma)$ the abstraction of $d|_\sigma$ over $\bar y_\sigma$. A node $\pi$ is \emph{derivable} from $\sigma$, $\sigma\to\pi$, if metavariable-free terms $\bar t=:\bar t(\sigma,\pi)$ satisfy $\beta_d(\sigma)[\bar t]=d|_\pi$ for all $d\in D$ ($\bar t$ is then unique). A \emph{normal configuration} is a set $S'$ of slots, none derivable from another, an antichain $A\supseteq S'$ of nodes of $\mathcal T(D)$ with every slot at or below a member of $A$, and a choice of $c(\pi)\in S'$ with $c(\pi)\to\pi$ for each $\pi\in A\setminus S'$. Its template has $M_\sigma(\bar y_\sigma)$ at $\sigma\in S'$, $M_{c(\pi)}(\bar t(c(\pi),\pi))$ at $\pi\in A\setminus S'$, and the common labels elsewhere.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Normal form]\status{proved; computed}\src{experiments Props E1-lit, E1-al; E0, E0b}\label{prop:exp:normal}
Let $D$ be finite and nonempty, and compare parameters by name.
\textup{(a)} Every normal configuration lies in $\DTF$ and covers $D$.
\textup{(b)} Every $T\in\DTF$ covering $D$ satisfies $T\gen N$ for a normal configuration $N$.
\textup{(c)} Hence the minimal normal configurations form a finite set $\Min^{\mathrm{lit}}_F(D)$; every covering $T\in\DTF$ lies above a member; members are minimal among all covering templates in $\DTF$; and if $D\subseteq\inst(T^*)$ with $T^*\in\DTF$, some member lies below $T^*$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
For (b): rigid symbols survive instantiation, so the skeleton of $T$ lies in $C(D)$, and no occurrence sits strictly below an F-slot (it would be a $0$-ary term metavariable with a closed value containing an open column). Push pattern occurrences down through common labels; drop argument places no datum uses; the remaining arguments are forced; replace an own slot derivable from another by the derived occurrence. Each step specializes $T$ and keeps it covering. Details in \cref{app:exp:normal}.
\end{proof}

With parameters compared up to renaming, the learner uses $\Min_F(D)$, the minimal elements of the union of $\Min^{\mathrm{lit}}_F(\alpha(D))$ over all \emph{alignments} $\alpha$ of the data's parameters; \cref{app:setting} proves (a)--(c) for it, given the finiteness that \cref{prop:exp:normal}(c) supplies. The literal set is not enough: the first record stated (c) for renaming-closed covering, and the referee found a counterexample with a rigid parameter (\cref{app:setting}). The aligned and literal sets differed on none of the $720$ data sets of E2 and in one call of all \DTRC{} runs of E3; no cap on alignments or configurations was hit. Computed checks against the prior referee's enumerator (E0) and on $800$ random data sets in both languages (E0b) found no failure of the aligned set; the literal one failed exactly when the generating template had a rigid parameter (\cref{app:exp:normal}).

\paragraph{World oracles.}
Two three-valued evaluators return True, False or unknown. \emph{PA} (truth in $\N$) evaluates numerals exactly, decides atoms with symbolic variables by polynomial normal forms, enumerates bounded quantifiers with numeral bounds, searches numeral counterexamples and witnesses, and case-splits an unbounded quantifier into $x\in\{0,1\}$ and $x=x'+2$. \emph{ZF} (truth in $V$) uses hereditarily finite sets of rank $\le3$ and \emph{generic objects}: an unbounded quantifier is split into $x$ in the transitive closure $E$ of the concrete values in scope, $x$ an in-scope generic, or $x$ a fresh generic. Both use Kleene's strong connectives \citep{kleene1952introduction} with supervaluation over unknown leaves \citep{vanfraassen1966singular}. Both first simplify by \emph{constant propagation}: propositional laws valid in every nonempty structure, after replacing by $\top$ or $\bot$ the atoms whose value does not depend on the variables ($t=t$; closed arithmetic atoms; $t<t$; $x\in x$, false by Foundation).

\begin{proposition}[The oracles are sound]\status{proved; computed}\src{experiments Prop E2; recheck R3, R4, R11}\label{prop:exp:oracle}
If the PA evaluator returns True (False) on a datum, its universal closure is true (false) in $\N$. If $V\models\ZF$ and the ZF evaluator returns True (False) on a datum, its universal closure is true (false) in $V$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Induction on the formula. For ZF the claim is made for every \emph{realization} of the generics: each denotes a set outside the transitive closure fixed when it was introduced, distinct from the generics then in scope. The atom rules are sound (e.g.\ $G\in G$ is false by Foundation), the three cases of a quantifier cover every set, Kleene's tables are monotone, and supervaluation enumerates a superset of the realizable assignments. Details in \cref{app:exp:oracle}.
\end{proof}

The referee's independent evaluators found $0$ contradictions on $2197$ PA and $592$ ZF verdicts logged during \DTRC{} runs and on $3600$ random sentences (\cref{app:exp:oracle}). Neither oracle is complete: no witness is constructed from a generic or a term, so $\forall a\exists b\forall x\,(x\in b\leftrightarrow a\in x)$ is not refuted and $\forall x\,(x=0\vee\exists y\,x=Sy)$ stays undecided. Both are truth oracles, not provability oracles.

\paragraph{Template refuter and \DTRC{}.}
A template is \emph{refuted} once the oracle returns False on one of its instances. The refuter tries, within a budget (default $80$ instances): all $\top/\bot$ assignments to the formula metavariables; one metavariable running through a pool of small bodies while the others are $\top$ (or $\bot$); enumerations of small bodies; and bodies read off the data. It is \emph{sound}, \emph{budgeted}, and \emph{history-dependent} (it caches verdicts and accumulates data-guided tries), so it is not a fixed oracle $\Ref_d$ (\cref{sec:setting}); the guarantees below are stated for it directly. Every ``refuted'' count is a lower bound on what an \emph{ideal} refuter, one that refutes every template with a false instance, would refute.

\DTRC{} as implemented: (1)~canonicalize and discard data the oracle refutes; (2)~agglomerate from singletons, trying pairs of clusters in decreasing order of common-prefix size, where the merge of $A$ and $B$ succeeds iff some member of $\Min_F(A\cup B)$ is not refuted, and a failed pair stays failed for clusters containing its parts; (3)~optionally a \emph{sharing pass}: for each final cluster $C$ and each kept datum $d\notin C$, in data order, add $d$ to $C$ if the test on $C\cup\{d\}$ succeeds (clusters may then overlap); (4)~per cluster, $\Acc(C)=\bigcap\{\inst(T):T\in\Min_F(C)\text{ not refuted}\}$, or $C$ if all are refuted; (5)~assert $\bigcup_C\Acc(C)$. This is $\DTRC$ of \cref{sec:many} with the class $\DTF$, a fixed merge order and a budgeted refuter.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
(i)~First-order $\lgg$ per tag \citep{plotkin1970note,reynolds1970transformational}, with binders named by level or with de Bruijn indices; instantiation may capture. (ii)~Higher-order pattern $\lgg$ per tag, in our reading of the merge rule of \citet{baumgartner2017higher} (their implementation was not run): the \emph{genuine} version generalizes a term disagreement containing a bound variable by a term variable $f(x,\dots)$; the \emph{formula-level} version of the first record generalizes the whole atom instead. (iii)~The cautious $k$-union verifier over $\DTF$, with and without refutation negatives. (iv)~\emph{Skeleton clustering}: \DTRC{} without refutation, stopped at the true number of targets. (v)~The tagged refutation-filtered verifier with \emph{gold labels} (the target a datum was generated from) or \emph{membership labels} (every target it instantiates).

\paragraph{Data sets and metrics.}
\emph{PA-mix}: Q1--Q7; $30$ induction instances with random motives; $8$, $6$, $4$ instances of $U_{\mathrm{add0}}=(z+0=z)$, $U_{\mathrm{mul0}}=(z\cdot0=0)$, $U_{\mathrm{0add}}=(0+z=z)$. In the main regime $z$ is a numeral uniform in $0..7$ (numerals written as digits); in the \emph{mixed} regime of the first record it is a numeral ($0.6$), a random closed term of depth $\le2$ ($0.25$) or a parameter ($0.15$). \emph{ZF-mix}: $\Ext,\Pair,\Union,\Pow,\Inf,\Found$; $12$ instances of $T_{\Sep}$, $10$ of Replacement $\ReplS$ ($\exists!$ spelled out, image consequent), $10$ of $T_{\EInd}$, with random small bodies. Stress sets are described with E4 and E9. Held-out sets contain schema instances only, disjoint from the training data. Metrics: the adjusted Rand index (ARI; \citealp{hubert1985comparing}) on unambiguous data; \emph{exactness}, by a sufficient syntactic test for $\Acc=\inst(T^*)$ (every unrefuted minimal template is $\gen T^*$ and one is $\preceq T^*$), reported separately for ground axioms, where it only means that the axiom stayed a singleton; held-out acceptance; and \emph{probes}: sampled accepted sentences, how many are instances of no target, and how many of those the oracle refutes. Probe counts are lower bounds on unsoundness (\cref{prop:exp:qF}).

\subsection{Guarantees for the implemented learner}\label{sec:exp:theory}

The following are the analogues of the theorems of \cref{sec:many} for $\DTF$, $\Min_F$ and the budgeted, history-dependent refuter. The world is $\N$ or $V$, and the refuter is sound.

\begin{proposition}[Merge monotonicity; within-target merges succeed]\status{proved}\src{experiments Prop E3}\label{prop:exp:mono}
\textup{(a)} Let $D\subseteq D'$ and the refuter be ideal. If every member of $\Min_F(D)$ is refuted, so is every member of $\Min_F(D')$.
\textup{(b)} If $\emptyset\neq X\subseteq\inst(T^*)$ with $T^*\in\DTF$ and all instances of $T^*$ true, then some $T_0\in\Min_F(X)$ with $T_0\preceq T^*$ is never refuted, so the merge test on $X$ succeeds for every sound refuter and budget.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) A member of $\Min_F(D')$ covers $D$, so it lies above a member of $\Min_F(D)$ and contains that member's false instance. (b) \Cref{prop:exp:normal}(c), with alignments, gives $T_0$; its instances are true.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\DTRC{} under refutation separation]\status{proved}\src{experiments Thm E4}\label{thm:exp:dtrc}
Assume (T) targets $T_1,\dots,T_k\in\DTF$ with all instances true; (D) data $D=D_1\sqcup\dots\sqcup D_k$, $D_i\subseteq\inst(T_i)$, each datum an instance of exactly one target; (R) a sound refuter; and (S) every merge test on $A\cup B$ with $\emptyset\neq A\subseteq D_i$, $\emptyset\neq B\subseteq D_j$, $i\neq j$, fails. Then \DTRC{} without sharing discards no datum, its final clusters are the nonempty $D_i$, and $\Acc(D_i)\subseteq\inst(T_i)$ (target-soundness), with equality whenever $D_i$ contains an anchor of $T_i$. If moreover \textup{(H-ref)} verdicts do not depend on the call history, $\Acc(D_i)$ equals the tagged verifier's acceptance set on $D_i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Clusters stay inside one $D_i$: cross merges fail by (S), within-label merges succeed by \cref{prop:exp:mono}(b). So inherited failures never block a within-label merge, and agglomeration ends with one cluster per label. The never-refuted $T_0\preceq T_i$ is intersected, which gives soundness; with an anchor every covering template contains $\inst(T_i)$. Details in \cref{app:exp:dtrc}.
\end{proof}

The first record claimed exactness \emph{exactly} where the tagged learner is exact; the ``only if'' half fails when instance sets overlap, hence the per-cluster statement. The implemented refuter violates (H-ref) \src{referee F8}.

\begin{proposition}[Per-cluster soundness does not depend on the budget]\status{proved}\src{experiments Prop E5}\label{prop:exp:budget}
If $C\subseteq\inst(T^*)$ with $T^*\in\DTF$ and all instances of $T^*$ true, then $\Acc(C)\subseteq\inst(T^*)$ for every sound refuter; more refutations only enlarge $\Acc(C)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$T_0$ of \cref{prop:exp:mono}(b) is intersected, so $\Acc(C)\subseteq\inst(T_0)\subseteq\inst(T^*)$; fewer intersected templates give a larger set.
\end{proof}

So a budgeted refuter harms \DTRC{} only through clustering: an unrefuted merge across targets or with a mistake. Such a mixed cluster is truth-sound if one of its unrefuted minimal templates is valid, and may accept false sentences otherwise (\cref{prop:exp:qF}).

\begin{proposition}[The sharing pass]\status{proved}\src{experiments Prop E4-share}\label{prop:exp:share}
Assume (T), (R), data $D\subseteq\bigcup_l\inst(T_l)$ (a datum may instantiate several targets), (S$^+$) every merge or sharing test on a set contained in no $\inst(T_l)$ fails, and (P) each final cluster $C$ of the agglomeration lies in exactly one $\inst(T_l)$. Then distinct final clusters of the agglomeration have distinct targets; after the sharing pass the cluster of $T_l$ is $C^+=D\cap\inst(T_l)$, in any order; and $\Acc(C^+)\subseteq\inst(T_l)$, with equality whenever $C^+$ contains an anchor of $T_l$. Under \textup{(H-ref)} $\Acc(C^+)$ is the acceptance set of the tagged learner with membership labels.
\end{proposition}

The proof is in \cref{app:exp:dtrc}. In the numerals regime every anchor of $U_{\mathrm{add0}}$ or $U_{\mathrm{0add}}$ contains $0+0=0$, by the following proposition; a partition gives this datum to at most one of them, the sharing pass to both.

\begin{proposition}[One-variable instance schemas]\status{proved; computed}\src{experiments Prop E7; referee R2}\label{prop:exp:numerals}
Let $T^*$ have one $0$-ary term metavariable $z$, at rigid positions, no other metavariable and no parameter, and let $D\subseteq\inst(T^*)$ contain a parameter-free datum. Then $\Min_F(D)=\{T^*\}$ iff the terms substituted for $z$ do not all have the same root symbol ($0$ and each parameter count as root symbols); otherwise $\Acc(D)\neq\inst(T^*)$. So for i.i.d.\ terms with root law $(p_h)$, $\Pr[\text{not exact after }N]=\sum_hp_h^N$, and numerals uniform in $0..7$ form an anchor iff they include $0$.
\end{proposition}

This is Plotkin's \evR{} for the implemented class (\cref{sec:univ}); the referee found no violation in $3000$ random data sets over $9$ templates. Proof in \cref{app:exp:dtrc}.

\begin{corollary}[Separation of the mixtures]\status{proved}\src{experiments Cors E8.2, E8.3}\label{cor:exp:mixsep}
With an ideal refuter, (S) holds iff every member of $\Min_F(\{a,b\})$ is refuted for all cross pairs $a\in D_i$, $b\in D_j$. Hence (S), and with it \cref{thm:exp:dtrc}, holds for PA-mix and ZF-mix data in which no datum instantiates two targets; $0+0=0$ is the only sentence that does.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
``Only if'' is the case $|A|=|B|=1$; ``if'' follows from \cref{prop:exp:mono}(a) with $\{a,b\}\subseteq A\cup B$. Then apply \cref{prop:exp:mixsep}; disjointness is read off its proof.
\end{proof}

A computed check (E10) of $9100$ random data pairs found the predicted shape and a certified false instance in every case.

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:exp:results}

\paragraph{E1: universal axioms (Q1).}
On six universal axioms ($2000$ runs each) the $\DTF$ verifier and the first-order $\lgg$ became exact at the same $N$ in every run. For the five one-variable axioms the mean $N$ was $3.27$ (predicted $3.25$, mixed regime) and $8.42$ (predicted $8.11$, numerals); these rows share seeds and form one sample, in which $8.42$ is $1.85$ standard errors off, and the referee's $200\,000$-run simulation gives $8.117$. Details and curves are in \cref{sec:univ} \src{experiments E1}.

\paragraph{E2: each schema from tagged instances (Q2).}
\Cref{tab:exp:e2} gives exactness. No learner is exact from one instance: a datum is covered by itself as a ground template. Probes ($N=2$ and $6$, $8$ seeds): the $\DTF$ learner accepted $0$ non-instances in $927$ probes. Both pattern $\lgg$s accepted none on the three ZF schemas; on induction $224$ of $229$ probes at $N=2$ were non-instances, $58$ of them refuted (genuine; formula-level $213/220$, $46$), in line with the prior result that pattern anti-unification generalizes induction data to $(A\wedge\forall x(P(x)\to Q(x)))\to\forall xP(x)$, which has false instances (\cref{sec:zf}) \src{prior induction C9}. First-order $\lgg$s were unsound on Separation (capture of the comprehension variable), Replacement and induction in both encodings, and on $\in$-induction in the named encoding; on $\in$-induction in de Bruijn indices, a first-order pattern there (\cref{sec:zf}), they were sound and complete ($0$ non-instances in $182$ probes). An exhibit certified false: $\forall x\exists y\forall z\,(z\in y\leftrightarrow z\in x\wedge\neg w_0\in y)$ (Separation, both encodings). More in \cref{app:exp:numbers}.

\paragraph{E3: \DTRC{} on untagged mixtures (Q3).}
\Cref{tab:exp:e3} gives totals over seeds $0$--$4$ for PA-mix with numerals. $T_{\Ind}$ is exact for every learner in every seed, $U_{\mathrm{mul0}}$ in seeds $1$--$4$ (seed $0$ has no $0\cdot0=0$). \begin{proposition}[Two hard ZF pairs]\status{proved; computed}\src{experiments Prop E9; E9, E9b}\label{prop:exp:hard}
\textup{(a)} The unique minimal covering template of $\mathrm{Union}_W=\forall a\exists b\forall x\,(\exists y(y\in a\wedge x\in y)\to x\in b)$ and $\mathrm{Power}_W=\forall a\exists b\forall x\,(\forall y(y\in x\to y\in a)\to x\in b)$ is the universal-set schema $U=\forall a\exists b\forall x\,(P(x,a)\to x\in b)$. The ZF oracle refutes its instance $P:=\top$, whereas no oracle sound for $\HF$ plus a universal element refutes any instance (\cref{sec:many}).
\textup{(b)} Let $\mathrm{EInd2}(\psi)=\forall x\,(\forall y{\in}x\,\forall z{\in}y\,\psi(z)\to\psi(x))\to\forall x\,\psi(x)$. When the motives' root symbols differ, the $\in$-induction motive does not begin with $\forall$, and some motive uses its variable (the most frequent case), the minimal template of an $\EInd$ datum and an $\mathrm{EInd2}$ datum is $T_{EE}=(\forall x(\forall y(y\in x\to P_0(y))\to P_1(x)))\to\forall x\,P_1(x)$, which is false. Other merges of this pair are valid, and some false ones are refuted only at budget $400$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
(a) After simplification the instance is $\exists b\forall x\,(x\in b)$; the only case for $b$ is a fresh generic $G$, and $x:=G$ fails because $G\in G$ is false: generics obey Foundation. (b) $P_0:=\bot$, $P_1(x):=\forall z\,\neg z\in x$ give the true premise $\forall x\,(x=\emptyset\to x=\emptyset)$ and a conclusion that fails at $\{\emptyset\}$. Details in \cref{app:exp:stress}.
\end{proof}

Constant propagation is needed for (b): without it $\forall y\,(y\in x\to\bot)$ and $\forall z\,\neg z\in x$ are different unknown leaves, and $0$ of $12$ random $\EInd/\mathrm{EInd2}$ merges are refuted, against $10$ of $12$ with it (E9b). The one survivor in E9 is false but is refuted only at budget $400$. Valid merges also occur ($\EInd(x=x)$ with $\mathrm{EInd2}(x=x)$; Separation with Separation from $\bigcup a$ on the body $x\in a$), so (S) fails for these pairs in general.

\emph{(b) Injected mistakes.} PA: $8$ mistakes (induction-like sentences with a wrong step $\varphi(x)\to\varphi(x)$, a wrong base $\varphi(S0)$, or the true conclusion $\forall x\,\varphi(Sx)$; and $n+0=0$) and $3$ true non-targets. \begin{proposition}[A false sentence is accepted]\status{proved; computed}\src{experiments Prop E10; referee F1, R15}\label{prop:exp:qF}
In E4(b), PA-mix seed $0$, both regimes, the wrong-step and the $\forall x\,\varphi(Sx)$ mistakes merge at refuter budgets $80$ and $400$ into a cluster whose only accepted template is
\begin{multline*}T_F=\bigl(\exists x(P_0(x,0)\wedge\neg P_1(x,0))\wedge\forall x\bigl(\exists y(P_0(y,x)\wedge\neg P_1(y,x))\to\exists y(y<Sx\wedge\neg P_1(y,Sx))\bigr)\bigr)\\\to\forall x\,\exists y\,(y<Sx\wedge\neg P_1(y,Sx)).\end{multline*}
Its instance $q_F$ with $P_0(y,x):=(x=0)$ and $P_1(y,z):=(z=2)$,
\begin{multline*}q_F=\bigl(\exists x(0=0\wedge\neg\,0=2)\wedge\forall x\bigl(\exists y(x=0\wedge\neg\,x=2)\to\exists y(y<Sx\wedge\neg\,Sx=2)\bigr)\bigr)\\\to\forall x\,\exists y\,(y<Sx\wedge\neg\,Sx=2),\end{multline*}
is false in $\N$ and an instance of no PA-mix target. \DTRC{} accepts $q_F$ at budgets $80$ and $400$, not at $2000$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first premise is true. The second is true: at $x=0$ take $y=0$, since $1\neq2$; at $x\ge1$ its antecedent is false. The conclusion fails at $x=1$. The rest is computed (E4(b); recheck R15).
\end{proof}

Refuting $q_F$ needs a case split on $x=0$ for the $\Pi_1$ premise: the first record's oracle returns unknown, the revised one False, but the refuter does not reach $q_F$ within $400$ tries, and the probes, which never sampled $q_F$, reported ``$0$ refuted''. The first record's claim that PA mistakes merge only into valid templates is withdrawn; the referee found this exhibit. So \emph{with mistakes in the data, \DTRC{}'s union is not truth-sound even in arithmetic}, and \emph{probe counts are lower bounds on unsoundness}. The other false templates met (two induction-step templates and five Separation-capture templates, all at budget $80$) disappear at budget $400$, and at $2000$ no false merge remains; the remaining residual merges are valid:

\begin{lemma}[Valid residual templates]\status{proved}\src{experiments Lemma V}\label{lem:exp:valid}
Every instance of the following is true in $\N$ (1--4) or $V$ (5): (1)~$S^n0+z=S^nz$; (2)~$z+1=Sz$, $z+0=z$, $0+z=z$; (3)~$(\alpha\wedge\dots)\to\dots$ with first conjunct $\alpha$ one of $z<0$, $0=Sz$, $\exists x(x<t\wedge\dots)$ for $t\in\{0,0+0,0\cdot0\}$; (4)~$(P(0)\wedge\forall x(P(x)\to P(Sx)))\to\forall x\,P(Sx)$; (5)~$\forall x(P(x)\to x=x)\to\forall x(x=x)$ and $\forall x(\forall y(y\in x\to P(y))\to x=x)\to\forall x(x=x)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) $n+t=S^nt$. (2) True schemas. (3) $\alpha$ is false. (4) Induction gives $\forall x\,P(x)$. (5) The conclusion is true.
\end{proof}

\emph{(c) Unequal frequencies} (PA: induction $60$, $U_{\mathrm{add0}}$ $20$, $U_{\mathrm{mul0}}$ $3$, $U_{\mathrm{0add}}$ $1$; ZF: Separation $30$, Replacement $4$, $\in$-induction $1$; 3 seeds). Induction and Separation are exact in $3/3$, Replacement and $U_{\mathrm{mul0}}$ in $2/3$, $U_{\mathrm{add0}}$ in $1/3$ ($2/3$ with sharing; $0+0=0$ is its only anchor). \subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:exp:limits}

(1)~Budgeted refutation can accept false sentences (\cref{prop:exp:qF}); whether a natural refuter refutes every false minimal template of a cross pair within a budget polynomial in the data is open. (2)~Valid residual merges cannot be separated by any world-based method; \DTRC{} is then truth-sound, not target-sound (\cref{sec:many}). (3)~The sharing pass needs (S$^+$) and (P); a guarantee without (P) is open. (4)~The oracles construct no witnesses. (5)~A one-instance target is never generalized, and an unrefuted singleton mistake looks like a ground axiom. (6)~$\DTF$ excludes positive-arity term metavariables, and the feature test of \cref{sec:single} is not implemented. (7)~Without (S) the partition can depend on the merge order. (8)~Separation is proved for the mixtures only; characterizing it for similar targets is open. (9)~Scale: pure Python, about ten minutes for all experiments.
