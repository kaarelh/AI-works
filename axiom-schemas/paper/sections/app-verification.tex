\section{How the results were established}\label{app:ver}

The results come from a process with built-in adversarial checking. This appendix describes the process, lists what it
corrected, and says what it did not cover. All roles below (researchers, referees, section writers, reviewers and
editors) were played by separate instances of Claude (Anthropic). No human has checked the mathematics or the code, and nothing has been verified
in a proof assistant. ``Independent'' means a separate session that wrote its own code and imported none of the author's
code; it does not mean an independent person. Author and referee were instances of the same model, so an error that the
model tends to make could survive both. The brute-force checks, which do not depend on the theory under test, are the
part of the verification least exposed to this.

\subsection{Process}\label{app:ver:process}

\paragraph{The earlier analysis.} The questions followed the earlier analysis of PA induction (\cref{sec:setting}),
whose record is in \url{research/prior/}; gray tags and the tables below call it ``prior''. It supplied the
determinate-template learner for induction, the first-order impossibility results and the Sub and $\IndEq$ encodings
(\cref{sec:zf:pa}). It had three tracks: motive-annotated instances, raw instances with first-order patterns, and
second-order templates. A referee attacked each track once with independent code
(\url{research/prior/induction/*-referee.md}). The notes were not revised afterwards, so this paper uses those
results as the referee reports correct them (\cref{tab:ver:corr}, rows 1--5). Three reviewers checked a short analysis
of untagged PA (\url{research/prior/pa-untagged/}) by proof and with brute-force version spaces (row 6).

\paragraph{Brief and tracks.} A brief (\url{research/00-brief.md}) fixed the commissioner's questions
(\cref{sec:intro:questions}), the conventions (closure-normal form, de Bruijn indices, the class $\DT$), what the prior
work had established, and a proposed method (\DTRC) with intended theorems. Tracks could change the method if they argued
for the change. Four tracks then worked in parallel, each developed by one researcher: ``single'' (one template,
\cref{sec:single}), ``cases'' (Questions~1 and~2, \cref{sec:univ,sec:zf}), ``untagged'' (many schemas without labels,
\cref{sec:many}) and ``experiments'' (the package \texttt{dtrc}, \cref{sec:exp}). Each researcher wrote a record
(\texttt{notes.md}) with complete proofs, a status for every claim, and scripts with saved outputs. When a track needed
another track's result that had not yet been refereed, it stated that result as a hypothesis. For example, the untagged
track assumed the finiteness of minimal covering templates, which is now \cref{thm:single:min}. Each record went
through one round of refereeing; results added afterwards were not refereed again (\cref{app:ver:unref}).

\paragraph{Adversarial refereeing.} A referee then received each record with the task of finding errors. The referee
checked every proof line by line and re-ran the author's scripts against their saved outputs. The referee also tested
the claims with code written from scratch: its own term representation, matcher, enumerator of covering templates,
anti-unifiers and evaluators. Bounded brute-force enumeration provided ground truth independent of the theory. Where the
author's evidence was circular (anchor status decided by the anchor theorem's own events), the referee replaced it with
exact computation. Each claim received one of four verdicts: holds, holds with fix, false, or unclear. A claim that
could not be confirmed was marked unclear (\cref{tab:ver:tracks}).

\paragraph{Revision.} Each researcher then wrote a final record (\texttt{notes-final.md}). It supersedes the first
version, which is kept unchanged. It restates every result, corrected where needed, and ends with a verification log
that answers every referee point. Withdrawn claims stay in the record, labelled as withdrawn, and refuted conjectures are
labelled refuted. The experiments track re-ran the referee's oracle checks against its revised code and found no
contradiction. The outputs of the first versions of both implementations are kept.

\paragraph{Writing.} Each section was written from the final records only. Its writer re-checked the proofs while
transcribing them, and recorded every deviation from the records in \url{paper/writer-notes.txt}. Where records
disagree, the paper follows the statement that a referee checked. For example, the experiments record says that the size-24
template of \cref{ex:single:c82} lies below two of the eight earlier templates. The single record and its referee say
instead that five of the eight lie strictly above it, and the paper follows them. Each theorem-like statement carries
a status and a gray tag naming its source, which leads to its proof, its scripts and its referee verdict.

\paragraph{Review of the paper.} The draft of \cref{sec:setting,sec:single,sec:univ,sec:zf,sec:many,sec:exp} and their
appendices was then reviewed by five reviewers, each with one lens: mathematical correctness (three reviewers, for
\cref{sec:setting,sec:single}, for \cref{sec:univ,sec:zf} and for \cref{sec:many,sec:exp}), consistency across
sections, and readability. The mathematical reviewers checked every statement against the final records and every
appendix proof line by line, compared every number of \cref{sec:exp} with the result files, and wrote their own scripts,
among them an implementation of de Bruijn terms, $\DT$ matching and features that imports no project code. They found
two false propositions, both among the results that no referee had attacked: an alignment result for parameters and the
guarantee of the sharing pass. They also found several overclaims and missing hypotheses (\cref{tab:ver:corr}, rows
31--37). One editor per section then applied the fixes under common decisions, re-ran the counterexamples, and reported
every change (\url{research/paper-review/}). The introduction, the discussion and this appendix were not reviewed in that
round; they were revised afterwards to match the edited sections.

\subsection{Conjectures and open problems}\label{app:ver:open}

The paper states two formal conjectures. The evidence for \cref{conj:single:kill} is weak: the greedy searches behind
it also missed the quadratic chains of \cref{prop:single:quadratic}. \Cref{conj:zf:root}, that root-level distinctness
is not necessary for $\SO$ anchors with several metavariables, rests on bounded searches (\cref{app:zf:evidence}). Two
hypotheses are assumed rather than proved in general. The first is refutation separation. It is proved for $\Q$'s
axioms with $T_{\Ind}$ and, for an ideal refuter, for the experimental mixtures (\cref{cor:exp:mixsep}); for ZF and ZFC it
was verified on every sampled cross pair (one or two sampled instances per schema) and on every run's data
(\cref{sec:many:examples}); and it fails for some similar targets (\cref{lem:exp:valid,prop:many:univset}). The second is
(H-ref), which the implemented refuter violates (\cref{thm:exp:dtrc}); the sharing pass needs, besides, (H4$^+$) and (H5)
and helps only targets that label a phase-one cluster (\cref{prop:exp:share}). The question whether ZF without Foundation proves Collection is open to us (row 11). Two problems
that one record left open are settled in another: finiteness (\cref{thm:single:min}) and the complexity of the
refutation test (\cref{thm:many:complexity}). \Cref{sec:disc:open} collects all open problems of the records.

\subsection{Reproducing the results}\label{app:ver:repro}

Everything is in \url{axiom-schemas/} in the accompanying repository.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Code} (\url{code/}, with \url{README.md}). There, \texttt{python3 -m pytest -q tests} runs $43$ unit
tests in about $10$\,s, and \texttt{sh run\_all.sh} regenerates every table, data file and figure of \cref{sec:exp} in
\url{results/}. It took $593$\,s on $4$ shared cores, and $674$\,s in the referee's re-run. In
\url{experiments/recheck/}, \texttt{sh run\_recheck.sh} re-runs the referee's oracle checks against the final code
(about $6$\,min). Outputs other than timings reproduce exactly; the first version's results are in
\url{results_v1/} (\cref{app:exp:repro}).
\item \emph{Research records} (\url{research/}). The brief is \url{00-brief.md}. Each \url{tracks/<track>/} holds
