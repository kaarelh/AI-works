GPT-6 (Codex) — 2026-09-12

These are new regressions of observed benchmark price, quality and date. Data are frozen from the [authors' repository](https://github.com/hansgundlach/Algorithmic_Progress_Inference), commit c34454ed3110e667a885d6d2b0167de4cbc98e8e (2026-07-04), retrieved 2026-09-12. The underlying [March 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23455v2) describes the approach; our later repository snapshot includes observations through April 2026, and these are independent fits rather than exact reproductions of its tables.

After removing invalid zero/missing scores and prices and the three independently verified date contradictions below, the observations number **165 for GPQA-Diamond (91 base-model clusters), 136 for AIME (80 clusters), and 31 for SWE-bench Verified (26 clusters)**. GPQA/AIME run from April 2024 to April 2026; SWE runs October 2024–March 2026. Dates are otherwise as recorded in the source, often first-of-month placeholders. Multiple price dates and inference configurations for a model are retained as observations and clustered together for resampling. Source benchmark-cost calculations account for token use; these are not simply token-price comparisons.

The primary fit excludes the GPQA row `Qwen3 235B A22B (Reasoning) 07/01/2024`: [Qwen's official announcement](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen3/) establishes that Qwen3 was released on 2025-04-29. It also excludes two AIME rows for Claude 4.1 Opus Extended Thinking dated 2025-07-01, including its Option 2/AIME variant: [Anthropic's announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-1) dates release to 2025-08-05. These dates precede the release month, so month-start rounding cannot explain them. Original source files remain unchanged; no replacement dates are invented. All exclusion details and primary sources are saved in `inference_results.json` under `date_audit`. `data_quality_sensitivity.json` retains point-estimate sensitivity to keeping those rows. A bounded check of same-model date groups and explicit ISO release-version dates found no additional clear pre-release-month contradictions; this is not a comprehensive certification of every source record.

The Qwen3 correction is material: that spurious early point also dominated later valid points, so removing it changes the open-Pareto sample from 43 to 44 observations even though the raw sample loses one row. With H=1.49 and G=3.4, the revised GPQA open-Pareto OLS predicts stopping at **5.174e119 FLOPs**, versus **4.448e119** when the invalid point is retained. Its robust and linear-score variants now give **4.774e119** and **4.674e119**, versus **4.104e119** and **3.740e119** with the invalid point. AIME open-Pareto point estimates are unchanged by excluding proprietary Opus rows: **5.263e119**, **4.922e119**, and **4.614e119**. Both benchmarks' bootstrap intervals use the corrected base-model grouping.

We estimate natural-log benchmark cost as an intercept plus a quality control plus a time trend. The default quality control is logit(score), clipped at 0.5%–99.5%; an alternative uses score linearly. OLS is supplemented with soft-L1 robust least squares with scale 0.5 log-price units. Software efficiency log growth s is minus the time coefficient minus log(H), where H is a hardware-performance-per-dollar improvement factor.

Primary H=1.49 is the [current Epoch chip-performance-per-dollar estimate](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/chip-performance-per-dollar). We also retain the paper's H=1/0.7. These are adjustments using hardware proxies, not direct measurements of inference FLOPs. Serving utilization, memory constraints, platform markups and subsidies can remain. Open-weight models are preferred to reduce competition/margin confounding. All-license and non-frontier fits are retained as sensitivity checks; they should not all be treated as equivalent measurements of algorithmic progress.

A historical Pareto row is one not dominated in both price and score by any model available at or before that date. Same-date competitors count; output does not depend on input row order. For open-weight variants we form the frontier within the open subset. The all-model samples intentionally retain legacy or otherwise non-frontier offerings, explaining some non-positive adjusted trends.

The translation to the user's question assumes cumulative raw research FLOPs grow exponentially at G per year, using G=3.4 as the primary [aggregate AI-compute-capacity proxy](https://epoch.ai/trends), with G=5 for frontier training-run growth as an alternative. It additionally assumes constant research allocation/utilization, approximately exponential prehistory, and transfer of benchmark efficiency to the toy multiplier. Then p=s/log(G), and under the direct raw-input power law:

$$
k(x)=\frac{1}{10^{27.5}+x/p},\qquad
x_*=\frac{p}{1+p}(10^{120}-10^{27.5}),\qquad
x_{120}=p(10^{120}-10^{27.5}).
$$

No second research-feedback multiplier is applied after fitting a relation to raw inputs. A structural effective-effort interpretation would be a different extrapolation.

The 400-replicate uncertainty exercise now resamples **same-base-model clusters**, conditional on the selected sample; it does not repeat Pareto selection. Cluster normalization groups price dates, `_low/_medium/_high/_xhigh`, token budgets such as `_16K/_32K/_64K` and parenthesized budgets, thinking/reasoning modes, spaced or unspaced Option labels, and AIME/SWE evaluation labels. It preserves parameter sizes, model release dates embedded in IDs, named release-month distinctions, and checkpoint revisions such as 0528/2507. Distinct labels are not joined through speculative aliases. This corrects the earlier narrower clustering, which left spaced Option and reasoning-budget variants independent. Conditional intervals only include positive adjusted slopes, and the fraction of positive draws is retained. They omit hardware/proxy uncertainty, residual dependence between different model releases, and future functional-form uncertainty. Chronological validation fits through 2025 and predicts 2026 observations when there are at least three test points; sample sizes remain small.

Every attempted method is in inference_results.json. All combinations of the two H values and two G values for successful fits are in all_answers.csv. Three open-frontier SWE variants were withheld because only six points were available; the predeclared fitting guard requires at least eight points and full-rank design. A negative hardware-adjusted slope is reported as incompatible with the positive k0 calibration rather than being converted into an invented stopping answer.

Reproduce with python fit_inference.py from this directory. The script imports no downloaded code, and source hashes are recorded in source_manifest.json.
