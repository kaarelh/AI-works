# AI-works

| [`paulsen/`](paulsen/linear-paulsen/) | *A linear bound for the Paulsen problem*: every ε-nearly equal-norm Parseval frame of n vectors in ℝᵈ is within squared distance Cεd of an equal-norm Parseval frame. With a complete Lean 4 formalisation. | [paper (PDF)](paulsen/linear-paulsen/paper/linear-paulsen.pdf) |
| [`neural-network-generalization/`](neural-network-generalization/) | *Bayesian neural-network generalization*: circuit-learning bounds in the strong-prior and γ≥1 regimes, exponential parity lower bounds for γ<1, and polynomial depth dependence under an explicit internal-stability restriction. Supporting work gives unrestricted critical-prior bounds at every realizable width, including polynomial samples for parity at sufficient logarithmic depth and polynomial learner-depth dependence for fixed-depth teachers, as well as continuous-domain learning bounds and an exponential-depth regression counterexample. New conventional results give literal quadratic teacher-width and linear-depth dependence under a strong digital-population restriction, and improve the original robust-prior depth powers to 17 throughout γ≥1 and 7 in its quartic stronger-scale variant. Supporting work also proves total online mistake bounds, circuit-induction converses, and learning for ReLU, GELU, and general Lipschitz activations throughout γ≥1; restricted-prior ReLU/GELU learning and a neural mistake converse have polynomial depth dependence. The original five main results and a second batch of ten papers have precisely scoped Lean proofs. A further Lean-verified theorem shows that, assuming nonuniform P/poly differs from TC¹, unrestricted tanh NNbayes cannot have a universal sample bound polynomial jointly in teacher width and depth and input dimension, independent of learner width. Includes a technical FAQ and experiments with documented reproduction scope. | [original main results](neural-network-generalization/README.md#main-results) · [second formalized batch](neural-network-generalization/README.md#second-batch-of-formalized-main-results) · [conditional impossibility](neural-network-generalization/README.md#conditional-obstruction-to-polynomial-circuit-learning) · [technical FAQ](neural-network-generalization/TECHNICAL-FAQ.md) · [Lean verification](neural-network-generalization/lean/VERIFICATION.md) · [all-paper writing and mathematical review](neural-network-generalization/verification/remaining-papers-2026-10-29.md) |

| [`solomonoff-operator-induction/`](solomonoff-operator-induction/) | *Solomonoff operator induction*: two companion papers by Kaarel Hänni and Claude. The first studies versions of Solomonoff's universal-prior solution to operator induction (predicting labels at new inputs from input–output pairs) and proves their basic theorems: how the versions compare and the failure of a coding theorem, learning bounds, order-blindness and history-aware hypotheses, the cyclic-permutation and Eisenstat examples, and neural-network Bayes as circuit SOI. The second studies Solomonoff's per-query solution with steeper simplicity priors: absorption, the model–randomness split, and the teacher's mistakes. | [paper 1 (PDF)](solomonoff-operator-induction/paper/main.pdf) · [paper 2 (PDF)](solomonoff-operator-induction/paper-steeper/main.pdf) |
| [`solomonoff-axiom-induction/`](solomonoff-axiom-induction/) | *Solomonoff Axiom Induction: Definitions, Basic Theorems, and What a Theory Is and Does*, by Kaarel Hänni and Claude. A universal prior over random axiom systems, with three-valued predictions (proved, refuted, independent): its basic theory, its exact relation to Solomonoff operator induction, random theories and Gödel phenomena, and what the formalism says, and fails to say, about what a scientific theory is and does, with Kepler's laws worked through on Mars data. | [paper (PDF)](solomonoff-axiom-induction/paper/main.pdf) |

| [`purpose-sensitivity/`](purpose-sensitivity/) | *Does an AI's work depend on what it's for?*: Claude and GPT models do identical tasks for an AI-ban campaign, MIRI, a frontier AI lab, a newsroom, and other principals. Study 1 (4 Claude models, replicated on 4 GPT models): no measurable penalty for AI-ban work; what shifts with the principal is strategic advice, and two GPT models refused some lobbying logistics for corporate clients only (exploratory). Study 2 (14 Claude models; 4 GPT models descriptively): no detectable penalty for AI-ban, AI-pause or self-deletion work in the Claude models, and no AI-ban penalty detected in the GPT models (with wider intervals); purpose matters through refusal, sharply for an obvious fraud. | [study 1](purpose-sensitivity/README.md) · [study 2](purpose-sensitivity/study2/README.md) |
| [`gd-circuit-growth/`](gd-circuit-growth/) | *Gradient descent on Boolean targets as noise-limited greedy feature growth*: what rich-regime (μP, Adam) networks do on Boolean targets, and discrete models of it. A noise-limited greedy feature-growth model and a two-constant closed form predict the networks' sample complexity out of sample, including a pre-registered test at d = 120, where it scales like d^(k−1) rather than Occam's k log d. A pre-registered composition test shows that leap complexity misses intermediate features. The whole sequence of functions a network passes through is characterized, and three models reproduce it on held-out targets. Generalization bounds of the form "short training ⇒ small circuit ⇒ generalization" are proved for the discrete model, diagonal linear and quadratic networks, and two-layer ReLU networks through the first escape, alongside distillation certificates for the trained networks. Proofs checked by AI referees, not humans. | [paper (PDF)](gd-circuit-growth/paper/gd-circuit-growth-paper.pdf) · [README](gd-circuit-growth/README.md) |
| [`circuit-output-fraction/`](circuit-output-fraction/) | *Beating Monte Carlo on rare circuit outputs*: estimating a tiny probability Pr[C(x)=1] for a Boolean circuit faster than sampling, with procedures that are sound on every circuit. Without a prover: envelopes and importance sampling, the Decompose–Force–Sample–Branch procedure, context-width compilation, a certified presumption of independence, certified annealing with exact samplers, local thresholds and affine structure. With a prover: proof-carrying importance sampling over any propositional proof system, with surprise accounting. Limits: no procedure beats sampling on all circuits unless NP ⊆ BPP, black-box verifiers cannot certify upper bounds, no complete argument system unless coNP ⊆ MA, and when the prover strictly helps. Code, experiments, and a full record of AI referee rounds; proofs checked by AI referees, not humans. | [paper (PDF)](circuit-output-fraction/paper/main.pdf) · [README](circuit-output-fraction/README.md) · [verification](circuit-output-fraction/verification/README.md) |
| [`compression-generalization/`](compression-generalization/) | *Compression bounds for trained networks, without validation data*: bounding a trained network's test error by compressing it, through an Occam bound on a short network g plus the disagreement Pr[f≠g]. With a known input distribution, the bounds for 289k-parameter MLPs on Boolean tasks are close to the test error, and on 20-bit inputs exact equivalence gives bounds valid for every input distribution. From the training set alone, a dithered transfer theorem gives a deterministic bound on the trained one-hidden-layer ReLU network itself. On MNIST it is non-vacuous for narrow networks (0.44) and for networks with sparse updates (0.31), and vacuous for wide dense ones. The random initialization costs nothing, and an init-kernel codec and an initialization study measure how it enters the description length. A follow-up asks how to find the short network efficiently. Worst-case search is as hard as learning circuits from queries. On Boolean tasks, influence search and restarted distillation find descriptions about as good as the true target's. Subsampling hidden units works only for mean-field networks. A twin trained on half the data gives 0.042 on MNIST, against 0.255 by compression. Proofs and code checked by AI referees, not humans. | [one-page summary (PDF)](compression-generalization/summary/summary.pdf) · [paper (PDF)](compression-generalization/paper/main.pdf) · [README](compression-generalization/README.md) · [follow-up](compression-generalization/search/README.md) · [verification](compression-generalization/verification/README.md) |
