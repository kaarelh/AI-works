## Q1. Which other argument families exist, and what does each claim? (Who, when, what date, reasoning and data, status at Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
Ten more families turned up. (A) Revenue extrapolation and (B) capex as investors' revealed belief. (C) Benchmark-saturation and capability-index extrapolation. (D) AI R&D automation trends and takeoff models (Davidson, Forethought, GATE). (E) The compute-supply "AGI by ~2030 or much later" deadline. (F) Algorithmic progress combined into effective compute. (G) Prediction markets and aggregated forecasts. (H) Interest-rate and market-implied timelines. (I) Lab leaders' stated dates. (J) Expert surveys and the skeptical "no trend to extrapolate" baseline (Erdil/Mechanize). Most of the "inside-view" families point to 2027–2032. The market-rate and researcher-survey families point much later (2040s+).

### Cited Findings

#### A. Revenue / adoption extrapolation
- **The explicit argument (AI Futures Project, Dec 2025).** It extends frontier-AI-company revenue until it reaches most of world GDP. An Epoch dataset put revenue near $20B, growing about 4.1x per year. That trend reaches $100T annualized revenue around the end of 2031. The authors concede that earlier fast-growing tech sectors plateaued, but argue AI may be different because it can in principle do almost any task. They call it "nonzero evidence" — [AI Futures Model: Dec 2025 Update](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/ai-futures-model-dec-2025-update).
- Daniel Kokotajlo says he trusts this method less than the group's main model but gives it some weight, and that it points to the early 2030s — [LessWrong, Dec 2025](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YABG5JmztGGPwNFq2/ai-futures-timelines-and-takeoff-model-dec-2025-update).
- Epoch's "AI in 2030" report (Sept 2025, commissioned by Google DeepMind; conclusions are the authors' own) argues that ~3x annual lab-revenue growth would put leading labs at revenues in the hundreds of billions before 2030, close to 1% of present US GDP. It adds that this would be enough to fund the >$100B investment needed in 2030 — [Epoch AI in 2030 PDF](https://epoch.ai/files/AI_2030.pdf); [Epoch blog](https://epoch.ai/blog/what-will-ai-look-like-in-2030).
- Epoch's Gradient Updates questioned whether OpenAI's projected $100B 2028 revenue was achievable, calling it unprecedented growth — [Epoch Gradient Updates](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/openai-is-projecting-unprecedented-revenue-growth).
- **Anthropic data, 2024–2026.** Run-rate went from $87M (Jan 2024) to ~$9B (end-2025) to $30B (April 2026). Amodei said the company "planned for 10x" annual growth but saw "80x" — [VentureBeat, Apr 2026](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-says-it-hit-a-30-billion-revenue-run-rate-after-crazy-80x-growth). Later figures: ~$47B in May and ~$65B at end-July 2026, per CNBC/Bloomberg reporting of an investor update (secondary) — [valueaddvc](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/anthropic-65-billion-arr-july-2026). Then ~$81B as of 31 Oct 2026, attributed to Anthropic's amended S-1 of 17 Nov 2026 (unverified; one blog plus one crypto site). Unaudited recognized revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was ~$33.2B (same source) — [Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/blog/anthropic-revenue); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/anthropic-revenue-run-rate-80-billion-ipo/).
- OpenAI disputed Anthropic's run-rate, saying it was overstated by ~$8B because of how cloud-partner revenue is counted (Bloomberg-sourced coverage) — [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/anthropics-revenue-run-rate-surges-to-30b-surpassing-openai/).
- **OpenAI data.** Run-rate was >$20B exiting 2025 and >$40B in Aug 2026. The FT (8 Oct 2026) reported it "approaching $50B" at end-Sept, net of partner shares. Axios (29 Sept) reported ~$70B on a different basis. Bloomberg reported a target of ≥$70B annualized by end-2026. The FT projects ~$36B recognized revenue in 2026 and $350B in 2030 — [Yahoo/Bloomberg](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-expects-70-billion-annualized-014338719.html); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/openai-revenue-run-rate-nears-50-billion/); [Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/blog/openai-revenue). OpenAI reportedly missed internal revenue targets in April 2026 (secondary) — [andrew.ooo](https://andrew.ooo/answers/is-the-ai-bubble-bursting-openai-revenue-miss-april-2026/).
- **Track record.** In the AI 2027 authors' grading of 2025 predictions, the sum of AGI companies' revenues was underpredicted by about 2x — [Epoch/AI Digest summary via search](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/how-well-did-forecasters-predict-2025-ai-progress); [AI Futures grading](https://blog.ai-futures.org/p/grading-ai-2027s-2025-predictions).
- **Economy-wide adoption is far lower than lab-internal adoption.** A January 2026 *Science* paper estimated that AI writes 29% of the Python functions committed by US developers (secondary citation) — [AI Code Share Tracker](https://provenbrief.com/story/the-ai-code-share-tracker-every-verified-public-number-on-ai-written-code-from-n).

- **2026 capex guidance for the big four hyperscalers** (aggregators; definitions differ): Amazon ~$220B (up from a $200B February guide); Alphabet $195–205B (raised twice, last in July); Microsoft ~$170B for calendar 2026, down from ~$190B after lease reclassification; Meta $135–145B (October update). Combined ~$720–745B — [yieldtheory](https://www.yieldtheory.app/research/hyperscaler-ai-capex-tracker-2026); [analysis-atlas](https://analysis-atlas.com/research/hyperscaler-ai-capex-2026/); [FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/hyperscalers-tap-external-financing-as-ai-capex-outruns-cash-flow). - Alphabet's July 2026 report, with its raised capex, triggered a sell-off and put other hyperscalers under "capex scrutiny" — [CNBC, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/28/hyperscalers-face-higher-capex-scrutiny-after-alphabet-report-panned.html). FactSet notes hyperscalers are turning to external financing as capex outruns cash flow — [FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/hyperscalers-tap-external-financing-as-ai-capex-outruns-cash-flow).
- Goldman Sachs forecasts global AI investment above $1T in 2026. It projects US AI capex rising from 1.8% of GDP (2026) to 2.8% (2028), within the 2–5%-of-GDP peaks of earlier general-purpose-technology buildouts — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/global-investment-is-forecast-to-exceed-1-trillion-in-2026).
- **The "revenue needed" framing.** Sequoia's 2024 "$600B question" estimated the annual AI revenue needed to justify the buildout. 2026 capex alone now exceeds that threshold (secondary) — [FutureLume](https://futurelume.net/startups/funding-venture-capital/the-ai-bubble-question-in-2026-what-the-funding-and-revenue-data-actually-shows/). A J.P. Morgan return-based estimate of ~$650B per year in needed AI revenue is cited only by a weak source — [No One's Happy](https://nooneshappy.com/article/the-ai-bubble/).
- **The formal "revealed belief" version.** Jessica and Jonathan Wachter, *What Investment Data Implies about the AI Transition*, NBER WP 35290 (June 2026), calibrate a model to the observed rise in AI investment projected through 2027. A near-term boom raises AI-sector productivity ~2.7x. Their slides show three scenarios (moderate, transformative, singularity) with AI shares of post-window output of 8.0%, 19.0% and 38.7%, and an elevated boom probability in 2028–2029. The meaning of the scenario probability labels (P=0.25) could not be confirmed from the captions — [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35290/w35290.pdf); [slides](https://jwachter3.github.io/slides/WachterWachterAISlides.pdf); [Windfall Trust brief](https://windfalltrust.substack.com/p/brief-13-ai-firms-are-spending-as).
- **Lab-side commitments** (weakly sourced): an OpenAI internal plan reportedly projects ~$278B of negative free cash flow over 2026–2030 against ~$856B of compute and infrastructure spending — [Luminix](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/industry-analysis/when-will-the-ai-bubble-burst). A ~$1.4T figure for circular AI-deal revenue commitments, attributed to the IMF, could not be verified — [Aiera](https://aiera.blog/openai-anthropic-capex-dependence/).
- **Nvidia as the demand barometer.** Fiscal Q3 2027 (quarter ended 25 Oct 2026, reported mid-Nov 2026): revenue $115.8B (+103% YoY), data center $107.6B (+110%), Q4 guide $130B ±3% — [247 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/cards/nvidia-q3-2027-earnings-nvda-01m7nph36m2fmpwcfatxg16f2z); [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/news/nvda-q3-fy2027-earnings-beat/). A year earlier (fiscal Q3 2026, Nov 2025) revenue was $57.0B and data center $51.2B — [Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/nvidia-q3-fy-2026-record-data-center-revenue-higher-q4-guide/). No reliable current market cap was found (~$4.5–5T per unconfirmed sources).
- **Physical buildout.** At Stargate Abilene, Oracle reported 6 of 8 buildings delivered (618 MW, ~75% of a 1.2 GW campus) on 10 Sept 2026, and a planned 600 MW expansion was dropped in March 2026 — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/oracleopenai-drop-plans-to-expand-flagship-abilene-stargate-site-meta-in-talks-to-pick-up-crusoe-capacity-with-nvidias-help/); [stargate.how](https://stargate.how/news/current-status-us-stargate-data-centers). #### C. Benchmark-saturation and capability-index extrapolation
- **Owen (Epoch), "How predictable is language model benchmark performance?"** (Epoch June 2023; arXiv Jan 2024). Aggregate benchmark performance is fairly predictable from training compute: ~6pp mean absolute error per order of magnitude of scaling. Individual BIG-Bench tasks are less predictable (~18pp average error per OOM) but still beat baselines. Owen concludes compute-based extrapolation is a promising forecasting method — [arXiv 2401.04757](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.04757); [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/publications/how-predictable-is-language-model-benchmark-performance).
- **Pimpale, Højmark, Scheurer, Hobbhahn (Apollo/MATS), "Forecasting Frontier Language Model Agent Capabilities"** (Feb 2025). A two-step method (release date → Elo → benchmark score), fitted on 38 models, forecast that by the beginning of 2026 state-of-the-art agents reach 87% on SWE-Bench Verified and low-elicitation agents 54%. With high elicitation, 90% was expected around March 2026 (95% CI Oct 2025–Sept 2027). The authors note that ignoring inference-compute scaling may make the method too conservative — [arXiv 2502.15850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15850); [Apollo blog](https://apolloresearch.ai/blog/forecasting-frontier-language-model-agent-capabilities).
- **Outcome on SWE-bench Verified.** In the AI Digest survey (421 forecasters), the median forecast for end-2025 was 88% and the actual was 81% — [Epoch review, Jan 2026](https://epochai.substack.com/p/how-well-did-forecasters-predict). By 2026, independent evaluations put the frontier at 96–97%, and OpenAI published a Feb 2026 note saying SWE-bench Verified no longer measures frontier coding capability. Harder successors (SWE-bench Pro and variants) sit at roughly 43–64% — [arXiv 2609.08149](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08149) (via search summary).
- **Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI).**
  - Trend: the ECI frontier has advanced ~14 points per year since reasoning models arrived — [Epoch data insight](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/eci-frontier-trend). Under Epoch's Bayesian ECI methodology the trend is 8.0 points/yr before reasoning models and 16.6 after (6.2 and 15.7 under the old method) — [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/publications/eci-bayesian-methodology).
  - Data points: Gemini 3 Pro scored 154 in Nov 2025, up from GPT-5.1's 151 — [Epoch on X](https://x.com/EpochAIResearch/status/1991945942174761050). On 17 Nov 2026 GPT-6.2 Astra scored 174, "on par with Fable 5.2", both above the reasoning-era trend of +16/yr — [Epoch on X](https://x.com/EpochAIResearch/status/2122762380463735010).
  - Unverified claim: a secondary summary says the pace rose to ~24 points/yr after a break around March 2026 — [Against Moloch #52](https://againstmoloch.substack.com/p/against-moloch-52).
  - Open-weight models have lagged closed frontier models by ~4 months on ECI since Jan 2026 — [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/open-closed-eci-gap).
- **FrontierMath.** Records: Gemini 3 Pro 38% on Tiers 1–3 and 19% on Tier 4 (Nov 2025); GPT-5.2 Pro 31% on Tier 4 (Jan 2026); GPT-5.4 Pro 38% on Tier 4 — [Epoch on X](https://x.com/EpochAIResearch/status/2014769359747744200); [Epoch Substack](https://epochai.substack.com/p/gpt-54-set-a-new-record-on-frontiermath). One tracker lists GPT-5.5 Pro at 39.6% on Tier 4 (Apr 2026) — [evals.report](https://evals.report/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier4?tab=scores). **Conflicting and unverified:** several trackers claim GPT-6.1 Sol scored 100% on Tier 4 (released 29 Sept 2026; problem set of ~41–50) — [Intelligence Progress](https://intelligenceprogress.com/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4); [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/frontiermathv2tier4); [Exponential Tracker](https://exponentialtracker.com/curve/frontiermath). No Epoch confirmation of the 100% figure was found.
- **Humanity's Last Exam.** Artificial Analysis lists Claude Fable 5.2 at 74.3% (Nov 2026). Other leaderboards show 54.8–70% depending on harness and date — [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/humanitys-last-exam); [chatjudge](https://chatjudge.ai/benchmarks/reasoning/humanitys-last-exam).
- **OSWorld** (secondary, unverified): agents first passed the ~72% human baseline in Dec 2025 (Agent S, 72.6%). OSWorld 2.0, released June 2026, had a top score of 20.6% — [buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/osworld-benchmark-ai-agents-passed-human-baseline-2026).
- **Forecasters vs benchmarks.** In Epoch's review of the 2025 AI Digest survey, forecasters were "mostly right" on benchmarks, with mixed results on real-world impacts. They overshot SWE-bench but undershot Cybench and the Hypermind contest. A Forecasting Research Institute study found that domain experts and superforecasters underestimated progress on MATH, MMLU and QuaLITY — [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/how-well-did-forecasters-predict-2025-ai-progress); [FRI](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/what-experts-and-superforecasters).

#### D. AI R&D automation extrapolations and takeoff models
- **Share of code written by AI.**
  - Amodei at the Council on Foreign Relations (10 Mar 2025): AI would write 90% of code in 3–6 months and "essentially all of the code" in 12 months. IBM's Arvind Krishna countered "more like 20–30%" — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.nl/anthropics-ceo-says-that-in-3-to-6-months-ai-will-be-writing-90-of-the-code-software-developers-were-in-charge-of/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/03/13/ai-transforming-software-development-jobs-meta-ibm-anthropic/); [aiproductivity.ai](https://aiproductivity.ai/news/dario-amodei-ai-code-prediction-12-months-later/).
  - Anthropic outcome: Amodei said ~90% at Dreamforce (Oct 2025). In Jan 2026 a spokesperson put the company-wide figure at 70–90%, while Boris Cherny said "pretty much 100%". CFO Krishna Rao said "90 plus percent" in May 2026. An Anthropic Institute report found >80% of code merged into production in May 2026 was authored by Claude — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-writing-code-anthropic-openai/); [Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2026/01/29/100-percent-of-code-at-anthropic-and-openai-is-now-ai-written-boris-cherny-roon); [AI Code Share Tracker](https://provenbrief.com/story/the-ai-code-share-tracker-every-verified-public-number-on-ai-written-code-from-n).
  - OpenAI: Greg Brockman said in May 2026 that AI writes ~80% of the lines committed to OpenAI's codebase — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-brockman-80-percent-code-ai-productivity-claim).
- **OpenAI's stated R&D-automation roadmap** (livestream, 28 Oct 2025): an intern-level AI research assistant by Sept 2026 and "a true automated AI researcher" by March 2028. Jakub Pachocki said deep learning systems may be "less than a decade away from superintelligence" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/28/sam-altman-says-openai-will-have-a-legitimate-ai-researcher-by-2028/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/openai-roadmap-revealed-ai-research-interns-by-2026-full-blown-agi-researchers-by-2028).
- **Measured R&D acceleration: METR, "Independent Assessment of AI R&D Acceleration at Anthropic in October 2026"** (5 Nov 2026). The figures below come mainly from secondary write-ups of the post.
  - Headline: Anthropic's rate of capability improvement on well-specified tasks was ~1.9x the March 2024–Oct 2025 rate (95% CI 1.5–2.8x), from ~15 to ~29 index points per year. METR guesses ~80% of the speedup is due to AI use, and is confident it is not faster training-compute growth.
  - Other estimates: staff Slack-poll self-reports average ~12x, which METR calls "very loose" evidence; interviews cluster around 2x. Anthropic's Haiku 5.5 system card reported 1.7x. Pooling all developers' public models gives 1.4x.
  - Threshold: Anthropic may have crossed its 2x early-warning threshold by 15 July 2026, but most relevant researchers lean "not crossed".
  - Evidence and outlook: "Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence we found most informative." METR detected no compute, data or algorithm bottlenecks over the next 12 months — [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/); [resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-06-metr-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration/); [aimodeling](https://www.aimodeling.com/en/news/slug/metr-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment).
- **Counter-evidence from randomized trials.** METR's 2025 RCT found experienced open-source developers took 19% longer with early-2025 AI tools, though they believed they were ~20% faster — [arXiv 2507.09089](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089). The Feb 2026 follow-up estimated −18% (CI −38% to +9%) for returning developers and −4% (−15% to +9%) for new ones. METR calls this an unreliable signal because developers unwilling to work without AI dropped out — [METR, 24 Feb 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/).
- **Davidson's compute-centric takeoff model** (Open Philanthropy, Jan 2023; interactive version at takeoffspeeds.com, built by Epoch). The headline metric is the time from AI that can readily automate 20% of cognitive tasks to AI that can automate 100%, with a median of ~3 years. Davidson's median AGI training requirement is 1e36 FLOP with 2020 algorithms, and his best-guess effective-compute gap is reported as ~10,000x. Conditional on AGI by 2100, he puts ~25% on a takeoff shorter than a year — [Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/Gc9FGtdXhK9sCSEYu/what-a-compute-centric-framework-says-about-ai-takeoff).
  - Dates: the original model predicted full economic automation around 2040. An update re-run with newer Epoch data moves this to ~2030 (central 2027–28, aggressive 2026, conservative 2035) — [LessWrong "Takeoff speeds update: crunch time"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/jLEcddwp4RBTpPHHq/takeoff-speeds-update-crunch-time-1); [readtheoom](https://readtheoom.substack.com/p/the-takeoff-speeds-model-predicts).
  - Another post reports that with Epoch's default parameters, the automated fraction of cognitive tasks only reaches 1% around 2029 — [EA Forum](https://forum-bots.effectivealtruism.org/posts/dbdx4TmngQYtdLafG).
- **Forethought, Davidson & Houlden, "How quick and big would a software intelligence explosion be?"** (Aug 2025). With compute held fixed, the model gives ~60% that software progress compresses >3 years of AI progress into <1 year, and ~20% that it compresses >10 years into <1 year. The figures come from Monte Carlo runs over log-uniform parameters, starting from the point where AI automates AI research — [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research/how-quick-and-big-would-a-software-intelligence-explosion-be). A critique ("Humans are not chinchillas") disputes the returns-to-software-R&D parameter and the ceiling — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xoChjkJeefmoP2ufH/humans-are-not-chinchillas-revisiting-how-quick-and-big).
- **Epoch's GATE (Growth and AI Transition Endogenous) model** (2025). An integrated assessment model in which compute investment drives gradual task automation, which feeds back into output and further investment. It predicts growth 2–20x the historical ~3%/yr — [Epoch, Announcing GATE](https://epoch.ai/blog/announcing-gate). The AI Futures Project reports that GATE with median parameters predicts AGI in 2034 — [AI Futures Dec 2025](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/ai-futures-model-dec-2025-update).

#### E. Compute-supply arguments and the "AGI by ~2030 or much later" deadline
- **Epoch, "Can AI Scaling Continue Through 2030?"** (Sevilla et al., Aug 2024). Training runs of ~2e29 FLOP are likely feasible by 2030, ~10,000x the scale of then-current models. The constraints:
  - Power: 1–5 GW campuses support 1e28–3e29 FLOP.
  - Chips: capacity for ~100M H100-equivalents, enough for ~9e29 FLOP.
  - Data: the most uncertain constraint; a text "data wall" in ~5 years, eased by synthetic data.
  - Latency: a wall at ~2e31 FLOP, with little effect before 2030.
  - Sources: [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/publications/can-ai-scaling-continue-through-2030); [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/SoWperLCkunB9ijGq/can-ai-scaling-continue-through-2030-epoch-ai-yes); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-scaling-could-increase-by-a-factor-of-10000-by-2030/).
- Epoch's "AI in 2030" (Sept 2025) reaffirms that exponential growth in all key inputs is likely to continue to 2030: ~2e29 FLOP training runs (secondary) and ~6 GW of power demand at 4x/yr growth. It names substantial R&D automation as a possible break from trend — [Epoch AI 2030 PDF](https://epoch.ai/files/AI_2030.pdf); [Epoch blog](https://epoch.ai/blog/what-will-ai-look-like-in-2030).
- **Benjamin Todd / 80,000 Hours, "The case for AGI by 2030"** (Apr 2025). Training compute has grown >4x/yr. Compute, algorithms and the research workforce are all set to keep growing until ~2028, perhaps 2032. But the drivers need exponential growth in investment and workforce, so they "cannot keep rising at current rates much beyond 2030". Hence "we'll likely either reach AGI by around 2030 or see progress slow significantly" — [80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/agi/guide/when-will-agi-arrive/); [Todd Substack](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/the-case-for-agi-by-2030); [LessWrong copy](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/NkwHxQ67MMXNqRnsR/the-case-for-agi-by-2030).
- **Status, Nov 2026.**
  - The largest known training run is ~1e27 FLOP (GPT-6 Astra on ≥100k GB200, ~233 MW, per Epoch's dataset as reported by a tracker; another tracker says 9.4e26). No confirmed run at ≥1e28 FLOP as of 7 Nov 2026 — [FutureSearch](https://futuresearch.ai/app/p/a/rsi-largest-training-compute); [AI 2027 Tracker](https://ai2027-tracker.com/predictions/training-compute/).
  - Epoch notes frontier training runs "can't get much longer" in duration — [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/longest-training-run).
  - METR saw no compute bottleneck over the next 12 months (see D).

#### F. Algorithmic progress and effective compute
- **Ho et al., "Algorithmic progress in language models"** (arXiv Mar 2024; NeurIPS 2024; Epoch, MIT FutureTech and Northeastern authors). From 200+ evaluations over 2012–2023, the compute needed to reach a fixed performance level halved roughly every 8 months. Compute scaling contributed more to gains than algorithms did. The study does not measure downstream tasks — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6b066da6a23bc55f9b887e7298102884-Abstract.html); [MIT CSAIL](https://www.csail.mit.edu/node/11516).
- **Range of estimates.** Epoch's canonical LLM figure is ~3x/yr less compute for a fixed loss. A Gradient Updates essay says it could be ~10x or more, with an uncertainty range of 1.1x–300x/yr, and that much "algorithmic" progress may really be data-quality improvement — [Epoch Gradient Updates](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/the-least-understood-driver-of-ai-progress); [Alignment Forum summary](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/v7LtZx6Qk5e9s7zj3/what-s-going-on-with-ai-progress-and-trends-as-of-5-2025).

#### H. Financial-market-implied timelines (interest rates)
- **Chow, Halperin & Mazlish, "AGI and the EMH"** (Jan 2023; later an academic paper). Transformative AI, aligned or not, should raise long-run real rates: through expected growth if aligned, through higher mortality-discounting if not. Low real rates therefore imply markets "decisively reject" 0–10-year timelines and are "not expecting aligned or unaligned AI in the next 30 years" — [EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8c7LycgtkypkgYjZx/agi-and-the-emh-markets-are-not-expecting-aligned-or); [Open Phil PDF](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/wp-content/uploads/Chow-Halperin-and-Mazlish-2023-Basil-Halperin.pdf).
- **Andrews & Farboodi, "Do Markets Believe in Transformative AI?"** (NBER WP 34243; MIT versions 2025–July 2026). Across 30 release dates from Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and xAI (Nov 2022–Dec 2025), long-maturity Treasury, TIPS and corporate yields fall and stay lower for weeks. In their consumption model, this means investors lowered growth expectations, lowered perceived existential risk, or both. Changes in growth uncertainty do not explain it. Larger yield drops coincided with larger upward moves in Metaculus AI-progress beliefs, and the authors caveat that markets may be pricing profit opportunities rather than transformative growth — [MIT PDF](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2026-07/Do%20Markets%20Believe%20in%20Transformative%20AI.pdf); [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/50583); [Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2025/09/do-markets-believe-in-transformative-ai.html).
- **Critiques.** One argues the EMH does not imply markets have long timelines — [EA Forum](https://forum-bots.effectivealtruism.org/posts/Go5CDwyna3hAfngKP/no-the-emh-does-not-imply-that-markets-have-long-agi). Another argues TAI need not raise rates once AI creates new goods — [Decker Substack](https://nicholasdecker.substack.com/p/will-transformative-ai-really-raise-interest-rates). A 2026 FRBSF working paper restates the rate logic — [FRBSF WP 2026-19](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/wp2026-19.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Revenue arithmetic** (my calculation from the figures above):
  - Combined run-rate: Anthropic (~$81B, 31 Oct, unverified) plus OpenAI (~$50B, end-Sept, net basis) ≈ $130B in autumn 2026, against ≈$29–30B at end-2025 (~$9B + >$20B). That is roughly 4–4.5x in ~10 months, above the 4.1x/yr trend the AI Futures Project used. Caveat: the two firms count revenue differently, and run-rate is not recognized revenue (Anthropic's recognized revenue for 9M 2026 was ~$33B).
  - Extrapolating from ~$130B: $100T is reached in ≈4.8 years at 4x/yr (≈ late 2031), ≈6 years at 3x/yr (≈2032–33), and ≈9.6 years at 2x/yr (≈2036).
  - So the revenue argument's date is very sensitive to the assumed growth multiple, and the 2026 data have so far *exceeded* the trend it assumed.
- **Capex vs revenue.** Big-four capex of ~$720–745B (2026) is roughly 5–6x the two leading labs' combined run-rate. Capex numbers can therefore support either reading. Bulls: actors with the most private information are betting on near-total automation of white-collar work. Bears: a buildout that has outrun proven demand.
- **The Wachter & Wachter paper** is the most rigorous version of the "investment reveals belief" argument found. It turns investment into scenario probabilities rather than an AGI date. Its 2028–2029 "boom window" is close to the lab-leader dates.
- **Compute check** (my calculation): ~1e27 FLOP in 2026 is roughly what Epoch's 4x/yr trend implies from GPT-4-era (~2e25, 2023) runs. Reaching 2e29 by 2030 needs ~200x in ~4 years (≈3.8x/yr), so the compute side of the "by 2030" deadline is still on schedule as of Nov 2026. The 2e25 GPT-4 figure is a commonly cited Epoch estimate that was not re-verified this session.
- **Effective compute:** combining ~4–5x/yr training compute with ~3x/yr algorithmic efficiency gives ~12–15x/yr, about 1.1 OOM/yr. On the higher catch-up estimates (16–60x) it would be 2+ OOM/yr. Davidson's ~4-OOM gap (20%→100% automation) would then close in ~2–4 years once 20% automation is reached. That is why the takeoff-model families give short gaps between "meaningful automation" and "AGI".
- **The ECI reading:** Gemini 3 Pro at 154 (Nov 2025) to GPT-6.2 Astra / Fable 5.2 at 174 (Nov 2026) is about +20 points in 12 months, above Epoch's 14–16.6/yr trend. This fits METR's ~1.9x Anthropic acceleration finding. ECI has no agreed "AGI threshold", so neither yields a date by itself.

## Q3. Which have track records yet (predictions resolvable by November 2026)?

## Q4. What is the steelman of each, and the strongest objection?

### Takeaway
The strongest short-timeline cases are the **AI-R&D feedback-loop** family and the **revenue/investment-as-revealed-belief** family. The first now has an independent measurement of ~2x acceleration; the second rests on data that beat its own trend in 2026. The strongest long-timeline cases are **interest rates** and **Erdil's "automated fraction of the economy is tiny"** argument. The strongest objection to nearly every short-date family is the same: the step from a measurable proxy (revenue, benchmarks, % of code, ECI) to "AGI" is assumed, not measured.

### Cited Findings
(Support for each steelman and objection; analysis in Inferences.)
- Revenue: AI Futures concede the plateau risk and argue AI's generality is the exception — [AI Futures](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/ai-futures-model-dec-2025-update). Partner-revenue accounting disputes add noise — [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/anthropics-revenue-run-rate-surges-to-30b-surpassing-openai/).

### Inferences
**A. Revenue extrapolation**
- *Steelman:* revenue is the least gameable capability measure, because customers pay only for work AI actually does. It has grown ~3–10x/yr for three years and beat forecasts by ~2x in 2025. Extrapolating at the long-run ~4x gives AI revenue the size of most of world GDP around 2031. Even at 2x/yr, it reaches a "transformative" share (say 10% of GDP) in the early 2030s.
- *Strongest objection:* every prior hypergrowth sector followed an S-curve. Run-rates exaggerate: Anthropic's recognized 9-month revenue was ~$33B against an $81B run-rate. Much revenue is coding and API resale that could saturate well before full automation. And the method has no mechanism linking dollars to "AGI".

**B. Investment / revealed belief**
- *Steelman:* ~$720–745B of 2026 capex plus multi-hundred-billion lab commitments is the costliest bet in corporate history. It is placed by the actors with the best private view of unreleased models, and only pays off if AI captures a large share of the global wage bill within the few-year asset life of the chips. Wachter & Wachter formalize this as a high-probability boom in 2028–29.
- *Strongest objection:* investment reflects expected *profits under competition* (fear of missing out, strategic positioning, circular vendor financing), not beliefs about AGI. Bond markets, which should price transformative growth, show no such expectation. Bubbles have been mispriced before.

**C. Benchmark saturation / ECI**
- *Steelman:* benchmark scores are predictable from compute and date (Owen; Pimpale's forecast was within ~6pp). Every hard benchmark made since 2023 has saturated in about 1–3 years: SWE-bench Verified ~97%, HLE ~74%, OSWorld v1 above human baseline. ECI is accelerating (+20 pts/yr against a 14–16 trend). If "AGI" means "matches experts on any test we can write", it is close.
- *Strongest objection:* saturation keeps failing to transfer. When SWE-bench Verified was retired, SWE-bench Pro was at ~43–64%. After OSWorld v1 was passed, OSWorld 2.0 opened at ~21%. The economy-wide share of automated tasks remains small. Benchmarks measure well-specified tasks, exactly where METR found acceleration, not open-ended work.

**D. AI R&D automation / takeoff models**
- *Steelman:* this is the family with a causal mechanism. Once AI speeds up AI research, progress compounds. There is now direct evidence that the loop has started: ~80–90%+ of lab code is AI-written, and METR's independent estimate is a ~1.9x faster capability trend at Anthropic, ~80% attributed to AI. Davidson's model puts ~3 years from 20% to 100% automation, and re-run on 2024–25 data it gives full automation ~2027–2030. Forethought gives ~60% that a software-only explosion compresses >3 years of progress into <1.
- *Strongest objection:* the measured acceleration (1.4–2x) is modest and partly redacted, and RCTs of real developer productivity find no speedup. Code share is a weak proxy for research automation: ideas, research taste and compute-bound experiments are the bottleneck. The takeoff models' dates hinge on contested parameters (training requirements of 1e36 FLOP with 2020 algorithms, returns to software R&D).

**E. Compute deadline ("AGI by ~2030 or much later")**
- *Steelman:* effective compute is the input that has tracked capability most reliably. Physical, financial and workforce growth rates can continue to ~2030 (2e29 FLOP feasible) but not much beyond, so the probability per year of AGI is front-loaded. Epoch's 2024 feasibility analysis is on track in 2026 (~1e27 runs, capex doubling).
- *Strongest objection:* the argument relies on how much effective compute AGI requires, which is unknown. If the requirement is 1e30+ FLOP, the deadline just means "later". If inference-time compute, algorithms or AI-driven R&D substitute for training compute, the deadline loosens. The physical buildout is already slipping in places: Abilene's expansion was dropped, and power permits are pending.

**F. Algorithmic progress + effective compute OOMs**
- *Steelman:* algorithms have contributed ~3x/yr, perhaps 10x+ including post-training, on top of ~4–5x/yr compute. That is over 1 OOM of effective compute per year, so a few OOMs between current systems and AGI closes this decade.
- *Strongest objection:* the uncertainty range is 1.1x–300x/yr. The measured gains are on perplexity, not downstream tasks. They may be data-quality gains that don't repeat, and "OOMs to AGI" requires a capability-to-compute map nobody has.

**G. Prediction markets / aggregated forecasts**
- *Steelman:* aggregated forecasters beat individual experts in most domains. Metaculus has 1,800+ forecasters and markets put money at stake. They currently put ~40–60% on AGI-ish milestones by 2030 and ~50% on Metaculus "general AI" by ~2030–33, and they have moved earlier as evidence came in.
- *Strongest objection:* long-dated AI markets are thin, with low stakes and no time-value-of-money correction. They resolve on idiosyncratic definitions (OpenAI "announcing" AGI) and correlate with hype. FRI found forecasters systematically *underestimated* benchmark progress, which cuts both ways: it shows their record on this topic is poor.

**H. Interest rates / market-implied**
- *Steelman:* bond markets are the deepest, most-informed markets in the world. Transformative growth or existential risk would raise real rates, yet rates show no such pricing, and yields even fall on model releases. So markets assign low probability to AGI within ~10 years.
- *Strongest objection:* no one can profitably arbitrage a singularity, and precautionary saving under AI uncertainty can lower rates. Andrews & Farboodi show yields *respond* to AI news, so markets are not ignoring AI. The direction of the response has competing explanations, and equities (Nvidia revenue doubling YoY) are pricing large AI cash flows.

**I. Lab leaders' statements**
- *Steelman:* leaders see unreleased models and internal R&D metrics. Their dates have converged on 2026–2030 across rival firms (Amodei, Altman, Hassabis, Musk, Huang). Some of their specific near-term claims came true within about a year: 90% AI-written code inside the lab, and revenue growth.
- *Strongest objection:* they have strong incentives (fundraising, recruiting, policy) to give short timelines. Their dates have repeatedly slipped (Musk 2025→2026; Anthropic "as early as 2026"→"1–2 years from 2026"), and each defines AGI to suit their firm. Altman himself reportedly admits overestimating diffusion.

**J. Skeptic baseline (Erdil/Mechanize; ESPAI)**
- *Steelman:* the only direct measure of "AGI" as economic substitution is the fraction of tasks actually automated in the economy. That fraction is still small (e.g. 29% of US Python functions, against ~90% at the labs). No smooth trend in it points to 100% this decade, so ~2045 is reasonable.
- *Strongest objection:* it ignores compounding. If AI R&D automation is real (METR ~2x), then lab-internal adoption leads the economy by only a few years. The 2023 survey medians had already moved 13 years earlier in one year, which suggests they lag rather than lead.

### Gaps
- No quantitative steelman of a "cognitive-labor-share crossover" or "energy per human-equivalent" argument with a date was found in 2025–26 sources (beyond the revenue/GDP and Wachter scenario framings).
- Could not verify the strength of the 2026 versions of the Chow–Halperin–Mazlish argument (no 2026 rate data gathered).
