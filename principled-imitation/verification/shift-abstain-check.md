**Corollary 3.6 (asking cannot stop). The displayed bound is CORRECT; the last sentence and the section title are WRONG as stated (overclaim).** The bound is Theorem 3.3(a) with N = h + 1. But the class depends on h: it has h + 1 members, so K = log(h + 1) grows with the horizon. For a fixed class the asking can stop: by the file's own Proposition 4.3(a), for a deterministic human with η < w(f)/W, the threshold rule whose abstentions are put to the human makes no silent error at all, on any input sequence of any length, and asks at most K/log(1/(1 − η)) < (W/w(f))·K ln 2 questions in total (roughly K·2^K ln 2). Counterexample to the sentence "A bound on the total number of silent errors over an unbounded deployment, as in Theorem 3.1(b), requires the human for ever": the thresholds with N members (K = log N), η = 1/(2N): Sil = 0 and Qu ≤ log N/log(1/(1 − 1/(2N))) ≈ 2N ln N for ever. Strongest true replacement: "with at most Q questions, (Sil + Abs) ≥ ¼(N/(Q + 1) − (Q + 1)/N) on a class of N = 2^K functions; so a bound on silent errors plus unanswered abstentions that does not grow with the horizon needs a number of questions that grows with 2^K (exponentially in the complexity of the human), and, for a stochastic human, Proposition 4.8 shows that no rule has both counts; but for a deterministic human and a fixed class the asking does stop after at most about 2^K·K ln 2 questions." The same correction applies to the title of Section 3 ("why asking cannot stop"), item (2) of "In brief", the reading of Corollary 3.6, and Section 7 wherever "the asking cannot stop" is stated without the dependence on K (the adversary's finding 1 is confirmed).
