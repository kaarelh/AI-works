**N1 (Section 2, paragraph "Compute"; source `research/orchestration/patches.py`).** The procedure is right, but two sentences claim slightly more than holds, and the cost for a human who varies is hidden.
* Sentence: "If the hypotheses are programs that always halt, for instance programs that carry their own time bound, and the weights are 2^(−length) in a decidable prefix-free code, a posterior draw can be made by rejection:"
  Replacement: "If the hypotheses are programs that always halt, for instance programs that carry their own time bound, and the weights are 2^(−length) in a decidable prefix-free code whose weights sum to 1, a posterior draw can be made by rejection:"
  Reason: with an incomplete code the reading of a program from coin flips does not end with probability 1 − Σ 2^(−length), and a dead prefix cannot in general be recognised; the expected time per draw is then infinite.
* Sentence: "The expected number of trials is at most 2^K divided by the probability that the human gives the recorded answers; this is the "very much compute" of the question."

This is the "very much compute" of the question."
* Sentence: "For the class of all programs a trial may not halt; the theorems then concern the mixture as a mathematical object, which can only be approximated from below."
