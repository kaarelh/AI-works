### 1.1 Theorem 3.8 (R6(c): select with the weights squared). CORRECT.

Hypotheses: A1 to A3, P in a countable class H with weights of total W ≤ 1, mixture-driven rehearsal of n rounds, ν̂ a draw from Π₂ or any member with w(ν̂)²ν̂(D_n) ≥ w(P)²P(D_n).

* Existence of a maximiser: since Σ w ≤ 1, only finitely many ν have w(ν) ≥ w(P)√P(D_n) > 0, and every ν with w(ν)²ν(D_n) ≥ w(P)²P(D_n) is among them (ν(D_n) ≤ 1). Correct.
* Step 1: given the past, the asked input is x_{J} of a run of M^(t) when J ≤ T, and y ~ P(·|x) independently (A1, A3); so E[ρ_t | past] = 1 − E[1{J ≤ T}·H²(ν, P)(x_J)] = 1 − (1/L)·E_{Q_M} Σ_{j≤T} H²(ν,P)(x_j) = 1 − h_t(ν), using Lemma 1.6(c) (which holds also for a semimeasure ν, with its missing mass on ⊥, because the human never answers ⊥). (1 − h)e^h ≤ 1 gives the supermartingale; h_t depends only on D_{t−1}, so the filtration may be the full past. Π_t ρ_t = √(ν(D_n)/P(D_n)) because rounds without a question contribute 1. Correct.
* Step 2: on {ν ∈ A}, √(ν(D_n)/P(D_n)) ≤ N_n(ν)e^(−a); Tonelli gives E[S] ≤ e^(−a)·W/w(P) ≤ e^(−a)·2^K = δ·W ≤ δ with a = K ln 2 + ln(1/δ). Π₂(A) ≤ Σ_A s_ν² ≤ S² and Π₂(A) ≤ 1, so Π₂(A) ≤ min(1, S²) ≤ S. A member of A that beats P in w²·likelihood has s_ν ≥ 1. Both conclusions follow from Markov's inequality. Correct.
* Step 3: ρ′_t with M^(t) in place of ν has conditional mean 1 − κ_t/L (H² is symmetric). By Lemma 1.6(a), Π_t ρ′_t = √(ξ(D_n)/(W·P(D_n))) ≥ √(w(P)/W) ≥ 2^(−K/2). Markov: N′_n < 1/δ with probability ≥ 1 − δ, hence Σκ_t < L(½K ln 2 + ln(1/δ)). Correct.
* Step 4: I read Corollary 2.3 of `proofs/hellinger-runs.md`: it is stated for answerers on a scaffold, runs of variable length, inputs depending on answers, with the expectation along the first answerer's runs; the mixture and every hypothesis (completed by ⊥) are answerers. So H²(Q_M, Q_ν) ≤ 4E_{Q_M}Σ_j H²(ν, M)(x_j) ≤ 4E_{Q_M}Σ_j [2H²(ν,P) + 2H²(P,M)](x_j) = 8L·h_t + 8κ_t, H²(Q_M, Q_P) ≤ 4κ_t, and (E4) for run laws gives 2(8Lh + 8κ) + 2·4κ = 16L·h_t + 24κ_t. Correct. (E4) itself is correct: √(2H²) is the Euclidean distance of root vectors.
* Step 5: the inequality of Step 4 holds for every ν and every t, so it holds for the random ν̂; averaging over t and the events of (a), (b) give 16L(K ln 2 + l) + 24L(½K ln 2 + l) = L(28K ln 2 + 40l), l = ln(1/δ); union bound 1 − 2δ. TV ≤ √(1 − BC²) ≤ √(2H²). Correct.
* "What is used": with c* = 2.964147 in place of 4 the constants are 7c* = 20.75 and 10c* = 29.64, stated as 21 and 30 (rounded up). Correct (S8).
* Numerical tests (my own code, S1 to S3). S1: Step 4 on 1500 random instances (L = 1, 2, 3, inputs that depend on the answers, runs of variable length, samplers, semimeasures, functions, tilted humans, random mixtures), exact run laws: largest ratio of H²(Q_ν, Q_P) to 16L·h + 24κ is 0.073; the two uses of Corollary 2.3 have ratios at most 0.32; no case with right side 0 and left side positive. S2: three instances (14 hypotheses: fill-in functions, patched humans, random samplers, a semimeasure; K ≈ 3.2 bits; n = 400; 200 rehearsals each), h_t and κ_t computed exactly in every round: the event of (a) failed in no rehearsal for the maximiser or for a draw from Π₂, that of (b) in at most 3.5% (δ = 0.5) and 1.5% (δ = 0.2), that of (c) in none; the largest H² of the maximiser was 0.015 against bounds 0.45 to 0.95. S3: part (a) exactly for two coins with L = 1 (360 cases of p, q, w(P), δ), at the first n for which the other coin lies in A: the expected Π₂-mass of A is at most 0.183·δ and the other coin beats P with probability at most 0.183·δ; with half the threshold the mass reaches 23.6·δ, so the test would detect an error of a factor 2 in the threshold. These tests confirm the steps; like the editor's, they do not test the constants 28 and 40 sharply.

### 1.3 Proposition 3.5 (posterior-draw-driven rehearsal and the factor 1/w_t(P)). CORRECT.

(a) Chain rule for KL along runs of samplers; the asked term has conditional mean (1/L)E_{Q̄_t}Σ_j KL(P‖M)(x_j) ≥ (w_t(P)/L)·KL(Q_P‖Q_M) because Q̄_t ≥ w_t(P)Q_P and the integrand is nonnegative; Lemma 1.6(b) sums the asked terms to at most K ln 2. Supermartingale: 1/w_t(P) = ξ(D)/(w(P)P(D)) and E_{y~P}[ν(y|x)/P(y|x)] ≤ 1; the asked input is chosen independently of the human's answer; the maximal inequality gives Pr[sup 1/w_t(P) ≥ 2^K/δ] ≤ (W/w(P))·δ2^(−K) ≤ δ. Correct.
(b) I rebuilt F′_z for R = 2 (256 members) and checked (i) and (iii) by enumeration, and (iv) by exact run laws for w_F ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.9, 1 − 2^(−6)}: TV = w_F/2 and KL = ½ln(1/(1 − w_F)) to machine precision (S4). The consequence: while F′ lives its odds stay at the prior value by (i), the driver is P with probability 2^(−K), and the round removes F′ with probability 2^(−K)·½·¼; for the mixture driver ½·¼ by (iii). A direct simulation of the actual rehearsal (per-input posteriors over F′_z tracked exactly, fresh inputs) gives mean removal rounds 16.1 ± 0.4, 32.3 ± 0.8, 63.1 ± 1.6 for K = 1, 2, 3 with posterior draws (expected 16, 32, 64) and 7.9 to 8.1 with the mixture (expected 8). The equality claim in the limit: 8·2^K·2^(−K)·½K ln 2 = 4K ln 2 = L·K ln 2 with W = 1. Correct.

### 1.4 Example 3.6 (codebook class). CORRECT.

The data (z_t, y_t) do not depend on the codebook whatever the driver (every call has input z), so a random codebook is independent of the data; on distinct inputs each function is consistent with probability 2^(−n) independently, and 1 − (1 − 2^(−n))^(2^m) ≥ 1 − exp(−2^(K−2)) with m = n + K − 2. A consistent function has posterior weight 4(1 − 2^(−K))2^(−K−n) ≥ 2·2^(−K−n) > 2^(−K)2^(−n). Averaging argument for the existence of a codebook: correct. TV(Q_s, Q_P) = 1 − 1/8. (b): (1 − 2^(−K))²2^(−2m) < 2^(4−n)·2^(−2K−n) ≤ 2^(−2K−n) already for n ≥ 4; n ≥ 5 as stated is correct. ### 1.5 Proposition 4.6 (class A′, traps slightly heavier than the human). CORRECT.

Weights add to 1; each question removes at most one trap (I rechecked against Definition 4.1: (b, 0) can remove only g_b, (b, a) with a ≠ 0 only g_a); at least n traps survive and each outweighs g_0, so the deployed member is a trap chosen independently of the run's z. Its run has at least ⌈L/4⌉ answers 1 iff its index occurs among the first m₀ = L − ⌈L/4⌉ + 1 symbols; m₀ ≥ 3L/4 + 1/4, m₀ ≤ L, Bonferroni gives (m₀/N)(1 − m₀/(2N)) ≥ (3L/(8n))(3/4) = (9/32)L/n. (b): each surviving trap has posterior mass (1 + ι)/(k(1 + ι) + 1) < 1/k ≤ 1/n, and a call is unfaithful only if the draw is the single trap answering 1 there. Exact check on n = 1..400, L = 1..n: smallest ratio of the exact probability to (9/32)L/n is 1.113 (S6); a simulation of a mixture-driven rehearsal followed by the highest-weight consistent member (n = 30, L = 12) gives 0.1557 against exact 0.1547.

### 1.6 Corollary 4.4(b) (restated). CORRECT.

With G_{a*} = {fewer than L/4 calls whose answer differs from g_{a*} at its input}: after at most ⌈L/4⌉ − 1 replacements every other answer is the human's at its input, so the human system tolerates these faults with failure 0; a run is outside G_{a*} iff it has at least ⌈L/4⌉ unfaithful calls; Proposition 4.3(a) then gives failure ≥ L/(8n) for some human, hence n ≥ L/(8δ) for failure δ at every human (within the family indexed by n, which has N = 2n). The mixture: m + 1 = ⌈L/4⌉ in Proposition 4.3(b); for m ≥ 2, (3L/(2n))^(m+1)/(m+1)! is decreasing in m when L ≤ n and its value at m = 2 is 0.5625(L/n)³ ≤ 0.57(L/n)³ (S8). Correct.

### 1.7 Corollary 4.4(c) (added). CORRECT.

Q_g does not depend on the human, so for a set G that does not refer to the human the failure of a deployed member g is Q_g(Gᶜ), the fault-free failure of the human system of g. In class B with G = {fewer than L/4 answers 1}, g_{a*} is outside G iff a* occurs among the first m₀ symbols: 1 − (1 − 1/N)^(m₀). Correct.

### 1.8 Remark 4.5, as used for the vote in the note's R5(b). CORRECT.

With at least n ≥ 16 members surviving and no answer 1 recorded, at an input (b, 0) only g_b answers 1 and at a flag s only g_s, so the plurality answer is 0; once an answer 1 is recorded the posterior is the human. The vote's unfaithful calls are the occurrences of a* in z: Binomial(L, 1/N), so more than k with probability at most (L/(2n))^(k+1)/(k+1)!, which is below the committee's bound L/(2n²) + (3L/(2n))^(k+1)/(k+1)! that the note states for both. Correct.
