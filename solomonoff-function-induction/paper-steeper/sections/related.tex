\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}\label{sec:related:work}

\paragraph{Generalised posteriors and two-part codes.}\label{rem:related:posteriors}
The tempered posterior $\Pi^{1/\lambda}$ is a generalised (Gibbs, fractional or safe) posterior with learning rate $1/\lambda$ \citep{zhang2006epsilon,bissiri2016general,bhattacharya2019fractional,grunwald2012safe,grunwald2017inconsistency}; its maximiser, the $\lambda$-MAP, is a two-part code with complexity weight $\lambda$ (\cref{def:setup:objectives}). Cold posteriors \citep{wenzel2020good}, which raise prior and likelihood to the same power, keep the ratio of $\lambda=1$.\label{rem:related:cold} With $\lambda=cn$ the prior changes with the sample size, as the Gaussian prior implicit in weight decay does (\cref{rem:teacher:weightdecay}); \citet{zhu2026overfitting} likewise read a large complexity weight in a PAC-Bayes rule as a sample-size-dependent prior. For i.i.d.\ density estimation with a countable list of densities, two-part (MDL) estimators are studied by \citet{barron1991minimum}; \cref{thm:recovery:main}(a),(b) is the supervised analogue, for sub-probability kernels, an arbitrary input law and every schedule $1\le\lambda_n=o(n)$, and it identifies the limit as the shortest $\mu$-almost everywhere equivalent programs. Its proof is the standard argument from Ville's inequality and the strong law, and we claim no novelty for it; what we add is the rate for fixed $\lambda>1$, the contrast with $\lambda=1$ and the necessity results (\cref{thm:recovery:main}(c),(d), \cref{prop:recovery:lambda1,prop:recovery:necessity}).

\paragraph{$\lambda$-weighted two-part MDL in noisy classification.}\label{rem:related:mdl}
The closest prior work studies two-part codes in noisy binary classification, where a hypothesis is a classifier together with a code for the label noise, essentially a product hypothesis. \citet{grunwald2007suboptimal} show that Bayes and MDL classifiers can behave suboptimally under misspecification. \citet{zhu2025quantifying} quantify overfitting along the regularisation path of two-part-code MDL in supervised classification, and \citet{zhu2026overfitting} study overfitting and generalisation of (PAC-)Bayesian prediction in noisy binary classification. We do not claim as new the qualitative finding that a bounded $\lambda$ fits the noise and that $\lambda$ must grow with $n$. Their target is the data distribution; for the teacher's errors ours deliberately differs from it (\cref{rem:limits:schedule}). An earlier report \citep[Section~8]{claude2026whatfollows} formalised the proposal for a general imitation channel; \cref{app:related:t5} lists what we re-derive from it.

\paragraph{Algorithmic statistics.}
The structure function and sophistication split the description of a string into a model and a data-to-model code \citep{koppel1987complexity,gacs2001algorithmic,vereshchagin2004kolmogorov,vereshchagin2017algorithmic}; restricting the model family, for example to Hamming balls \citep{vereshchagin2010rate}, changes the structure function. Product models, a parameter with private noise at each input, are such a restriction; \cref{sec:kinks} uses their structure function, and \cref{sec:parity} compares the two splits for a noisy parity.

\paragraph{Imitation and identifiability from behaviour.}
Behavioural cloning treats imitation as supervised learning of a demonstrator's actions given observations \citep{bain1999framework,pomerleau1991efficient,dehaan2019causal}; because this ignores the causal structure of demonstrator and environment, more information can make the imitator worse \citep{dehaan2019causal}, and \citet{zhang2020causal} give a graphical criterion, from a causal model, for when imitation is possible if the learner does not observe everything the demonstrator's decisions depend on. Inverse reinforcement learning infers a reward for which the observed behaviour is optimal, and many rewards qualify \citep{ng2000algorithms}; a policy does not determine its decomposition into a planner and a reward, and even a simplicity prior does not single out the true decomposition \citep{armstrong2018occam}, while demonstrations in sufficiently different environments identify an entropy-regularised reward up to a constant \citep{cao2021identifiability}. \citet{cohen2022fully} give a Bayesian imitation learner for environments that never reset. Our negative results are of the same kind, for one program selected from i.i.d.\ imitation data: $\lambda$ cannot be chosen from imitation data alone, since twin worlds with one teacher channel need different selections (\cref{prop:limits:separable,prop:limits:noident}), and no procedure reliably copies the sampler in each of several worlds with one data law (\cref{prop:imit:limits}(a)).

\paragraph{Novelty.}\label{rem:related:new}
Known are $\lambda$-weighted MDL in noisy classification, two-part MDL for density estimation, the structure function, sophistication and restricted model families, generalised and tempered posteriors, and the proof techniques we use (dominance bounds, Ville's inequality, the strong law, Kakutani's dichotomy, list decoding, Hoeffding's inequality). The hull--penalty relation itself is elementary convex geometry, and the method of \cref{thm:recovery:main}(a),(b) is standard. To our knowledge the following are new: the contrast under a steeper prior between classes with a universal member and the private-randomness class; the exact-recovery rates for fixed $\lambda>1$, with the contrast at $\lambda=1$ and the necessity results; the kink vocabulary, the converse and the flattening by a universal member; the product kink of the noisy parity against set models; the teacher theorem over all hypotheses through list decoding, and its extension to flip rates below $0.1233$; and the reuse factor.
