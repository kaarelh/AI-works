\section{Limits of steeper selection}\label{sec:limits}

\Cref{thm:teacher:main} needs $\lambda$ to grow linearly in $n$ and the error rule to be incompressible relative to the model. Neither requirement can be dropped, and steeper selection by description length has further failure modes. The setting is \cref{def:teacher:instance} unless stated otherwise; proofs are in \cref{app:limits}.

\subsection{Why \texorpdfstring{$\lambda$}{lambda} must be proportional to \texorpdfstring{$n$}{n}}\label{sec:limits:schedule}

\begin{proposition}[Plain Bayes, MDL and SI learn the errors]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Props 2.9, 2.10; checks c3, c6(G), c7(G)}\label{prop:limits:plain}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Let $P$ be a shared hypothesis with $P\ge2^{-\ell(\pi)-\kappa}\nu_\pi$ for every $\pi\in\Hcl{\times}$, such as $\Mprod$ or $\MF$. For every sequence of distinct inputs and every $n$, $\sum_{i\le n}-\log P(y^*(x_i)\mid x_i,D_{i-1})\le\ell(\pi_b)+\kappa\le d+\lceil L_S\rceil+\kappa_b+\kappa$. So at most $d+L_S+O(\log d+\log m)$ inputs in total (the memoriser can name $s_0$ through $r$) get probability below $\frac12$ for the teacher's label (semimeasure convention, hence also normalised), and every other revealed error input gets the erroneous label with probability at least $\frac12$.
\item Let $B<\infty$, let $\lambda=\lambda_n\ge1$ be fixed or depend on $n$, and draw $n\le2^d$ inputs uniformly without replacement. With probability at least $1-\delta$, every $\lambda$-MAP $\hat\pi$ has $R_B(\hat\pi)\le\lambda\hat L/n+B\sqrt{(\hat L\ln2+\ln(2/\delta))/(2n)}$ with $\hat L:=d+\lceil L_S\rceil+\kappa_b$, hence $e(\hat\pi)\ge2^{-R_B(\hat\pi)/p}-2^{-B}$. In particular $e(\hat\pi)\ge1-2^{-B}-o(1)$ whenever $(\lambda_n/n)\hat L\to0$ and $\hat L/n\to0$, along any sequence of instances $(d_n)$ with fixed $m$, $r$ and error rule and $n\le2^{d_n}$. On a fixed instance the bound is informative only once $(\lambda_n/n)(d+L_S)$ is small compared with $p$; for slowly decaying schedules such as $\lambda_n=n^{0.9}$ at the example's sizes this never happens for $n\le2^d$.
\item On the interleaved string with i.i.d.\ uniform inputs, Solomonoff's $M$ has expected total label loss at most $d+L_S+O(\log d+\log m)$, and for fixed $\lambda$ the steeper SI predictor pays at most $\lambda\ell_U$ more (\cref{thm:absorb:absorption}(d)).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

For instance, at the example's sizes with all inputs revealed and the machine constant set to $0$ (an illustration), a direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) shows that the exceptions in (a) are a fraction at most $9.6\cdot10^{-24}$ of the error inputs.

\emph{Proof idea.} (a) Telescope $P(D_n)\ge2^{-\ell(\pi_b)-\kappa}$; for $\MF$ this is \citet[Corollary~5.4]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals}.% P1: cor:learn:mistakes
\ (b) is \cref{lem:teacher:finite} with the memoriser as reference. With the machine constant set to $0$, $B=30$ and $\delta=0.01$, a direct computation gives $e(\hat\pi)\ge0.649$ at $n=10^{13}$ for $\lambda=1$, and $e(\hat\pi)\ge0.734$ at $n=10^{15}$ for $\lambda_n=\sqrt n$, whatever $\delta_S$ (\cref{app:limits:plain}).

\begin{remark}[Fixed $\lambda$ versus $\lambda\propto n$]\status{remark (from proved results); known}\src{steeper end of \S2.5, \S4.4}\label{rem:limits:schedule}
So steeper selection needs $\lambda=\Theta(n)$: $\lambda/n$ must stay above $2^{-m}$ (\cref{thm:teacher:main}(iii),(iv)), and every schedule with $(\lambda_n/n)(d+L_S)\to0$ along a sequence of instances eventually reproduces the errors (\cref{prop:limits:plain}(b)). A fixed $\lambda>1$ removes noise (\cref{thm:split:private}), not finite-complexity errors. With $\lambda=cn$ the learner discards every component whose rate is below $c$, valid or not (\cref{prop:limits:separable}): it is deliberately inconsistent for the teacher's label distribution. Its price is a batch excess: $-\log\xi_\lambda(D_n)\le n\,(c\,\ell(\pi)+\hat R(\pi))$ for every $\pi$, with $\hat R$ the empirical risk, so the code length exceeds $\pi$'s own loss by at most $c\,\ell(\pi)$ bits per sample (the guaranteed excess), against $\ell(\pi)/n$ at $\lambda=1$. This is not a sequential regret, since $\lambda=cn$ is not one Bayesian predictor (\cref{rem:setup:persample}). We do not claim as new the qualitative finding that a bounded $\lambda$ fits the noise and that $\lambda$ must grow with $n$; $\lambda$-weighted two-part MDL and PAC-Bayes prediction in noisy binary classification are studied by \citet{zhu2025quantifying,zhu2026overfitting} (\cref{rem:related:mdl}). Their target is the data distribution, which here contains the teacher's errors, and by \cref{prop:limits:plain}(b) every schedule with $(\lambda_n/n)(d+L_S)\to0$ along a sequence of instances learns them.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Which error rules defeat steeper selection}\label{sec:limits:rules}

The example of \cref{sec:teacher} fixes only a density and a description length, and realistic systematic errors are often low-complexity patterns. Split the rule as $S=S_1\sqcup S_2$, $|S|=s_0=p2^m$, with $s_i:=|S_i|$, $p_1:=s_1/2^m$ and $p_2:=s_2/(2^m-s_1)$, where $S_1$ is \emph{cheap}, $K(S_1\mid m)\le k_1$; every input of $E_1:=S_1\times\{0,1\}^{d-m}$ is an error input. The \emph{envelope} $\pi_e$ predicts $f_a\oplus1$ with probability $1$ on $E_1$ and $f_a$ with flip probability $p_2$ elsewhere; it has risk $R_e=(1-p_1)H(p_2)<H(p)$ and length at most $d+k_1+\kappa_e$, $\kappa_e:=2\log d+2\log m+2\log(s_2+1)+\kappa$. Put $J_e(c):=c(d+k_1+\kappa_e)+R_e$, $\zeta:=c(k_1+\kappa_e+4+\Lambda_1)+\epsilon$ and $c_{\rm env}:=(H(p)-R_e)/(k_1+\kappa_e+4+\Lambda_1)$.

\begin{proposition}[Error envelopes]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Prop 2.17 (T5 Prop 4.5, all-hypothesis form); check c7(B); referee q4(C)}\label{prop:limits:envelope}
Let $B=\infty$, and let $J_c(\pi)\le J_e(c)+\epsilon$ with $J_e(c)+\epsilon<\Phi_2(c)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\lev(\pi)=1$ and $R(\pi)\le R_e+\zeta$. So for $c<c_{\rm env}$ and $\epsilon$ small enough that $\zeta<H(p)-R_e$, no program with $R(\pi)\ge H(p)$, the good model included, is $\epsilon$-near-optimal.
\item With $B_2:=K(S_2\mid a,K(a))$, the average probability of reproducing the cheap errors is
\begin{gather*}
e_1(\pi):=\Ex\big[\nu_\pi(y^*(x)\mid x)\,\big|\,x\in E_1\big]\ \ge\ 2^{-\bar R_1},\\
\bar R_1:=\tfrac1{p_1}\big(\zeta+(1-p_1)H(p_2)-\min(c,2^{-m})\,(B_2-K(S_1))^+\big).
\end{gather*}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof idea.} (i) is \cref{thm:teacher:main}(o) with (D1). For (ii), code $S_2$ given $S_1$ with the hypothesis, as in \cref{lem:teacher:coding}; this bounds its risk off $E_1$ from below, so its risk on $E_1$ is small (\cref{app:limits:envelope}).

At the example's sizes take $S_1=\{z:z_1=\dots=z_j=0\}$ and $S_2$ uniformly random. For $j=13$ ($128$ cheap and $896$ random points; $\log\binom{2^m-s_1}{s_2}=10{,}418$ bits), a direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) gives $c_{\rm env}=1.64\cdot10^{-5}$, which is $31\%$ of the logarithmic width of the window $(9.54\cdot10^{-7},8.06\cdot10^{-3}]$ of \cref{thm:teacher:main} with the machine constant set to $0$ ($25\%$ at $\kappa=30$), and $e_1\ge0.545$ at $c=1.01\cdot2^{-m}$ ($\kappa=0$, $B_2$ typical). For $j=11$ ($512+512$ points, $6{,}364$ bits): $47\%$ of the log-width and $e_1\ge0.862$ (table in \cref{app:limits:envelope}).

So density and description length alone do not suffice: the rule with $j=13$ has density $2^{-10}$ and about $10{,}400$ bits, close to the $10{,}000$ bits of the example, yet on almost a third of the window's log-width no hypothesis that fits no better than the good model is near-optimal, and near the lower end near-optimal hypotheses reproduce the cheap errors. (Its deficiency is about $1{,}300$ bits; \cref{thm:teacher:main} applies to it with this value of $\delta_S$ in its bounds, which are correspondingly weaker, and \cref{prop:limits:envelope} says nothing about it above $c_{\rm env}$.) The example is favourable to the proposal; its conclusion holds for error rules incompressible relative to the model (\cref{thm:teacher:main}). For structured rules the dangerous hypotheses are envelopes, not universal ones: a cheap, dense error region has a high rate and is learned like any valid component. The extreme case is a cheap, frequent rule.

\begin{proposition}[Rate blindness]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Prop 2.11; checks c3, c6(A)}\label{prop:limits:rateblind}
Let $B=\infty$ and $\ell(\pi_{\rm coin})\le\kappa$.
(i) If $H(p)/(\ell(\pi_b)-\ell(\pi_g))\ge(1-H(p))/(\ell(\pi_g)-\ell(\pi_{\rm coin}))$, then at every $c$ the good model is beaten by the memoriser or beaten or tied by the coin.
(ii) If
\[
\frac{H(p)}{\lceil L_S\rceil+\kappa_b+4+\Lambda_1}\ \ge\ \frac{1-H(p)}{d-4-\Lambda_1-\kappa}\quad\text{and}\quad d-4-\Lambda_1-\kappa>0,\tag{$\star$}
\]
then at every $c$ every program with $u>\frac34$ and $R\ge H(p)$ is beaten by the memoriser or beaten or tied by the coin.
(iii) For $c<2^{-m}$, near-optimal programs reproduce the errors, under the condition of \cref{thm:teacher:main}(iii).
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[The criterion is robust in the machine constant]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Lemma 2.11a; check c6(A)}\label{lem:limits:robust}
Fix $m$ and $r$, let $\kappa$ bound the machine constants in $\Lambda_1$, $\kappa_b$ and $\ell(\pi_{\rm coin})$, and put $H:=H(2^{-r})$, $q:=(1-H)/H$, $\alpha_0:=2+4q$, $\beta_0:=4+2\log m+2\kappa+q(\lceil L_S\rceil+4\log m+2\log s_0+2\kappa+4)$ and $F(d):=d-\alpha_0\log d-\beta_0$. (i) For $d\ge1$, $(\star)$ holds if and only if $F(d)\ge0$. (ii) If $\beta_0\ge\alpha_0/\ln2$, then $\{d\ge1:F(d)\ge0\}=[d_0,\infty)$ for a unique real $d_0=d_0(\kappa)$. (iii) $d_0(\kappa)$ grows linearly in $\kappa$, with slope $(2+2q)(1+o(1))$.
\end{lemma}

For a single $3$-bit prefix as error rule ($m=3$, $p=1/8$), a direct computation gives $d_0=49$ with the machine constant set to $0$ and $169$ at $\kappa=30$; for a single $6$-bit prefix, $450$ and $1005$ (\cref{app:limits:robust}). So for each fixed cheap rule and each $\kappa$, once the simple model is long enough, no program that knows the parity and fits no better than noise is ever the unique optimum: at every $c$ it is beaten by the memoriser or beaten or tied by the coin. The incompressible $11{,}710$-bit rule of the example, by contrast, leaves a window of a factor of about $8500$ ($\kappa=0$) or $3800$ ($\kappa=30$).

\subsection{Other pathologies}\label{sec:limits:other}

\begin{proposition}[Separable components]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Prop 2.12 (T5 Thm 3.2, Thm 4.1); check c5(a)}\label{prop:limits:separable}
Let the inputs split into regions $X_1,\dots,X_k$ and the hypotheses be products of per-region choices with additive code lengths. Each region selects a vertex of its own hull for slope $c$ (\cref{lem:setup:hull}). If region $j$ offers a default or a component $j$ of extra length $k_j$ and population risk reduction $r_j$, component $j$ is selected if and only if $r_j/k_j>c$ (ties arbitrary). So a valid component whose rate is at most an error component's rate cannot be kept without the error, except at a common tie value. Two worlds with the same teacher channel and input law, one where a component is an error and one where it is a rare valid rule, get the same selection at every $c$.
\end{proposition}

The steeper penalty sorts components by \emph{rate}, risk reduction per bit, not by validity. Three consequences follow (proofs in \cref{app:limits:guard}). A guard that restricts a rule to its proper contexts is dropped when its rate is below the rule's: on an interval of $c$ the unguarded rule is the unique selection and applies the rule where the teacher never did (\cref{prop:limits:guard}; in a numerical instance the interval spans a factor of $67$ in $c$). The selection depends on the input law (\cref{prop:limits:inputlaw}); \cref{thm:teacher:main}'s window depends on it by the same mechanism, which is proved here only in the separable case. And $\lambda$ cannot be chosen from imitation data: every strictly proper scoring rule prefers the teacher's channel, errors included \citep{gneiting2007strictly}; log-loss validation along the selection path picks the smallest, error-memorising, value of $c$; and in the twin worlds of \cref{prop:limits:separable} every rule that chooses $c$ from imitation data alone is wrong in one of them (\cref{prop:limits:noident}).

\subsection{Unseen properties}\label{sec:limits:ood}

For $\MF$, a simple property absent from the training data gets a constant probability of bad behaviour, $2^{-K(Q,b)-O(1)}$, where $K(Q,b)$ is the length of a shortest self-delimiting code for a decider of a set $Q$ and a program for a behaviour $b$ \citep[Proposition~8.1]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals}.% P1: prop:ood:hanni
\ The steeper counterpart is as follows (proof in \cref{app:limits:ood}).

\begin{proposition}[Steeper simplicity and unseen properties]\status{proved}\src{steeper Prop 2.16}\label{prop:limits:ood}
In $\Hcl{\times}$, let $Q\subseteq X$ be decidable, $b$ partial computable, $D$ a data set with $\dom D\cap Q=\emptyset$, and $Z\subseteq Q\cap\dom b$ finite. For every $\lambda\ge1$: (i) a family of programs that answer $b(x)$ on $Q$ and run an arbitrary program of the class elsewhere (one for each such program) carries posterior mass exactly $2^{-\lambda(K(Q,b)+\kappa)}$; hence all programs with this behaviour together carry at least this mass, and $\xi_\lambda(D\cup b|_Z)\ge2^{-\lambda(K(Q,b)+\kappa)}\xi_\lambda(D)$; (ii) if $\nu_{\pi_1}(D)=\nu_{\pi_2}(D)>0$, then $\Pi_\lambda(\pi_1\mid D)/\Pi_\lambda(\pi_2\mid D)=2^{-\lambda(\ell(\pi_1)-\ell(\pi_2))}$.
\end{proposition}

At $\lambda=1$, (i) is the statement of \citet[Proposition~8.1(a)]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals} with $\Mprod$ in place of $W$ and $\MF$.% P1: prop:ood:hanni
\ With $\lambda=cn$ the lower bound of (i) degrades to $2^{-cn(K(Q,b)+\kappa)}$, so a constant lower bound no longer follows; whether the probability of $b$ on $Q$ vanishes depends, by (ii), on what the shortest hypotheses that fit the training data do on $Q$. If the shortest one extrapolates well, steeper SFI is more reliable out of distribution than SFI; if a shorter one fits the training data and is wrong on $Q$, it is more confidently wrong. No guarantee about unseen properties follows.

\begin{question}[structured errors and the choice of $c$]\label{q:limits:open}
(a) For envelopes whose cheap region has error density $\theta<1$, only part (i) of \cref{prop:limits:envelope} carries over (\cref{app:limits:envelope}). Do near-optimal hypotheses then memorise the random part $S_2$, and what is selected above $c_{\rm env}$? (b) Is there a window as in \cref{thm:teacher:main} for $p<0.123$ and $c$ well above $2^{-m}$ (\cref{rem:limits:method})? (c) Is there a rule for choosing $c$, using information other than the imitation data, that keeps rare valid rules and drops errors? By \cref{prop:limits:separable,prop:limits:noident} imitation data alone cannot tell them apart.
\end{question}
