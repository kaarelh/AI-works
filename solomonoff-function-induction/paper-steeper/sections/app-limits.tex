\section{Proofs for \texorpdfstring{\cref{sec:limits}}{Section \ref*{sec:limits}} (limits of steeper selection)}\label{app:limits}

The setting is \cref{def:teacher:instance} unless stated otherwise; (F1)--(F5) are the facts of \cref{app:setup:facts}.

\subsection{Plain Bayes, MDL and SI}\label{app:limits:plain}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:limits:plain}]
(a) The memoriser $\pi_b$ is deterministic and consistent with every $D_n$, so $P(D_n)\ge2^{-\ell(\pi_b)-\kappa}$. Since $P$ is a shared hypothesis, its sequential log loss telescopes: $\sum_{i\le n}-\log P(y^*(x_i)\mid x_i,D_{i-1})=-\log P(D_n)+\log P(\emptyset)\le\ell(\pi_b)+\kappa$. Each conditional is at most $1$, so each term is nonnegative, and a label with probability below $\frac12$ adds more than one bit; normalised conditionals are at least the raw ones. On an error input the teacher's label is the erroneous one. The memoriser can be given $s_0=2^{m-r}$ through $r$, so $\ell(\pi_b)\le d+\lceil L_S\rceil+O(\log d+\log m)$. If all $2^d$ inputs are revealed there are $p2^d$ error inputs, and at most $d+\lceil L_S\rceil+\kappa_b+\kappa$ of them get the erroneous label with probability below $\frac12$. At $d=100$, $m=20$, $p=2^{-10}$ and $\kappa=0$ there are $1.238\cdot10^{27}$ error inputs and at most $11{,}853$ exceptions, a fraction $9.57\cdot10^{-24}$ (code in the accompanying repository). For $\MF$, $P\ge2^{-K(\nu_\pi)-O(1)}\nu_\pi$ because product laws are machine laws, and $K(\nu_\pi)\le\ell(\pi)+\kappa$ by (F1).

(b) Apply \cref{lem:teacher:finite} with $\pi_{\rm ref}=\pi_b$, whose per-input losses are $0$, so $A=0$ and $\epsilon_1=0$, and with $c=\lambda_n/n$; then its $\hat L$ is $\ell(\pi_b)\le d+\lceil L_S\rceil+\kappa_b$. A $\lambda$-MAP minimises $\hat J_c$, so with probability at least $1-\delta$, $R_B(\hat\pi)\le J^B_c(\hat\pi)\le c\,\ell(\pi_b)+B\sqrt{(\hat L\ln2+\ln(2/\delta))/(2n)}$. The lemma holds for each $n$ separately, so $\lambda$ may depend on $n$. The error-label bound is the last step of the proof of \cref{thm:teacher:main}(iii): $p\,\Ex[\min\{B,-\log\nu\}\mid E]\le R_B$ and Jensen's inequality. If $(\lambda_n/n)\hat L\to0$ and $\hat L/n\to0$, with $\delta$ fixed, both terms of the bound on $R_B$ tend to $0$, and $e(\hat\pi)\ge1-2^{-B}-o(1)$. On a fixed instance the bound $2^{-R_B/p}-2^{-B}$ is informative only if $R_B$ is small compared with $p$, and the bound on $R_B$ is at least $(\lambda_n/n)\hat L\ge(\lambda_n/n)(d+L_S)$. For $\lambda_n=n^{0.9}$ at the example's sizes, $\lambda_n/n=n^{-0.1}\ge2^{-10}=p$ for every $n\le2^{100}$, so $(\lambda_n/n)(d+L_S)>100p$ and the bound says nothing for any $n\le2^d$. Numbers (code in the accompanying repository; the machine constant set to $0$, $B=30$, $\delta=0.01$): for $\lambda=1$, $e\ge0.255$, $0.649$, $0.958$, $0.996$ at $n=10^{12},10^{13},10^{15},10^{17}$; for $\lambda_n=\sqrt n$, $e\ge0.045$, $0.734$, $0.970$ at $n=10^{13},10^{15},10^{17}$. At $n=10^{12}$, $\lambda_n/n=10^{-6}$ still lies in the window of \cref{thm:teacher:main}, and the bound says nothing.

(c) Encode the interleaved sequence with fixed-length blocks; with uniform i.i.d.\ inputs and $y_i=y^*(x_i)$ its law $\Prob$ is computable from $a$, $S$, $d$, $m$ and $r$, so it has a program $\pi_{\Prob}\in\Hcl{\mathrm{SI}}$ with $\ell(\pi_{\Prob})\le d+\lceil L_S\rceil+O(\log d+\log m)$. Since $M\ge2^{-\ell(\pi_{\Prob})}\Prob$, the chain-rule argument in the proof of \cref{thm:absorb:absorption}(d) gives $\Ex_{\Prob}\sum_{i\le n}-\log M(y_i\mid x_1y_1\cdots x_i)\le\ell(\pi_{\Prob})$. For $\lambda>1$ apply \cref{thm:absorb:absorption}(d) itself, with $\kappa_0=0$ in $\Hcl{\mathrm{SI}}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rate blindness: the criterion in the machine constant}\label{app:limits:robust}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:limits:rateblind}]
(i) $J_c(\pi_b)<J_c(\pi_g)$ if and only if $c<H(p)/(\ell(\pi_b)-\ell(\pi_g))$, and $J_c(\pi_{\rm coin})\le J_c(\pi_g)$ if and only if $c\ge(1-H(p))/(\ell(\pi_g)-\ell(\pi_{\rm coin}))$; under the hypothesis these two ranges cover all $c>0$. (ii) Let $u(\pi)>\frac34$, so $\lev(\pi)=1$, and $R(\pi)\ge H(p)$. Below the left side of $(\star)$, \cref{thm:teacher:main}(iv) gives $J_c(\pi_b)<J_c(\pi)$. At or above it, $c\,(d-4-\Lambda_1-\kappa)\ge1-H(p)$ by $(\star)$, so (D1) gives $J_c(\pi)\ge c(d-4-\Lambda_1)+H(p)\ge1+c\kappa\ge J_c(\pi_{\rm coin})$. (iii) is \cref{thm:teacher:main}(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:limits:robust}]
(i) With $B=\infty$, $\Lambda_1=2\log d+2\log m+\kappa$ and $\kappa_b=2\log d+2\log m+2\log s_0+\kappa$. The first denominator of $(\star)$ is positive. When the second is positive, $(\star)$ is equivalent to $H\,(d-4-\Lambda_1-\kappa)\ge(1-H)(\lceil L_S\rceil+\kappa_b+4+\Lambda_1)$. Dividing by $H$, the difference of the two sides is
\[
d-4-2\log d-2\log m-2\kappa-q\big(\lceil L_S\rceil+4\log d+4\log m+2\log s_0+2\kappa+4\big)=d-\alpha_0\log d-\beta_0=F(d).
\]
Conversely, $F(d)\ge0$ implies $d-4-\Lambda_1-\kappa=d-4-2\log d-2\log m-2\kappa\ge F(d)+4q\log d+q(\lceil L_S\rceil+4)>0$, so the side condition holds as well.

(ii) $F'(d)=1-\alpha_0/(d\ln2)$, so $F$ is strictly increasing on $[\alpha_0/\ln2,\infty)$ and tends to $\infty$. On $[1,\alpha_0/\ln2]$, $F(1)=1-\beta_0<0$ because $\beta_0\ge4$, and for $d>1$, $F(d)<d-\beta_0\le\alpha_0/\ln2-\beta_0\le0$. So $F<0$ on $[1,\alpha_0/\ln2]$, and $\{d\ge1:F(d)\ge0\}=[d_0,\infty)$ for the unique root $d_0>\alpha_0/\ln2$.

(iii) $\beta_0$ is affine in $\kappa$ with slope $2+2q$, $\alpha_0$ does not depend on $\kappa$, and $d_0=\beta_0+\alpha_0\log d_0$.

Taking all three constants equal to a common upper bound is legitimate because $(\star)$ only becomes harder to satisfy when any of them grows.
\end{proof}

A direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) gives $d_0(\kappa)$, the least integer $d$ with $F(d)\ge0$, for five cheap error rules. In every row $\beta_0\ge\alpha_0/\ln2$; $(\star)$ fails at $d_0-1$, holds at $d_0$, and holds at each of about $400$ values of $d$ in $[d_0,10^6]$.

\subsection{Separable components, guard erosion, non-identifiability, input law}\label{app:limits:guard}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:limits:separable}]
The objective $c\,\ell+R$ is a sum over regions of (extra length times $c$) plus (risk on the region), and the choices in different regions are independent, so it is minimised region by region; in each region \cref{lem:setup:hull} applies. With two options, component $j$ lowers the objective if and only if $c\,k_j<r_j$. If a valid component with rate $\rho_v$ is kept, then $c<\rho_v\le\rho_e$ (up to ties), so an error component with rate $\rho_e$ is kept as well. Finally, $J_c$ depends on the data only through the teacher's channel and the input law, which coincide in the two worlds.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Guard erosion]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Prop 2.13 (T5 Prop 4.4); checks c5(b), c6(G), c7(E); referee q1(I)}\label{prop:limits:guard}
In a region of contexts a schema $\sigma$ is one of $n_{\rm st}$ possible steps, and a guard $G$ splits the region into $G$-contexts (input mass $\mu(G)$), where the teacher plays $\sigma$ with probability $1-\epsilon_G+\epsilon_G/n_{\rm st}$ and every other step with probability $\epsilon_G/n_{\rm st}$, and $\neg G$-contexts (mass $\mu(\neg G)$), where it plays one fixed step other than $\sigma$. Over a common base, ``none'' is uniform (length $0$), ``unguarded $\sigma$'' predicts the $G$-context law everywhere (length $k_\sigma$), and ``guarded $\sigma$'' does so in $G$-contexts and is uniform over the other $n_{\rm st}-1$ steps elsewhere (length $k_\sigma+k_G$). With $H_G$ the entropy of the $G$-context law, $g_G:=\log n_{\rm st}-H_G$ and $\Delta_G:=\log(n_{\rm st}/\epsilon_G)-\log(n_{\rm st}-1)$, put $\rho_\sigma:=(\mu(G)g_G-\mu(\neg G)\log(1/\epsilon_G))/k_\sigma$ and $\rho_G:=\mu(\neg G)\Delta_G/k_G$. If $\rho_G<\rho_\sigma$, then for every $c\in(\rho_G,\rho_\sigma)$ the unguarded rule is the unique selection, and it applies $\sigma$ where the teacher never did. Otherwise the selection goes from guarded to none.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:limits:guard}]
Per context of the region, the expected log losses are: for ``none'', $\log n_{\rm st}$ everywhere; for ``unguarded $\sigma$'', $H_G$ in $G$-contexts and $\log(n_{\rm st}/\epsilon_G)$ in $\neg G$-contexts, where the teacher's fixed step has probability $\epsilon_G/n_{\rm st}$ under the $G$-context law; for ``guarded $\sigma$'', $H_G$ in $G$-contexts and $\log(n_{\rm st}-1)$ in $\neg G$-contexts. Relative to ``none'', the unguarded option lowers the risk by $\mu(G)g_G-\mu(\neg G)\log(1/\epsilon_G)=k_\sigma\rho_\sigma$ for $k_\sigma$ bits, and the guard lowers it by a further $\mu(\neg G)\Delta_G=k_G\rho_G$ for $k_G$ more bits. So the three options are the points $(0,0)$, $(k_\sigma,-k_\sigma\rho_\sigma)$ and $(k_\sigma+k_G,-k_\sigma\rho_\sigma-k_G\rho_G)$ in the (length, risk) plane, after subtracting the common base. Adding back the risk $\log n_{\rm st}$ of ``none'' per context shifts all three objectives by the same constant and makes the risks nonnegative, so \cref{lem:setup:hull} applies. The middle point is a vertex of the hull if and only if the slope $-\rho_\sigma$ of the first segment is steeper than the slope $-\rho_G$ of the second, that is, $\rho_\sigma>\rho_G$. By \cref{lem:setup:hull}(iii) it is then the unique minimiser for $c\in(\rho_G,\rho_\sigma)$. Otherwise the hull has only the two outer points, and the selection switches at the slope between them, $(k_\sigma\rho_\sigma+k_G\rho_G)/(k_\sigma+k_G)$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Numbers} (code in the accompanying repository). With $n_{\rm st}=64$ and $\epsilon_G=0.05$, the $G$-context law puts $0.95078$ on $\sigma$, $H_G=0.5773$, $g_G=5.4227$ and $\Delta_G=4.3446$. For $\mu(G)=0.05$, $\mu(\neg G)=5\cdot10^{-4}$, $k_\sigma=22$, $k_G=12$: $\rho_\sigma=1.2226\cdot10^{-2}$, $\rho_G=1.8103\cdot10^{-4}$, and as $c$ increases the selection goes guarded, unguarded (on $(1.8103\cdot10^{-4},1.2226\cdot10^{-2})$), none. For $\mu(G)=\mu(\neg G)=0.002$: $\rho_\sigma=1.0007\cdot10^{-4}<\rho_G=7.2411\cdot10^{-4}$, and the selection goes from guarded to none at $c=3.2032\cdot10^{-4}$. The teacher has noise $\epsilon_G$ in $G$-contexts; what it never does is apply $\sigma$ outside $G$.

\begin{proposition}[$\lambda$ cannot be chosen from imitation data]\status{proved; computed; (i) known}\src{steeper Prop 2.14 (T5 Thm 4.3); check c5(c)}\label{prop:limits:noident}
(i) Every strictly proper scoring rule has expected score on the teacher's data uniquely maximised by the teacher's channel $P^*(\cdot\mid x)$, errors included \citep{gneiting2007strictly}, so validation by such a score prefers the teacher's channel to the intended model. (ii) Along the population selection path the held-out log loss $R(h_c)$ is nonincreasing as $c$ decreases (\cref{lem:setup:hull}(ii)), so log-loss validation over a grid of values of $c$ is minimised at the smallest, error-memorising, value. (iii) In the twin worlds of \cref{prop:limits:separable}, every rule that chooses $c$ from imitation data alone is wrong in one of them.
\end{proposition}

For instance, for the three hypotheses of \cref{rem:teacher:hanni}, with the machine constant set to $0$ (an illustration), a direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) gives the held-out losses $1.0$, $0.0112$, $0.0112$ and $0$ at $c=10^{-1},10^{-3},10^{-5},10^{-7}$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:limits:noident}]
(i) A scoring rule is strictly proper if, for each $x$, the expected score of a forecast $Q$ under the label law $P^*(\cdot\mid x)$ is uniquely maximised at $Q=P^*(\cdot\mid x)$ \citep{gneiting2007strictly}; average over $x$. (ii) The held-out log loss of the population selection $h_c$ is $R(h_c)$, and by \cref{lem:setup:hull}(ii), $c<c'$ implies $R(h_c)\le R(h_{c'})$. (iii) An imitation-only rule sees the same data distribution in both worlds, since the teacher's channel and the input law are the same, and \cref{prop:limits:separable} gives the same selection at every $c$; the selection that is right in one world keeps an error or drops a valid rule in the other.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The selection depends on the input law]\status{proved}\src{steeper Prop 2.15}\label{prop:limits:inputlaw}
In the setting of \cref{prop:limits:separable}, let the inputs have law $\mu$ and let $\Delta_j$ be component $j$'s expected log-loss reduction per input of $X_j$. Then component $j$ is selected if and only if $\mu(X_j)\Delta_j>c\,k_j$. So the window that keeps a valid component $j$ and drops an error component $j'$ is $c\in(\mu(X_{j'})\Delta_{j'}/k_{j'},\,\mu(X_j)\Delta_j/k_j)$; it moves with $\mu$ and is empty once $\mu(X_{j'})/\mu(X_j)\ge\Delta_jk_{j'}/(\Delta_{j'}k_j)$, and after a shift to $\mu'$ a $c$ chosen in $\mu$'s window keeps the error if and only if $\mu'(X_{j'})\Delta_{j'}>c\,k_{j'}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:limits:inputlaw}]
The population risk is $\sum_j\mu(X_j)\Ex[\text{loss}\mid x\in X_j]$, so choosing component $j$ changes $J_c$ by $c\,k_j-\mu(X_j)\Delta_j$; the other claims are rearrangements of this criterion.
\end{proof}

A direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) confirms the thresholds of \cref{prop:limits:separable} by brute force in $8000$ pairs of an instance and a value of $c$.

\subsection{Error envelopes}\label{app:limits:envelope}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:limits:envelope}]
\emph{The envelope.} Off $E_1$ the inputs with prefix in $S_2$ are the error inputs, a fraction $p_2$ of the inputs off $E_1$, so $R(\pi_e)=(1-p_1)H(p_2)$. Since $p=p_1\cdot1+(1-p_1)p_2$, concavity of $H$ gives $H(p)\ge p_1H(1)+(1-p_1)H(p_2)=R_e$. The program $\pi_e$ needs $a$, $d$, $m$, a description of $S_1$ given $m$, and $s_2$ (from which $p_2$ follows), which gives the length bound.

(i) The error set still has density $p$, so \cref{thm:teacher:main}(o) applies: programs of level at least $2$ have $J_c\ge J^B_c\ge\Phi_2(c)>J_e(c)+\epsilon$, so $\lev(\pi)=1$. Then (D1) gives $\ell(\pi)\ge d-4-\Lambda_1$, and $c(d-4-\Lambda_1)+R(\pi)\le J_c(\pi)\le c(d+k_1+\kappa_e)+R_e+\epsilon$, that is, $R(\pi)\le R_e+\zeta$.

(ii) Write $R(\pi)=p_1R^{(1)}+(1-p_1)R^{(2)}$, where $R^{(1)}$ and $R^{(2)}$ are the risks conditional on $E_1$ and off $E_1$, and put $x:=(1-p_1)R^{(2)}$.

\emph{Coding $S_2$ given $S_1$.} For $z\notin S_1$ define $q_z(b)$ as in the proof of \cref{lem:teacher:coding} with $B=\infty$. Then $Q(S'):=\prod_{z\notin S_1}q_z(\ind_{S'}(z))$ is a lower-semicomputable semimeasure on subsets of $\{0,1\}^m\setminus S_1$, given $(\pi,a,m,S_1)$. For $z\notin S_1$ the teacher's label is $f_a\oplus\ind_{S_2}(z)$, so $-\log Q(S_2)=(2^m-s_1)R^{(2)}=2^mx$. By (F2), $K(S_2\mid a,\pi,S_1)\le2^mx+2\log m+\kappa$.

\emph{Chain.} By (F3), (F1) and \cref{lem:teacher:list} with $t=1$, $K(a,S_2\mid S_1)\le\ell(\pi)+4+2\log d+2^mx+2\log m+\kappa$. By (F3) and (F4), $K(a,S_2\mid S_1)\ge K(a,S_2)-K(S_1)-\kappa\ge d+B_2-K(S_1)-\kappa$. With $\Lambda_1=2\log d+2\log m+\kappa$ and (D1),
\[
\ell(\pi)\ \ge\ d-4-\Lambda_1+\max\big\{0,\ B_2'-2^mx\big\},\qquad B_2':=\big(B_2-K(S_1)\big)^+ .
\]

\emph{Combination.} $J_c(\pi)=c\,\ell(\pi)+p_1R^{(1)}+x\le J_e(c)+\epsilon$ gives
\[
p_1R^{(1)}\le\zeta+(1-p_1)H(p_2)-\big[x+c\max\{0,B_2'-2^mx\}\big].
\]
Over $x\ge0$ the bracket is piecewise linear: for $x\le B_2'/2^m$ it equals $cB_2'+(1-c2^m)x$, and for larger $x$ it equals $x$. So its minimum is $B_2'/2^m$ (at $x=B_2'/2^m$) if $c2^m\ge1$, and $cB_2'$ (at $x=0$) if $c2^m<1$; that is, $\min(c,2^{-m})B_2'$. Hence $R^{(1)}\le\bar R_1$, and Jensen's inequality gives $e_1(\pi)\ge2^{-R^{(1)}}\ge2^{-\bar R_1}$.

For $c\ge2^{-m}$, use $(2^m-s_1)H(p_2)\le\log\binom{2^m-s_1}{s_2}+\log(2^m+1)$, which gives $\bar R_1\le\big(\zeta+(\delta_2+\log(2^m+1)+K(S_1))/2^m\big)/p_1$ with $\delta_2:=\log\binom{2^m-s_1}{s_2}-B_2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Unseen properties}\label{app:limits:ood}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:limits:ood}]
(i) Let $V'$ be the prefix machine that reads a self-delimiting code $z$ of a pair (decider $q$ for $Q$, program $q'$ for $b$), then a program $\pi'\in\dom V$, and outputs an index of the conditional semimeasure that is the point mass at $b(x)$ (or $0$ while $b(x)$ is undefined) if $q$ accepts $x$, and $\nu_{\pi'}(\cdot\mid x)$ otherwise; this is lower semicomputable. By closure under prefix-free simulation (\cref{def:setup:class}) there is $\sigma$ with $V(\sigma z\pi')=V'(z\pi')$; let $\kappa:=|\sigma|$ and take $|z|=K(Q,b)$. Distinct $\pi'$ give distinct programs $\sigma z\pi'$. Since $\dom D\cap Q=\emptyset$, $\nu_{\sigma z\pi'}(D)=\nu_{\pi'}(D)$, and since $Z\subseteq Q\cap\dom b$, $\nu_{\sigma z\pi'}(D\cup b|_Z)=\nu_{\pi'}(D)$. So
\[
\sum_{\pi'\in\dom V}2^{-\lambda|\sigma z\pi'|}\,\nu_{\sigma z\pi'}(D)=2^{-\lambda(K(Q,b)+\kappa)}\sum_{\pi'}2^{-\lambda|\pi'|}\nu_{\pi'}(D)=2^{-\lambda(K(Q,b)+\kappa)}\,\xi_\lambda(D),
\]
which is the posterior mass claim, and the same sum with $D\cup b|_Z$ in place of $D$ is a lower bound for $\xi_\lambda(D\cup b|_Z)$.

(ii) Equal likelihoods cancel in the ratio of posteriors, which leaves the ratio of prior weights $2^{-\lambda\ell(\pi_1)}/2^{-\lambda\ell(\pi_2)}$.
\end{proof}

On a conjunction ladder \citep[Theorem~9.10 and Remark~G.11]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals},% P1: thm:comp:ladder, rem:comp:uniformties
\ deterministic selection of a least-size consistent formula, a $\lambda$-MAP rule, makes exponentially many mistakes on some ordering for every tie rule, while the $\lambda\to\infty$ power posterior, uniform over those formulas, makes $O(d^2)$ Gibbs mistakes. The danger in (ii) is therefore sharpest for single-hypothesis selection.

\subsection{A limitation of the method}\label{app:limits:method}

\begin{remark}[A limitation of the method]\status{remark; computed}\src{steeper end of \S2.6; check c7(C)}\label{rem:limits:method}
The window of \cref{thm:teacher:main} needs $G_1>H(p)$, that is, $p<0.056$. The sharper floor of \cref{lem:teacher:sharpfloor} gives the kink for every $p<1/4$ (\cref{prop:algstat:parity}), but does not extend the window as the argument stands: with it and no deviation term the level-$2$ floor $1-\log(3/2-p)$ exceeds $H(p)$ only for $p<0.123$; and a program of level at least $2$ has $I(\pi)$ bounded only through its length, which near $c=2^{-m}$ allows $I(\pi)\approx L_S$ and a deviation term of order $\sqrt{H(p)/p}$. Whether a window exists for $p<0.123$ and $c$ well above $2^{-m}$ is part of \cref{q:limits:open}.
\end{remark}
