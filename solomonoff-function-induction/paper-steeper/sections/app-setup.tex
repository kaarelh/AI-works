\section{Proofs for \texorpdfstring{\cref{sec:setup}}{Section \ref*{sec:setup}} (setting)}\label{app:setup}

\subsection{Standard facts}\label{app:setup:facts}

The following facts are standard \citep{li2008introduction}; $\kappa$ is as in \cref{sec:setup:classes}.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(F1)] $K(\pi)\le\ell(\pi)+\kappa$ for every program $\pi$ of a class $\mathcal H$. The identity map on the c.e.\ prefix-free set $\mathcal H$ is a prefix machine, which the universal machine simulates with a fixed prefix; for canonical classes, $\mathcal H=\dom V$ and the fact is immediate.
\item[(F2)] \emph{Conditional coding theorem.} If a fixed program lower-semicomputes from $z$ a semimeasure $Q_z$ on a countable set, then $K(w\mid z)\le-\log Q_z(w)+\kappa$.
\item[(F3)] $K(x)\le K(y)+K(x\mid y)+\kappa$, and $K(x,y\mid z)\le K(x\mid z)+K(y\mid x,z)+\kappa$.
\item[(F4)] \emph{Symmetry of information.} $K(x,y)=K(x)+K(y\mid x,K(x))+O(1)$.
\item[(F5)] $K(j)\le2\log(j+1)+\kappa$ for every integer $j\ge0$, by a doubling code.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The hull lemma}\label{app:setup:hull}

\begin{lemma}[Hull lemma]\status{proved; computed; strengthening of (iv) refuted}\src{steeper Lemma 1.2 (T5 Lemma 3.1); checks c5(a), c6(F)}\label{lem:setup:hull}
Let $\mathcal A:=\{(\ell(\pi),R(\pi))\}$ with $R\in[0,\infty]$ and finitely many points below each $\ell$-level, let $C:=\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal A\cap\{R<\infty\})+\mathbb R^2_{\ge0}$, and call the extreme points of $C$ its \emph{vertices}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For $c>0$, every minimiser of $c\,\ell+R$ lies on the boundary of $C$, on a supporting line with normal $(c,1)$.
\item If $c<c'$ and $h,h'$ minimise $J_c,J_{c'}$, then $\ell(h)\ge\ell(h')$ and $R(h)\le R(h')$.
\item A point of $\mathcal A$ is the unique minimising point of $c\,\ell+R$ for every $c$ in some nonempty open interval if and only if it is a vertex of $C$; the least-$\ell$ vertex and the least-$R$ vertex, if it exists, count. A hypothesis is then the unique minimiser if and only if no other hypothesis has the same point.
\item (Absolute length bound.) Every minimiser $h_c$ of $c\,\ell+R$ ($c>0$) solves $\min\{R(h):\ell(h)\le\ell(h_c)\}$. Conversely, a solution of $\min\{R(h):\ell(h)\le L\}$ minimises $c\,\ell+R$ for some $c>0$ if and only if its point lies on the boundary of $C$ and not on the open horizontal ray to the right of the least-$R$ vertex.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[The converse in (iv)]\label{rem:setup:hullconverse}
The converse in (iv) cannot be weakened to ``on the boundary of $C$'': in $\mathcal A=\{(0,1),(10,0),(20,0)\}$ the point $(20,0)$ lies on the boundary and solves the constrained problem for $L\ge20$, but $(10,0)$ is strictly better for every $c>0$.
\end{remark}

\emph{Examples.} In $\{(0,1),(10,0.5),(20,0)\}$ the middle point lies on the segment between the other two, so it is never the unique minimiser; the end points are the unique minimisers on $(0.05,\infty)$ and on $(0,0.05)$. In $\{(0,1),(5,0.9),(10,0)\}$ the point $(5,0.9)$ lies above the boundary of $C$; it solves the constrained problem for $L\in[5,10)$ and minimises no penalised objective.

We prove \cref{lem:setup:hull}. Write $\mathcal A':=\mathcal A\cap\{R<\infty\}$ and $\langle(c,1),(\ell,R)\rangle=c\,\ell+R$.

\emph{(i).} Since $(c,1)\ge0$, the infimum of $c\,\ell+R$ over $C=\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal A')+\mathbb R^2_{\ge0}$ equals its infimum over $\mathcal A'$. A minimiser $a$ over $\mathcal A$ therefore minimises a nonzero linear functional over $C$. So the line $\{c\,\ell+R=c\,\ell(a)+R(a)\}$ supports $C$ at $a$, and $a$ lies on the boundary of $C$.

\emph{Structure of the boundary.} Fix $a\in\mathcal A'$ and $s>0$. A point $b\in\mathcal A'$ with $\ell(b)>\ell(a)$ and $R(b)\le R(a)-s(\ell(b)-\ell(a))$ has $\ell(b)\le\ell(a)+R(a)/s$, because $R(b)\ge0$; only finitely many points satisfy this. So from each point the steepest descending direction to the right is attained, and the lower-left boundary of $C$ is a polygonal line. It consists of a vertical ray above the least-$\ell$ vertex, segments between consecutive vertices with slopes $-s$, $s\in(0,\infty)$, strictly increasing from left to right, and, if a point of least $R$ exists, a horizontal ray to the right of the least-$R$ vertex. Its corners are extreme points of $C$, and every extreme point of $C$ lies in $\mathcal A'$, because the extreme points of a convex hull belong to the generating set and adding the orthant creates none.

\emph{(iii).} Let $a$ be a vertex. Its two incident boundary pieces have slopes $-s_-<-s_+\le0$, with $s_-=\infty$ at the least-$\ell$ vertex and $s_+=0$ at the least-$R$ vertex. For $c\in(s_+,s_-)$ the line with normal $(c,1)$ through $a$ meets $C$ only at $a$, so $a$ is the unique minimising point. Conversely, if $a$ is the unique minimising point for every $c$ in an open interval, then $a$ is an exposed point of $C$, hence an extreme point, hence a vertex. A boundary point that is not a vertex lies in the relative interior of a segment between two vertices, or on one of the two rays. In the first case, whenever it minimises $c\,\ell+R$, so do both endpoints of its segment. A point on the vertical ray is beaten by the vertex below it (smaller $R$), and a point on the horizontal ray by the least-$R$ vertex (smaller $\ell$, same $R$). So no such point is ever the unique minimising point. The statement about hypotheses is immediate: a hypothesis is the unique minimiser exactly when its point is the unique minimising point and no other hypothesis has that point.

\emph{(ii).} Add $c\,\ell(h)+R(h)\le c\,\ell(h')+R(h')$ and $c'\ell(h')+R(h')\le c'\ell(h)+R(h)$ to get $(c'-c)(\ell(h')-\ell(h))\le0$, so $\ell(h')\le\ell(h)$. Then the first inequality gives $R(h)\le R(h')+c(\ell(h')-\ell(h))\le R(h')$.

\emph{(iv).} If $\ell(h)\le\ell(h_c)$, then $R(h)\ge R(h_c)+c(\ell(h_c)-\ell(h))\ge R(h_c)$, so $h_c$ solves the constrained problem with $L=\ell(h_c)$. For the converse, by the description of the boundary: a vertex minimises $c\,\ell+R$ for every $c$ in its interval $(s_+,s_-)$, which contains positive values; a point on a segment between two vertices, of slope $-s$ with $s>0$, lies on the supporting line with normal $(s,1)$ and so minimises $s\,\ell+R$. A point on the open horizontal ray is beaten, for every $c>0$, by the least-$R$ vertex. A point on the vertical ray above the least-$\ell$ vertex is never a constrained solution, since that vertex is feasible whenever the point is and has smaller $R$. A point not on the boundary minimises no linear functional with normal $(c,1)$ over $\mathcal A$, by (i).

\emph{\Cref{rem:setup:hullconverse}.} In $\mathcal A=\{(0,1),(10,0),(20,0)\}$ the point $(20,0)$ lies on the open horizontal ray to the right of the least-$R$ vertex $(10,0)$. It solves $\min\{R:\ell\le L\}$ for $L\ge20$, since it ties with $(10,0)$ there, but $20c>10c$ for every $c>0$.

A direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) confirms the equivalence in (iii) on $401$ random point sets with duplicate and collinear points, and the minimisers on $300$ further point sets.

\subsection{Proof of the coding bound}\label{app:setup:coding}

We prove \cref{lem:setup:coding}. Given $\pi$ and the finite set $\dom D$, the map $y\mapsto P_\pi(\dom D\mapsto y)$ on $Y^{\dom D}$ is lower semicomputable, uniformly in $(\pi,\dom D)$. It is a semimeasure. For a product hypothesis its total mass is $\prod_{x\in\dom D}\sum_y\nu_\pi(y\mid x)\le1$. For a shared hypothesis, repeated use of the one-point inequality gives $\sum_{y}P_\pi(\dom D\mapsto y)\le P_\pi(\emptyset)\le1$. By (F2), $K(y_D\mid\pi,\dom D)\le-\log P_\pi(D)+\kappa$. By (F3) and (F1),
\[
K(y_D\mid\dom D)\le K(\pi)+K(y_D\mid\pi,\dom D)+\kappa\le\ell(\pi)-\log P_\pi(D)+\kappa.
\]
\qed
