\section{Computable hypothesis classes}\label{sec:computable}\label{sec:related:computable}

The lower bounds of \cref{sec:teacher} use only coding properties of the class, so they survive in resource-bounded classes (proofs in \cref{app:related}).

\begin{proposition}[Computable product classes]\status{proved; the examples are remarks}\src{steeper Prop 4.4}\label{prop:related:computable}
Let $\mathcal H'$ be a hypothesis class of product hypotheses (\cref{def:setup:class}).
(a) \Cref{lem:teacher:list,lem:teacher:floor,lem:teacher:coding,lem:teacher:twopart} and the lower-bound parts of \cref{thm:teacher:main} ((o), the bound on $\Phi$ in (i), (ii), and the level and $R_B$ statements of (iii)) hold for every $\pi\in\mathcal H'$, with $\kappa$ replaced by a constant that depends on $\mathcal H'$. The parts that use reference programs ($\Delta(c)$, $J_g$, $J_b$, (iv)) hold if $\mathcal H'$ contains a good model $\pi'_g$ and a memoriser $\pi'_b$, with $\kappa_g:=\ell(\pi'_g)-d$ and $\kappa_b:=\ell(\pi'_b)-d-\lceil L_S\rceil$.
(b) \Cref{thm:split:private} holds in every countable product class with Kraft weights that contains a program for the sampler.
(c) \Cref{prop:absorb:price}(ii) holds in every product class with a prefix-free code.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Examples]\label{rem:computable:examples}
Examples are probabilistic programs clocked at $t(|x|)$ steps, as in time-bounded SFI \citep[time-bounded SFI]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals},% P1: thm:comp:timebounded
\ where generous time bounds change $\kappa_g,\kappa_b$ by logarithmic terms, and circuits with private output noise, where a parity costs about $d\log d$ bits, so $\kappa_g$ is of order $d\log d$ and $c_{\rm hi}$ shrinks by a factor of order $\log d$. \Cref{thm:absorb:absorption} does not transfer, since a mixture of all time-$t$ programs is not a time-$t$ program; so the contrast between product and shared classes is established only for unbounded classes.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Computability of the steeper predictor]\status{proved}\src{steeper Prop 4.5}\label{prop:related:computability}
Let $\lambda\ge1$ be computable, the class $\Hcl{\times}$ or $\Hcl{F}$, $X$ infinite and $\{0,1\}\subseteq Y$. (a) $\xi_\lambda(D)$ is lower semicomputable uniformly in $D$. (b) Neither the raw conditional $\xi_\lambda(D+(x,y))/\xi_\lambda(D)$ nor its normalised version is computable. (c) Both are limit-computable.
\end{proposition}

Part (b) is the diagonal argument for $\MF$ \citep[the computability proposition]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals}:% P1: prop:setting:computability
\ a computable conditional yields a computable labelling on which the predictor loses a constant per input, against the weight $2^{-\lambda(K(f)+\kappa)}$ of a program for it.

\begin{remark}[Neural-network Bayes]\status{remark}\src{steeper Rem 4.7}\label{rem:related:nn}
With labels independent given the parameters $\theta$, a network's Bayes posterior is a mixture of product laws, an object of the type of $\Mprod$ \citep[NN Bayes is function-space Bayes]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals}.% P1: prop:nn:functionspace
\ \Cref{thm:split:private} applies to countable discretisations with weights $p(\theta)$: under the power prior $p(\theta)^\lambda$, $\lambda>1$, deterministic parameter settings that reproduce the noisy labels get posterior mass exponentially small in the sampled randomness, if the noise model is in the family. Whether a parameter prior behaves like a description-length prior, as \cref{thm:teacher:main} needs, is the bridge question of the companion paper, which the slides of \citet{vaintrobhanni2026nnbayes} motivate;% P1: rem:nn:bridge, q:nn:bridges
\ nothing here settles it.
\end{remark}

\begin{question}[resource bounds and computability]\label{q:related:open}
(a) Does some time-bounded shared class contain a short member that absorbs everything at $\lambda>1$, as in \cref{thm:absorb:absorption}? If not, steeper simplicity could also work in time-bounded Solomonoff induction. (b) For computable $\lambda\ge1$ and the class $\Hcl{\times}$, is there a computable map from data sets to $\lambda$-MAPs?
\end{question}
