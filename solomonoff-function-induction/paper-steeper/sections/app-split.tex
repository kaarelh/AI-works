\subsection{Proof of the Ville lemma}\label{app:split:ville}

We prove \cref{lem:split:ville}. Let $Z_n:=\xi(o_n)/\nu(o_n)=2^{\delta_n}$. With frozen labels and a repeated query, $Z_{n+1}=Z_n$. Otherwise, given the past and the next input $x_{n+1}$, which the rule fixes before $y_{n+1}$ is drawn, $\Ex[Z_{n+1}]\le\sum_y\xi(o_n+(x_{n+1},y))/\nu(o_n)\le Z_n$, where $o_n+(x,y)$ is the data after the new query: the first step sums over the labels with $\nu(y\mid x_{n+1})>0$, and the second is the one-point inequality, which for a shared hypothesis and a new distinct input is \cref{def:setup:shared}, and for $\xi_{\mathcal C}$ holds termwise because $\sum_y\nu_\pi(y\mid x)\le1$, per query or on a new distinct input (\cref{lem:recovery:ratios}(a)). So $(Z_n)$ is a nonnegative supermartingale with $Z_0=\xi(\emptyset)\le1$, whatever the rule, and $\nu$ enters only in the denominator. Ville's maximal inequality \citep{ville1939etude} gives $\Prob[\sup_nZ_n\ge2^j]\le2^{-j}$. Finally $\xi_{\mathcal C}\ge2^{-\ell(\pi)}\nu_\pi$, so $\nu_\pi(o_n)\le2^{\ell(\pi)+\delta_n}\nu(o_n)$. \qed

\subsection{Fill-ins from stored stream prefixes}\label{app:split:stream}

\begin{lemma}[Fill-ins from stored stream prefixes]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Lemma 3.2$'$ (adapts foundations Prop 4.4); check c7(A)}\label{lem:split:stream}
Let the class be $\Hcl{\times}$, let $\nu$ be a sampler with a program $\pi_\nu$ of length $k$, and let $D$ be a data set with $n$ inputs, all of length at most $d$, with $\beta_D:=\min_{(x,y)\in D}\nu(y\mid x)>0$. Put $i_D:=\lceil\log(6n/\beta_D)\rceil$ and $t_D:=\lceil\log(2d+2+2\log(i_D+1))\rceil$. Then
\[
\Wdet(D)\ \ge\ 2^{-k-2\log(t_D+1)-\kappa}\,\nu(D).
\]
Since $\beta_D\ge\nu(D)$, $i_D\le\log(6n)+\log(1/\nu(D))+1$; so the cost $2\log(t_D+1)$ is doubly logarithmic in $d$ and triply logarithmic in $n$ and in the sampled randomness $-\log\nu(D)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof} Order $Y=\{y^{(1)},\dots,y^{(|Y|)}\}$.

\emph{Private streams.} Let $j(x)$ be the length-lexicographic index of $x$, so $j(x)\le2^{d+1}-2$ for $|x|\le d$, and let $\langle a,b\rangle:=(a+b)(a+b+1)/2+b$ be the Cantor pairing, injective and increasing in $b$. For a finite string $w$ (bits indexed from $0$), the \emph{stream prefix} of $x$ in $w$ is $w_{\langle j(x),0\rangle}w_{\langle j(x),1\rangle}\cdots$, continued while the positions are below $|w|$. Distinct inputs read disjoint positions.

\emph{Certification.} For an input $x$ put $F_h:=\sum_{h'\le h}\nu(y^{(h')}\mid x)$, so $F_0=0$ and $F_{|Y|}=1$. At depth $i\ge1$ compute, by a fixed algorithm from the program for $\nu$, rationals $q_{i,h}$ with $|q_{i,h}-F_h|\le2^{-i-2}$ ($q_{i,0}=0$, $q_{i,|Y|}=1$). A stream prefix $b_1\cdots b_i$ names the dyadic interval $I=[0.b_1\cdots b_i,\ 0.b_1\cdots b_i+2^{-i})$. It \emph{certifies} $y^{(h)}$ at depth $i$ if $q_{i,h-1}+2^{-i-2}\le\min I$ and $\sup I\le q_{i,h}-2^{-i-2}$; then $I\subseteq[F_{h-1},F_h]$.

\emph{Consistency.} If the stream of $x$ certifies $y^{(h)}$ at depth $i$ and $y^{(h')}$ at depth $i'$ with $h<h'$, the two intervals are nested, since one stream prefix extends the other; the inner one has positive length and lies in $[F_{h-1},F_h]\cap[F_{h'-1},F_{h'}]$, which is empty or the single point $F_h$, a contradiction. So $\varphi_w(x)$, the label certified at some depth within the stream prefix of $x$ in $w$ (undefined if none), is a well-defined partial computable function, and an index of it is computable from $(V(\pi_\nu),w)$.

\emph{Probability.} Every depth-$i$ dyadic interval inside $[F_{h-1}+2^{-i-1},F_h-2^{-i-1}]$ certifies $y^{(h)}$, because $q_{i,h-1}+2^{-i-2}\le F_{h-1}+2^{-i-1}$ and $q_{i,h}-2^{-i-2}\ge F_h-2^{-i-1}$. These intervals cover that segment up to at most $2\cdot2^{-i}$, and the segment has length $\nu(y^{(h)}\mid x)-2^{-i}$. So a uniform stream prefix of length at least $i$ gives $\varphi_w(x)=y^{(h)}$ with probability at least $\nu(y^{(h)}\mid x)-3\cdot2^{-i}$.

\emph{Independence.} For $w$ uniform on $\{0,1\}^L$ the stream prefixes of distinct inputs are independent and uniform. If $L\ge(2^{d+1}+i)^2$, every input of length at most $d$ has a stream prefix of length at least $i$, because $\langle j(x),i-1\rangle<(j(x)+i)^2\le(2^{d+1}+i)^2$. Take $i=i_D$; then $3\cdot2^{-i_D}\le\beta_D/(2n)$, and
\[
\Pr_w[\varphi_w\supseteq D]\ \ge\ \prod_{(x,y)\in D}\big(\nu(y\mid x)-3\cdot2^{-i_D}\big)\ \ge\ \nu(D)\Big(1-\frac1{2n}\Big)^n\ \ge\ \frac{\nu(D)}2 .
\]

\emph{Programs.} Let $V'$ be the prefix machine that reads $\pi_\nu$ (by simulating $V$), then a self-delimiting code of an integer $t$ of length at most $2\log(t+1)+1$, then $L_t:=2^{2^t}$ bits $w$, and outputs an index of the deterministic conditional semimeasure $\varphi_w$. By closure under prefix-free simulation (\cref{def:setup:class}) there is $\sigma$ with $V(\sigma u)=V'(u)$, and the programs $\pi_w:=\sigma\,\pi_\nu\,\code(t)\,w$ are distinct for distinct $w$. With $t=t_D$, $2^{t_D}\ge2d+2+2\log(i_D+1)$, so $L_{t_D}\ge(2^{d+1}(i_D+1))^2\ge(2^{d+1}+i_D)^2$.

\emph{Sum.}
\[
\Wdet(D)\ge\sum_w2^{-|\pi_w|}\ind[\varphi_w\supseteq D]=2^{-|\sigma|-k-|\code(t_D)|}\Pr_w[\varphi_w\supseteq D]\ge2^{-k-2\log(t_D+1)-|\sigma|-2}\,\nu(D).\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

A direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) gives the cost $2\log(t_D+1)$ as $5.6$ bits at $d=20$, $n=10^3$ ($\beta_D=1/3$); $6.3$ bits at $d=100$, $n=10^6$ ($\beta_D=1/3$ or $2^{-10}$); $7.2$ bits at $d=1000$, $n=10^{12}$ ($\beta_D=10^{-3}$); and $8.9$ bits at $d=10^6$, $n=10^{15}$ ($\beta_D=1/3$).

\subsection{Solomonoff induction at \texorpdfstring{$\lambda=1$}{lambda=1}}\label{app:split:si}

For \cref{thm:split:lambda1}: a sampler that gives every label probability at least $1/3$, such as the $\Bern(1/3)$ threshold hypotheses named after the theorem, has $\beta_n\ge1/3$ and so $i_n\le\lceil\log(18|\dom D_n|)\rceil$. In SI the analogous bounds hold, and in its representation by deterministic programs every hypothesis is a fill-in:

\begin{proposition}[SI at $\lambda=1$]\status{proved; (iii) known}\src{steeper Prop 3.3}\label{prop:split:si}
Let a string $z_{1:n}$ be drawn from a computable measure $\nu$ with a program $\pi_\nu\in\Hcl{\mathrm{SI}}$ of length $k$, and let $\delta^*:=\sup_n\log(M(z_{1:n})/\nu(z_{1:n}))$, where $M=\xi_1$ is the mixture of $\Hcl{\mathrm{SI}}$.
(i) $\Pi_1(\pi_\nu\mid z_{1:n})\ge2^{-k-\delta^*}$ for all $n$, and $\Prob[\delta^*\ge j]\le2^{-j}$. (ii) The deterministic hypotheses consistent with $z_{1:n}$ (point masses on computable infinite extensions) have total weight at least $2^{-k-K(n)-\kappa}\nu(z_{1:n})$. (iii) In the deterministic-program representation of $M$ every hypothesis is a fill-in; the sampler appears only as the programs ``decoder for $\nu$, then a code of $z$'', of total weight at least $2^{-k-\kappa}\nu(z)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof} (i) Under $\nu$, $M(z_{1:n})/\nu(z_{1:n})$ is a nonnegative supermartingale with initial value at most $1$, because $M(z0)+M(z1)\le M(z)$; Ville's inequality gives the tail, and $\Pi_1(\pi_\nu\mid z_{1:n})=2^{-k}\nu(z_{1:n})/M(z_{1:n})$. (ii) Let $w:=z_{1:n}0^\infty$ and $\mathbf 1_w$ the point mass on its prefixes. Given $n$ and $\pi_\nu$, the restriction of $\nu$ to $\{0,1\}^n$ is a computable measure, so (F2) gives $K(z_{1:n}\mid n,\pi_\nu)\le-\log\nu(z_{1:n})+\kappa$, and (F1), (F3) give $K(z_{1:n})\le K(n)+k-\log\nu(z_{1:n})+\kappa$. An index of $\mathbf 1_w$ is computable from $z_{1:n}$, so the class contains a program for $\mathbf 1_w$ of length at most $K(z_{1:n})+\kappa$, consistent with $z_{1:n}$. (iii) The programs ``a fixed decoder, a shortest program for $\nu$, then a Shannon--Fano code of $z$ under $\nu$'' have total weight at least $2^{-k-\kappa}\nu(z)$; this is the usual proof of $M\ge2^{-K(\nu)-O(1)}\nu$ \citep{li2008introduction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No split in SI's program representation]\status{proved}\src{steeper Rem 4.3}\label{rem:split:siprograms}
Solomonoff's $M$ is a mixture over deterministic programs. Tempering does not change deterministic hypotheses (\cref{lem:setup:basic}(iii)), and a power prior $2^{-\lambda\ell(\pi)}$ charges sampled bits and model bits alike, so it is indifferent between ``decoder for $\nu$ plus a code of the randomness'' and a literal fill-in of the same length. The split needs a representation in which the randomness is not part of the program: a product hypothesis with private coins.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Proof of the private-randomness theorem}\label{app:split:private}

We prove \cref{thm:split:private}. Write $o=o_n$ and $\Lambda_n(\pi):=\nu_\pi(o_n)/\nu(o_n)$, so that $\Lambda_n(\pi_\nu)=1$ for all $n$ almost surely.

\emph{(i).} $J_\lambda(\hat\pi;o)\le J_\lambda(\pi_\nu;o)=\lambda k+L_n(\nu)$, and by \cref{lem:split:ville}, $L_n(\hat\pi)\ge L_n(\nu)-\ell(\hat\pi)-\delta^*$. Substituting gives $(\lambda-1)\ell(\hat\pi)\le\lambda k+\delta^*$. The upper bound on the fit is $J_\lambda(\hat\pi;o)\le J_\lambda(\pi_\nu;o)$ with $\lambda\ell(\hat\pi)\ge0$.

\emph{(ii).} $\sum_{\ell(\pi)>\bar\ell}2^{-\lambda\ell(\pi)}\nu_\pi(o)\le2^{-(\lambda-1)\bar\ell}\xi_{\mathcal C}(o)\le2^{-(\lambda-1)\bar\ell+\delta^*}\nu(o)$, and the normaliser $\xi_\lambda(o)$ is at least the sampler's term $2^{-\lambda k}\nu(o)$.

\emph{(iii).} A fill-in $\pi$ has $1=\nu_\pi(o)\le2^{\ell(\pi)+\delta^*}\nu(o)$, which is the length bound. Summing over fill-ins, $\sum2^{-\lambda\ell(\pi)}=\sum2^{-\ell(\pi)}2^{-(\lambda-1)\ell(\pi)}\le2^{-(\lambda-1)(L_n(\nu)-\delta^*)}\sum2^{-\ell(\pi)}$, with $\sum2^{-\ell(\pi)}\le\xi_{\mathcal C}(o)\le2^{\delta^*}\nu(o)$ and a normaliser of at least $2^{-\lambda k}\nu(o)$. For the tempered posterior the fill-ins' weight is at most $2^{\delta^*}\nu(o)$ and the normaliser at least $2^{-k}\nu(o)^\eta$.

\emph{(iv), bounded length, every $\lambda\ge1$.} A $\lambda$-MAP has $J_\lambda(\hat\pi;o)\le J_\lambda(\pi_\nu;o)$, so $\log\Lambda_n(\hat\pi)\ge\lambda(\ell(\hat\pi)-k)$. For $\bar\ell\ge k$ and $\ell(\hat\pi)>\bar\ell$ this and $\lambda\ge1$ give $\log\Lambda_n(\hat\pi)\ge\ell(\hat\pi)-k$, hence $Z^{\bar\ell}_n\ge2^{-k}$. By \cref{lem:recovery:ratios}(a), which holds for every input rule and for both kinds of labels, $\Prob[\exists n:Z^{\bar\ell}_n\ge2^{-k}]\le2^kt_{\bar\ell}\to0$, and these events decrease in $\bar\ell$. So almost surely some finite $\bar\ell_0$ bounds every $\lambda$-MAP at every $n$. (For $\lambda>1$ this also follows from (i).)

\emph{(iv), MAPs.} By \cref{lem:recovery:ratios}(b) and countability (with frozen labels along the distinct inputs; $\Lambda$ is constant at a repeated query), almost surely $\Lambda_n(\pi)\to0$ simultaneously for every $\pi$ that is not $\nu$-equivalent on the run. A $\lambda$-MAP has $\log\Lambda_n(\hat\pi)\ge-\lambda k$, and each of the finitely many such programs of length at most $\bar\ell_0$ eventually has $\Lambda_n<2^{-\lambda k}$, so eventually none is a $\lambda$-MAP.

\emph{(iv), posterior.} Dividing by $\nu(o)$, the normaliser is at least $2^{-\lambda k}$, so for every $\bar\ell$
\[
\Pi_\lambda(\text{not $\nu$-equivalent on the run}\mid o_n)\ \le\ 2^{\lambda k}\Big[\sum_{\ell(\pi)\le\bar\ell,\ \text{not equivalent}}2^{-\lambda\ell(\pi)}\Lambda_n(\pi)+Z^{\bar\ell}_n\Big].
\]
The finite sum tends to $0$. Choose $\bar\ell_j$ with $t_{\bar\ell_j}\le4^{-j}$; by \cref{lem:recovery:ratios}(a) and the Borel--Cantelli lemma, almost surely $\sup_nZ^{\bar\ell_j}_n<2^{-j}$ for all large $j$, so the $\limsup$ is at most $2^{\lambda k-j}$ for every large $j$. \qed
