\section{Proofs for \texorpdfstring{\cref{sec:computable}}{Section \ref*{sec:computable}} and the correspondence with the earlier report}\label{app:related}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:related:computable}]
We go through the proofs and list, for each, the property of the class it uses and the constant that changes. Since $\mathcal H'$ is c.e.\ and prefix-free, (F1) holds for it with a constant $\kappa_2$ that depends on $\mathcal H'$: $K(\pi)\le\ell(\pi)+\kappa_2$.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt]
\item \Cref{lem:teacher:list}: the agreement $u(\pi;a')$ is lower semicomputable uniformly in $(\pi,a',d)$, as in every class with uniformly lower-semicomputable likelihoods; the constant of the enumeration of the list depends on the class.
\item \Cref{lem:teacher:coding}: $q_z$ is lower semicomputable uniformly in $(\pi,a,m,B)$, so (F2) applies with a constant that depends on the class.
\item \Cref{lem:teacher:floor}: Jensen's inequality only; no constant changes.
\item \Cref{lem:teacher:twopart}: the two lemmas above and (F1) with $\kappa_2$.
\item \Cref{thm:teacher:main}: the lemmas above, and the reference programs only through $J_g$ and $J_b$, whose constants are $\kappa_g$ and $\kappa_b$ of $\pi'_g$ and $\pi'_b$.
\item \Cref{thm:split:private}: Ville's inequality for the class's mixture $\xi_{\mathcal C}$ (\cref{lem:split:ville}), Kraft's inequality and countability; no constant enters.
\item \Cref{prop:absorb:price}(ii): the coding theorem for the semimeasure $\tilde R$ built from the class, which holds with a constant that depends on the class.
\item \Cref{thm:recovery:main,thm:recovery:kink}: \cref{def:split:setting} and the proofs in \cref{app:recovery} take an arbitrary countable class $\mathcal C$ with Kraft weights and sub-probability kernels that contains a program for $\nu$, and use nothing else: bounds for single programs are combined by Ville's inequality for $\xi_{\mathcal C}$ and by union bounds weighted by $2^{-\ell(\pi)}$, which Kraft's inequality makes summable. No machine constant enters, and $\ell_0$, $\mathcal E_0$ and the margins are computed from the class's own lengths and kernels; so a time-bounded class may select a longer but faster program than $\Hcl{\times}$ would.\qedhere
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Computability of the steeper predictor]\status{proved}\src{steeper Prop 4.5}\label{prop:related:computability}
Let $\lambda\ge1$ be computable, the class $\Hcl{\times}$ or $\Hcl{F}$, $X$ infinite and $\{0,1\}\subseteq Y$. (a) $\xi_\lambda(D)$ is lower semicomputable uniformly in $D$. (b) Neither the raw conditional $\xi_\lambda(D+(x,y))/\xi_\lambda(D)$ nor its normalised version is computable. (c) Both are limit-computable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:related:computability}]
(a) Each term $2^{-\lambda\ell(\pi)}P_\pi(D)$ is lower semicomputable uniformly in $(\pi,D)$, because $\lambda$ is computable, and the sum of uniformly lower-semicomputable nonnegative terms is lower semicomputable.

(b) This is the diagonal argument for $\MF$ \citep[Proposition~A.10(c)]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals}.% P1: prop:setting:computability
\ Identify $\mathbb N$ with infinitely many inputs. Suppose the raw conditional were computable. Define $f(t)$, for $t\in\mathbb N$, as the first $y\in\{0,1\}$ that a dovetailed search certifies to satisfy $\xi_\lambda(y\mid D_t,t)<0.6$, where $D_t:=\{(s,f(s)):s<t\}$. The two conditionals sum to at most $1$, so one of them is at most $\frac12$ and the search halts. Then $f$ is total computable, and by telescoping $\xi_\lambda(D_n)\le\xi_\lambda(\emptyset)\cdot0.6^n\le0.6^n$. But the class contains a deterministic program for $f$ of length at most $K(f)+\kappa$, so $\xi_\lambda(D_n)\ge2^{-\lambda(K(f)+\kappa)}$ for every $n$, a contradiction for large $n$. For the normalised conditional, run the same construction with it in place of the raw one; then its cumulative loss on $D_n$ is at least $n\log(1/0.6)$. Each normaliser $\sum_{y'}\xi_\lambda(D_t+(t,y'))$ is at most $\xi_\lambda(D_t)$, so the normalised conditional is at least the raw one, and the normalised cumulative loss is at most the raw one, $-\log\xi_\lambda(D_n)+\log\xi_\lambda(\emptyset)\le\lambda(K(f)+\kappa)$. This is again a contradiction for large $n$.

(c) Lower-semicomputable reals are computable in the limit, and the denominators are positive, because every finite data set has a consistent deterministic program.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Correspondence with the earlier report}\label{app:related:t5}

\label{rem:related:t5}\Citet[Section~8]{claude2026whatfollows} formalised the proposal for a general imitation channel, mostly by comparing a few hypotheses by rate. We re-derive the results we use from this earlier report: per-input and per-block pricing, the sequence-case threshold, the lemmas behind its kink theorem (here for semimeasures), the hull lemma, rate thresholds, rate blindness, non-identifiability, guard erosion and error envelopes. The table names them as they are named there. Two of its statements change here. Its full-table window at $d=20$ has upper end about $(1-H(p))/d\approx0.049$ when logarithmic terms are ignored; with the $2\log d$ terms, as large as $d$ there, a direct computation gives $c_{\rm hi}=0.01139$ with the machine constant set to $0$, an illustration (\cref{cor:teacher:fulltable}). And its lemma that the randomness of the error set removes the slack $2p$, stated for computable hypotheses, holds for semimeasures (\cref{lem:teacher:sharpfloor}).
