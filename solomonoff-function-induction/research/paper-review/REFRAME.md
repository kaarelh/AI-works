## What this means

1. **The papers present the material for the first time.**
   * Each definition, question, example and result is motivated on its own merits: mathematical naturalness, the analogy with Solomonoff induction (SI), and relevance to supervised learning or to neural networks.
   * The voice is "we".
   * The author's working notes (`hanni2026notes`), the note on grokking (`hanni2026grokking`) and the earlier draft in the notes are one internal stage of the research. They are not the paper's interlocutor.
2. **No visible text frames a result as answering, confirming, refining, correcting or quoting the notes.**
   * Delete or rewrite every phrase of these kinds:
     * "the author's notes ask / observe / suggest / suspect / conjecture / intend / list / define / propose / give / state";
     * "as anticipated in", "answers a remark in", "settles one half of an open problem posed in";
     * "makes precise the claim of an earlier draft", "read as intended", "the example of failure in the author's notes, made precise";
     * "The author conjectured", "a heuristic argument of the author", "as proposed in [Hän26]";
     * "the dominance statement of [Hän26]", "the converse asked for in [Hän26]";
     * "We call M_F SFI because it formalises the definition in the author's notes".
   * State the mathematics directly. Examples:
     * **Kolmogorov versus semimeasure (versions.tex).** Not "The author's notes ask whether …". Instead: SFI has at least two natural formalisations. A Kolmogorov-style version scores data by the shortest program consistent with them; a semimeasure version scores them by the universal function semimeasure, or by the program mixture. For sequences, Levin's coding theorem makes the analogous versions agree up to an additive constant, so it is natural to ask whether they agree here. They do not: …
     * **The SFI definition (setting.tex).** Not a quotation from the notes. Instead, an informal definition in our own words: put the universal prior on programs, regard the input–output pairs as generated by a program drawn from that prior, and condition on the pairs seen. Then the formal one.
     * **"So the converse asked for in [Hän26], that SFI works well if …, fails"** becomes "The natural converse, that …, fails."
     * **"This answers a remark in the author's polytime note"** becomes a plain statement of what is proved.
3. **Results that existed to correct the notes stay only if their mathematics informs the reader.** They are reframed as natural statements or natural proof strategies that fail.
   * `prop:hist:sketchfails`: "why a direct simulation fails". A natural proof strategy, simulating the reveal law with private randomness for each input, loses the cross-input correlations.
   * `prop:ood:draftfalse`: "two natural two-sided bounds fail". The candidate bounds are stated as natural candidates.
   * **The dominance theorem for reveal laws, with its Readings A/B/C.** Present the dominance statement as our question and our theorem. The readings become different natural formulations: "one might hope for the oracle form …, which fails (…); the statement that holds is …". The paper never says which reading the notes intended.
   * **Same treatment for:**
     * `thm:hist:chain`, `thm:hist:skipped`, `thm:hist:randchain`, `rem:hist:thm1open`;
     * `prop:ood:converse`;
     * the remark on simplest-consistent learning (`rem:comp:majority`);
     * the Paper 2 rows of the questions table.
   * Section and theorem titles carry no reference to the notes.
4. **Where the notes go.**
   * **Running text.** No citation of `hanni2026notes` or `hanni2026grokking` in the running text of any section or appendix. The exceptions are the acknowledgements and the footnotes below.
   * **Footnotes for credit.**
     * Short neutral footnotes give credit where a definition, question, example, construction or argument first appeared in the notes. For example: "This question first appeared in the author's working notes \citep{hanni2026notes}." or "An earlier version of this example appears in \citet{hanni2026notes}."
     * Merge credits where possible: one footnote per section is the norm. Aim for at most 10 such footnotes in Paper 1 and at most 5 in Paper 2.
     * No footnote evaluates the notes. A footnote may record neutrally that a statement there needs the hypotheses given here, but only where a reader of the notes would otherwise be misled. Example: "An earlier, informal version of this statement appears in [Hän26]; the hypotheses (A1), (A2) are needed."
     * Never put a footnote inside a theorem-like statement or a float. Attach it to the sentence before or after.
   * **Acknowledgements (both papers).** They keep the sentence "Working from the author's notes \citep{hanni2026notes} and under the author's direction, …". Add one sentence: "Several of the questions and examples studied here first appeared in those notes." For Paper 2, also cite `hanni2026grokking` there, as the note in which the proposal first appeared.
   * **Introductions (both papers).** Delete the paragraph "Relation to the author's notes" (Paper 2: "Relation to the author's notes and to other work"; move its "other work" content to related work if it is not already there). Nothing replaces it in the introduction: the credit is in the footnotes and the acknowledgements. The contributions list never mentions the notes. For example, "The proof sketch in the notes fails, and a stronger (oracle) reading is false" becomes "a direct simulation argument fails, and the stronger oracle form is false".
   * **Paper 2.**
     * The steeper-prior proposal is the paper's subject: "We study the following proposal: …". A single footnote at its first statement credits the note \citep{hanni2026grokking}. That footnote also absorbs the existing LessWrong footnote: "The proposal first appeared in the author's note \citep{hanni2026grokking} and in an online discussion (on LessWrong), where the absorption point below was also raised."
     * `tab:intro:claims` keeps its rows but no longer refers to the notes: caption "Questions studied in this paper and where they are answered."
     * The three-hypothesis calculation is a motivating calculation in our voice.
5. **No quotations from the notes or the grokking note,** including the SFI definition, "each input gets its own private randomness", "a higher coefficient …", "1 bit paying for 1 bit" and "I …" sentences. Paraphrase in formal English.
6. **The slides with Dmitry Vaintrob (`vaintrobhanni2026nnbayes`)** are joint prior work, cited as a normal reference for the claims they make: "The slides of Vaintrob and Hänni [VH26] state …; we prove … and leave … open".
   * Do not quote the slides' caveats about their own appendices. Replace them with a neutral sentence: "The slides give these claims with proof sketches; we use only what is proved here."
   * Keep the credit for the conjunction-ladder construction. It may be in the text, because the slides are a citable joint work.
7. **"The author" in the body.** Avoid it; use "we". Two exceptions:
   * the title-page footnote and the acknowledgements (unchanged);
   * Eisenstat's example stays "due to Sam Eisenstat (personal communication)" in the text and the acknowledgements. Its heuristic argument becomes "A heuristic argument (from correspondence with S. Eisenstat) …" or simply "The following heuristic argument suggests …", with the correspondence in a footnote.
8. **Mathematics is unchanged.**
   * Do not change any statement, hypothesis, number, proof step or caveat. Only motivation, attribution and framing change.
   * Where a sentence mixes framing with a mathematical caveat, keep the caveat. For example: "The dominance statement holds only under (A1)–(A2), and the f-seeing case is open."
   * **Numbering is frozen in Paper 1,** because Paper 2 cites Paper 1 by number:
     * do not add or delete numbered environments (theorem, proposition, lemma, corollary, remark, example, definition, conjecture, question, assumption), sections, subsections or numbered equations in Paper 1;
     * retitling is fine;
     * an environment whose only content was framing keeps a short mathematical remark, or its content is merged into it.
   * In Paper 2 the same rule holds unless a change is necessary.
   * Labels are never renamed.
   * The `\status{…}` and `\src{…}` calls stay; they print nothing. `%` comments may stay.
9. **Length:** the same or shorter.
