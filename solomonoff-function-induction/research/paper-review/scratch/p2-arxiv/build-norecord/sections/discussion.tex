\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What counts as one hypothesis.} Whether a steeper simplicity prior can separate a model from its noise depends on what counts as one hypothesis. When a universal mixture is a member of the class, as in SI and in SFI with shared randomness, it cannot: selected hypotheses are shorter than a bound that does not depend on the data, and a computable teacher's labels, errors included, are eventually predicted (\cref{thm:absorb:absorption}). With private randomness per input no member is universal, and steeper selection can work. The same contrast appears in the split between a sampler and its filled-in bits (\cref{thm:split:private} against \cref{thm:split:shared}) and in algorithmic statistics (\cref{prop:algstat:parity}): the product view counts as model what is reused across inputs and as noise what is private to each input. This is a modelling choice with a price on latent causes shared by several inputs (\cref{rem:absorb:why,prop:absorb:price}), and the protection ends when inputs carry labelled history (\cref{prop:absorb:history}).

\paragraph{The window and its price.} For error rules incompressible relative to the model, near-optimal product hypotheses are the simple model with noise for $2^{-m}<c\le c_{\rm hi}$ (\cref{thm:teacher:main}). The lower end is sharp because a bit of information about the error rule costs $c$ per sample in length and saves about $2^{-m}$ per sample in risk. So $\lambda$ must grow linearly with $n$, and the learner is then deliberately inconsistent for the teacher's labels: it discards every component whose rate is below $c$, valid or not, and its guaranteed code length exceeds a program's own loss by $c\,\ell(\pi)$ bits per sample (\cref{rem:limits:schedule}). Imitation data cannot justify this price, because strictly proper scores prefer the teacher's channel, errors included (\cref{prop:limits:noident}).

\paragraph{The gap to realistic errors.} Human labelling errors are often cheap, frequent patterns rather than incompressible sets, and for such errors the trick provably fails (\cref{prop:limits:envelope,prop:limits:rateblind}): steeper simplicity sorts components by rate, not by validity (\cref{prop:limits:separable}), so by itself it does not tell errors from rare valid rules. This agrees with H\"anni's assessment that ``it's very unlikely this steeper penalization is anywhere close to a full solution to the philosophical problem here'', with ``some hope that it works in some specific toy cases'' \citep{hanni2026lesswrong}: it provably works in one such case and fails in others.

\paragraph{The model--randomness split.} At $\lambda=1$ a Bayes mixture is indifferent, up to bounded factors, between a sampler and the programs that fill in its sampled bits (\cref{thm:split:lambda1}). A steeper prior over product hypotheses breaks the tie in favour of samplers, in the limited sense of \cref{rem:split:reading}; in SI's representation by deterministic programs no form of steepness does (\cref{rem:split:siprograms}).

\paragraph{Relation to other work.} The objective is that of $\lambda$-weighted two-part MDL in noisy classification, and the posteriors are generalised, fractional or safe posteriors; those works aim at the data distribution, while the target here differs from it by design (\cref{rem:related:mdl,rem:related:posteriors}). Cold posteriors are a different direction (\cref{rem:related:cold}). A neural network whose labels are independent given its parameters has a Bayes posterior that mixes product laws, so \cref{thm:split:private} applies to countable discretisations; whether a parameter prior behaves like a description-length prior, as \cref{thm:teacher:main} would need, is the bridge question raised by \citet{vaintrobhanni2026nnbayes} and is not settled here (\cref{rem:related:nn}).

\subsection{Open problems}\label{sec:discussion:open}

The open questions of the sections, collected:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Structured error rules.} For envelopes whose cheap region has error density below one, only part (i) of \cref{prop:limits:envelope} carries over; whether near-optimal hypotheses memorise the random part, and what is selected above $c_{\rm env}$, are open (\cref{q:limits:open}). A theory for realistic, low-complexity errors is the main gap between the trick and its motivation.
\item \emph{Other model classes and flip rates.} Which simple-model classes give a window (parities do; majority-like functions presumably do not)? Can the window method reach flip rates up to $1/4$, where the kink exists (\cref{q:teacher:open}, \cref{rem:limits:method})?
\item \emph{Non-uniform inputs and finite samples.} A version of \cref{thm:teacher:main} for non-uniform input laws (only the separable case is proved, \cref{prop:limits:inputlaw}), and guarantees at realistic sample sizes, far below the $10^{11}$ samples that uniform convergence needs (\cref{q:teacher:open}).
\item \emph{Choosing $c$.} A principled choice of $c$ that does not use the imitation data, and the extra information that would tell errors from rare valid rules (\cref{q:limits:open}).
\item \emph{Shared hypotheses.} Is every lower-semicomputable law of a random partial function dominated by $\MF$ up to a constant (\cref{q:absorb:shared})?
\item \emph{Resource bounds and computability.} Does a time-bounded shared class contain a short member that absorbs everything at $\lambda>1$? Is the $\lambda$-MAP computable from the data (\cref{q:related:open})?
\item \emph{Lower-semicomputable product models.} Does \cref{prop:algstat:coarse} hold for them (\cref{q:algstat:lsc})?
\item \emph{Literature.} The novelty claims of \cref{rem:related:new} need a comparison with the full texts of the classification papers and of the work on restricted model families (\cref{q:related:open}).
\end{enumerate}
