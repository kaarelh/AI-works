### versions.tex

6. **l.4, section opening.**
   * Old: "The author's notes ask whether the Kolmogorov and semimeasure versions of SFI are equivalent, noting that this would need a coding theorem \citep{hanni2026notes}. They are not: …"
   * New: "SFI has at least two natural formalisations: a \emph{Kolmogorov version} scores a data set by the length of its shortest consistent program, a \emph{semimeasure version} by a universal mixture such as $\MF$ or the program mixture $W$. For strings the two agree up to an additive constant (the coding theorem), so it is natural to ask whether they agree here.\footnote{This question first appeared in the author's working notes \citep{hanni2026notes}.} They do not: …"
   * Why: this is REFRAME §2's own example, carried out.
   * Deviation from the REFRAME wording: I say "for strings" and not "for sequences". The coding theorem $K(x)=-\log\mm(x)+O(1)$ is about finite strings with prefix complexity. For infinite sequences, monotone complexity and $-\log M$ do not agree up to a constant (Gács 1983, cited in `app-versions.tex`), so "for sequences" would be false.
   * Mathematics: unchanged; "They do not" and the rest are verbatim.

   * New: "$W$ reads the random program of the informal definition as …"
   * Why: it now points to our informal definition in §setting.
   * Mathematics: none involved.

8. **l.72, first line of `sec:versions:coding`.**

   * Why: change 6 now states the fact about strings, so this line avoids repeating it, and it is shorter.
   * Mathematics: unchanged. It is the same claim, which `thm:versions:nocoding` proves.
