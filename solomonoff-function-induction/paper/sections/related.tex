\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

This section describes the closest work informally; the objects are defined from \cref{sec:setting} on.

\paragraph{Solomonoff induction and operator induction.} Solomonoff induction predicts sequences with a universal prior \citep{solomonoff1978complexity,li2008introduction}. Solomonoff's operator induction \citep{solomonoff2008three} predicts the second element of a new pair from an unordered set of ordered pairs, the setting of supervised learning. SFI is a universal prior for this setting in which a random program, reading random bits shared by all inputs, labels every input. The per-query mixture $\Mpq$ (\cref{sec:versions}) is a mixture in the spirit of operator induction that gives each query fresh randomness; it equals the mixture of product laws on distinct inputs (\cref{rem:versions:operator}) and is the natural object for fresh label noise (\cref{prop:learn:perquery}). SFI differs from both in sharing randomness across inputs (\cref{prop:versions:shared}).

\paragraph{Chronological semimeasures.}\label{rem:learn:hutterEX}
Chronological semimeasures and a universal mixture over them were introduced by Hutter for the AIXI model \citep{hutter2000theory,hutter2005universal}, with actions and percepts; here the inputs play the actions and the labels the percepts. We define $\MH$ directly and do not use any identification with Hutter's construction. \citet{hutter2007aixi} lists supervised learning among the problem classes of the AIXI model. When its hypotheses see the list of previous inputs, SFI becomes \SFIhist{}, whose law $\MH$ is a universal lower-semicomputable chronological semimeasure (\cref{cor:hist:H4}). \citet{wood2013non} show that Solomonoff's and Levin's constructions define the same class of priors for sequences; \cref{thm:hist:representation} is the corresponding statement for chronological semimeasures. \citet{lattimore2011universal} show that normalised Solomonoff induction learns simple recursive sub-patterns of otherwise unstructured sequences, while the unnormalised version can fail; this bears on Solomonoff induction applied to the interleaved sequence $x_1y_1x_2y_2\cdots$ (\cref{prop:hist:lhg}).

\paragraph{Universal priors for classification.} \citet{lattimore2013nofree} give an off-line classifier inspired by Solomonoff induction and show that random selection of training data can reduce misclassification. \citet{poland2005asymptotics} show that two-part MDL prediction can have loss bounds exponentially worse than Bayes mixtures; the slides of \citet{vaintrobhanni2026nnbayes} compare this with the gap between simplest-consistent learning and weighted majority (\cref{rem:comp:selection}). \citet{leike2015bad} show that the optimality of AIXI depends on the reference machine; \cref{prop:ood:machine} is a supervised analogue. \citet{ryabko2006pattern} studies classification with deterministic labels and objects that are conditionally independent given the labels.

\paragraph{Learning theory.} The guarantees of \cref{sec:learn} are of the Occam \citep{blumer1987occam}, PAC-Bayes \citep{mcallester1999some}, MDL \citep{barron1991minimum,grunwald2007minimum}, halving and weighted-majority \citep{littlestone1988learning,littlestone1994weighted}, expert-advice \citep{vovk1990aggregating,cesabianchi2006prediction} and online-to-batch \citep{littlestone1989online,cesabianchi2004generalization} kinds, with the complexity $K$ in place of $\log|H|$ (\cref{prop:learn:occam}). Conformal prediction \citep{vovk2005algorithmic} is an exchangeability-based framework for supervised prediction, related to the random splits of \cref{prop:learn:splits} (see also \citealp{vovk1992universal}). Inductive inference of recursive functions \citep{gold1967language,barzdin1972prediction,case1983comparison} is the deterministic ancestor: identification in the limit and prediction of recursive functions, mostly for inputs in the order $0,1,2,\dots$.

\paragraph{Computable versions and networks.}\label{rem:nn:literature}
Speed priors \citep{schmidhuber2002speed,filan2016loss} are time-bounded universal priors for sequences; \cref{sec:comp} gives time-bounded mixtures in the function setting. \citet{dingle2018input} show that input--output maps are strongly biased towards simple outputs, and \citet{valle2019deep} argue that the parameter--function map of deep networks is biased towards simple functions. The simplicity-bias upper bound holds for every map, while the lower bound needed for a simplicity prior fails (\cref{prop:nn:typical,prop:nn:nolower}); the slides of \citet{vaintrobhanni2026nnbayes} argue the same. \citet{mingard2021sgd} report that SGD samples functions with probabilities close to the Bayesian posterior, and \citet{mingard2025deep} attribute much of the generalisation of deep networks to a prior bias towards simple functions together with the structure of the data. \citet{buzaglo2024} show that random interpolators under a flat parameter prior generalise when a narrow teacher exists; the slides call this the closest prior work. \citet{goldblum2023no} relate no-free-lunch theorems to Kolmogorov complexity, and \citet{grau2024learning} train networks on data from universal machines to approximate Solomonoff induction. Steeper simplicity priors, related to $\lambda$-weighted MDL, are the subject of the companion paper \citep{claudeSFI2026steeper}, summarised in \cref{sec:steep}.

\paragraph{Novelty.}\label{rem:related:new}
To our knowledge the following are new: the name and the function-machine formulation of SFI; the separations between SFI and \SFIhist{} and the comparisons with Solomonoff induction on the interleaved sequence (\cref{sec:hist,sec:ex}); the out-of-distribution results \cref{prop:ood:hanni} (in this form), \cref{thm:ood:twosided}, \cref{prop:ood:budget,prop:ood:converse,prop:ood:sandwich,prop:ood:labelled} and \cref{ex:ood:easyhard}; the time-bounded mixtures in the function setting (\cref{sec:comp:time}; the speed priors are sequence analogues); the every-ordering form of circuit Bayes with explicit constants (\cref{thm:comp:circuitbayes}); the padding bound for uniform circuit priors (\cref{prop:nn:uniform}); and the statements on Gaussian-process classification and anti-correlation in \cref{rem:nn:oddparity}(b),(c). Known are operator induction and the universal chronological semimeasure; Solomonoff-inspired classifiers and two-part MDL for classification; Occam, PAC-Bayes and online-to-batch bounds, including the exchangeability argument of \cref{prop:learn:splits}; the halving bound, its contrast with simplest-consistent selection on the same prior, and the conjunction ladder \citep{vaintrobhanni2026nnbayes}; the Walsh diagonalisation and the dimension bound for kernels and the vanishing odd eigenvalues of bias-free ReLU kernels (\cref{thm:nn:walsh}, \cref{rem:nn:oddparity}(a)); the fact that $-\log P\approx K$ on $P$-most objects (\cref{prop:nn:typical}); and the dependence of universal priors on the reference machine.
