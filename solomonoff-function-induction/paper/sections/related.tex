\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Solomonoff's operator induction.} \citet{solomonoff2008three} describes three kinds of induction; the third concerns ``an unordered set of ordered pairs'', where one predicts the second element of a new pair from the first (we have seen only the abstract). Secondary sources describe its prediction as a mixture of computable conditional models weighted by $2^{-|O^j|}\prod_iO^j(a_i\mid q_i)$, with a convergence theorem bounding the total error by $\ln2$ times the complexity of the source \citep{ozkural2015ultimate,ozkural2017ultimate}. Read this way it is the per-query mixture $\Mpq$, which equals $\Mprod$ on distinct inputs (\cref{rem:versions:operator}); x-only dominance on distinct inputs is then Solomonoff's, and $\Mpq$ is the natural object for fresh label noise (\cref{prop:learn:perquery}). SFI differs in sharing randomness across inputs (\cref{prop:versions:shared}).

\paragraph{Hutter's chronological semimeasures.} With inputs as actions and labels as percepts, \SFIhist{} is a universal chronological semimeasure in the sense of AIXI \citep{hutter2000theory,hutter2005universal} (\cref{cor:hist:H4}); that it agrees with Hutter's construction up to constant factors is expected but was not checked against his texts; Hutter lists supervised learning from examples among AIXI's problem classes \citep{hutter2007aixi} (\cref{rem:learn:hutterEX}). \citet{lattimore2011universal} show, according to their abstract, that normalised Solomonoff induction learns computable sub-patterns of otherwise unstructured sequences while the unnormalised version can fail; this bears on SI applied to $x_1y_1x_2y_2\cdots$ (\cref{prop:hist:lhg}). \citet{wood2013non} state, in their abstract, that Solomonoff's and Levin's constructions define the same class of priors for sequences; \cref{thm:hist:representation} is the corresponding statement for chronological semimeasures. The observation that a history-aware universal mixture is the natural ideal learner from examples is therefore prior work in substance.

\paragraph{Universal priors for classification.} \citet{lattimore2013nofree} use algorithmic information theory to argue against no-free-lunch pessimism and give a Solomonoff-inspired off-line classifier; whether their random-selection result coincides with \cref{prop:learn:splits} we have not checked. \citet{poland2005asymptotics} show that two-part MDL prediction can have loss bounds exponentially worse than Bayes mixtures; the slides compare this with the gap between Occam and weighted majority (\cref{rem:comp:selection}). \citet{leike2015bad} show that AIXI's optimality depends on the reference machine; \cref{prop:ood:machine} is a supervised analogue. \citet{ryabko2006pattern} studies deterministic labels with conditionally independent objects.

\paragraph{Learning theory.} The guarantees of \cref{sec:learn} are of the Occam \citep{blumer1987occam}, PAC-Bayes \citep{mcallester1999some}, MDL \citep{barron1991minimum,grunwald2007minimum}, halving and weighted-majority \citep{littlestone1988learning,littlestone1994weighted}, expert-advice \citep{vovk1990aggregating,cesabianchi2006prediction} and online-to-batch \citep{littlestone1989online,cesabianchi2004generalization} kinds; \cref{prop:learn:occam} makes the comparison. Conformal prediction \citep{vovk2005algorithmic} is an exchangeability-based supervised framework related to \cref{prop:learn:splits}; Vovk's earlier universal forecasting algorithms \citep{vovk1992universal} we have not examined. Inductive inference of recursive functions \citep{gold1967language,barzdin1972prediction,case1983comparison} is the deterministic, identification-in-the-limit ancestor, mostly for inputs in the order $0,1,2,\dots$.

\paragraph{Computable versions and networks.} Speed priors \citep{schmidhuber2002speed,filan2016loss} are time-bounded universal priors for sequences; \cref{sec:comp} gives function versions. The simplicity-bias literature on neural networks is discussed in \cref{rem:nn:literature}, and the steeper-prior material, with its relation to $\lambda$-weighted MDL, in \cref{sec:steep}.

\begin{remark}[what exists and what appears new]\label{rem:related:new}
\status{known (snippets; unverified)}\src{context \S6; history \S4.6; foundations \S6.6}
At the level of a search that saw abstracts and secondary sources only, the following exist: the per-query product mixture for supervised pairs with its convergence theorem (operator induction, as secondary sources describe it); the universal chronological semimeasure; Solomonoff-style classifiers and discrete MDL for classification; Occam, PAC-Bayes and online-to-batch bounds, including the exchangeability argument of \cref{prop:learn:splits}; the halving bound and its contrast with argmax selection on the same prior (the slides); the Walsh diagonalisation and dimension bound for kernels and the vanishing odd eigenvalues of bias-free ReLU kernels (\cref{thm:nn:walsh,rem:nn:oddparity}(a)); machine dependence of universal priors; and the conjunction ladder (the slides). We found no prior use of the name ``Solomonoff function induction'' and none of the following: the function-machine form of the universal chronological semimeasure, the separations between SFI and \SFIhist{}, and the comparisons with SI on the interleaved sequence (\cref{sec:hist}); the OOD results of \cref{sec:ood}; the length-activated, round-clocked and history-aware time-bounded mixtures (\cref{sec:comp:time}); the every-ordering form of circuit Bayes with explicit constants (\cref{thm:comp:circuitbayes}; the statement itself is H\"anni's claim); the uniform-circuit padding bound (\cref{prop:nn:uniform}); and the GP-classification and anti-correlation statements of \cref{rem:nn:oddparity}(b),(c). Each of these should be checked against \citet{solomonoff2008three}, \citet{hutter2005universal} and \citet{lattimore2013nofree} before any claim of novelty.
\end{remark}
