\section{Function semimeasures, function machines and SFI}\label{sec:setting}

H\"anni's definition of Solomonoff function induction (SFI) reads: ``you have a bunch of input-output pairs. you have the usual prior on all programs. solomonoff function induction := you think of the input-output pairs as being generated by picking a random program from this prior and generating using it, and so you update on seen pairs as you should'' \citep{hanni2026notes}. A random program may be deterministic or read random bits, private to each input or shared by all inputs. We take the most general reading that still yields a random function: one random tape read by all inputs (\cref{def:setting:machine}); \cref{sec:versions} compares the other readings. The notion and several of the claims checked below are H\"anni's. Proofs are in \cref{app:setting}.

\subsection{Data and conventions}\label{sec:setting:data}

\begin{definition}[Data]\src{foundations \S1}\label{def:setting:data}
Inputs $X$, labels $Y$; by default $X=Y=\{0,1\}^*$, but any infinite decidable $X$ and countable $Y$, $|Y|\ge2$, will do unless stated. $\bot$ means ``undefined'', $\Ybot:=Y\cup\{\bot\}$. A \emph{data set} is a finite partial function $D:X\rightharpoonup Y$; $D+(x,y):=D\cup\{(x,y)\}$ for $x\notin\dom D$; $D|_T$ is a restriction. A partial function $f$ \emph{extends} $D$, $f\supseteq D$, if $f(x)=y$ for all $(x,y)\in D$. A sequence $(x_t,y_t)_{t\le n}$ is \emph{consistent} if $D_n:=\{(x_t,y_t):t\le n\}$ is a partial function; $h_t:=(x_1,y_1,\dots,x_t,y_t)$ is the history. For $\nu$ from data sets to $[0,1]$ the \emph{conditional} is $\nu(y\mid D,x):=\nu(D+(x,y))/\nu(D)$ for $x\notin\dom D$ ($0$ if $\nu(D)=0$), and $\nu(y\mid D,x):=\ind[y=D(x)]$ for $x\in\dom D$ (the \emph{repeated-input convention}).
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Complexity and constants.} $V$ is a fixed universal prefix machine, $K$ prefix complexity, $\varphi_e$ the $e$-th partial computable function $X\rightharpoonup Y$, $\mm(i):=\sum_{V(p)=i}2^{-|p|}\ge2^{-K(i)}$, and $K(\nu):=\min\{K(i):\nu_i=\nu\}$ in an effective enumeration $(\nu_i)$; ``lsc'' means lower semicomputable. We use, without re-checking the sources \citep{levin1974laws,gacs1974symmetry,chaitin1975theory}, \citep[\S4.3]{li2008introduction}, that $\mm$ dominates every lsc discrete semimeasure up to a constant factor, and the conditional coding theorem: if $P(a\mid z)$ is lsc uniformly in $(a,z)$ with $\sum_aP(a\mid z)\le1$, then $K(a\mid z)\le-\log P(a\mid z)+K(P)+c$; both relativise. $\log=\log_2$; losses are in bits; $c$ and $O(1)$ depend only on the fixed machines and codings. $\Unif$ is the fair-coin measure on tapes $\omega\in\{0,1\}^\infty$, and $\omega_{1:L}$ the first $L$ tape bits.

\paragraph{Two loss conventions.} The predictors below issue sub-probabilities; the missing mass is the probability of ``undefined''. The \emph{semimeasure convention} scores the raw conditionals, the \emph{normalised convention} divides each by its sum. Statements about losses name their convention; upper bounds in the semimeasure convention hold in both, lower bounds need not (\cref{prop:setting:normalise,rem:setting:conventions}).

\subsection{Three notions of function semimeasure}\label{sec:setting:weak}

\begin{definition}[Weak function semimeasure]\src{foundations Def 2.1}\label{def:setting:weak}
A map $\nu$ from data sets to $[0,1]$ with $\nu(\emptyset)\le1$ and $\nu(D)\ge\sum_{y\in Y}\nu(D+(x,y))$ for every $D$ and $x\notin\dom D$.
\end{definition}

Then $\nu$ is monotone, and $\sum_{D:\dom D=S}\nu(D)\le\nu(\emptyset)$ for finite $S$ (sum out one input at a time). Call a predictor $q$ (sub-probabilities $q(\cdot\mid D,x)$, with the repeated-input convention) \emph{order-independent} if, for every $D$, the product $\nu_q(D)$ of its conditionals along a reveal order of $D$ is the same for all orders.

\begin{proposition}[Predictor reading]\status{proved}\src{foundations Prop 2.1a (= context Prop 5.1(a))}\label{prop:setting:predictor}
(a) If $\nu$ is weak with $\nu(\emptyset)=1$, its conditionals form an order-independent predictor $q_\nu$ with $\nu_{q_\nu}=\nu$. (b) If $q$ is order-independent, $\nu_q$ is weak with $\nu_q(\emptyset)=1$, and $q(y\mid D,x)=\nu_q(D+(x,y))/\nu_q(D)$ when $\nu_q(D)>0$. So weak semimeasures with $\nu(\emptyset)=1$ correspond one-to-one to order-independent predictors, identified where $\nu(D)>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Random partial function]\src{foundations Def 2.2}\label{def:setting:rpf}
A \emph{random partial function} is a random element $F$ of $\Ybot^X$ (product $\sigma$-algebra) with law $P$; its \emph{containment functional} is $\nu_P(D):=P(F\supseteq D)$.
\end{definition}

Every containment functional is weak, since the events $\{F\supseteq D+(x,y)\}$, $y\in Y$, are disjoint subsets of $\{F\supseteq D\}$. The converse fails (\cref{ex:setting:weaknotrpf}). If every conditional of $q$ sums to $1$, $\nu_q$ is the law of a random total function, and $\MF$ dominates every such lsc law (\cref{prop:setting:normtotal,cor:setting:normuniversal}, \cref{app:setting:mobius}). For finite $T\subseteq X\setminus\dom D$ put $\Mob_T\nu(D):=\sum_{E:\,\dom E\subseteq T}(-1)^{|E|}\nu(D\cup E)$.



\begin{proposition}[Möbius characterisation]\status{proved}\src{foundations Prop 2.3 (= context Prop 5.1(c)); checks c6, r4}\label{prop:setting:mobius}
A weak $\nu$ with $\nu(\emptyset)=1$ is the containment functional of a random partial function if and only if $\Mob_T\nu(D)\ge0$ for all $D$ and all finite $T\subseteq X\setminus\dom D$. The law is then unique, and $\Mob_T\nu(D)=P(F\supseteq D,\ F|_T\equiv\bot)$.
\end{proposition}

The weak inequality is the case $|T|=1$. The proposition is the function-space analogue of the correspondence between completely monotone set functions and random sets \citep{choquet1954theory,matheron1975random,shafer1976mathematical} \status{known; unverified}; we have not checked the analogy against these sources.

\begin{example}[Weak but not a random function]\status{proved; computed}\src{foundations Ex 2.4; checks c1, c6, c9(b), r1, r4}\label{ex:setting:weaknotrpf}
(a) Inputs $a,b$: $\nu(\emptyset)=\nu(\{(a,0)\})=\nu(\{(b,0)\})=1$, all other values $0$. It is weak, but $\Mob_{\{a,b\}}\nu(\emptyset)=-1$: ``$F(a)=0$ surely and $F(b)=0$ surely, but never both''.
(b) Inputs $a,b,c$, labels $\{0,1\}$: $\nutri(\emptyset)=1$, and $\nutri=\frac12$ on the six one-point data sets and on $E_1=\{(a,0),(b,1)\}$, $E_2=\{(b,0),(c,1)\}$, $E_3=\{(c,0),(a,1)\}$, and $0$ elsewhere. $\nutri$ is weak (c1), and $E_1,E_2,E_3$ are pairwise inconsistent with total mass $\frac32$; so no law $P$ of a random partial function has $P(F\supseteq\cdot)\ge c\,\nutri$ with $c>\frac23$, and the uniform law on $(F(a),F(b),F(c))\in\{(0,1,0),(1,0,1),(1,0,0)\}$ attains $c=\frac23$.
\end{example}

\subsection{Function machines and universality}\label{sec:setting:machines}

\begin{definition}[Function machine]\src{foundations Def 2.5}\label{def:setting:machine}
A \emph{function machine} is a c.e.\ set $T\subseteq X\times\{0,1\}^*\times Y$ of triples that is \emph{consistent}: if $(x,w,y),(x,w',y')\in T$ and $w,w'$ are comparable, then $y=y'$. For a tape $\omega$ put $f^T_\omega(x):=y$ if some prefix $w$ of $\omega$ has $(x,w,y)\in T$, and $f^T_\omega(x):=\bot$ otherwise. The \emph{machine law} of $T$ is $\nu_T(D):=\Unif\{\omega:\ f^T_\omega\supseteq D\}$.
\end{definition}

Equivalently, $T$ is a Turing machine with a one-way random tape which on query $x$ reads finitely many tape bits and halts with a label or diverges; all queries read the same tape.

\begin{proposition}[Machine laws]\status{proved}\src{foundations Prop 2.6 and the enumeration remark}\label{prop:setting:machinelaw}
Every machine law is the containment functional of a random partial function, with $\nu_T(\emptyset)=1$, and is lsc uniformly in $D$ and an index of $T$. Function machines have an effective enumeration $(T_i)$, to which $K(\nu)$ for machine laws refers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Universal function machine]\status{proved}\src{foundations Thm 3.2}\label{thm:setting:universal}
$T_U:=\{(x,\,pw,\,y):\ p\in\dom V,\ (x,w,y)\in T_{V(p)}\}$ is a function machine. Its law $\MF:=\nu_{T_U}$ has $\MF(\emptyset)=1$ and, for every $D\ne\emptyset$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:setting:mixture}
\MF(D)=\textstyle\sum_i\mm(i)\,\nu_{T_i}(D),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \MF\ \ge\ 2^{-K(\nu)}\,\nu\quad\text{for every machine law }\nu .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

So $\MF$ is the Bayes mixture ``pick machine $i$ with probability $\mm(i)$, then a random tape''; the posterior weight of $i$ after $D$ is $\mm(i)\nu_{T_i}(D)/\MF(D)$.

\begin{proposition}[What machine laws include]\status{proved}\src{foundations Prop 3.3}\label{prop:setting:includes}
(a) \emph{Product laws (H\"anni's samplers that see only the input).} If $P(y\mid x)$ is lsc with $\sum_yP(y\mid x)\le1$ (a \emph{conditional semimeasure}), then $\nu_P(D):=\prod_{(x,y)\in D}P(y\mid x)$ is a machine law with $K(\nu_P)\le K(P)+O(1)$; probabilistic machines that read $x$ and output $y$ (halting or not) define exactly these $P$.
(b) \emph{Deterministic programs.} For partial computable $\varphi$, $D\mapsto\ind[\varphi\supseteq D]$ is a machine law of complexity at most $K(\varphi)+O(1)$.
(c) \emph{Computable laws.} A law of a random partial function whose cylinder probabilities $P(F|_S=v)$, $v\in\Ybot^S$, are computable uniformly in $(S,v)$ is a machine law; if $Y$ is finite or the function is total, computable $\nu_P$ suffices.
(d) \emph{Mixtures.} If $(\nu_i)$ are machine laws with computable indices and $w$ is an lsc discrete semimeasure, $D\mapsto\sum_iw_i\nu_i(D)$ ($D\ne\emptyset$) is a machine law.
\end{proposition}

In (a) each input samples from a private stream of tape bits, as H\"anni proposed: ``use different bits of randomness from the universal semimeasure's infinite read tape for sampling on different inputs; one can do this using dovetailing'' \citep{hanni2026notes}. With \cref{thm:setting:universal} this gives his dominance theorem for such samplers (\cref{thm:learn:dominance}).

\begin{remark}[Per input, not per query]\src{foundations remark after Prop 3.3}\label{rem:setting:perinput}
In (a) a repeated query returns the same label; a sampler run afresh at each query would not. This choice fits H\"anni's setting of ``input-output pairs from a function'' and makes $\nu_P$ a random function; the per-query reading gives a different object, $\Mpq$ (\cref{def:versions:variants,prop:learn:perquery}).
\end{remark}



\begin{theorem}[Universal weak semimeasure]\status{proved; trimming computed}\src{foundations Thm 3.4; check r3}\label{thm:setting:weakuniversal}
There is an lsc weak semimeasure $\Mw$ with $\Mw\ge2^{-K(\nu)}\nu$ for every lsc weak semimeasure $\nu$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Weak versus machine universality]\status{proved; computed}\src{foundations Thm 3.5 and the remark after it; checks c1, c6, c9, r1}\label{thm:setting:weakvsmachine}
(i) $\MF\le2^{O(1)}\Mw$. (ii) For every lsc weak $\nu$ and every $D$, $\MF(D)\ge2^{-K(\nu)-K(\dom D)-O(1)}\nu(D)$; so $\Mw(D)\le2^{K(\dom D)+O(1)}\MF(D)$. (iii) For every $k$ some $D_k$ with $|\dom D_k|=2k$ has $\log(\Mw(D_k)/\MF(D_k))\ge k\log\frac32-K(k)-O(1)$. (iv) This bound holds on average over the $3^k$ explicit data sets $E_{\vec\imath}$ of the proof. (v) The constant $(2/3)^k$ behind (iii) is exact for $\nutri^{\otimes k}$.
\end{theorem}

In (ii) the labels of $\dom D$, read in a fixed order, form a semimeasure on strings that a monotone machine realises exactly (\cref{lem:setting:realise}). In (iii) $k$ blocks of $\nutri$ give $3^k$ pairwise inconsistent data sets of $\Mw$-mass $\ge2^{-k-K(k)-O(1)}$ each, while $\MF$ gives them total mass $\le1$; there $K(\dom D_k)\le k\log3+K(k)+O(1)$, so at least a fraction $\log\frac32/\log3\approx0.37$ of the term $K(\dom D)$ is needed.



\begin{remark}[Weak and machine universality differ]\status{refuted (weak definition); open (random-function laws)}\src{foundations \S3, claim F10}\label{rem:setting:h1a}
A Levin-style equivalence ``lsc function semimeasures, or equivalently monotone function machines'' (a natural conjecture, by analogy with sequences \citep{zvonkin1970complexity}) is refuted for the weak definition: $\Mw$ and $\MF$ agree up to $2^{K(\dom D)+O(1)}$, not up to a constant. For random-function laws it is open (\cref{q:setting:rpf}).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Open question.}\label{q:setting:rpf} Is every lsc law of a random partial function dominated by $\MF$ up to a constant factor? Yes for the classes of \cref{prop:setting:includes} and for computable filling-in processes (\cref{rem:setting:fillin}); up to $2^{K(\dom D)+O(1)}$ always. It would suffice (proof sketch, \cref{app:setting:rpf}) that every such law has lower approximations increasing on all up-sets of the extension order, not only on the sets $\{F\supseteq D\}$; Strassen's theorem \citep{strassen1965existence} then gives couplings that fill in values.

\subsection{The definition of SFI and its conventions}\label{sec:setting:sfi}

\begin{definition}[Solomonoff function induction]\src{foundations Def 5.1}\label{def:setting:sfi}
\emph{SFI} is the random partial function $f_\omega:=f^{T_U}_\omega$, $\omega\sim\Unif$, with law $\MF$. After data $D$ the \emph{posterior} is the law of $f_\omega$ given $f_\omega\supseteq D$, with containment functional $E\mapsto\MF(D\cup E)/\MF(D)$. The \emph{predictive conditional} at $x\notin\dom D$ is $\MF(y\mid D,x)=\MF(D+(x,y))/\MF(D)$; its defect $1-\sum_y\MF(y\mid D,x)$ is the posterior probability that $f_\omega(x)=\bot$; at $x\in\dom D$ it is the stored label with probability $1$. The \emph{normaliser} is $\Znorm(D,x):=\sum_y\MF(D+(x,y))$, and the \emph{normalised conditional} is $\MFbar(y\mid D,x):=\MF(D+(x,y))/\Znorm(D,x)$. For a weak $\nu$, $L_\nu(D):=-\log\nu(D)$, and SFI's loss is $\LSFI(D):=L_{\MF}(D)$.
\end{definition}

$\MF(D)>0$ for every $D$ (the lookup table of $D$ is a program).

\begin{proposition}[Order independence]\status{proved; computed}\src{foundations Prop 5.2 (= context Fact A, history Prop 5.1); check c4}\label{prop:setting:order}
Semimeasure convention. For every weak $\nu$ and consistent $(x_t,y_t)_{t\le n}$, $\sum_{t\le n}-\log\nu(y_t\mid D_{t-1},x_t)=L_\nu(D_n)+\log\nu(\emptyset)\le L_\nu(D_n)$, which depends only on the set $D_n$, not on the order or on repetitions. For SFI it is $\LSFI(D_n)$. On an inconsistent sequence it is infinite.
\end{proposition}

(A repeated input contributes the factor $1$ or $0$; the other factors telescope.)

\begin{proposition}[Normalisation]\status{proved; computed}\src{foundations Prop 5.3; checks c3, r8, r13; context c3}\label{prop:setting:normalise}
Let $\LSFIbar:=\sum_{t\le n}-\log\MFbar(y_t\mid D_{t-1},x_t)$ along a given consistent sequence. (a) $\LSFIbar\le\LSFI(D_n)$, so upper bounds on $\LSFI$ hold in both conventions. (b) For laws of random partial functions the normalised loss can depend on the order. (c) For random total functions it equals the semimeasure loss.
\end{proposition}

For (b), let $(F(a),F(b))$ be uniform on $\{(0,0),(0,\bot),(1,1),(\bot,\bot)\}$: $D=\{(a,0),(b,0)\}$ has normalised probability $\frac23$ if $a$ comes first and $\frac12$ if $b$ does, semimeasure probability $\frac14$ in both orders (c3). On a random six-point mixture the semimeasure loss is $10.919980315195$ bits in all $720$ orders, the normalised loss between $7.33$ and $10.64$ bits (context c3).



What does transfer is collected in \cref{prop:setting:transfer} (\cref{app:setting:normalised}): with $Z_t$ the posterior probability that $f_\omega(x_t)$ is defined, $\LSFIbar=\LSFI(D_n)-\sum_t\log(1/Z_t)$, so single-point lower bounds transfer; and two general lower bounds, $K^{\emptyset'}(y_{1:n}\mid x_{1:n})-O(1)$ and the chronological Bayes loss, which are $O(1)$ in the chain, the skipped strings and the cyclic permutation (\cref{sec:hist,sec:ex}).

\begin{remark}[Which lower bounds are semimeasure-only]\status{proved (as a list); transfer open where stated}\src{foundations Rem 5.6, updated by history Prop 2.4, examples Prop 2.9, context \S1.2}\label{rem:setting:conventions}
Lower bounds on $\LSFI(D_n)$, or for every order-free predictor, do not transfer automatically to $\LSFIbar$. In this paper they hold
\emph{in the semimeasure convention only} for: \cref{thm:setting:coding}(a) beyond single points; \cref{ex:setting:swap}; the $\mathrm{GF}(2^q)$ example (\cref{thm:hist:gf}); the separations of \cref{prop:hist:strict,prop:hist:strictinf}; the cyclic permutation over the full cycle (\cref{thm:ex:cycle}); Eisenstat's example (\cref{thm:ex:sandwich,thm:ex:expected}).
\emph{Also normalised:} the chain and the skipped strings, up to $O(n\log n)$ and $O(\ell\log\ell)$ bits (\cref{prop:hist:normchain}); the cyclic permutation at steps $t\le2^{(1-\delta)d}$, at $\delta d-O(\log d)$ bits per step (\cref{prop:ex:normalised}).
\emph{In both conventions:} the out-of-distribution lower bounds of \cref{sec:ood}, which bound the posterior mass that is defined and wrong.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Open question.}\label{q:setting:normorder} Is the normalised loss of $\MF$ order-dependent, and which semimeasure-only lower bounds hold for $\MFbar$? On two inputs the exact condition is the criterion $(\star)$ of \cref{prop:setting:twopoint}: the normaliser leaks order information exactly when whether one input is defined says something about another input's label. A failure of $(\star)$ for $\MF$ is expected but not proved.

\begin{remark}[Recommendation]\src{foundations \S5 ``Recommendation''}\label{rem:setting:recommend}
We call $\MF$ ``SFI'': it is the object H\"anni describes; it has a posterior over functions ($\Mw$ has none); it dominates every machine law with constant $2^{-K(\nu)}$; it is invariant (\cref{thm:setting:invariance}); its semimeasure-convention loss is order-independent. The variants $W$, $\KF$, $\Mprod$, $\Mpq$, $\Mw$ (\cref{sec:versions}) agree with it up to $K(\dom D)+O(1)$ bits, differ by unbounded amounts, and should be kept distinct.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Basic properties}\label{sec:setting:basic}

\begin{theorem}[Invariance]\status{proved}\src{foundations Thm 6.2}\label{thm:setting:invariance}
(a) If $\MF,M'_F$ are built from two universal prefix machines and two enumerations of function machines, $|\log\MF(D)-\log M'_F(D)|\le c$ for all $D$.
(b) For computable injections $\alpha:X\to X'$, $\beta:Y\to Y'$, with $D^{\alpha\beta}:=\{(\alpha(x),\beta(y)):(x,y)\in D\}$ and $M'_F$ SFI over $X',Y'$: $|\log\MF(D)-\log M'_F(D^{\alpha\beta})|\le K(\alpha,\beta)+O(1)$ for all $D$. So cumulative losses on original and re-encoded data differ by at most $K(\alpha,\beta)+O(1)$ bits, uniformly over data sets and reveal orders.
(c) The same holds for $W$, $\Mprod$ and $\Mw$ (\cref{def:versions:variants}).
\end{theorem}

This makes precise H\"anni's heading ``input encoding doesn't matter much'' \citep{hanni2026notes}. The constant is not small for complex encodings, which can change losses by up to that much (only the upper bound is proved); non-computable encodings are not covered.

\begin{theorem}[Coding-theorem bounds]\status{proved}\src{foundations Thm 6.3 (= history Lemma 1.1, examples Lemma 1.1, context Prop 5.4(1))}\label{thm:setting:coding}
For every data set $D$:
(a) $\LSFI(D)\ge K(D|_T\mid T)-O(1)$ for every $T\subseteq\dom D$; in particular $\MF(D)\le2^{-K(D\mid\dom D)+O(1)}$, and $\MF(D)\le2^{-K(y\mid x)+O(1)}$ for every $(x,y)\in D$;
(b) $\LSFI(D)\le\min\{\KF(D),\ \sum_{(x,y)\in D}K(y\mid x)\}+O(1)\le K(\dom D)+K(D\mid\dom D)+O(1)$, with $\KF$ the shortest consistent program (\cref{def:versions:variants});
(c) $\LSFI(\{(x,y)\})=K(y\mid x)\pm O(1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{example}[Swap data]\status{proved; computed}\src{foundations Ex 6.4; checks c9(c), r8}\label{ex:setting:swap}
For distinct $x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}^d$ let $D:=\{(x_1,x_2),(x_2,x_1)\}$. Then $K(D\mid\dom D)=O(1)$, but $\LSFI(D)\ge\max\{K(x_2\mid x_1),K(x_1\mid x_2)\}-O(1)$. Conversely $\LSFI(D)\le d+O(1)$ for every pair: the machine $F(x):=x\oplus\omega_{1:|x|}$ has complexity $O(1)$ and extends $D$ exactly when $\omega_{1:d}=x_1\oplus x_2$, with probability $2^{-d}$ (c9(c)). For every $x_1$ some $x_2$ has $K(x_2\mid x_1)\ge d$, and then $\LSFI(D)=d\pm O(1)$; also $\KF(D)\le d+2\log d+O(1)$.
\end{example}

This is \emph{not} a separation between SFI and history-aware prediction: a history-aware predictor shown $x_1$ first must predict $x_2$ from $x_1$ alone and pays $K(x_2\mid x_1)-O(1)$ bits. Such separations are \cref{thm:hist:chain,thm:hist:skipped,thm:ex:cycle}.

\paragraph{Open question.}\label{q:setting:twosided} A two-sided characterisation of $\LSFI(D)$ up to $O(1)$ by standard complexities, other than the tautology $\LSFI(D)=\min_\nu[K(\nu)-\log\nu(D)]\pm O(1)$ over machine laws $\nu$.

\begin{proposition}[Sequence induction is SFI on initial segments]\status{proved}\src{foundations Prop 7.1}\label{prop:setting:si}
Let $X=\mathbb N$, $\{0,1\}\subseteq Y$, and $M$ Solomonoff's universal monotone semimeasure \citep{zvonkin1970complexity}. For $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ put $D_x:=\{(i,x_{i+1}):i<n\}$. Then $2^{-O(1)}M(x)\le\MF(D_x)\le2^{O(1)}M(x)$.
\end{proposition}

So SFI generalises Solomonoff induction, and is in turn dominated by universal prediction that sees the history (\cref{lem:hist:chrono,cor:hist:H4}).

\begin{proposition}[Computability]\status{proved}\src{foundations Prop 7.3}\label{prop:setting:computability}
(a) $\MF(D)$ is lsc uniformly in $D$, and positive. (b) $\MF$ is not computable. (c) Neither $\MF(y\mid D,x)$ nor $\MFbar(y\mid D,x)$ is computable as a function of $(D,x,y)$. (d) Both are computable relative to the halting problem $\emptyset'$.
\end{proposition}

(c) is diagonal: a computable $f$ that always takes a label of conditional below $0.6$ would have $\MF(D_n)<0.6^n$. So SFI is an ideal reference, not an algorithm (computable versions: \cref{sec:comp}).

\paragraph{Open question.}\label{q:setting:level} The exact arithmetical level of the conditionals; for Solomonoff's $M$ see \citet{leike2015computability} (not checked against the source).
