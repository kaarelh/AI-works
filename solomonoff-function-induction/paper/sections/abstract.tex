\begin{abstract}
Solomonoff function induction (SFI) is Bayesian prediction of labels from input--output examples under a universal prior over random functions: a random program, reading random bits shared by all inputs, labels every input. A universal function machine exists; its law dominates the law of every function machine and does not depend, up to a constant factor, on the universal machine or on computable encodings. The shortest consistent program, the program mixture, the mixture of product laws, SFI and the universal weak semimeasure agree up to $K(\mathrm{dom}\,D)$ bits, but no two of the first three agree up to a constant: there is no coding theorem for function programs. On every consistent data sequence, SFI's cumulative log loss exceeds that of every computable random labeller by at most the labeller's complexity plus a constant; with i.i.d.\ inputs from any distribution and computable labels, the averaged predictor has risk $O(K/n)$. SFI cannot model fresh label noise on repeated inputs; a per-query mixture can. SFI cannot use the order of the data: on a random cyclic permutation revealed along its cycle it pays about $(d-\log_2e)2^d$ bits in the semimeasure convention, while Solomonoff induction on the interleaved sequence pays $O(1)$ in expectation. Hypotheses that see the list of previous inputs give the universal chronological semimeasure. A dominance theorem for computable reveal orders holds in its intended reading, though an earlier proof sketch fails. Further sections treat Sam Eisenstat's coin example, out-of-distribution behaviour, computable versions and neural-network Bayes. A companion paper treats steeper simplicity priors.
\end{abstract}
