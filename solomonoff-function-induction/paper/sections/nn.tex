\section{Neural-network Bayes and circuit SOI}\label{sec:nn}

Bayesian learning with a neural network can be viewed as a circuit version of SOI \citep{vaintrobhanni2026nnbayes}. This section states what is proved about this link, what the slides of \citet{vaintrobhanni2026nnbayes} claim, and what is open. An \emph{architecture} is a parameter space $\Theta$ with a prior $\varpi$ and a map $\theta\mapsto f_\theta:\{0,1\}^d\to Y$.

\begin{proposition}[NN Bayes is function-space Bayes]\label{prop:nn:functionspace}
\status{proved (trivial)}\src{context Prop 4.1}
If the likelihood depends on $\theta$ only through the values of $f_\theta$ on the data (a 0--1 likelihood, a margin rule, any label-noise model), the network's posterior predictive equals the function-space Bayes predictive with prior $P_A(f):=\varpi\{\theta:f_\theta=f\}$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof.} Group the parameter integral by the value of $f_\theta$. \qed

So NN Bayes is Bayesian prediction with the prior $P_A$ on functions, and the question is how $P_A$ compares with $2^{-K}$, with $\MF$, or with a circuit prior.

\begin{proposition}[Coding-theorem upper bound]\label{prop:nn:upper}
\status{proved (given the coding theorem)}\src{context Prop 4.2}
If $P_A$, as a semimeasure on truth tables, is lower-semicomputed by a program $a$ (encoding the architecture, the prior and $d$), then $P_A(f)\le2^{-K(f\mid a)+c}\le2^{-K(f)+K(a)+c'}$, with $c$ independent of the architecture.
\end{proposition}

This is the conditional coding theorem for the family indexed by $a$. $P_A$ is lsc for finite parameter sets with rational probabilities. We only sketch this for Gaussian priors on real weights with sign outputs: up to a null boundary, $\{\theta:f_\theta=f\}$ is an open semialgebraic set, approximable from below by certified rational boxes. The bound is an exact, uncomputable form of the simplicity-bias bound of \citet{dingle2018input}. It owes nothing to the networks: every prior lower-semicomputed by a short program satisfies it, and nearly saturates it on its own typical set (\cref{prop:nn:typical}), which can omit most simple functions.\footnote{A version of \cref{prop:nn:upper} with this observation, and the questions of comparing uniform and simplicity priors on circuits, of the Gaussian limits and of whether few mistakes imply a small circuit for NN Bayes, first appeared in the author's working notes \citep{hanni2026notes}.}

\begin{proposition}[No general lower bound]\label{prop:nn:nolower}
\status{proved}\src{context Prop 4.4}
(a) Let $F_d$ be the set of functions an architecture $A_d$ can compute, with $|F_d|<2^{2^d}$ and membership decidable given $d$ (any finite parameter set; any real-weight threshold architecture, by Tarski's decision procedure). The first truth table $f_d\notin F_d$ has $K(f_d)\le K(d)+c$ and $P_{A_d}(f_d)=0$. So no bound $P_A(f)\ge2^{-aK(f)-b}$ with $a,b$ independent of $d$ holds for such a family. (b) The parameter--function map alone implies no simplicity bias: a lookup-table architecture with uniform parameters gives every $f$ probability $2^{-2^d}$.
\end{proposition}

The slides make the point of (a) with functions that diagonalise against all small circuits. So the right comparison object for networks is \emph{circuit} SOI, not program SOI. The circuit analogue of a fixed architecture with random parameters is the uniform prior over circuits of one size; one half of its comparison with the circuit simplicity prior holds (\cref{def:comp:circuits} for $N_s$ and $\csize$):

\begin{proposition}[Uniform circuit priors contain the circuit simplicity prior]\label{prop:nn:uniform}
\status{proved}\src{context Prop 4.5}
Let $P_{{\rm unif},S}$ be uniform over circuits of size exactly $S$. For every $f$ with $\csize(f)<S$, $P_{{\rm unif},S}(f)\ge1/(N_{\csize(f)}\cdot16(d+1+S)^2)$. Hence Bayes with this prior makes at most $(\ln2)(\log N_{\csize(f)}+2\log(d+1+S)+4)=O(\csize(f)\log(\csize(f)+d)+\log S)$ Gibbs mistakes on every ordering.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof idea} (\cref{app:nn}). Pad a minimal circuit with arbitrary dead gates and a final gate copying its output. The other half of the comparison, that the uniform prior is not much \emph{larger} than the simplicity prior on functions without small circuits, is open (\cref{q:nn:bridges}(a)). A two-sided bound of this kind would transfer the mistake bounds of circuit Bayes to NN Bayes (\cref{rem:nn:bridge}).

\paragraph{The Gaussian-process limits.} In the infinite-width Gaussian limits (NNGP and NTK) learning reduces to kernel methods, and one may ask whether a network then behaves like circuit SOI or more like a polynomial fit. On the cube $\{\pm1\}^d$ with uniform measure, let $\chi_S(x)=\prod_{j\in S}x_j$ be the Walsh characters and, for a kernel $\mathcal K$, $(T_{\mathcal K}g)(x):=\Ex_{x'}\mathcal K(x,x')g(x')$.

\begin{theorem}[Walsh diagonalisation; dimension bound]\label{thm:nn:walsh}
\status{proved; (a) known, (b) standard; computed (context c2)}\src{context Thm 4.6}
(a) (cf.\ \citealp{yang2019fine}; also in Appendix~J of the slides.) If $\mathcal K(x,x')=\Psi(\langle x,x'\rangle/d)$, every $\chi_S$ is an eigenfunction of $T_{\mathcal K}$, with eigenvalue $\Lambda_{|S|}$ depending only on $|S|$. This covers the NNGP and NTK kernels of fully connected networks with i.i.d.\ Gaussian weights and biases, which are functions of $\langle x,x'\rangle$ on the cube.
(b) (The standard dimension argument; cf.\ \citealp{kamath2020approximate}.) If $\mathcal V$ is any space of functions on the cube of dimension at most $n$ (for instance $\mathrm{span}\{\mathcal K(x_i,\cdot)\}_{i\le n}$, which contains the GP posterior mean and the kernel ridge regression solution), then $\binom dk^{-1}\sum_{|S|=k}\min_{g\in\mathcal V}\|\chi_S-g\|^2\ge1-n/\binom dk$.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof idea} (\cref{app:nn}). (a) $\mathcal K(x,x')$ depends only on $x\odot x'$, so characters are eigenfunctions; permutation symmetry makes the eigenvalue depend on $|S|$ only. (b) The squared projections of all characters onto $\mathcal V$ sum to $\dim\mathcal V$.

\begin{corollary}[Kernels versus SOI on sparse parities]\label{cor:nn:separation}
\status{proved; computed (context c2)}\src{context Cor 4.7}
For $|S|=k$ and i.i.d.\ uniform inputs, SOI ($\nu\in\{W,\MF\}$) has cumulative posterior error at most $(\ln2)(K(\chi_S)+c)$ on every sequence of inputs, with $K(\chi_S)\le\log\binom dk+2\log(k+1)+2\log(d+1)+c$, and the squared error of its posterior-mean predictor averaged over the first $n$ steps is at most $4(\ln2)(K(\chi_S)+c)/n$. Every kernel method or GP posterior mean needs $n\ge(1-\varepsilon)\binom dk$ samples for average squared error at most $\varepsilon$ over random $k$-sparse targets, while SOI needs $O(k\log d/\varepsilon)$ samples for Ces\`aro-averaged squared error $\varepsilon$, and $O((k\log d+\log(1/\varepsilon))/\varepsilon)$ for the error at a single step (\cref{cor:learn:perstep}(a)).
\end{corollary}

The separation is in squared error of the real-valued predictor; we do not claim a 0--1 version in general. For one kernel family a 0--1 failure is exact:

\begin{remark}[Bias-free two-layer ReLU kernels and odd parities]\label{rem:nn:oddparity}
\status{proved; computed (context c2, c10, c12); known}\src{context Rem 4.8(a)--(c)}
(a) For the bias-free two-layer ReLU NNGP, $\Psi_1(\rho)=(\sqrt{1-\rho^2}+\rho(\pi-\arccos\rho))/\pi$, and for the NTK, $\Psi_1(\rho)+\rho\Psi_0(\rho)$ with $\Psi_0(\rho)=(\pi-\arccos\rho)/\pi$, the function $\Psi(\rho)-a\rho$ is even ($a=\frac12$, resp.\ $a=1$). Hence $\Lambda_{|S|}=0$ for every odd $|S|\ge3$, $\mathcal V:=\mathrm{span}\{\mathcal K(x_i,\cdot)\}\perp\chi_S$, and $\|\chi_S-\hat g\|^2=1+\|\hat g\|^2\ge1$ for every $n$: the kernel predictor never learns these parities. This is the Boolean-cube analogue of the finding of \citet{basri2019convergence} that a network without bias cannot represent or learn simple, low-frequency functions with odd frequencies (on the sphere). Biases or a third layer make the eigenvalues nonzero.
(b) Let $z\sim\mathrm{GP}(0,\mathcal K)$ for one of these kernels and $|S|\ge3$ odd. Then $\Ex_x[z(x)\chi_S(x)]$ is Gaussian with variance $\Lambda_{|S|}=0$, so it vanishes almost surely, while $\mathrm{sign}\,z=\chi_S$ would force it to equal $\Ex|z|>0$. So the GP prior, and every posterior obtained by conditioning on an event of positive probability (sign or margin likelihoods), gives probability exactly $0$ to the classifier $\chi_S$.
(c) For these two kernels and odd $|S|\ge3$, let $x_1,\dots,x_n$ be distinct training inputs with labels $\chi_S(x_i)$ and $U$ the set of the other $2^d-n$ points. Every $g\in\mathcal V$ has $\sum_{x\in U}g(x)\chi_S(x)=-\sum_{i\le n}g(x_i)\chi_S(x_i)$. For kernel ridge regression (the GP mean with noise variance $\beta>0$) the training sum is $y^\top G(G+\beta I)^{-1}y\ge0$, with $G$ the training Gram matrix, so the predictor is weakly anti-correlated with the target on the unseen points.
A direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) gives kernel ridge regression a sign accuracy on unseen points of $0.05$ to $0.07$ at $n=900$, $d=10$, far below chance; we do not prove this.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Claims of the slides]\label{rem:nn:slides}
\status{known; unverified (claimed in the slides); warm-up arithmetic re-checked (context c8)}\src{context \S4.5; slides 10--17, 29--37, App.\ A, F, J, K}
The slides of \citet{vaintrobhanni2026nnbayes} note that Bayesian learning and SGD are ``importantly different''. They state (i) an $O(C)$ sample bound for programs with prior $\propto3^{-|p|}$ ($C$ the length of a shortest program for $f$) and a relevance-based sample bound for countable classes; (ii) a $\mathrm{poly}(d,C)$ sample bound for MLPs of depth $L$ and any width $N\ge C:=C_L(f)$, the least width and weight bound of a depth-$L$ MLP implementing $f$, with Gaussian weights, for each width, with the prior scale small enough and outputs conditioned with margin $\frac12$; (iii) that the orderings by $C_L$ and by prior mass are ``scrambled only polynomially''; (iv) an in-distribution mistake bound and a circuit bound polynomial in $d$ and the number of mistakes; (v) a mean-field analogue \citep{vaintrob2026width}, while the NNGP ``does not have a good circuit prior''; (vi) that out of distribution the in-distribution guarantee ``is simply false''; an online mistake bound might still hold for NN Bayes (the slides give a heuristic argument and list it as open), while the analogous claim for simplest-consistent learning over circuits fails (their Appendix~K; \cref{thm:comp:ladder}). The slides give proofs or proof sketches for several of these claims; we use only what is proved here. Of these claims, this paper proves the Markov half of the relevance-based bound in (i) (\cref{cor:learn:perstep}(a)), the character argument of their Appendix~J (\cref{thm:nn:walsh}(a)) and the ladder of their Appendix~K (\cref{thm:comp:ladder}); we do not prove the other claims.
\end{remark}

\begin{question}\label{q:nn:bridges}
(a) Is the uniform prior over circuits of size $S$ also bounded above by the circuit simplicity prior, $P_{{\rm unif},S}(f)\le\mathrm{poly}(d,S)\,2^{\csize(f)}\,\Wcirc(f)$ (the converse of \cref{prop:nn:uniform})? (b) Is there a lower bound on the log prior of a function under NN Bayes at a fixed (not vanishing) prior scale? (c) Does the main theorem of the slides hold as stated, and do few mistakes imply a small circuit for NN Bayes? (d) What is the per-point test error of GP classification on odd parities (\cref{rem:nn:oddparity}(b))? (e) Does the sign accuracy of kernel ridge regression on unseen points fall below $\frac12$ (\cref{rem:nn:oddparity}(c))?
\end{question}
