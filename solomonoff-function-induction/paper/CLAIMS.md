> **BINDING NOTE (Hänni, 2026-10-12): do not call it "Wyeth's objection".** Absorption by a universal hypothesis under a steeper penalty is a standard point. It is known in algorithmic statistics, and Hänni's own note already makes it ("pathology of not specifying even the hypothesis in the seq prediction case"); he was aware of it before the LessWrong exchange. In visible text, call it **the absorption objection** or **the standard absorption point**. Do not name sections, theorems or remarks after Wyeth, and do not quote his comment. At most, one neutral footnote may record that the point was raised in the LessWrong thread. Internal LaTeX labels such as rem:absorb:wyeth may stay as they are (they are invisible), but their printed headings must not mention Wyeth.


Binding for the writers of `paper/`. Every theorem-like item of the paper is a row here, and every row is an item of the paper. Notation in the statements is that of `NOTATION.md` (tape measure $\Unif$, $d$ = input length, $N=2^d$, $n$ = number of pairs, $\Kpad$, $\Ktail$, $\MH$, $j$ = confidence exponent).

Statement column: one line, faithful to the source. Writers must not state more than the source proves. Where a row says "semimeasure convention", the paper's statement must say so.

**R3. "Give the function oracle access to the list of previous x (not y); then it becomes as good as SI."**

"As good as SI" holds against SI's joint loss on $\code(h_n)$ (`thm:hist:si`); against SI's conditional label loss the relation is the input-loss identity (`prop:hist:inputloss`); for unnormalised $M$ both directions fail conditional on the Lattimore–Hutter–Gavane phenomenon (`prop:hist:lhg`); the normalised case is open. The oracle must give the **list**: the set of previous inputs is not enough (`prop:hist:strict`, `prop:ex:cycset`). Literature: Hutter's chronological semimeasure is prior work in substance (unverified), `rem:related:new`.

**R4. Cyclic permutation ("very low loss under SI but high loss under SFI").**
Confirmed. SFI pays $\log((N-1)!)\approx(d-\log e)2^d$ over the cycle (semimeasure convention) and $\log(N-1)_{k-1}\pm O(K(k))$ after $k$ steps (`thm:ex:cycle`, `thm:ex:ksteps`); the cost is intrinsic to order-free prediction (`prop:ex:orderfree`); the normalised predictor pays $\ge\delta d-O(\log d)$ per step for $t\le2^{(1-\delta)d}$, open over the full cycle (`prop:ex:normalised`). SI with a prefix code pays $O(1)$ uniformly in $d$ (`prop:ex:cycsi`); \SFIhist{} pays $O(1)$ (`prop:ex:cychist`); \SFIset{} pays as much as SFI (`prop:ex:cycset`). **Corrected detail:** with raw undelimited blocks SI pays about $K(d)$ (`prop:ex:cycraw`); the delimiters matter.

**R5. Sam Eisenstat's example and Hänni's e-mail argument.**
In the semimeasure convention, $\Ex\LSFI=\frac{H(1/3)}2n\pm O(\log n)$ (`thm:ex:expected`), and almost surely $|\LSFI-UB_n|\le\log n+O(\log\log n)+c_b$ for all large $n$ (`thm:ex:sandwich`); pathwise, $UB_n$ itself fluctuates by $\Theta(\sqrt n)$ around $\frac{H(1/3)}2n$ (`prop:ex:asymp`); this is optimal among order-free predictors up to $O(\log n)$ (`prop:ex:law`). His e-mail claim is correct in the semimeasure convention, and his sketch is correct in substance with two repairs (`rem:ex:email`); for the normalised predictor it is open (`q:ex:normalised`). SI and \SFIhist{} have bounded expected loss, $\le3\vartheta+c$ with $\vartheta=0.7995$ (`thm:ex:eisensi`, `prop:ex:burnin`). The brief's rule for SI is exact after the last touching step and makes exactly 2 errors in expectation, none with probability exactly 1/2 (`cor:ex:rule`, `prop:ex:rulestats`). "Our threshold function" is made precise in `prop:ex:UB`.

**R6. The teacher's-mistakes trick via a stronger simplicity prior.**
Paper 2 (summarised in `sec:steep`). Confirmed for error rules that are incompressible relative to the model, with his numbers, over all product hypotheses (Paper 2 `thm:teacher:main`); **corrected:** refuted as a statement about every error rule with his density and description length (error envelopes, Paper 2 `prop:limits:envelope`); open in general; λ must grow linearly in $n$ (Paper 2 `prop:limits:plain`).

**R7. Preference for a simple sampler over a model with filled-in bits (the model–randomness split).**
Paper 2. At λ = 1 both get similar posterior in SFI and in SI, with explicit factors (Paper 2 `thm:split:lambda1`, `prop:split:si`), confirming his remark. For fixed λ > 1 with private randomness the sampler wins in a precise, limited sense (Paper 2 `thm:split:private`); with shared randomness or in SI the universal mixture wins and there is no split (Paper 2 `thm:split:shared`). Algorithmic-statistics reading: Paper 2 `prop:algstat:parity`.

**Later request. "Include an equivalence between the Kolmogorov complexity version and the universal semimeasure version, if they are equivalent; if not, explain."**
They are not equivalent (`sec:versions`). The five objects agree up to $K(\dom D)+O(1)$ bits (`thm:versions:chain`). No two of $\KF$, $W$, $\MF$ agree up to a constant: there is no coding theorem for function programs (`thm:versions:nocoding`); $\KF$ falls short of $\MF$ by $\log d\pm O(\log\log d)$ at worst on two points (`cor:versions:twopoint`); $W$ falls short of $\MF$ unboundedly but only by $\Kpad$ of the needed tape length (`prop:versions:Wgap`, `prop:versions:tape`); $\Mprod$ and $\MF$ differ unboundedly (`prop:versions:shared`); $\MF$ and $\Mw$ differ by up to linear amounts (`thm:setting:weakvsmachine`). Table: `tab:versions:compare`; meaning for prediction: `rem:versions:prediction`; open: `q:versions:open`, `q:versions:explicit`, `q:setting:rpf`.
