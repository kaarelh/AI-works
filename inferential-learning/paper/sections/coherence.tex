\section{Coherence: what contradictions fix}\label{sec:coherence}

Imitation cannot tell a systematic human error from a rule (\Cref{cor:imitation:systematic}). The user's second ingredient is a coherence loss: penalize having good arguments for both $P$ and $\neg P$. In our setting this is channel~(C) of \Cref{def:setting:channels}: a family $\Ac$ of \emph{designated contexts} is certified coherent, and a derivation of $\bot$ from one of them shows that one of its steps is invalid. This section determines what that signal fixes. In brief: coherence is negative data, but only for \emph{bold} learners, and it controls only one of two error types (\Cref{sec:coherence:negative}); it pins the target exactly when the target sits at the top of its coherent version space, as classical propositional logic does by Post-completeness, and it is blind for intuitionistic logic (\Cref{sec:coherence:post}); in arithmetic it is Popperian (\Cref{sec:coherence:arith}); Carnap's categoricity problem is Gold's problem in the dual space, and is solved by data with two denials, not by coherence (\Cref{sec:coherence:carnap}); coherence catches tonk but not non-conservativity (\Cref{sec:coherence:tonk}); what survives is the set of coherent uniform alternatives, the Kripkensteinian residue (\Cref{sec:coherence:fallacies}); and the use of coherence here is eliminative, which the coherentist impossibility theorems do not touch (\Cref{sec:coherence:eliminative}).

Most results are known in substance or trivial once set up. Post-completeness \citep{post1921,wojcicki1988theory}, Glivenko's theorem, Gold's limit-point argument, halving and weighted majority \citep{littlestone1988learning,littlestone1994weighted}, the multiple-conclusion answer to Carnap \citep{carnap1943formalization,shoesmith1978multiple}, Belnap's conservativity \citep{belnap1962tonk} and the $\Sigma_1/\Pi_1$ asymmetry of limiting inquiry \citep{putnam1965trial,gold1965limiting,kelly1996logic} are classical. New, as far as we know, are the reduction of contradictions to coalitions and the two-sided trade-off, the denial-rank trichotomy and twelve-datum tell-tale as learning results, the rule-level analysis of complete theories, and the systematic reading of all of these as statements about what a coherence loss can teach.

$\Cn_2$ is classical propositional consequence ($\varphi\in\Cn_2(X)$ iff every Boolean valuation making $X$ true makes $\varphi$ true), $\Taut=\Cn_2(\emptyset)$, and $\CFm(X)=\Fm$ is the trivial operator. Hypotheses are step sets (\Cref{def:setting:step}) or finitary consequence relations $h$, with $h(A)=\{\varphi:A\der_h\varphi\}$; $h$ is \emph{$\Ac$-coherent} if $A\nder_h\bot$ for all $A\in\Ac$, read as non-triviality $h(A)\neq\Fm$ in languages without $\bot$. Unless stated otherwise $\Ac$ is \emph{fixed and known}, independently of the target.

\subsection{Coherence as negative data}\label{sec:coherence:negative}

Here hypotheses are step sets $R$ in a countable class $\Hc$ with prior $w$, $\sum_Rw(R)=1$, and target $\Rstar$; every $A\in\Ac$ is target-coherent. An \emph{argument} $\pi$ from $A$ to $\bot$ is a derivation of $\bot$ from $A$; $\Steps(\pi)$ is its finite set of steps.

\begin{definition}[Online coherence protocol]\src{T2 \S2.2}\label{def:coherence:protocol}
In round $t$ the learner holds a version space $\VS_t\subseteq\Hc$ ($\VS_0=\Hc$) and announces an \emph{accepted set} $\hat R_t$, whose steps the reasoner may chain. The environment, possibly adversarial, possibly the learner's own proof search, then either \textup{(P)} presents some $s\in\Rstar$, and the learner deletes every $R\in\VS_t$ with $s\notin R$; or \textup{(N)} exhibits an argument $\pi$ from some $A\in\Ac$ to $\bot$ with $\Steps(\pi)\subseteq\hat R_t$, and the learner deletes every $R\in\VS_t$ with $\Steps(\pi)\subseteq R$; or does nothing. A (P)-round with $s\notin\hat R_t$ is an \emph{incompleteness error}; an (N)-round is a \emph{detection}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Negative bags; trivial]\src{T2 Lemma 2.1, as repaired}\label{lem:coherence:bag}
Let $\pi$ be an argument from a designated $A$ to $\bot$. \textup{(a)} $\Steps(\pi)\not\subseteq\Rstar$. \textup{(b)} Every $R\supseteq\Steps(\pi)$ has $\bot\in\Cl_R(A)$, witnessed by $\pi$. \textup{(c)} The designation alone excludes every $h$ with $A\der_h\bot$; deletion by the witness removes only $\{h:\Steps(\pi)\subseteq h\}$, which can be strictly smaller even for consequence relations (target $\Cn_2$, $A=\{p\}$, $\pi$ with steps $p\step q$, $q\step\bot$, and $h$ the least consequence relation containing $p\step\bot$). \hfill\leanok{negative\_bag}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Otherwise chaining gives $\bot\in\Cl_{\Rstar}(A)$. (b) Chain in $R$. (c) $h(X)=X\cup\{\bot\}$ if $p\in X$, else $h(X)=X$.
\end{proof}

So a contradiction is a multiple-instance label \citep{dietterich1997solving}: ``not all of these steps are valid''. For a learner that can test $A\step\bot\in h$ by membership it collapses to an ordinary negative example and $\pi$ is only a checkable witness; the bag structure matters for step verifiers that are not cut-closed, such as process reward models and ensembles.

\begin{proposition}[Caution receives no signal]\src{T2 Prop 2.9(a)--(c), as repaired}\label{prop:coherence:caution}
\textup{(a)} If $\hat R_t\subseteq\Rstar$, no detection can occur; since deletions never remove $\Rstar$, a \emph{certified-sound} learner ($\hat R_t\subseteq\bigcap\VS_t$) never sees one.
\textup{(b)} Certified soundness can be expensive. For $k\ge1$ and distinct atoms $a_i,p_i$ ($1\le i\le k$), $\ell^1_i=p_i$, $\ell^0_i=\neg p_i$, let $h_b=\{\Gamma\step\varphi:\{a_i\to\ell_i^{b_i}\}_{i\le k}\cup\Gamma\models\varphi\}$ for $b\in\{0,1\}^k$, and $\Ac=\{\emptyset\}$. Every certified-sound learner makes $2^k-1=|\Hc|-1$ incompleteness errors on some text.
\textup{(c)} If $\Ac$ is fixed and known and membership in and $\Ac$-coherence of each $h\in\Hc$ are decidable, coherence is prior knowledge (a restriction of $\Hc$), not data.
\end{proposition}

(a) and (c) are immediate; for (b), $\step\bigvee_i(a_i\to\ell_i^{1-c_i})$ lies in $h_b$ iff $b\neq c$, so presenting it while $h_c$ survives is an error that deletes only $h_c$ (Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:tradeoff}). So coherence feedback is a dividend paid on boldness, and with $\Ac$ fixed and coherence decidable it is not even data. It is an informative \emph{signal} when consistency is undecidable ($\Pi_1$; \Cref{thm:coherence:complexity}), so that a prover must find contradictions for the learner, and when designations are data about the target (\Cref{prop:coherence:informant}).

\paragraph{Oligarchic acceptance.} How should a bold learner accept, so that each contradiction teaches it something? Not by voting: stepwise majority over coherent verifiers can derive $\bot$ while implicating no member (\Cref{thm:search:doctrinal}).

\begin{definition}[Oligarchic halving]\src{T2 \S2.2}\label{def:coherence:oh}
OH chooses a \emph{coalition} $S_t\subseteq\VS_t$ with $w(S_t)\ge\frac12w(\VS_t)$ and announces $\hat R_t=\bigcap_{R\in S_t}R$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Oligarchic halving]\src{T2 Thm 2.2}\label{thm:coherence:halving}
Under OH with $w(\Rstar)>0$: \textup{(i)} $\Rstar$ is never deleted; \textup{(ii)} there are at most $\log_2(1/w(\Rstar))$ detections (at most $\log_2|\Hc|$ for the uniform prior), and the bound says nothing about incompleteness errors; \textup{(iii)} if $\Rstar\in S_t$, then $\Cl_{\hat R_t}(B)\subseteq\Cl_{\Rstar}(B)$ for all $B$; \textup{(iv)} always $\Cl_{\hat R_t}(B)\subseteq\Cl_R(B)$ for all $R\in S_t$, so chaining never leaves what each coalition member accepts. \hfill\leanok{thm\_2\_2} (finite classes)
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) (P)-data lie in $\Rstar$, and (N)-deletions spare $\Rstar$ by \Cref{lem:coherence:bag}(a). (ii) In a detection every $R\in S_t$ contains $\hat R_t\supseteq\Steps(\pi)$, so all of $S_t$ is deleted and $w(\VS_{t+1})\le\frac12w(\VS_t)$; (P)-rounds never add weight. So $w(\Rstar)\le2^{-D}$ after $D$ detections. (iii), (iv) $\hat R_t\subseteq R$ for $R\in S_t$.
\end{proof}

The halving argument is that of \citet{barzdin1972prediction} and \citet{littlestone1988learning}; new is only the reduction of a bag to a coalition. The choice of $S_t$ is the learner's style (``simplest half'' is MDL; ``boldest half'' is the boldest oligarchic choice). Oligarchy needs \emph{truth maintenance}: lemmas certified by an old coalition must be re-validated, or the union over coalitions reproduces the doctrinal paradox across time.

\begin{proposition}[Per-round halving forces oligarchy]\src{T2 Prop 2.3, as repaired}\label{prop:coherence:oligarchy}
Let $\VS$ be countable with $w(\VS)>0$, and assume every finite $P\subseteq\hat R$ with $P\not\subseteq\bigcap\VS$ (i.e.\ legal for some target in $\VS$) can occur as $\Steps(\pi)$. Then every possible detection deletes at least $\frac12w(\VS)$ iff $\hat R\subseteq\bigcap S$ for some $S\subseteq\VS$ with $w(S)\ge\frac12w(\VS)$. \hfill\leanok{prop\_2\_3} (finite version spaces)
\end{proposition}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:oligarchy}. The scope is per-round halving against a worst-case prover; coalitions of weight $\frac13$ still give $\log_{3/2}(1/w(\Rstar))$. Under universal domain, independence and unanimity, and for suitably rich agendas, aggregation rules yielding deductively closed collective judgments are oligarchic \citep{gardenfors2006representation,dietrich2008judgment}; \Cref{prop:coherence:oligarchy} is the learning-theoretic face of that result. Designations can be wrong (``air pressure is $0$'' plus full background knowledge is inconsistent), so we also need a robust version.

\begin{theorem}[Mis-designated contexts]\src{T2 Thm 2.5}\label{thm:coherence:robust}
On a detection multiply $w(R)$ by $\beta\in[0,1)$ for every $R\supseteq\Steps(\pi)$; choose $S_t$ with $w_t(S_t)\ge\frac12W_t$ ($W_t$ the total weight, $W_0=1$) and announce $\bigcap S_t$; positive data are noise-free. If $m$ of the $D$ detections are \emph{false alarms} (their context is target-incoherent), then
$D\le\bigl(\ln(1/w_0(\Rstar))+m\ln(1/\beta)\bigr)/\ln\bigl(2/(1+\beta)\bigr)$, with $0\cdot\ln(1/0):=0$. \hfill\leanok{thm\_2\_5}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each detection penalizes a superset of $S_t$, so $W_{t+1}\le\frac{1+\beta}2W_t$; (P)-rounds never raise $W_t$ and never delete $\Rstar$. $\Rstar$ is penalized only in false alarms, so $\beta^mw_0(\Rstar)\le w_T(\Rstar)\le(\frac{1+\beta}2)^D$.
\end{proof}

This is the bound of \citet{littlestone1994weighted}. A structural learner that trusts a false alarm fully fares worse: it must become sub-classical everywhere (\Cref{prop:physics:misdesignation}).

\begin{proposition}[Boldness is a two-sided trade-off]\src{T2 Prop 2.9(d),(e), added after verification}\label{prop:coherence:tradeoff}
\textup{(a)} On the class of \Cref{prop:coherence:caution}(b) with uniform prior, every learner with $\hat R_t\subseteq R$ for some $R\in\VS_t$ in every round, in particular every OH learner, makes $2^k-1$ incompleteness errors on some text for some target. The non-oligarchic union learner $\hat R_t=\bigcup\VS_t$ makes no error of either type there, and is silently unsound.
\textup{(b)} On the class of \Cref{thm:search:doctrinal}, no accepted set guarantees that every possible incompleteness error and every possible detection deletes half the weight.
\end{proposition}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:tradeoff}. \emph{Reading.} Oligarchy makes each contradiction worth a bit, robustly. It does not make boldness cheap: on this class every oligarchic learner, including the boldest, pays exactly the price of caution in missed valid steps, and a learner that avoids this price must at some round accept a set inside no surviving hypothesis, hence a target-invalid step. The two-sided minimax is open (\Cref{sec:open}); \Cref{sec:twotier} resolves the trade-off architecturally, with cautious assertion and a bold sandbox under attack.

\paragraph{What coherence cannot see.} Now let hypotheses be finitary consequence relations with target $\dstar$, let $h\oplus D$ be the least consequence relation containing $h\cup D$, and let the environment be complete. Call $h$ \emph{text-refutable} if ${\dstar}\not\subseteq h$, \emph{coherence-refutable} if it is $\Ac$-incoherent, and \emph{refutable by coherence with data} if $h\oplus D$ is $\Ac$-incoherent for some finite $D\subseteq{\dstar}$ (accepted human conclusions used as premises).

\begin{theorem}[Silent over-generalizations; trivial]\src{T2 Thm 2.6}\label{thm:coherence:silent}
\textup{(i)} $h$ is refutable by coherence with data iff $h\oplus{\dstar}$ is $\Ac$-incoherent. \textup{(ii)} The hypotheses refuted in none of the three ways are exactly $U(\dstar)=\{h:{\dstar}\subseteq h,\ h\ \Ac\text{-coherent}\}$. \textup{(iii)} Each $h\in U(\dstar)\setminus\{\dstar\}$ is unsound, and if it is a possible target, every finite stage of every data stream for $\dstar$ is one for $h$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) $h\oplus{\dstar}$ is the directed union of the $h\oplus D$, and a finite derivation of $\bot$ lies in one of them. (ii) If ${\dstar}\not\subseteq h$, $h$ is text-refutable; otherwise $h\oplus D=h$ for all $D\subseteq{\dstar}$. (iii) $h\supsetneq{\dstar}$ accepts an invalid sequent; data for $\dstar$ are data for $h$, and $\Ac$ is the same.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Coherent chains have coherent unions]\src{T2 Prop 2.7}\label{prop:coherence:chains}
The union of a chain of $\Ac$-coherent finitary consequence relations is one: cut and $A\step\bot$ involve finitely many sequents, which lie in one member.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed designations do not remove limit points]\src{T2 Cor 2.8, as repaired}\label{cor:coherence:limitpoints}
Let $\Ac$ be fixed and known independently of the target. If the possible targets include a strictly increasing chain $h_1\subsetneq h_2\subsetneq\cdots$ of $\Ac$-coherent hypotheses together with $h_\omega=\bigcup_nh_n$, no learner, computable or not, identifies them in the limit (EX or BC) from text plus coherence information.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The coherence information is target-independent, so fold it into the learner. A learner for $h_\omega$ has a locking sequence $\sigma$ \citep{blum1975toward}, or its BC analogue; its finite content lies in some $h_k$, and on a text for $h_k$ extending $\sigma$ the learner conjectures $h_\omega$ forever.
\end{proof}

This is Gold's limit-point theorem \citep{gold1967language}. The hypothesis ``$\Ac$ fixed'' is essential: designations that are data about the target, as when a problem setter certifies a setup consistent with the true theory, are genuine negative data.

\begin{proposition}[Target-dependent designations are negative data; trivial]\src{T2 Prop 2.8$'$, repair}\label{prop:coherence:informant}
Suppose the environment designates only target-coherent finite contexts, and eventually all of them. If every hypothesis satisfies classical reductio ($\Gamma\der_h\varphi$ iff $\Gamma\cup\{\neg\varphi\}\der_h\bot$), text plus designations is an informant, and every countable such class is EX-identifiable by enumeration. Example: for $h_n=\Cn_2\{\neg p_i:i<n\}$ and $h_\omega=\Cn_2\{\neg p_i:i\in\omega\}$, $\{p_j\}$ is $h_n$-coherent iff $j\ge n$, so the limit point is identified.
\end{proposition}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:informant}. So with fixed designations coherence prunes only over-generalizations that are not limit points, and positive-data structure (\Cref{sec:imitation}) decides the rest. Text plus coherence \emph{refutes} every wrong hypothesis exactly when $U(\dstar)\cap\Hc=\{\dstar\}$ (a condition for refutation, not identifiability: $\{\dstar,h\}$ with $h\supsetneq{\dstar}$ is identifiable from text). \emph{Reading:} a coherence loss removes over-acceptance that \emph{conflicts}; the silent over-generalizations $U(\dstar)$ are the precise content of ``coherent but wrong'', and whether they exist depends on the target, not on the learner.

\subsection{Post-completeness: when coherence suffices}\label{sec:coherence:post}

A consequence operator $C$ is \emph{structural} if $\sigma C(X)\subseteq C(\sigma X)$ for every substitution $\sigma$: rules are uniform schemas, so a learner that generalizes steps to schemas (\Cref{sec:setting:schemas}) is a structural learner.

\begin{theorem}[Structural Post-completeness; known]\src{T2 Thm 3.1}\label{thm:coherence:post}
If $\Fm$ contains $\neg$ and one of $\wedge,\vee,\to$, all interpreted by their truth functions, every structural closure operator $C\supseteq\Cn_2$, finitary or not, is $\Cn_2$ or $\CFm$. The same holds for pure $\{\to\}$. \hfill\leanok{Post.eq\_Cn2\_or\_eq\_trivial}\ \leanok{Post.impFrag\_postComplete}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (main case)]
Let $\varphi\in C(X)\setminus\Cn_2(X)$, with $v[X]=1$, $v(\varphi)=0$. Fix a tautology $\top_0$ in one atom and $\bot_0=\neg\top_0$, and let $\sigma_v(p)=\top_0$ or $\bot_0$ as $v(p)=1$ or $0$. Every Boolean $u$ has $u(\sigma_v\psi)=v(\psi)$, so $\sigma_vX\subseteq\Taut\subseteq C(\emptyset)$ and $\sigma_v\varphi$ is a contradiction. By structurality $\sigma_v\varphi\in C(\sigma_vX)\subseteq C(C(\emptyset))=C(\emptyset)$, hence $\Fm=\Cn_2(\{\sigma_v\varphi\})\subseteq C(\emptyset)$. For $\{\to\}$ see Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:post}.
\end{proof}

At the level of theorems this is \citet{post1921}; the consequence-level form is standard too \citep{pogorzelski1971structural,wojcicki1988theory,pogorzelski2008completeness}, and \Cref{prop:search:post} is the same substitution trick for step sets. One falsifying row of one bad rule yields a derivation of $\bot$ from no premises. Without a theorem in the language there is one more extension: in the $\{\wedge,\vee\}$ fragment ($\Cn_2(\emptyset)=\emptyset$) let $\Cai(\emptyset)=\emptyset$ and $\Cai(X)=\Fm$ for $X\neq\emptyset$ (\emph{almost inconsistent}).

\begin{proposition}[The theorem-free fragment; presumably folklore]\src{T2 Prop 3.2}\label{prop:coherence:almost}
In the $\{\wedge,\vee\}$ language the structural closure operators extending $\Cn_2$ are exactly $\Cn_2$, $\Cai$ and $\CFm$.
\end{proposition}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:post}. So there coherence must be enforced on a non-empty designated context. For sequents $D$ let $\langle D\rangle$ be the least structural consequence relation containing $D$, and for a designated $\Cn_2$-consistent $A_0$ let $\mathcal S(D,A_0)=\{h\text{ structural}:D\subseteq h,\ A_0\nder_h\bot\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Coherence pins classical logic]\src{T2 Thm 3.3, as repaired}\label{thm:coherence:cpc}
Let the language be as in \Cref{thm:coherence:post}.
\textup{(a)} For $D\subseteq\Cn_2$: $\mathcal S(D,A_0)=\{\Cn_2\}$ iff $\langle D\rangle=\Cn_2$, and a finite $D$ suffices (with $\to$ in the language, Hilbert-style bases with modus ponens as the only rule; without $\to$, by \citealt{rautenberg1981two}; Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:post}).
\textup{(b)} Let $\Hc=(h_i)$ be any uniformly decidable family of structural consequence relations with $\Cn_2\in\Hc$. The learner outputting the least $i$ such that $h_i$ contains the data so far and $A_0\nder_{h_i}\bot$ identifies $\Cn_2$ in the limit from every text, with at most $i^*$ mind changes ($i^*$ the least index of $\Cn_2$), for \emph{every} ordering of $\Hc$: no simplicity or minimality bias is needed.
\textup{(c)} Each assumption is needed: $\Cn_2+\{\step p\}$ ($p$ not in $A_0$) is non-structural, $A_0$-coherent and contains every text; $\CFm$ contains every text; in the theorem-free $\{\wedge,\vee\}$ fragment, $\Cai$ survives the empty context. \hfill\leanok{Post.versionSpace\_eq\_singleton\_iff}\ \leanok{Post.learner\_identifies}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
(a) Members of $\mathcal S$ extend $\langle D\rangle=\Cn_2$, so are $\Cn_2$ or $\CFm$, and $A_0$ excludes $\CFm$. (b) For $j<i^*$, either the text refutes $h_j$, or $h_j\supsetneq\Cn_2$ is $\CFm$ and $A_0$ rejects it. Details in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:post}.
\end{proof}

The form of the basis matters: if the $\wedge$ clauses are sequent rules ($p\wedge q\step p$, \dots) while modus ponens is the only rule for $\to$, then $\step p\to(q\to p\wedge q)$ is underivable (countermodel in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:post}); giving them as $\to$-axioms repairs this.

\paragraph{Reading.} This is a setup of the user's shape that provably works for formal propositional mathematics. The algorithm is trivial; the content is \Cref{thm:coherence:post}, whose proof explains why: an invalid rule has a falsifying row, and substituting $\top/\bot$ along it turns the rule into ``from tautologies infer a contradiction''. A uniform learner cannot hide an invalid schema from coherence. It must generalize schematically, though: closure under \emph{renaming} atoms is not enough, since the least renaming-invariant extension of $\Cn_2$ containing $p\step q$ is $\emptyset$-coherent with theorems $\Taut$ (\src{L5 Prop 2.5}; Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:post}). In Experiment~B (\Cref{sec:experiments}) a bold learner over classical natural deduction accepted all 13 valid and refuted all 44 invalid structural candidate schemas, each by an explicit derivation of $\vdash\bot$ (median $2.5$ steps for generalizations of target rules), while the non-structural $\vdash p$ survived all eight designated contexts \status{computed; quick run}.

\begin{proposition}[Theorems versus consequence]\src{T2 Props 3.4, 3.5, as repaired}\label{prop:coherence:adm}
For a substitution-closed theorem set $\mathrm{Th}$ let $\Cadm(X)=\{\varphi:\forall\sigma(\sigma X\subseteq\mathrm{Th}\Rightarrow\sigma\varphi\in\mathrm{Th})\}$.
\textup{(a)} $\Cadm$ is the largest structural closure operator with theorems $\mathrm{Th}$; on finite premise sets it is the structural completion (admissible rules).
\textup{(b)} For $\mathrm{Th}=\Taut$ in a language as in \Cref{thm:coherence:post}, $\Cadm=\Cn_2$ (structural completeness); in the $\{\wedge,\vee\}$ fragment $\Cadm=\Cai$.
\textup{(c)} For $\IPC$, $\Cadm\supsetneq{\der_{\IPC}}$ (Harrop's rule $\neg p\to q\vee r\,/\,(\neg p\to q)\vee(\neg p\to r)$ is admissible, not derivable; \citealt{harrop1960concerning,rybakov1997admissibility,iemhoff2001admissible}), and the two have the same theorems and the same coherence profile, $A\der^{\IPC}_{\mathrm{adm}}\bot$ iff $A\der_{\CPC}\bot$. \hfill\leanok{Post.le\_Cn2\_of\_empty\_eq\_Taut}
\end{proposition}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:post}. So a learner can learn the theorems and then take the structural completion; a learner that is not bold here may settle on $X\mapsto X\cup\Taut$, which has the right theorems but cannot chain inferences from premises. And no learner fed theorems and coherence data can tell $\IPC$-derivability from $\IPC$-admissibility.

\begin{theorem}[Intuitionistic logic: coherence is blind]\src{T2 Thm 3.6}\label{thm:coherence:ipc}
\textup{(a)} For every intermediate logic $L$ and finite $A$: $A\der_L\bot$ iff $A\der_{\CPC}\bot$.
\textup{(b)} A learner outputting a maximal coherent structural hypothesis never outputs $\IPC$.
\textup{(c)} There is a strictly increasing chain of finitely axiomatizable intermediate logics $L_1\subsetneq L_2\subsetneq\cdots$ whose union $L_\omega$ is intermediate, and no learner identifies $\{L_k:k\le\omega\}$ from text plus coherence information, for any $\Ac$, even with target-dependent designations.
\textup{(d)} The conservative learner outputting $\langle D_n\rangle$ BC-identifies every finitely axiomatizable structural target.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} (a) is Glivenko's theorem \citep{glivenko1929quelques}. (c) uses Jankov formulas of an infinite antichain of finite Heyting algebras \citep{jankov1968construction,chagrov1997modal}; by (a) all designations are the same for every member, so \Cref{cor:coherence:limitpoints} applies. Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:ipc}.

So for $\IPC$ the work is done by \emph{minimality} (\Cref{sec:imitation}), not coherence. Minimality is right when the target is at the bottom of its coherent version space, coherence plus boldness when it is at the top; classical logic is at both ends. In Experiment~B a bold learner over intuitionistic natural deduction accepted excluded middle, double-negation elimination, reductio, Peirce's law, weak excluded middle and Dummett's linearity, all $\IPC$-underivable \status{computed; quick run}.

\paragraph{Complete theories.} Much of the algebra in a physics calculation is real-closed-field reasoning, and $\RCF$ is complete. Is it pinned like $\CPC$?

\begin{proposition}[Complete theories: theorems, and rules under $\exists$-closure]\src{T2 Prop 3.11, repaired}\label{prop:coherence:complete}
Let $T$ be a complete consistent first-order theory.
\textup{(a)} The only consistent closed theory in the language of $T$ containing $T$ is $T$; if $T$ is decidable, the learner of \Cref{thm:coherence:cpc}(b), run on a uniformly decidable family of theories containing $T$, identifies $T$ from a text and the coherence datum $\emptyset$.
\textup{(b)} Add parameters (fresh constants); the target is $\der_T$ ($\Gamma\der_T\varphi$ iff $T\cup\Gamma\vdash\varphi$). If $h\supseteq{\der_T}$ is closed under substituting terms for parameters and under $\exists$-elimination (from $\Gamma\der_h\exists x\psi$ and $\Gamma,\psi(d)\der_h\chi$, $d$ fresh, infer $\Gamma\der_h\chi$), and $\emptyset\nder_h\bot$, then $h={\der_T}$.
\textup{(c)} Without $\exists$-closure (b) fails, even for a non-empty context. For $T=\RCF$, the least parameter-structural $h_2\supseteq{\der_{\RCF}}$ containing all instances of $t^3-4t+1=0\step t>1$ has $h_2(\emptyset)=\Cn_{\RCF}(\emptyset)$ and $h_2(\{c^3-4c+1=0\})=\Cn_{\RCF}(\{c^3-4c+1=0,\ c>1\})$: coherent on both, yet unsound (the cubic has three real roots and Galois group $S_3$). The context $\{c^3-4c+1=0,\ c<0\}$ catches it.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} (b) An invalid $\Gamma(\bar c)\der_h\varphi(\bar c)$ gives $T\vdash\exists\bar x(\bigwedge\Gamma\wedge\neg\varphi)$; a fresh instance derives $\bot$ in $h$, and $\exists$-elimination discharges the parameters one at a time. (c) The rule fires only on terms the context forces to be roots, and $\mathbb Q(r)$ contains no other root. Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:complete}, which also gives a simpler example ($t\cdot t=2\step t>0$) caught by $\{c\cdot c=2\}$.

\emph{Reading.} At the level of theorems, $\RCF$, Presburger arithmetic and Tarski's geometry are pinned as cleanly as $\CPC$. At the level the user cares about, rules applied in contexts with parameters, a step verifier that is merely a set of uniform rules is \emph{not} pinned by one coherence datum; it is pinned if its hypotheses are closed under $\exists$-elimination, a discharge meta-rule rather than a step, or if the designated contexts are rich enough. (Whether a fixed finite family of contexts suffices for $\RCF$ is open. With $\sin$ on $\R$, which defines $\mathbb Z$, incompleteness enters; for $\exp$ the question is open: $\mathrm{Th}(\R,\exp)$ is model complete \citep{wilkie1996model}, and decidable if Schanuel's conjecture holds \citep{macintyre1996decidability}.) Designation is soundness-critical in the other direction too: designating an idealization together with the background it contradicts forces every structural coherent learner to lose a classical schema \emph{everywhere} (\Cref{prop:physics:misdesignation}), which is the formal content of the user's air-pressure worry and the subject of \Cref{sec:physics}.

\subsection{Arithmetic: coherence is Popperian}\label{sec:coherence:arith}

Hypotheses are r.e.\ theories $T\supseteq\PA$, world feedback is a $\Delta_0$-oracle, and $\N$ is the intended model; $\RobQ$ is Robinson arithmetic.

\begin{lemma}[$\Delta_0$ feedback is subsumed by coherence; standard]\src{T2 Lemma 3.7}\label{lem:coherence:delta0}
A consistent $T\supseteq\RobQ$ proves every true $\Delta_0$ sentence and no false one, so no $\Delta_0$ feedback refutes it.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\RobQ$ is $\Sigma_1$-complete \citep{boolos2007computability}; if $T$ proved a false $\delta$ it would also prove $\neg\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No maximal r.e.\ hypothesis; cited]\src{T2 Thm 3.8, as repaired}\label{thm:coherence:rosser}
Every consistent r.e.\ $T\supseteq\RobQ$ has a $\rho$ with $T+\rho$ and $T+\neg\rho$ consistent \citep{godel1931formal,rosser1936extensions,tarski1953undecidable}.
\end{theorem}

So a bold learner with r.e.\ hypotheses and $\Ac=\{\emptyset\}$ has no maximal choice. (If $\Ac$ contains all finite sets of true sentences, $T$ is $\Ac$-coherent iff $T\subseteq\Th(\N)$, and at most one refinement survives.)

\begin{theorem}[Turing-chain impossibility]\src{T2 Thm 3.9, as repaired}\label{thm:coherence:turing}
Let $T_0=\PA$, $T_{k+1}=T_k+\Con(T_k)$, $T_\omega=\bigcup_kT_k$ \citep{turing1939systems,feferman1962transfinite}, and let $\Ac$ be any fixed family of finite sets of \emph{true} sentences. No learner identifies a class containing $\{T_k:k\le\omega\}$ from text, $\Ac$-coherence and a $\Delta_0$-oracle.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All $T_k$ are sound, so all side information is target-independent; the chain is strict by G\"odel~II; apply \Cref{cor:coherence:limitpoints}.
\end{proof}

The $\Sigma_1$-unsound $T_k+\neg\Con(T_k)$ are natural \emph{hypotheses}: consistent, unrefuted by $\Delta_0$ data, and refuted by coherence only relative to accepted premises proving $\Con(T_k)$. Restricting designations to true contexts is essential: if $\{\neg\Con(T_k)\}$ could be designated as data about the target, the chain would be identifiable (\Cref{prop:coherence:informant}).

\begin{theorem}[Coherence as a $\Pi_1$-truth detector; the $\Sigma_2$ barrier]\src{T2 Thm 3.10, as repaired}\label{thm:coherence:popper}
\textup{(a)} For $\Pi_1$ $\pi$, $\PA+\pi$ is consistent iff $\pi$ is true. \textup{(b)} For $\Sigma_1$ $\sigma$, truth implies consistency of $\PA+\sigma$, not conversely ($\sigma=\neg\Con(\PA)$).
\textup{(c)} The \emph{Popperian} learner (``true'' on a $\Pi_1$ sentence until $\PA$ refutes it; ``false'' on a $\Sigma_1$ sentence until $\PA$ proves it) converges to the truth on $\Sigma_1\cup\Pi_1$ with at most one mind change per sentence.
\textup{(d)} The \emph{naively bold} learner (accept $\varphi_n$ iff consistent with $\PA$ plus the earlier accepted sentences) converges to a Lindenbaum completion that depends on the enumeration, and on every enumeration beginning with $\neg\Con(\PA)$ it is $\Sigma_1$-unsound.
\textup{(e)} Every computable learner using a computable text, coherence searches and a $\Delta_0$-oracle fails to converge to the truth value of some $\Sigma_2$ sentence.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} (a) A false $\Pi_1$ sentence has a true, hence $\PA$-provable, $\Sigma_1$ negation. (e) Convergent computable guesses define a $\Delta_2$ set \citep{shoenfield1959degrees}, and the true $\Sigma_2$ sentences are $\Sigma_2$-complete. Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:popper}.

This is Popper's asymmetry in its standard learning-theoretic form \citep{putnam1965trial,gold1965limiting,kelly1996logic}, read for coherence-learners. The same boundary appears for credences, in a self-contained form of a theorem of \citet{sawin2013computable}. Quantifier blocks may be empty, so $\Pi_1$ sentences also count as $\Pi_2$ and $\Sigma_2$.

\begin{theorem}[The coherence/$\Pi_1$/$\Pi_2$ trilemma]\src{L3 Thm D, as repaired}\label{thm:coherence:trilemma}
Let $P_t(\varphi)\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]$ be computable uniformly in $t,\varphi$, with \textup{(i)} $\lim_tP_t(\pi)=1$ for every true $\Pi_1$ $\pi$, and \textup{(ii)} $\limsup_t(P_t(\pi)+P_t(\varphi))\le1$ whenever $\pi$ is $\Pi_1$ and $\PA\vdash\pi\to\neg\varphi$. Then infinitely many true $\Pi_2$ sentences have $\liminf_tP_t(\varphi)=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A false $\Pi_2$ $\varphi=\forall x\exists y R$ has a true $\Pi_1$ refuter $\forall y\neg R(\bar x_0,y)$, so $\limsup_tP_t(\varphi)=0$ by (i), (ii). Thus $\{\varphi\in\Pi_2:\liminf_tP_t(\varphi)>0\}$, a $\Sigma_2$ set, contains no false $\Pi_2$ sentence; if it contained all but finitely many true ones, the $\Pi_2$-complete set of true $\Pi_2$ sentences would be $\Sigma_2$, contradicting Post's hierarchy theorem.
\end{proof}

So one can have at most three of: computability, weak coherence, $\Pi_1$-convergence, and non-dogmatism on true $\Pi_2$ sentences; logical induction drops $\Pi_1$-convergence. Coherence does \emph{not}, however, confine credences to $\Delta_2$:

\begin{proposition}[What weak coherence costs]\src{L3 Thm D$'$, as repaired}\label{prop:coherence:costs}
Say computable credences \emph{gradually verify} $C$ if $P_t(\varphi)\to1$ iff $\varphi$ is true, for $\varphi\in C$. \textup{(a)} Convergent credences, coherent or not, have a $\Pi_2$ success set, so never gradually verify the $\Sigma_2$ sentences. \textup{(b)} Credences as in \Cref{thm:coherence:trilemma}(ii) never gradually verify all $\Pi_2$ sentences. \textup{(c)} Some computable credences, weakly coherent for all pairs, gradually verify the $\Sigma_2$ sentences (necessarily without converging on all of them).
\end{proposition}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:costs}. Incoherent computable credences can gradually verify the $\Pi_2$ sentences convergently (L3 Thm~G(f), a standard construction given there as a sketch); so among convergent credences, and among the classes $\Sigma_1,\Pi_1,B(\Sigma_1),\Sigma_2,\Pi_2,\Pi_3$ of that success table, weak coherence costs exactly the $\Pi_2$ class, and part of what incoherent credences reach beyond that is paid for by giving up convergence, not coherence.

\paragraph{Reading.} In arithmetic, computation adds nothing beyond coherence with a $\Sigma_1$-complete base. What matters is how boldness is applied: boldly on universal claims, which coherence can refute, cautiously on existential ones, which need a witness. Beyond $\Delta_2$ no computable channel decides truth in the limit (\Cref{thm:coherence:popper}(e)); only the weaker gradual verification, with credence tending to $1$ exactly on truths, reaches further (\Cref{prop:coherence:costs}). The G\"odel--Rosser alternatives are a permanent residue, removed only by accepting stronger principles (reflection, $\Con(\PA)$, a stronger theory): a justification that is not a $\PA$-proof (\Cref{sec:philosophy}).

\subsection{Carnap's problem is Gold's problem}\label{sec:coherence:carnap}

Under inferentialism, learning valid inferences should be learning meanings. \citet{carnap1943formalization} observed that the classical rules do not fix the truth tables: the all-true valuation, and ``true iff tautologous'', respect every classical inference.

\begin{definition}[Meanings]\src{T2 \S4.1}\label{def:coherence:valuations}
A \emph{valuation} is any $v:\Fm\to\{0,1\}$, identified with its true-set in Cantor space $2^{\Fm}$; a \emph{meaning} is a set $V$ of valuations; $\BV$ is the set of Boolean ones. For finite $\Gamma,\Delta$, $\Gamma\models^1_V\varphi$ iff every $v\in V$ with $v[\Gamma]=1$ has $v(\varphi)=1$, and $\Gamma\models^{\mathrm m}_V\Delta$ iff no $v\in V$ has $v[\Gamma]=1$, $v[\Delta]=0$. $\Val(S)$ is the set of valuations satisfying the sequents $S$; $V^\cap$ closes $V$ under intersections of true-sets, including the empty one $\valtop\equiv1$; $\valtaut$ is the characteristic function of $\Taut$. $V$ is \emph{structural} if $v\in V$ implies $v\circ\sigma\in V$ for all substitutions $\sigma$.
\end{definition}

The sequent $\Gamma\step\Delta$ excludes exactly the clopen set of valuations realizing the position $\pos{\Gamma}{\Delta}$. So \emph{inference data are negative data about valuations}, while \emph{coherence certifications} (``$\pos AD$ is in bounds'') and world feedback are \emph{positive} data: Restall's reading of consequence \citep{restall2005multiple} turned into a statement about data.

\begin{lemma}[Duality]\src{T2 Lemma 4.1}\label{lem:coherence:duality}
\textup{(a)} $\Val(\models^{\mathrm m}_V)=\overline V$. \textup{(b)} For closed $V$, $C_V(X)=\bigcap\{v\in V:X\subseteq v\}$ is finitary and the valuations satisfying every valid single-conclusion sequent form $V^\cap$. \textup{(c)} $\BV^\cap$ is the set of characteristic functions of classical theories, including $\Fm$. \hfill\leanok{Val\_mrel}\ \leanok{lemma\_4\_1\_b}\ \leanok{intClosure\_BV\_eq}
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Carnap's problem in learning form]\src{T2 Thm 4.2, as repaired}\label{thm:coherence:carnap}
${\models^1_{\BV}}={\models^1_W}$ whenever $\BV\subseteq W\subseteq\BV^\cap$. So no data that are a function of the single-conclusion relation (text, informant, or metarules with discharge read \emph{globally}, as closure conditions) distinguish $\BV$ from $W$. The non-Boolean members of $\BV^\cap$ are the $v_T$ for theories $T$ that are not maximal consistent (including $T=\Fm$): $\valtop$ violates the table for $\neg$; $\valtaut$ and every non-maximal consistent $v_T$ violate those for $\neg,\vee,\to$; all respect $\wedge$. $\BV^\cap$ is closed and structural. \hfill\leanok{carnap\_single}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:coherence:duality}(b) every $w\in W$ satisfies $\models^1_{\BV}$, and $W\supseteq\BV$ gives the converse. For consistent non-maximal $T$, some $\varphi$ has $\varphi,\neg\varphi\notin T\ni\varphi\vee\neg\varphi$. Lemma~\ref{lem:coherence:duality} is proved in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:carnap}.
\end{proof}

Under the \emph{local} reading of rule validity \citep{garson2013what}, $\to$I does exclude $\valtaut$, but only by imposing the two-conclusion sequent $\step\varphi,\varphi\to\psi$. The right measure is the number of denials.

\begin{theorem}[Denial-rank trichotomy]\src{T2 Thm 4.3}\label{thm:coherence:rank}
With $\neg$ in the language, let the \emph{denial rank} $d(v)$ be the least $|\Delta|$ over classically valid $\Gamma\step\Delta$ (finite) that $v$ violates ($\infty$ if none). Then $d(v)=0$ iff $v$'s true-set is inconsistent; $d(v)=1$ iff it is consistent but not closed; $d(v)=2$ iff it is a consistent, closed, non-maximal theory; $d(v)=\infty$ iff $v\in\BV$. \hfill\leanok{denialRank\_eq\_two\_iff}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$d=0$ is a finite inconsistent subset; for consistent $v$, $d\le1$ says some $\Gamma\subseteq v$ entails some $\varphi\notin v$; a consistent closed non-maximal $T$ omits $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$, violating $\step\varphi,\neg\varphi$; maximal theories are Boolean.
\end{proof}

Neither removes $\valtaut$. These are van Fraassen's supervaluations \citep{vanfraassen1966singular}, the honest semantics of ``settled by the premises''. Pinning classical meaning needs a loss with \emph{two} denials. With rank-2 data finitely many data suffice. Let $\TTs$ be the eleven truth-table schemata: $\varphi,\neg\varphi\step$ and $\step\varphi,\neg\varphi$; $\varphi\wedge\psi\step\varphi$, $\varphi\wedge\psi\step\psi$, $\varphi,\psi\step\varphi\wedge\psi$; $\varphi\step\varphi\vee\psi$, $\psi\step\varphi\vee\psi$, $\varphi\vee\psi\step\varphi,\psi$; $\step\varphi,\varphi\to\psi$, $\psi\step\varphi\to\psi$, $\varphi,\varphi\to\psi\step\psi$. $\TTs(p,q)$ are their instances in distinct atoms.

\begin{theorem}[Bilateral determinacy with a twelve-datum tell-tale]\src{T2 Thm 4.4, as repaired}\label{thm:coherence:telltale}
Let the language be $\{\neg,\wedge,\vee,\to\}$ (with $\bot,\top$ add $\bot\step$ and $\step\top$; the Lean version has $13+1$ data).
\textup{(a)} $\Val(\TTs)=\BV$, which is closed.
\textup{(b)} If $V$ is structural, every $v\in V$ satisfies the eleven sequents $\TTs(p,q)$, and $V\neq\emptyset$ (one coherence datum), then $V=\BV$.
\textup{(c)} So among structural meanings $\BV$ is identified finitely by twelve data; without the coherence datum $V=\emptyset$ survives, the Gold over-generalization.
\textup{(d)} Without structurality $\BV$ is not identifiable in the limit at all, even with the coherence datum: every finite set of valid sequents is valid in a closed meaning $\BV\cup\{v'_\chi\}$, $v'_\chi$ a Boolean valuation flipped at a formula $\chi$ the set does not mention. And the dense non-structural $\BV\setminus\{u_0\}$ has the same valid sequents and in-bounds positions as $\BV$. \hfill\leanok{structural\_eq\_BV}\ \leanok{BV\_not\_BC\_learnable}
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} (a) Each schema enforces one table row. (b) $v$ satisfies $\sigma(s)$ iff $v\circ\sigma\in V$ satisfies $s$; and any Boolean $u$ is $v\circ\sigma$ for $\sigma$ sending atoms to $p_0\vee\neg p_0$ or its negation. (d) No tell-tale, then the locking-sequence argument. Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:carnap}.

The three two-conclusion schemata in $\TTs$ are exactly the rank-2 data that $\valtaut$ violates. Brute force over the sixteen formulas of depth $\le1$ in two atoms confirms (a), \Cref{thm:coherence:carnap} and all $2^{16}$ denial ranks; the T2 verifier found, over all structural meanings of two- and three-element matrices, no non-Boolean survivor of the twelve data and each datum necessary \status{computed}. A second remedy restricts the hypothesis class, as in Gold's setting:

\begin{proposition}[A compositional prior suffices]\src{T2 Prop 4.5}\label{prop:coherence:compositional}
Exactly one two-element matrix with designated value $1$ validates explosion, $\neg\neg p\step p$, $p\step\neg\neg p$, the three $\wedge$ rules, $\vee$-introduction, disjunctive syllogism, modus ponens, $q\step p\to q$ and $\neg p\step p\to q$: the standard one.
\end{proposition}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:carnap}; compare \citet{bonnay2016compositionality}, whose compositionality is more general. World feedback alone fixes nothing, since the actual valuation is Boolean and never refutes $V\supseteq\BV$; under the compositional prior it identifies the tables once every row has been observed \src{T2 Prop 4.6}.

\subsection{Tonk, harmony and conservativity}\label{sec:coherence:tonk}

\citet{prior1960runabout}'s tonk ($\varphi\step\varphi\tonk\psi$, $\varphi\tonk\psi\step\psi$) is the paradigm of a rule search exploits (\Cref{thm:search:tonk}); \citet{belnap1962tonk} demanded that new rules be \emph{conservative}.

\begin{proposition}[Tonk]\src{T2 Prop 5.1}\label{prop:coherence:tonk}
Under cut, tonk gives $\varphi\der\psi$ for all $\varphi,\psi$, and coherence refutes it in two steps ($a\step a\tonk\bot\step\bot$) if $\Ac$ has a non-empty context or the logic has a theorem. In a theorem-free base with $\Ac=\{\emptyset\}$ it survives: classical $\{\wedge,\vee\}$ plus tonk is $\Cai$. Without cut tonk need not trivialize \citep{cook2005whats,ripley2015anything}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One cut gives the first claim. In the theorem-free case every rule has premises, so nothing is derivable from $\emptyset$, while from any premise tonk yields everything.
\end{proof}

Transitivity is double-edged: it lets one bad rule poison everything, and it lets coherence catch it. In Experiment~B a bold learner offered the two tonk rules in either order accepted the first and rejected the second (each alone has a truth-table reading): boldness is order-dependent when individually coherent rules are jointly incoherent \status{computed; quick run}.

\begin{proposition}[Coherence is not conservativity]\src{T2 Prop 5.2, as repaired}\label{prop:coherence:conservativity}
Intuitionistic $\to$ plus $\neg$-introduction, $\neg$-elimination and $\neg\neg$-elimination is the $\{\to,\neg\}$ fragment of $\CPC$: coherent, but not conservative over $\IPC_\to$ (it proves Peirce's law). $\neg\neg$-elimination alone would be conservative. Conservativity implies coherence when coherence is non-triviality on the old vocabulary, or $\bot$ with ex falso is in the base, but not in general ($\IPC_\to+\{\step\bot\}$, with $\bot$ new and without ex falso).
\end{proposition}

Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:tonk}. So Belnap's learner rejects classical negation over intuitionistic positive logic, and the coherence learner accepts it. Harmony \emph{with normalization} gives conservativity \citep{prawitz1965natural,dummett1991logical}, but local harmony alone does not: Read's bullet \citep{read2000harmony}, introduced from a derivation of $\bot$ from $[\bullet]$ and eliminated from $\bullet,\bullet$ to $\bot$, has local reductions yet derives $\bot$ from nothing. Over a Post-complete base the two notions coincide \src{L5 Cor 2.4}: if $C'$ is structural on $L'\supseteq L$ with $C'|_L\supseteq\Cn_2$ ($L$ as in \Cref{thm:coherence:post}), then $C'|_L$ is $\Cn_2$ or $\CFm$, so $C'$ is conservative iff it is coherent on one classically consistent context. Belnap's existence condition is, over classical logic, non-triviality; harmony matters only over sub-classical bases.

\begin{theorem}[Coherence is $\Pi_1$, conservativity $\Pi_2$]\src{T2 Thm 5.3}\label{thm:coherence:complexity}
For finite rule systems (elementary formal systems; \citealt{post1943formal,smullyan1961theory}): \textup{(a)} $\{R:R\nder\bot\}$ is $\Pi_1$-complete; \textup{(b)} for some fixed finite base $B$, $\{N:B+N\text{ conservative over }B\}$ is $\Pi_2$-complete; \textup{(c)} so coherence is decidable in the limit with one mind change, while conservativity is not decidable in the limit by any computable learner, only refutable in the limit \citep{kelly1996logic}; \textup{(d)} for decidable $B$ (e.g.\ $\CPC$, $\IPC$) conservativity is $\Pi_1$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} (a) Simulate $\varphi_e(e)$ by rules and add ``halt $\step\bot$''. (b) A new symbol $c$ with $\step c\,\bar e$ and $c\,y,\mathrm{Num}(x)\step T(x,y)$ over a universal base is conservative iff $\varphi_e$ is total. Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:tonk}.

\emph{Reading.} A coherence filter can be built into a convergent learner (\Cref{thm:coherence:cpc}(b)); a conservativity filter cannot in general (over the base of (b)). Coherence plus decidable local checks such as harmony are the learner-friendly norms. Where non-conservativity is the point, as for theoretical terms in physics, a concept whose rules smuggle in a substantive inference (Dummett's \emph{Boche}) is coherent, and only world feedback can test it.

\subsection{Fallacies and the Kripkensteinian residue}\label{sec:coherence:fallacies}

A learner with a \emph{generalization operator} $\mathcal G$ (e.g.\ structural closure of least generalizations) turns finitely many fallacious human steps $F$ into rules $\mathcal G(F)$. Let $\hstar$ be the target consequence relation.

\begin{theorem}[Elimination criterion; trivial]\src{T2 Thm 6.1}\label{thm:coherence:elimination}
Coherence (with data) eventually refutes the generalized fallacy iff $\hstar\oplus\mathcal G(F)$ is $\Ac$-incoherent; for OH the refutation is charged to the budget of \Cref{thm:coherence:halving}. If $\hstar$ is sound for a class of intended structures and each $A\in\Ac$ holds in one that also validates $\mathcal G(F)$, the fallacy survives.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\hstar\oplus\mathcal G(F)$ contains every positive datum; apply \Cref{thm:coherence:silent}. A structure validating $\hstar$, $\mathcal G(F)$ and $A$ shows coherence on $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[All structural propositional fallacies die, quickly]\src{T2 Cor 6.2}\label{cor:coherence:fallacies}
If $\mathcal G$ is structural closure and $\hstar\supseteq\Cn_2$ is structural and coherent on a designated context (so $\hstar=\Cn_2$), every classically invalid rule is eliminated: substituting $\top/\bot$ along a falsifying row makes its premises tautologies and its conclusion a contradiction (\Cref{tab:coherence:fallacies}). Given the row, the $\bot$-derivation has polynomial size in any Frege system \status{proof sketch for the size bound}; finding the row is an NP search.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\hstar\oplus\mathcal G(F)$ is a structural proper extension of $\Cn_2$, hence $\CFm$ (\Cref{thm:coherence:post}).
\end{proof}

Experiment~B shows the negative-data role end to end \status{computed; quick run}. From 50 simulated human proofs with systematic fallacies (affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent, and an illegitimate discharge that was never learned) and $5\%$ corrupted steps, imitation ended with 22 unsound active rules, 13 of them tonk-like, and a budgeted adversarial prover derived $\vdash\bot$ and all 34 of its false goals. Coherence pruning on five designated contexts, with blame assigned by a minimum-weight hitting set over the bags found, removed every unsound rule and the two fallacies; all 13 target rules were recovered (exactly or with a stronger guard), completeness matched the target calculus under the same prover budget, and the adversary proved none of its goals. From only 10 proofs affirming the consequent survived, plausibly because the learned calculus lacked the rules needed to derive the contradiction: coherence is only as strong as the calculus that searches for it. Outside a Post-complete target, fallacies can survive.

\begin{example}[Survivors]\src{T2 \S6, as repaired}\label{ex:coherence:survivors}
\textup{(1)} \emph{Quantifier swap} $\forall x\exists yR\,/\,\exists y\forall xR$ is sound in one-element structures, hence $\emptyset$-coherent; its alternative meaning is ``the domain is a singleton'', and any designated context with $0\neq1$ kills it. \textup{(2)} The \emph{gambler's fallacy} is incoherent with independence, but coherent without it: an anti-persistent stationary Markov chain with switching probability $0.6$ has fair marginals and validates it. Only frequencies or an accepted independence premise remove it. \textup{(3)} \emph{Base-rate neglect} as a uniform schema $P(E|H)=a\,/\,P(H|E)=a$ yields $P(H|E)+P(\neg H|E)=1.8$ when $P(E|H)=P(E|\neg H)=0.9$, and dies with additivity. \textup{(4)} \emph{Conditional perfection} (from $q$ and a law $p\to q\in K$, infer $p$; \citealt{geis1971invited}) is coherent whenever the per-law converse $\{q\to p:(p\to q)\in K\}$ is consistent with $K\cup A$. This is sufficient, not necessary ($K=\{p_1\to q_1,p_2\to q_2\}$, $A=\{q_1\vee q_2,\neg p_1,\neg p_2\}$: the converse is inconsistent with $K\cup A$, yet neither $q_i$ is derivable, so the step rule never fires and the fallacy survives, for step-set hypotheses without proof by cases). It dies when two exclusive laws share a consequent ($\mathit{rain}\to\mathit{wet}$, $\mathit{sprinkler}\to\mathit{wet}$, $\neg(\mathit{rain}\wedge\mathit{sprinkler})$, $A=\{\mathit{wet}\}$). The per-law converse is not Clark's completion \citep{clark1978negation}, which is consistent there.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Without uniformity, coherence is toothless; trivial]\src{T2 Prop 6.3}\label{prop:coherence:quus}
If $\hstar\oplus F$ is $\Ac$-coherent, the non-structural $\hstar\oplus F$ survives forever; e.g.\ $\Cn_2+\{\step p_{17}\}$, the ``quus'' of propositional logic, when every designated context is consistent with $p_{17}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[The Kripkensteinian residue]\src{T2 Thm 6.4, as repaired}\label{thm:coherence:residue}
$\Alt_{\mathcal G}(\hstar,\Ac)=\{h\ \mathcal G\text{-closed}:h\supseteq\hstar,\ h\ \Ac\text{-coherent}\}$ is, in the limit of complete data, the set of $\mathcal G$-closed hypotheses never refuted by positive data and coherence (\Cref{thm:coherence:silent}).
\textup{(i)} $\mathcal G$ structural, $\hstar=\Cn_2$, $\Ac\neq\emptyset$: $\Alt=\{\Cn_2\}$.
\textup{(ii)} $\mathcal G$ structural, $\hstar=\IPC$, $\Ac$ a non-empty family of classically consistent contexts: $\Alt=\{h\text{ structural}:\IPC\subseteq h\subseteq\CPC\}$, all intermediate logics.
\textup{(iii)} $\mathcal G$ deductive closure, $\hstar=\PA$, $\Ac=\{\emptyset\}$: $\Alt$ is the set of all consistent closed extensions of $\PA$, e.g.\ $\PA+\neg\Con(\PA)$ and its non-standard completions. Designating all finite sets of true sentences removes exactly the unsound ones; the sound $T_k$ of \Cref{thm:coherence:turing} remain, and cannot be discriminated.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} (i) is \Cref{thm:coherence:post}. For (ii) $\subseteq$, a classically invalid sequent in a structural $h\supseteq\IPC$ becomes, under $\sigma_v$, an $\IPC$-provable premise set with an $\IPC$-refutable conclusion. Appendix~\ref{app:coherence:residue}.

\paragraph{Division of labour.} \emph{Community practice} (positive data) eliminates under-generalizations. \emph{Uniformity} (structurality, least generalization) eliminates gerrymanders such as \Cref{prop:coherence:quus}; it is vocabulary-relative (Goodman's point), and it converts an instance-level fallacy into a schema that coherence can refute. \emph{Coherence} eliminates uniform alternatives that conflict with accepted uniform rules: tonk; affirming the consequent; ``quus'' as a second rule for $+$ beside $x+Sy=S(x+y)$. \emph{Nothing} in the setup eliminates coherent uniform alternatives that agree with all data. For classical propositional logic there are none; for arithmetic they are the G\"odel--Rosser and non-standard alternatives, a precise mathematical counterpart of Kripkenstein for arithmetic \citep{kripke1982wittgenstein}, removable only by accepting reflection or stronger theories on other grounds (\Cref{sec:philosophy}).

\subsection{A remark on eliminative versus confirmational coherence}\label{sec:coherence:eliminative}

Coherentist epistemology has impossibility results: no informative coherence measure is truth-conducive ceteris paribus \citep{bovens2003solving,bovens2003bayesian,olsson2005against,olsson2005impossibility}. They do not threaten the use of coherence in this section, and they do refute a tempting other use \src{L11 \S3.4}.

\begin{remark}[Three uses of coherence]\label{rem:coherence:uses}
\textup{(U1) Eliminative}: ``$h$ derives $\bot$ from a context certified consistent, so $h$ has an invalid rule.'' This is modus tollens, not a measure. Conditioning a prior on ``$h$ is $\Ac$-coherent'' multiplies every coherent hypothesis by $1/\Prob(\text{coherent})$: under truthful designation the target's posterior weakly rises and odds among coherent rivals are unchanged (L11 TC4). The impossibility theorems, about degrees of coherence of consistent information sets, do not apply; the prices are \Cref{thm:coherence:silent} and the need for truthful designation. \textup{(U2) Probabilistic consistency} of credences: de Finetti constraints, satisfied by the noncommittal assignment, the credal analogue of gappy valuations. \textup{(U3) Agreement as evidence}: annotators or sampled derivations agree. This is the witness scenario of \citet{lewis1946analysis} that the impossibility theorems address, and they bite in full.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Consensus does not amplify under a common cause]\src{L11 TC3}\label{rem:coherence:consensus}
Let $m$ annotators judge a step. With probability $\pi>0$, independently of validity, they all copy one shared judgment that says ``valid'' with probability $a$ for valid and $b>0$ for invalid steps; otherwise they judge independently with rates $p>q$. Then
\[
\frac{\Prob(\text{all say valid}\mid\text{valid})}{\Prob(\text{all say valid}\mid\text{invalid})}=\frac{\pi a+(1-\pi)p^m}{\pi b+(1-\pi)q^m}\le\frac1{\pi b}
\]
for every $m$, against $(p/q)^m$ when $\pi=0$. So unanimity among annotators sharing an error source, or self-consistency across samples of one model, moves the odds by a bounded factor. Human errors in mathematics are often systematic in this sense, which is why corroboration must come from channels whose errors are independent of the human ones, Daniels' independence constraint \citep{daniels1979wide}.
\end{remark}

The eliminative use has a quantitative safety guarantee whose converse shows what it depends on. Let $\mathcal W\subseteq\{0,1\}^S$ be the worlds allowed by constraints on a finite set $S$ of sentences, $K$ their convex hull, and score credences $x$ in world $w$ by $\|x-w\|^2$.

\begin{lemma}[Coherentizing is safe iff the constraints are sound]\src{L3 Lemma E$'$, as repaired}\label{lem:coherence:projection}
For $x\notin K$ with Euclidean projection $x^*$ onto $K$, every $w\in K$ has $\|x-w\|^2\ge\|x-x^*\|^2+\|x^*-w\|^2$. Conversely, if the actual world's indicator $v$ is not in $\mathcal W$, then $v\notin K$, and projecting $x=v$ raises the actual loss from $0$ to $\|v-v^*\|^2>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\langle x-x^*,w-x^*\rangle\le0$. For the converse, a $0/1$ vector is an extreme point of the cube, so it lies in the convex hull of $0/1$ vectors only if it is one of them.
\end{proof}

This is the accuracy-dominance argument of \citet{definetti1974theory} and \citet{joyce1998nonpragmatic} (Bregman projections for other proper scores; \citealt{predd2009probabilistic}). It belongs to the projection, not to arbitrary arbitrage removal: for $x=(0.6,0.6)$ on $\{\varphi,\neg\varphi\}$, moving to $(1,0)$ costs $2.0$ in world $(0,1)$, against $0.52$ before and $0.5$ for the projection. The converse is the probabilistic form of the air-pressure worry: coherence pressure from an idealized context the world violates, or from untrusted learned rules, can make credences strictly worse.

\paragraph{Reading.} The setup's use of coherence is eliminative, hence immune to the coherentist impossibility theorems, but weak: it never confirms, it only removes what conflicts. Corroboration must come from channels with independent errors: computation, randomized evaluation, measurement (\Cref{sec:setting:channels}). Where the target is coherence-pinned, uniqueness of the coherent extension, not truth-conduciveness of coherence, does the work. \Cref{sec:philosophy} reads this division as vindicated wide reflective equilibrium \citep{goodman1955fact,daniels1979wide}.
