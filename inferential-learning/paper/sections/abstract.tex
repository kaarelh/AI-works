\begin{abstract}
\noindent Can a learner that acquires inference rules come to reason soundly, and with what justification? We consider a learner that imitates human inferences, is then conditioned on coherence (no good arguments for both $P$ and $\neg P$), and receives occasional feedback from the world. We answer this precisely, for formal mathematics, informal mathematics, and the checking of physics solutions.

The answer is a division of labour among the learning signals, each with provable strengths and a provable residue.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Search forces worst-case soundness.} Accuracy on the distribution of human steps says nothing about a reasoner that searches: one tonk-like rule of arbitrarily small probability mass makes everything derivable. Cautious acceptance over schematic hypotheses is sound against every adaptive prover. Its price is a combinatorial dimension of the hypothesis class.
\item \textbf{Imitation identifies rules.} Positive examples identify a schematic calculus exactly, at coupon-collector rates, and this generalizes to derivations of any size. But systematic human errors are indistinguishable from rules.
\item \textbf{Coherence is eliminative negative data.} Its power is exactly Post-completeness. It pins down classical logic and complete theories, is blind for intuitionistic logic, and is Popperian for arithmetic. Carnap's categoricity problem turns out to be Gold's problem in the dual space of valuations.
\item \textbf{Formal mathematics.} We prove an end-to-end theorem for a two-tier learner (audit, then assert; bold conjectures in a sandbox), and show that each ingredient is necessary.
\item \textbf{Informal mathematics.} We model it as a latent formalization. A bounded-gap verifier is sound against adaptive provers and identifies validity exactly up to informal-validity equivalence. Counterexample objects are exponentially more informative than paradoxes.
\item \textbf{Physics.} Contexts are given a filter semantics in which eternalism is provably impossible. Exports from idealized contexts need certificates of finite information radius. A structured-solution checker is sound against adversarial solvers relative to a judgment layer that we prove ineliminable.
\end{itemize}
We also settle two questions raised in the notes that motivated this work: a steeper simplicity penalty as a route from imitation to norms, and a ``philosophical completeness theorem''.

Every main result passed adversarial verification. Core discrete results are machine-checked in Lean~4, and experiments illustrate the phenomena. We argue that principled justification beyond proof has a definite shape: proof within contexts, certified bridges between them, and an explicit, minimized, ineliminable judgment layer, with learning signals whose one-sided limits are known.
\end{abstract}
