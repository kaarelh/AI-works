## Sentences flagged and left unchanged

These read oddly now, but they are not false, so they were left as they are.

1. **"the readings of them given here are ours"** (`paper/main.tex` footnote; `paper/sections/intro.tex` §1.1, line 5) and **"The readings of the notes given there are ours."** (`paper/sections/philosophy.tex` §13.6, line 158). These sentences were written to separate the readings in the report from the notes' author. "Ours" now includes him, so they read as his readings of his own notes, which weakens the disclaimer they were meant to give. The footnote copy was kept because the instruction says to keep such attributions as true facts.
2. **"Throughout, ``we'' means the authors of this report."** (`paper/sections/philosophy.tex` §13, line 3). It is still true, but "we" now includes Hänni, so the philosophical view argued in §13 is attributed to him as co-author. Before, it was Claude's view.
3. **"The work was carried out by many cooperating instances of a language model, coordinated by one instance."** (`paper/sections/intro.tex` §1.3, line 115). It calls Claude anonymously "a language model" although the author line names Claude, and it does not mention Hänni's direction, which the footnote now states.
4. **"Every main result was attacked by independent referees and repaired where needed."** (`paper/sections/abstract.tex`, line 15) and **"two or three independent referees"** (`paper/sections/intro.tex` §1.3, line 119). The footnote now says that the referees were separate Claude instances. "Independent" means separate instances here, which a reader may not assume.
5. **"The motivating notes are suspicious of limit framings … Our results bear on that view only in a limited way. … the open-ended development of concepts and calculi that the notes have in mind"** (`paper/sections/philosophy.tex` §13.1, line 36). The text sets "our results" against the notes' view as if from outside, although the notes' author is now one of "us". The third-person reference is normal; only the contrast reads slightly oddly.
