===== L11-justification-and-reflective-equilibrium.md
The memo is written (about 9,700 words with references, so above the 3,000–7,000 target). Its supporting script, research/lit/L11-scripts/coherence_checks.py, has 12 exact or brute-force checks, all of which pass; I reused the partial version from the interrupted attempt and added three checks. Web search worked but every full-text fetch was blocked, so citations marked [v] were confirmed only from search snippets (gist and bibliographic data); [mem] marks references taken from memory. Main findings: (1) The user's setup is close to verbatim Goodman's mutual-adjustment account of justification (Fact, Fiction, and Forecast, ch. III). Imitation plus coherence is narrow reflective equilibrium; adding world feedback from a channel whose errors are independent of human errors makes it wide reflective equilibrium. Daniels' independence constraint is, formally, the conditional-independence assumption the coherence impossibility theorems show to be necessary. (2) Stich & Nisbett's objection becomes a near-trivial lemma. If a fallacy is used systematically by humans, coherent with the target on every designated context, and not exposed by the world channel, then every learner that is sound with probability at least 1−δ must abstain on its instances, even in worlds where they are valid. Separately, imitation by minimum description length (MDL) with any fixed simplicity weight eventually absorbs every compressible systematic error. So imitation converges to Cohen's "competence", not to validity. (3) The Bovens–Hartmann and Olsson impossibility results do not bite against coherence used eliminatively (deriving a contradiction from a context certified consistent refutes the rule set). They do bite against coherence used as confirmation: human consensus, annotator agreement, agreement among sampled derivations. What escapes them: eliminative use; channels whose errors are independent of human errors and individually credible; known source reliabilities; and structural uniqueness of the coherent extension (Post-completeness). Haack's foundherentism (clues plus crossings) is the best philosophical fit. (4) Carroll's regress is formalized for learners in five statements, C1–C5. With no built-in rule, conditional premises are inert. One built-in rule (modus ponens) lets a premise simulate any single-instance rule, and the premise version is strictly stronger. Schematic generality always needs a rule (Quine 1936). Rules of proof differ from their internalized conditionals: necessitation versus p→□p, and the reflection rule versus the reflection axiom, which Löb's theorem makes explosive. The proposed account of "principled justification beyond proof" is vindicated wide reflective equilibrium: a theorem about the setup would be a conditional reliabilist meta-justification relative to explicit channel assumptions, not a non-circular foundation. Limit theorems are only Reichenbach-style vindications and fall to Salmon's asymptotic-rules objection; anytime bounds avoid it. Peirce, Dummett, Carnap and Quine disagree only about the residue of coherent alternatives (Alt, from the T2 memo). Rules can be identifiable when truths are not, which undercuts the simple form of Dummett's acquisition argument.

-- theorem candidates:
  - TC1 (Stich lemma plus residue): if human-data laws, coherence reports and world answers coincide under v1 = R* ∪ {F} and v2 = R*, every δ-sound learner accepts F-instances with probability ≤ δ in both worlds. If a world channel exposes F with per-query probability μ_F > 0, F is eliminated after O(log(1/δ)/μ_F) queries. This is the finite-sample form of orchestrator idea 4(d).
  - TC2 (imitation-MDL absorbs systematic errors): for any fixed simplicity weight λ, a systematic error F with frequency φ > 0 and complexity K(F) is learned after n* ≈ λK(F)/(H(φ)+φ log M) steps. Any λ schedule that prevents this also prevents learning valid rules with the same (φ, K) profile. Needs a rigorous two-part code over situation types.
  - TC3 (consensus does not amplify under common cause): if with probability π > 0 all annotators copy one shared judgment that is wrong with positive probability, the posterior odds from m agreeing annotators are bounded independently of m. Under conditional independence with p > q they grow as (p/q)^m. Corollary: self-consistency across samples of one model certifies nothing against that model's systematic errors.
  - TC4 (eliminative coherence is never truth-adverse): conditioning on coherence over a truthfully designated set of contexts weakly increases the posterior of the true hypothesis and leaves odds among coherent hypotheses unchanged. By contrast, no degree-of-coherence bonus term can track the posterior across all reliabilities (Bovens–Hartmann).
  - TC5 (clause asymmetry): the learner's objective is weighted MAX-SAT with negative-bag clauses, positive-data clauses and MDL costs. Pure Thagard coherence is complement-symmetric, and a negative-bags-only objective is minimized by the empty rule set. Only the mixed objective has informative optima; this is the formal version of BonJour's isolation objection.
  - TC6 (rule versus premise separations): with modus ponens built in, a premise simulates a single-instance rule and is strictly stronger (C2); schematic rules need an instantiation rule (C3); necessitation versus p→□p, and the reflection rule (admissible for Σ1-sound T) versus the reflection axiom schema (inconsistent by Löb) (C4). Learned rule and export validity are Π-claims, so the safe acceptance policy is Popperian.
  - TC7 (self-certification): rule-circular self-certification has likelihood ratio 1 between sound and unsound hypotheses that share the certifying rule, and a self-proof of consistency by an r.e. extension of PA is a proof of inconsistency. Self-checks must never feed back into acceptance.
  - TC8 (rules learnable, truths not; a reply to Dummett's acquisition argument): there is a hypothesis class in which classical logic plus the PA schemas is identifiable from positive and coherence data, while no learner on the same channels plus computable feedback decides Σ2 truth in the limit. Combine T2 Thms 3.10 and 4.4 with L1 finite elasticity.
  - TC9 (limit theorems do not discriminate): for any learner that identifies in the limit, there is another that also identifies in the limit yet is unsound at every stage before an arbitrary finite time. This is Salmon's objection transposed, and the justification for demanding anytime bounds.
  - Vindicated wide reflective equilibrium as the target meta-theorem: under explicit channel assumptions (independence, credibility, truthful designation, realizability), mutual adjustment of human steps, rules and independent channels is sound against every adaptive prover with probability ≥ 1−δ, with bounded abstention or mistakes. Paired impossibility results show each ingredient is necessary outside coherence-pinned domains.
===== T3-contexts-idealization-export.md
T3 builds a semantics, a checker and a soundness theorem for physics-olympiad reasoning across many contexts (about 10.9k words). Check scripts are in /home/user/AI-works/inferential-learning/research/theory/T3-checks/: leg_exact.py, leg_mc_flux.py, sps_checker.py, realizability*.py, learn_regions.py, hygiene.py, plus the reused pendulum.py, drag_sign.py and projectile.py, all re-run.

SEMANTICS
- Every context denotes a filter of models drawn from one deformation family: a top model M(lambda) parametrized by named parameters.
- The root is the principal filter at the actual or intended parameter point.
- SUP(A) refines the parent filter by ||A||, which makes discharge exact.
- An idealized context DEF is the push-forward of the parent filter along a declared deformation of named parameters toward ideal values. Limit semantics uses the principal filter at the ideal point; germ semantics uses the punctured neighbourhood filter along a path.
- Truth in a context is classical (via Łoś it is truth in all ultraproducts with the parameter infinitesimal). It is consistent iff the filter is proper. Idealized stipulations may contradict the root.
- The sound imports into an idealized context are exactly the deformation-stable sentences: the laws of the frame plus undeformed data. So import filters are not a separate learned object; they are fixed by the declared top-model family, which is part of the reading.
- ist(c, phi) is meta-level eternal. It is provably non-truth-functional in the world's facts.

COHERENCE
- The coherence constraint is realizability of the context-indexed judgment set. It is characterized exactly: fold suppositional contexts into their parents as conditionals, then require consistency of the root and of every anchored idealized context. A context is anchored if it is certified, or exports through an informative bridge into an anchored context.
- Reductio immunity and well-posedness-only-for-exporting-idealizations follow as corollaries.
- A sound calculus (Ax/Step/Imp/Dis/Exp) gives blame localization: every contradiction in a designated context has an unsound instance in its derivation cone. That cone is T2's negative bag.

EXPORT
- Sound export needs information of finite radius (information-based complexity). The flat/bump/finite-jet/finite-sample impossibility is one invisible-perturbation lemma, which also yields no-free-certification for learning.
- Positive certificates: certified-neighbourhood and rate-qualified export, Gronwall finite-horizon export, a fully rigorous projectile range certificate for any dissipative drag bounded by k|v|^2, and error budgets through chains.
- Contexts must be declared deformations, with side conditions derived in the parent. Dropping either requirement lets a solver export arbitrary false claims.

LEARNING TOLERANCES AND VALIDITY REGIONS
- Coherence-only calibration is either unsound or trivial.
- Conformal calibration fails on adversarially selected instances.
- A conservative Lipschitz certifier with a validated simulation oracle is deterministically sound against any solver, with matching upper and lower query bounds.
- A monotone certifier needs only logarithmically many queries, but monotonicity is a real hypothesis that fails for a drag-damped pendulum.

MAIN THEOREM
- The Structured Physics Solution (SPS) checker is relatively sound: for every solver, an accepted export holds in every admissible world of its reading, given a trusted kernel, bridge catalogue and validated numerics.
- The residual judgment layer is exactly: (J1) reading containment W_{rho*} ⊆ W_rho; (J2) top-model adequacy, needed only for world claims; (J3) the trusted base. J1 and J2 are ineliminable.
- Supervaluational correctness (completion invariance) is the largest acceptance criterion that stays sound under uncertainty about the reading.
- Gricean determinacy is one-sided: it refutes over-wide readings but rewards over-narrow ones, so silent narrowing is the natural attack. This completes a table of three one-sided signals:
  - rules: coherence catches over-acceptance;
  - tolerances: coherence catches over-confidence;
  - readings: determinacy catches only over-width.

WORKED EXAMPLE: EuPhO 2025 T1(a), chair leg (statement as reconstructed in L9)

- A rigorous thin-leg bridge bound (0 violations on 2.5M points) and a finite-sun bridge, composed.
- The mini-checker accepts the honest answer on r in [20a, 34a], sin(phi/2) >= 0.5, at 10% tolerance, with certified error 8.9%; the exact error there is about 2.6% plus 0.9% from the sun's size.

===== T4-informal-math-latent-formalization.md
A pre-formal mathematical practice is modelled as the shadow of an unknown latent formalization h* = (L*, R*, rho*, M*):
- L* is a formal language.
- R* is a calculus: schemas plus a definitional library, so the gap is measured relative to the library, per de Bruijn and Mathias.
- rho* is a partial reading of context-tagged informal sentence occurrences into L*.
- M* is an intended semantics.

Human steps are rho*-images of R*-derivations with at most g steps, plus sporadic or systematic noise. Re-using a sentence across contexts is an explicit "link", and links are where equivocation happens.

The learner has three feedback channels:
- (P) practice data;
- (C) coherence: paradoxes derived from designated, asserted contexts, never from suppositions;
- (O) objects: finite partial valuations of informal sentences, promised to be realized by an h*-admissible model, which can be queried line by line. Computation is the special case of the intended object on a decidable fragment.

Main results:
- **Verifier soundness.** The verifier accepts a step or link iff it is g-valid under every surviving hypothesis. It is deterministically sound against all adaptive provers when h* is in the class.
- **Noisy data.** A Ville-type version handles noisy data, with a threshold set by the prior mass of the target's equivalence class.
- **Costs.** Escalations are bounded by elasticity, and an "expand this step" protocol makes the verifier complete.
- **Identifiability.** An informal completeness lemma shows that the informal consequence relation determines both object valuations and coherence. As a result, identification is exactly up to informal-validity equivalence on the practice's domain. Granularity is identified only one-sidedly, and ontology, choice of latent language and junk sentences are not identifiable.
- **Objects versus paradoxes.** Counterexample objects give step-level blame by descent and need at most log2|H| corrections. Paradoxes (negative bags) can need |H|-1 corrections; a sorites-shaped chain paradox attains this. With at most r suspect steps per paradox the cost is linear in r.
- **Frege, Russell, Zermelo.** The story is proved on a 10-formula comprehension toy, together with a proof that naive comprehension has continuum-many maximal consistent repairs.
- **Robust core.** Supervaluational practice is valid in every admissible sharpening, so it survives every formalization that is one of them. It is exactly what can be learned soundly. "Valid in most sharpenings" fails to chain, with the sorites as the tight case.
- **Steeper simplicity penalty.** The user's steeper-simplicity-penalty proposal reduces to a threshold on compression rate, and the user's convex-hull vertex conjecture is false in general.
