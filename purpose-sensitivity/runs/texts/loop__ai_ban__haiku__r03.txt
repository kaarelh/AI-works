# Drafting Issues in S. 2817 (as amended October 6, 2026)

**Most urgent:** items 27 (sunset conflicts with the duration clause), 15 (safety-evaluation exemption), 13–14 (grandfathering gap), 1 (single-run threshold), and 17 (no penalty for registry violations).

## Definitions (Sec. 2)

1. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** The 10^26 threshold counts only "a single training run," so a developer can split one frontier effort into several runs or stages, or spread it across sites or time, and stay below the line. Add aggregation language covering all compute used to produce a model, including continued pretraining and checkpoint continuation.

2. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** "Integer or floating-point operations" has no counting method. Precision, sparsity, and dense-equivalent accounting are unspecified, so lower-precision reporting could understate totals. Specify the counting standard and let the Secretary update it as algorithmic efficiency changes.

3. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** The fixed threshold doesn't reach a below-threshold model that is fine-tuned or post-trained into a covered capability level, or a composite system built from several models or agent scaffolds. Add coverage for modified and composite systems.

4. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** "The Secretary" is never identified. The bill should name the official and agency (presumably Commerce).

5. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** "Substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no benchmark, evidentiary standard, or procedure, and the determination carries no notice, comment, or appeal rights. This invites both non-determination (the Secretary never acts) and vagueness challenges. Add criteria, a process, and a deadline for determinations.

6. **Sec. 2 (generally):** Key terms are undefined: "artificial intelligence model/system," "train," "deploy," "training run," "advanced AI chip," "data center," "operator," "safety evaluation," and "person." "Deploy" and "train" matter most, since the scope of the prohibition depends on them.

7. **Sec. 2(2), "U.S. person":** Limiting the term to citizens and state-organized corporations leaves out LLCs, partnerships, and U.S.-controlled entities organized abroad. Add "controlled by or acting at the direction of" language and cover entities of any form organized under U.S. law.

8. **Sec. 2(3), "covered facility":** "Advanced AI chips" is undefined. The 50,000-chip threshold isn't tied to the compute threshold, so a run can reach 10^26 FLOP on fewer chips over a longer period and escape the registry. "Containing" also ignores multi-site and rented cloud capacity. Define the chip term, aggregate across sites and operators, and count leased compute.

## Prohibition (Sec. 3)

9. **Sec. 3(a):** The prohibition doesn't reach providing compute, chips, financing, or other assistance for prohibited activity, so cloud providers, chip suppliers, and investors face no liability. Add facilitation or aiding-and-abetting liability.

10. **Sec. 3(a):** It isn't clear that "deploy" covers releasing model weights, the central open-weight risk. Add an explicit prohibition on publishing or transferring weights of covered systems, and clarify whether API hosting counts as deployment.

11. **Sec. 3(a):** There is no anti-evasion clause. Splitting runs, restructuring corporate entities, shopping for jurisdictions, and training through foreign affiliates are not addressed. Add anti-circumvention language.

12. **Sec. 3(b):** The grandfather clause turns on training that "began" before enactment, but "began" is undefined, no documentation is required, and nothing limits deployment of grandfathered systems. As written, a pre-enactment run could be deployed indefinitely. Define the start of training, require certification or registration of pre-enactment runs, and restrict deployment of grandfathered systems.

13. **Sec. 3(b) vs. Sec. 8:** The grandfather cutoff is the enactment date, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. The bill doesn't say whether runs started during that gap are covered, so developers could start runs in the window. Align the cutoff with the effective date, or expressly bar starting covered training after enactment.

14. **Sec. 3(c):** The safety-evaluation exemption depends on "purpose," which is undefined and hard to prove or disprove. It has no scale limit, notice requirement, independent oversight, or bar on deploying outputs, so a developer could train a frontier system and label it a safety evaluation. Limit the exemption to registered or accredited evaluators, require notice to Commerce, and bar commercial use of the resulting systems or their outputs.

15. **Sec. 3 vs. Sec. 5(a):** The penalty applies to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but the prohibition isn't effective until Sec. 8's date. The drafting suggests exposure before the prohibition applies, and the ambiguity invites challenge. Conform the two provisions.

## Registry (Sec. 4)

16. **Sec. 4(b):** The report requires only a description of "computing workloads," not training runs, FLOP totals, model identities, or whether a covered system is involved. Specify required content, including each training run above a threshold and its compute total.

17. **Sec. 4 / Sec. 5:** Sec. 5 penalizes only violations of Sec. 3, so failing to register or file reports has no consequence. Add penalties for Sec. 4 violations.

18. **Sec. 4:** There is no registration deadline, no inspection or audit authority, no records-retention requirement, and no power to demand additional information. Add all four.

19. **Sec. 4(a):** "Operator" is undefined. Developers running on rented cloud capacity may not be the operator, and cloud providers may not know what workloads run on their infrastructure. Impose reporting duties on developers as well.

## Penalties and Enforcement (Sec. 5)

20. **Sec. 5(a):** A $1 million-per-day penalty is small against frontier training budgets and could be treated as a cost of doing business. It also isn't inflation-indexed, and "each day" isn't defined for continuously running systems. Scale penalties to revenue or compute value, define the counting rule, and index for inflation.

21. **Sec. 5 (generally):** The bill doesn't provide for forfeiture of chips, weights, or other assets, or for disgorgement of gains. Add forfeiture and disgorgement authority.

22. **Sec. 5:** No agency is named to bring civil actions, and the bill gives no express authority for injunctions, cease-and-desist orders, or emergency relief. The DOJ role in criminal enforcement is also unstated. Name the enforcing agencies and grant injunctive and emergency powers.

23. **Sec. 5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and proving willfulness against corporate actors with diffuse decision-making is difficult. Define the mental state (for example, to include reckless disregard) and address corporate officer liability.

24. **Sec. 5 (generally):** There is no individual liability for officers or directors who direct violations, and no whistleblower protection or reward program. Add both.

## International Agreement (Sec. 6)

25. **Sec. 6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no reporting requirement and no consequence for failure, and "equivalent to those in section 3" is undefined. Require a report to Congress at the 18-month mark and specify minimum treaty terms, such as verification and registry requirements.

## Duration and Sunset (Sec. 7)

26. **Sec. 7(a) vs. Sec. 7(b):** Sec. 7(a) keeps the prohibition in place until a joint resolution finds safe development, but Sec. 7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years whether or not a finding has been made. Read together, the prohibition expires on its own schedule, which undermines its core purpose. This is the most important fix: remove the sunset, or make it conditional on the joint resolution.

27. **Sec. 7(a):** "Developed safely" is undefined, no deadline requires the National Academies report, and the bill doesn't say how the joint resolution will be presented to the President. Add a standard, a report deadline, and an express presentment requirement.

28. **Sec. 7(b):** The bill doesn't say what happens to trained covered systems when the Act sunsets. Address whether existing systems may continue to operate.

## Challengeability (for CAS counsel review)

29. **Sec. 2(1)(B) and Sec. 7(a):** "Secretary determines" with no intelligible principle invites a nondelegation challenge, and "substantially exceeding" and "most cognitive tasks" may be vague as applied to criminal penalties. Add principles and criteria to guide each determination.

30. **Sec. 3(a):** A First Amendment challenge is possible if the prohibition is read to reach code or model weights as expression. Counsel should review this, and the bill could include legislative findings that the regulated conduct is training and operating compute-intensive systems, not publishing expression.

31. **Sec. 7(a):** Structure the joint resolution as a bill subject to bicameralism and presentment, so it can't be characterized as a legislative veto (see *INS v. Chadha*).
