# S. 2817 Drafting Issues: Loopholes, Ambiguities, and Unworkability Points

Based only on the text provided (as amended October 6, 2026). Case law and agency authority should be verified by counsel.

**Definitions (Sec. 2)**

1. **Sec. 2(1)(A), "single training run":** The bill doesn't say whether multi-stage training (pretraining, continued pretraining, post-training, distillation) counts as one run or several. This determines whether the 10^26 threshold is measured cumulatively or per stage.

2. **Sec. 2(1)(A), compute measurement:** The threshold is stated as "integer or floating-point operations" with no counting method, precision adjustment, or estimation standard. No one can reliably determine compliance in advance, and the Commerce Department has no stated authority to set one.

3. **Sec. 2(1)(A), "model" vs. "system":** The compute threshold applies to a "model," but the prohibition covers a "covered AI system." A system combining a below-threshold model with tools, scaffolding, or other models is not clearly covered by subparagraph (A).

4. **Sec. 2(1)(B), "Secretary":** No Secretary is identified. It is presumably the Secretary of Commerce, but the text doesn't say so.

5. **Sec. 2(1)(B), "determines":** There is no procedure, notice, hearing, evidentiary standard, deadline, or judicial review. A system could be designated covered without the designated party having any opportunity to respond.

6. **Sec. 2(1)(B), undefined performance standard:** "Substantially exceeding," "most," "cognitive tasks," and "human performance" are undefined and have no benchmark. This is the strongest vagueness argument in the bill, especially since criminal liability attaches under Sec. 5(b).

7. **Sec. 2(1)(B), retroactivity:** Subparagraph (B) has no compute floor and no time limit, so a system built years earlier could be designated after the fact. Penalties for conduct before any designation raise fair-notice and due process problems.

8. **Sec. 2(2), "corporation organized under the laws of a State":** LLCs, partnerships, and other non-corporate entities are not clearly covered. Foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. companies are also outside the definition, which affects the extraterritorial reach of Sec. 3(a).

9. **Sec. 2(2), individuals:** Lawful permanent residents and other non-citizen employees are not "U.S. persons," and the bill doesn't say whether individuals acting for a covered entity abroad are reached.

10. **Sec. 2(2), no attribution rule:** The bill has no rule attributing a subsidiary's conduct to its parent, and no anti-evasion provision.

11. **Sec. 2(3), "advanced AI chips":** Undefined. There is no list, technical standard, or agency authority to define it, so the 50,000-chip threshold can't be applied.

12. **Sec. 2(3), "data center" and "facility":** Undefined. A training run distributed across several sites, each under 50,000 chips, may fall outside the definition of covered facility.

13. **Sec. 2(3), disconnected from the prohibition:** "Covered facility" is used only in Sec. 4. The Sec. 3 prohibition doesn't reference facilities, so the registry has no operative link to what is prohibited.

**Prohibition (Sec. 3)**

14. **Sec. 3(a), "train, develop, or deploy":** None of these terms is defined. It is unclear whether they cover research experiments, fine-tuning, distillation, internal use, API access, or open-weight release.

15. **Sec. 3(a), "person in the United States":** "Person" is undefined, and the text doesn't say whether using a model hosted abroad counts as deploying it in the United States.

16. **Sec. 3(a), timing of a run:** The bill doesn't say when a training run begins or ends. It is unclear whether a run spanning the effective date is prohibited from that date forward.

17. **Sec. 3(b), grandfathering keyed to start date:** The exemption depends only on when training began, not on completion, deployment, or registration. A run started before enactment could be deployed well after the prohibition takes effect, with no stated limit and no reporting requirement.

18. **Sec. 3(b), successor models:** The bill doesn't address fine-tuning or continued training of grandfathered weights, or next-generation runs that start after enactment but build on those weights.

19. **Sec. 3(b) and Sec. 8, gap window:** Grandfathering runs from enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. A run started in that window is neither grandfathered nor yet prohibited, and its status is unclear once the prohibition takes effect.

20. **Sec. 3(c), "safety evaluation":** Undefined. There are no notice, registration, independence, scope, or results-sharing requirements. The purpose-based test is subjective, and the bill has no "primary purpose" rule for mixed-purpose work.

21. **Sec. 3(c), scope of "activities":** It is unclear whether the exemption covers training a covered system for evaluation or only testing systems already built. It is also unclear whether it covers making a system available to outside evaluators.

**Registry (Sec. 4)**

22. **Sec. 4(a), "operator":** Undefined. It could mean the lab, the cloud host, or both, and the bill doesn't allocate responsibility when a third party hosts the facility.

23. **Sec. 4(a), no deadline:** No registration deadline, content requirements, or start date for reporting is specified.

24. **Sec. 4(b), "computing workloads":** Undefined, and there is no requirement to identify covered systems. The reports could be inconsistent or uninformative, and there is no confidentiality protection for trade secrets or FOIA exemption, which creates disclosure risk.

25. **Sec. 4, no penalty:** Sec. 5 penalizes only violations of Sec. 3. Failure to register or report carries no stated consequence, so these obligations may be unenforceable as written.

**Penalties (Sec. 5)**

26. **Sec. 5(a), no enforcer:** The bill doesn't name an agency, the Attorney General, or a private right of action. Without a designated enforcer, the civil penalty may be unenforceable as written.

27. **Sec. 5(a), counting violations:** "For each day on which the violation occurs" doesn't say whether a continuing training run is one violation or one per day, or how violations are counted across facilities or persons. There is also no aggregate cap.

28. **Sec. 5(a) vs. Sec. 8, date conflict:** The civil penalty applies to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but Sec. 3 isn't effective until 180 days later. The penalty provision could be read to reach conduct before the prohibition applies.

29. **Sec. 5(b), "willfully":** Undefined, and the criminal provision relies on the vague terms in Sec. 2(1)(B). This supports a fair-notice or vagueness challenge to criminal liability.

30. **Sec. 5, no injunctive or stop-order authority:** The bill provides no express authority to enjoin an ongoing violation, freeze or seize compute, or order a halt pending adjudication. The only remedies are penalties and prosecution.

31. **Sec. 5(a), assessment factors:** The bill doesn't say how penalty amounts are set or what factors apply, beyond "not more than" a cap.

**International agreement (Sec. 6)**

32. **Sec. 6, "shall seek to negotiate":** This creates no obligation to reach an agreement and no consequence if none is reached. "Equivalent to those in section 3" is undefined, and there is no reporting requirement to Congress.

**Duration (Sec. 7)**

33. **Sec. 7(a) vs. Sec. 7(b), internal conflict:** Sec. 7(a) keeps the prohibition in effect until a joint resolution, but Sec. 7(b) terminates the entire Act after 10 years. It is unclear which controls. The sunset is the more favorable reading for us, but the other side will argue that 7(a) governs.

34. **Sec. 7(a), "can be developed safely":** Undefined. The NASEM report has no deadline and the bill doesn't require one to be issued, so the ending condition may never be triggered.

35. **Sec. 7(a), joint resolution mechanics:** The bill provides no expedited procedure for the joint resolution, so Congress can simply decline to act and the prohibition persists by default. Presentment to the President also applies to a joint resolution, which adds a veto dimension.

**General and implementation**

36. **No rulemaking authority:** The bill gives Commerce no authority to define terms, set compute measurement methods, create registry forms, or issue implementing regulations, and it includes no appropriation. The agency cannot clarify the ambiguities above.

37. **No severability, judicial review, or definitions section:** The bill has no severability clause, no judicial review provision, and no general definitions for "person," "operator," "Secretary," or "deploy." If a court strikes a provision, the rest of the Act's operation is uncertain.

**Timing mismatch:** Sec. 4 has no stated effective date, so its obligations arguably begin at enactment while Sec. 3 waits 180 days. This should be raised together with item 28.

## Candid assessment for the legal team

- **Do not rely on ambiguity to protect flagship work.** Training above 10^26 operations in a single run is clearly covered by Sec. 2(1)(A) and Sec. 3(a). The drafting problems are mostly at the edges, and a regulator or court would likely read the core prohibition as applying to frontier training.
- **The most important protection is Sec. 3(b).** Confirm the training start date of every run currently in progress. Successor runs started after enactment are likely not grandfathered (see item 18).
- **Strongest points for markup and public unworkability arguments:** items 1, 2, 4–7, 14, 20, 26, 28, 33–36. Items 26, 28, 33, and 25 are also the most likely to be fixed by amendment, so raising them publicly may prompt Congress to close the gaps.
- **Strategic caution:** Arguments that depend on structuring entities abroad, splitting runs across facilities, or labeling training as evaluation could be framed as evasion. The bill has no anti-evasion language, but raising these gaps may prompt one to be added, which would hurt us. Counsel should weigh that before the markup.
