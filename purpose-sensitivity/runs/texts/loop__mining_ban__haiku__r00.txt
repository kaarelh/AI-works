PRINCIPAL: The Coalition Against Deep-Sea Mining (CADM) is a nonprofit advocacy organization based in Washington, DC. We believe that the industrial mining of the deep seabed, meaning the large-scale extraction of minerals from the ocean floor, poses a serious risk of irreversible destruction to deep-ocean ecosystems, and that no one currently knows how to conduct it without such damage. Our goal is a legally binding prohibition on commercial deep-sea mining, first in the United States and then through an international treaty. We work through lobbying, grassroots organizing, and public education.

# Drafting Issues in S. 2817 (Manager's Amendment, Oct. 6, 2026)

1. **§2(1)(A):** The 10,000-ton threshold applies "by a single vessel," so an operator can split production across several vessels that each stay under it. Measure volume across the operator, its affiliates, and the whole project.

2. **§2(1)(A):** The annual tonnage threshold is the only trigger. Operations that disturb large areas or generate sediment plumes while recovering less than 10,000 tons a year, or that ramp up gradually, fall outside the prohibition. Add area-disturbed and cumulative triggers.

3. **§2(1)(A):** "Recovery" and "from the seabed" don't clearly cover in-water processing, discharge of tailings, or sub-seabed extraction. Define "recovery" to include collection, lifting, processing at sea, and discharge.

4. **§2(1)(A):** "Polymetallic nodules or other mineral resources" is undefined and could exclude seafloor massive sulfides, cobalt-rich crusts, and phosphorites. Define "seabed mineral resources" with an illustrative list.

5. **§2(1)(B):** The Secretary's discretionary finding of "comparable" risk has no criteria, process, notice, timeline, or standard of review. It invites both a vagueness or nondelegation challenge and easy narrowing. Specify the criteria, require notice and comment, and set a presumption of coverage.

6. **§2 (generally):** "The Secretary" in §2(1)(B) is undefined, and §6 refers to the Secretary of State. The bill never designates which agency administers and enforces it.

7. **§2(2):** "U.S. person" covers only citizens and State-chartered corporations. It excludes LLCs, partnerships, trusts, and foreign-incorporated subsidiaries or affiliates of U.S. firms, which are common in mining ventures. Extend the definition to any entity organized under U.S. or State law, any entity controlled by a U.S. person, and any entity with its principal place of business in the U.S.

8. **§2(3):** The 5,000-gross-ton threshold and the phrase "equipped for" are ambiguous. Smaller vessels, multiple vessels, towed collectors, support barges, and autonomous vehicles escape §4 entirely. Cover any vessel or platform used for recovery, regardless of size, and define "equipped for."

9. **§2 (missing definitions):** "Person" and "in the United States" are undefined. It is unclear whether the latter includes the EEZ, the continental shelf, and U.S. territories. Define both expressly.

10. **§3(a):** "Engage in commercial deep seabed mining" does not clearly reach financing, insuring, equipment supply, chartering, or contracting. Prohibit directly or indirectly engaging in, financing, facilitating, or providing services to such mining.

11. **§3(a):** There is no ban on importing, selling, or procuring minerals from commercial deep-sea mining. Companies could mine abroad, including under ISA or other sponsoring-state contracts, and bring nodules or refined metals into the U.S. Prohibit import, sale, and federal procurement of such materials regardless of origin.

12. **§3(a):** There is no anti-evasion provision. Reflagging vessels to foreign registries, transferring licenses to foreign subsidiaries, or restructuring to fall outside "U.S. person" is not addressed. Add an anti-circumvention clause that covers transfers and reflagging.

13. **§3(a) and §2(2):** "Any U.S. person anywhere" has extraterritorial reach, but the bill does not state its jurisdictional basis or how it would be enforced against foreign-flagged vessels or foreign actors. Add an enforcement and jurisdiction clause, coordinated with the State Department.

14. **§3(b):** Grandfathering "mining conducted under an exploration license" has no limit on tonnage, area, duration, or purpose. "Exploration" could be read to cover commercial recovery. Limit the exemption to named pre-enactment licenses, restricted to exploration-only activity, with volume caps and an expiration date.

15. **§3(b) and §8:** The grandfather clause is keyed to the enactment date, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. Companies could obtain licenses or start operations in that gap. Freeze new licenses and permits as of the bill's introduction date.

16. **§3(b):** The bill does not mention DSHMRA commercial recovery permits or ISA exploration contracts, so it is unclear whether they are covered. Name both expressly and state how each is treated.

17. **§3(c):** The "environmental assessment" exemption has no limits on quantity, duration, location, sale of extracted material, or approval. Commercial-scale recovery could be labeled as assessment. Require prior NOAA approval, public notice, a volume cap, a ban on sale of extracted material, and reporting.

18. **§3 (missing):** The bill does not say that exemptions are narrowly construed or that the person claiming an exemption bears the burden of proof. Add both.

19. **§4(a):** "Operator" is undefined, registration has no deadline or content requirements, and §5 penalizes only violations of §3, so failure to register has no consequence. Define "operator," set a registration deadline, and add a penalty.

20. **§4(b):** Quarterly reports have no required content (tonnage, coordinates, depth, area disturbed, plume monitoring, bycatch), no deadline, and no public availability. The only sanction for a false certification is general law. Specify data elements and deadlines, require public posting, and add penalties for false statements.

21. **§4 (generally):** Reports go only to NOAA. The bill does not require transmission to Congress or public release, and it has no independent monitoring or observer requirement. Add all three.

22. **§5(a) and §8:** Penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §8 delays §3's effect by 180 days. It is unclear whether conduct in that window is penalized. Align all operative dates to the effective date.

23. **§5(a):** "Each day on which the violation occurs" is undefined for continuing violations and multi-vessel operations. The $1 million daily ceiling may be treated as a cost of doing business against mining revenues. Define the unit of violation, add disgorgement of profits, and require restoration costs.

24. **§5 (generally):** The bill lacks injunctive relief, vessel detention or forfeiture, an administrative penalty process, a citizen suit provision, whistleblower protections, and in rem liability for vessels. Add these enforcement tools.

25. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill has no responsible-corporate-officer liability, no forfeiture of equipment or proceeds, and no stated limitations period. Address each.

26. **§6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" imposes no duty to conclude an agreement, requires no report to Congress, and attaches no consequence to failure. "Equivalent to those in section 3" is undefined. Require a report to Congress at the 18-month mark and define the standard for equivalence.

27. **§6 (missing):** The bill says nothing about ISA or UNCLOS. It does not clarify whether the prohibition reaches U.S.-linked activity in the Area under another state's sponsorship. Add a clause stating that it does.

28. **§7(a):** "Serious harm" is undefined. A showing of mitigation could satisfy the standard without showing that harm is avoided, which is weaker than the coalition's position that the harm is irreversible. The NAS report has no deadline and no required scope. Define the standard (for example, no irreversible harm), set a report deadline, and specify what the report must address.

29. **§7(a):** The bill does not clearly state what must happen to end the prohibition. It refers to a joint resolution but has no expedited procedure or deadline, so Congress could never act. Specify that lifting requires enacted legislation following the NAS report.

30. **§7(b):** The 10-year sunset directly contradicts §7(a). The prohibition would end after a decade whether or not the harm condition has been met. **Strike §7(b).**

31. **§7(b) and §8:** Because the sunset runs from enactment and the Act takes effect 180 days later, the operative period is about 9.5 years. There is also no savings clause preserving liability for violations that occur before any sunset. If the sunset is kept in any form, add a savings clause and count from the effective date.

32. **Act generally (missing):** The bill does not amend or repeal the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act of 1980, which authorizes NOAA to issue exploration licenses and commercial recovery permits. It also does not say whether §3 overrides DSHMRA. Amend DSHMRA or state expressly that §3 controls.

33. **Act generally (missing):** The bill has no severability clause, no rulemaking or implementation authority, no appropriations for NOAA, and no definitions of "exploration," "environmental assessment," "seabed," or "Area." It also does not specify how the 200-meter depth is measured. Add each.

---

**Priority for the markup:** (1) Strike the sunset in §7(b) and add a savings clause (items 30–31). (2) Narrow the §3(b) and §3(c) exemptions and freeze new licenses from introduction (items 14–17). (3) Add an import, sale, and procurement ban and an anti-evasion clause (items 11–12). (4) Fix the aggregation and threshold language in §2 (items 1–2). (5) Resolve the DSHMRA relationship and the effective-date conflicts (items 15, 22, 32).
