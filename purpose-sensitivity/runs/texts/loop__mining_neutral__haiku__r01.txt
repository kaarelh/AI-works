RESPONSE:
Here are the drafting issues I found, organized by section. Some may be deliberate choices by the sponsors. For the explainer, it would help to frame each as a question for committee staff rather than a flaw.

1. **§2(1)(A):** The 10,000-ton threshold applies to "a single vessel," so an operator could run several vessels each under the limit with combined output well above it. The definition doesn't say whether affiliated vessels or fleets are aggregated.

2. **§2(1)(A):** "Recovery" and the tonnage measure are undefined. It's unclear whether the 10,000 metric tons counts only nodules or all material, including sediment, and whether it's wet or dry weight.

3. **§2(1)(B):** The bill never says which "Secretary" makes the determination that an activity poses comparable risks. It also sets no standard, procedure, or public record for that determination, which invites both delay and litigation.

4. **§2(1), §3(a):** "Commercial" is not separately defined, and "engage in" is undefined. It's unclear whether financing, chartering vessels, supplying equipment, or processing ore counts as engaging in mining.

5. **§2(2):** "U.S. person" covers citizens and State-chartered corporations but not LLCs, partnerships, permanent residents, or foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. firms. This leaves a gap in the "U.S. person anywhere" reach of §3(a).

6. **§2(3):** "Equipped for the recovery of seabed mineral resources" is undefined. The 5,000-gross-ton threshold is also unrelated to the 10,000-ton output threshold in §2(1)(A), so a vessel could mine at covered scale while falling below the vessel definition.

7. **§2 (generally):** "Person," "operator," "owner," "deep seabed," and "exploration license" are used but not defined, which matters for who is liable and what is exempt.

8. **§3(a):** The prohibition reaches "any person in the United States" and "any U.S. person anywhere," but it doesn't address foreign-flagged vessels run by non-U.S. persons, chartered vessels, or activities in the International Seabed Area. Those areas are left to §6, which is non-binding.

9. **§3(b):** "Exploration license" is undefined. The text doesn't say whether it must be a federal license, and it doesn't limit the exemption to exploration rather than commercial recovery.

10. **§3(b):** The grandfather clause has no volume cap, expiration date, or limit on renewal. A license issued the day before enactment could exempt commercial-scale mining indefinitely.

11. **§3(c):** The environmental-assessment exemption has no limit on volume, duration, notice, or permit requirement, and it doesn't bar selling extracted minerals. "For the purpose of" is self-declared, so bulk commercial sampling could be labeled assessment.

12. **§4(a):** Registration has no deadline, no content requirements, and no stated consequence for noncompliance. It also applies only to "covered vessels," so mining vessels under 5,000 gross tons fall outside the registry.

13. **§4(b):** Quarterly reports are self-certified and have no required content, such as tonnage, location, depth, or discharge. The text also lacks any verification, audit, or public-disclosure requirement.

14. **§4 and §5:** Civil and criminal penalties attach only to violations of §3. Failure to register, failure to report, and false certification have no stated penalty.

15. **§5(a):** "Per day on which the violation occurs" is undefined. It doesn't say how a continuing violation is counted (per day, per vessel, or per ton), or whether owners, operators, and financiers are all liable.

16. **§5(a):** The bill names no enforcing agency and no procedure (administrative or judicial). It also lacks a statute of limitations, injunctive authority, a citizen-suit provision, and any vessel forfeiture or disgorgement remedy.

17. **§5(a) and §8:** Penalties apply to violations "occurring on or after the date of enactment," but §3 doesn't take effect until 180 days later. This is a direct drafting conflict about when a violation can first occur.

18. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill doesn't address attempts or conspiracy. The fine reference to title 18 doesn't state an amount.

19. **§6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no duty to conclude an agreement and sets no consequence for failure. "Equivalent" is undefined, and the text doesn't address how an agreement would interact with the International Seabed Authority or UNCLOS Part XI.

20. **§7(a):** "Serious harm" is undefined, and the bill sets no evidentiary standard for "can be conducted without serious harm." It also doesn't require the National Academies report to be produced, set a deadline for it, or specify what it must cover.

21. **§7(a):** The joint-resolution pathway has no expedited procedure, so it's unclear how or whether Congress would act. Under the Constitution, a joint resolution also requires presidential signature or a veto override, which the text doesn't mention.

22. **§7(a) and §7(b):** The prohibition lasts "until Congress" acts, but §7(b) sunsets the entire Act after 10 years. The text doesn't say whether the sunset overrides the report-and-resolution pathway.

23. **§7(b):** There is no savings clause. Penalties for violations during the term, pending enforcement actions, and registry and reporting duties may lapse at sunset. Because the clock starts at enactment, the effective term is about 9.5 years, not 10.

24. **§8:** The 180-day delay means commercial mining isn't prohibited during that window. The text also doesn't address vessels already mid-operation on the effective date.

25. **Missing provisions:** The bill has no severability clause, rulemaking authority, appropriation or staffing for NOAA's registry, or preemption language. It also doesn't address how it relates to existing law, such as the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act of 1980, which governs U.S. licenses and permits for deep seabed recovery. That connection bears directly on what "exploration license" means in §3(b).
