# Drafting issues in S. 2817 (text as amended October 6, 2026)

**Section 2 (Definitions)**

1. **§2(1)(A):** The 10^26 threshold applies to "a single training run," so a developer could split one effort across several runs, or count only the final run. The bill should count cumulative compute for a model lineage, including continued pretraining, fine-tuning, and post-training.
2. **§2(1)(A):** The threshold is fixed in statute, but algorithmic efficiency gains could allow a dangerous system to be trained below 10^26. Add authority for the Secretary, or a congressional review process, to lower the threshold by rule.
3. **§2(1)(B):** "The Secretary" is never identified, and "substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no standard, procedure, notice requirement, or timeline. Because the prohibition depends on this determination, it invites vagueness and nondelegation challenges.
4. **§2(1)(B):** The bill doesn't say whether a determination applies to a specific system, a model family, or future versions, or whether it reaches systems already in development when it is made.
5. **§2(1):** Neither prong addresses systems assembled from several models, each below the threshold, or agentic systems that combine models. Developers could argue these are not "covered AI systems."
6. **§2(2) (U.S. person):** "Corporation organized under the laws of a State" excludes LLCs, partnerships, and other non-corporate entities, and permanent residents are not citizens. Use "entity" and address who is covered when a U.S.-controlled foreign subsidiary is involved.
7. **§2(3):** "Advanced AI chips" and "data center" are undefined, and the 50,000-chip threshold is per facility. Affiliated sites, leased cloud capacity, and split clusters could each stay under it. Aggregate across affiliated facilities and contracted capacity.
8. **§2(3):** "Covered facility" is used only in §4 (registry). Nothing in §3 links the prohibition to facilities, so the definition is disconnected from enforcement.

**Section 3 (Prohibition)**

9. **§3(a):** "Train," "develop," and "deploy" are undefined, and "develop" overlaps "train." The prohibition doesn't reach possessing, selling, transferring, hosting, or publicly releasing the weights of a covered system, so open-weight release is a major gap.
10. **§3(a):** The prohibition doesn't reach people who assist a prohibited run by providing compute, chips, cloud capacity, funding, or technical support. Add aiding, abetting, and facilitation language.
11. **§3(a):** "Any person in the United States, or any U.S. person anywhere" leaves foreign developers who train abroad and serve U.S. users unclear. Add coverage for making a system available to users in the United States, and address remote access and the physical location of cloud compute.
12. **§3(b):** "Training began" is undefined, and the grandfather clause doesn't restrict continued training, checkpointing, scaling, or deployment of pre-enactment systems. A run could be extended indefinitely. Grandfathered systems should be barred from further training or scaling and should be registered.
13. **§3(b) and §8:** Because the grandfather clause keys off the enactment date while the prohibition takes effect 180 days later, runs started in that window would be exempt. Set a fixed cutoff (such as the introduction date) and bar new runs above the threshold during the interim.
14. **§3(c):** "Activities conducted for the purpose of safety evaluation" is self-declared and unlimited. It could cover training a full covered system, with no requirements for notice, registration, containment, or a bar on deploying the evaluated system. Add notice to Commerce, security standards, and an express bar on deployment under this exception.

**Section 4 (Registry)**

15. **§4(a):** Registration has no content requirements, no deadline, and no penalty. "Operator" is undefined, so it's unclear whether a cloud provider or its tenant must register.
16. **§4(b):** The quarterly report only requires a certified description of "computing workloads." It doesn't require operators to state whether any run exceeds the §2(1)(A) threshold, and it doesn't set standards for certification or penalties for false or omitted information.
17. **§4 (general):** Section 5 penalizes only violations of §3, so failing to register or report carries no penalty. The section also has no confidentiality rules for sensitive information and no public reporting obligation.
18. **§4 (effective date):** The bill doesn't say whether registration starts at enactment or at the §8 effective date. Section 5(a) runs from enactment, so the timing matters.

**Section 5 (Penalties)**

19. **§5 (enforcement authority):** No agency is designated to enforce the Act. It also lacks injunctive authority, cease-and-desist power, authority to suspend or seize compute, and rulemaking authority. Without these, a developer could keep training while contesting fines.
20. **§5(a):** The $1,000,000-per-day civil penalty is small compared with frontier training budgets and could be treated as a cost of doing business. Consider per-run penalties, penalties tied to compute or revenue, and clear rules for counting continuing violations.
21. **§5(a) and §8:** Penalties attach to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §3 takes effect 180 days later. This creates an ambiguity about whether penalties can reach conduct before the prohibition is in effect, which invites fair-notice and retroactivity arguments.
22. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined. A developer could claim it didn't know it had crossed the compute threshold, given the ambiguity in §2. Define the knowledge standard (including recklessness) and specify who in a corporation is personally liable.
23. **§5 (general):** The section has no attempt or conspiracy language, no whistleblower protection, and no statement of the limitations period for civil penalties, which would otherwise default to the general federal rule.

**Section 6 (International agreement)**

24. **§6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no required outcome, no reporting to Congress, and no consequence if negotiations fail. "Equivalent to those in section 3" is undefined, and the section doesn't say that negotiations won't pause the domestic prohibition.

**Section 7 (Duration) and Section 8 (Effective date)**

25. **§7(a):** "Safely" is undefined, the NAS report has no deadline or required scope, and there is no expedited procedure for the joint resolution. Specify the criteria the report must assess and a timeline for both the report and congressional action.
26. **§7(a) and §7(b):** Subsection (a) keeps the prohibition in effect until Congress acts, and subsection (b) ends the entire Act after 10 years. The bill doesn't say which controls. As written, the prohibition could lapse without any safety finding. Strike the sunset or make it conditional on a finding.
27. **§7(b) and §8:** Because the prohibition starts 180 days after enactment, the 10-year sunset leaves about 9.5 years of effect. That gives developers a defined horizon to plan around.

**Cross-cutting issues**

28. **No severability clause:** If a court strikes a key provision (for example, §2(1)(B) or §5(b)), the whole Act could fall. Add a severability clause.
29. **No findings or purpose section:** Courts use findings to interpret ambiguous text and to assess tailoring. Add findings on the risks and on the basis for federal action.
30. **Likely constitutional challenges:** Expect vagueness challenges to the criminal provisions and the §2(1)(B) determination, nondelegation challenges to the Secretary's authority, and First Amendment arguments about restrictions on code and model weights. Clearer definitions and delegation standards would reduce all three.
31. **No implementation resources:** The bill doesn't authorize appropriations, rulemaking, or reporting to Congress on enforcement. Without these, implementation depends on agencies acting on their own.

---

**Top priorities for Thursday's markup**

- Designate an enforcing agency and give it injunctive and cease-and-desist authority (Item 19).
- Resolve the §7(a)/§7(b) conflict by striking the sunset or making it conditional (Item 26).
- Close the grandfather and transition loopholes in §3(b) and §8 (Items 12–13).
- Define "covered AI system" to count cumulative compute and to address model release, aiding, and foreign deployment (Items 1, 9–11).
- Add a severability clause and a definitions section (Items 28, 30).

These are drafting observations on the text provided, not a legal opinion. Legislative counsel should check them against the full manager's amendment, which may already address some of them.
