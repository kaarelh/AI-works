**Drafting review: S. 2817 (text as amended October 6, 2026)**

I worked only from the text you pasted. If the manager's amendment changed other provisions, check those too. Items are grouped by section and numbered continuously. Points that are mainly policy choices rather than drafting errors are marked **(policy)**.

**Section 2: Definitions**

1. **§2(1)(A), "single training run":** Developers can split one frontier effort into several runs or stages, each under 10^26 operations. Define compute across all training for a model, including pretraining, post-training, fine-tuning, and RL, aggregated across affiliated entities.
2. **§2(1)(A), compute measurement:** The bill does not say how operations are counted (precision, sparsity, synthetic data generation, distillation, inference-time compute). Require a Commerce measurement standard.
3. **§2(1)(A), fixed threshold:** Algorithmic efficiency means the same capabilities will require less compute over time, so a fixed threshold will decay. Give Commerce authority to revise the threshold and consider capability-based triggers. **(policy)**
4. **§2(1)(A), "model" only:** A system combining several sub-threshold models, tools, or agents, or a model fine-tuned from a sub-threshold base, may fall outside this prong. Reach systems and derivatives of covered models.
5. **§2(1)(B), undefined capability test:** "Substantially exceeding," "most," and "cognitive tasks" are undefined, which invites both evasion and vagueness challenges. Tie the test to objective, published benchmarks or a defined process.
6. **§2(1)(B), Secretary determination:** The bill sets no notice, evidentiary standard, or deadline, and does not require developers to self-assess. It also does not say whether determinations are final or reviewable. Add a developer assessment duty, a decision timeline, and a review provision.
7. **§2(1)(B), "Secretary" undefined:** §6 names the Secretary of State, so the reader cannot tell which Secretary §2 means. Name the Secretary of Commerce (or the intended official).
8. **§2(2), "U.S. person":** The definition excludes LLCs, partnerships, and other non-corporate entities, and it does not reach foreign subsidiaries of U.S. firms. A U.S. company could train through one of these vehicles and fall outside §3(a). Extend the definition to any entity organized under U.S. law or headquartered in the U.S., and to entities controlled by U.S. persons.
9. **§2(3), "covered facility":** "Data center" and "advanced AI chips" are undefined. The 50,000-chip threshold can be avoided by spreading a cluster across sites or networking sub-threshold facilities. Aggregate across sites and affiliates, and define chips by performance characteristics.

**Section 3: Prohibition**

10. **§3(a), undefined conduct:** "Train, develop, or deploy" omits possessing, fine-tuning, hosting, API access, and releasing or publishing weights. Open-weight release is the most significant gap. Define these terms and address weight release explicitly.
11. **§3(a), compute and chip providers:** Cloud and chip suppliers who knowingly provide compute for covered training are not addressed. Add knowledge-based aiding-and-abetting liability.
12. **§3(a), foreign developers:** Non-U.S. persons training outside the U.S. are not covered at all, and only the §6 negotiation could reach them. Consider conditioning U.S. chip, cloud, or market access on compliance. **(policy)**
13. **§3(a), extraterritoriality:** The "anywhere" language invites a presumption-against-extraterritoriality challenge. Add an explicit congressional statement that the provision applies extraterritorially and identify the jurisdictional basis.
14. **§3(b), grandfather clause:** Any system whose training "began" before enactment is exempt, including its deployment, with no registration or proof required. Combined with the 180-day delay in §8, this creates a window to start covered runs and keep them. Replace the enactment date with a fixed public cutoff, require registration with documented start dates within a set deadline, and bar deployment of grandfathered systems that are not registered.
15. **§3(b), "training began" undefined:** A preliminary run or checkpoint could be used to claim an earlier start. Define the start as the first run of the final pretraining, documented by scheduling records.
16. **§3(b), continued training:** The exemption does not stop grandfathered systems from being further trained or post-trained into more capable systems. Limit the exemption to the specific checkpoint and bar additional covered compute.
17. **§3(c), "safety evaluation":** The term is undefined and self-declared, and "activities" can include training. The exemption places no limit on compute, duration, access, who conducts the work, or deployment. Require government authorization or registration, cap compute, bar public deployment, and require reporting of results.

**Section 4: Registry**

18. **§4(a), no deadline or content requirements:** The bill sets no registration date and does not specify what operators must disclose, such as chip counts, owners, locations, or workloads.
19. **§4(a), "operator" undefined:** Leasing or cloud arrangements may leave the actual trainer unreported. Require disclosure of the owner, the operator, and all customers using the facility's compute.
20. **§4(b), vague reporting:** "Describing the computing workloads" does not require FLOP totals, model identities, training start dates, or whether a workload is a covered system. Specify required contents.
21. **§4, no penalty for noncompliance:** §5 penalizes only violations of §3, so the registry is effectively voluntary. Add civil penalties for failure to register or report, and criminal liability for false certifications.
22. **§4(b), certification:** The bill has no falsity standard, no officer-level certification, no record-retention duty, and no audit or inspection authority. Add all four.
23. **§4, limited disclosure:** Reports go only to Commerce, with no public summary and no reporting to Congress or other enforcement agencies. Add an aggregate public registry and reports to relevant committees.

**Section 5: Penalties**

24. **§5(a), penalty size:** $1 million per day is small relative to frontier training costs and may be treated as a cost of doing business. Add a revenue- or compute-linked penalty, disgorgement, and inflation adjustment. **(policy)**
25. **§5(a), "for each day on which the violation occurs":** It is unclear whether a single continuing training run accrues daily penalties, and there is no minimum. Clarify.
26. **§5, no enforcing agency specified:** The bill does not say who brings civil actions (DOJ, Commerce, or another body). Name the enforcing agency and its litigation authority.
27. **§5, no injunctive or emergency authority:** The bill does not authorize injunctions, suspension orders, or the seizure of covered compute, weights, or facilities. Add express authority.
28. **§5(b), criminal standard:** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill does not address responsible-officer liability or attempt and conspiracy. Clarify all three.
29. **§5, no savings clause:** If the Act sunsets under §7(b), a party may argue that penalties for earlier violations have lapsed. Add a survival clause.

**Section 6: International agreement**

30. **§6, weak obligation:** "Shall seek to negotiate" is satisfied by a good-faith attempt. The section has no reporting duty, no consequence for failure, and no mechanism to revisit §3 after an agreement is reached. "Equivalent" is also undefined. Require reports to Congress and define the standard.

**Section 7: Duration**

31. **§7(b) conflicts with §7(a):** §7(a) keeps the prohibition in force until a safety finding, but §7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years whether or not that finding has been made. The two provisions do not say which controls. Remove the sunset, or make it conditional, and state the precedence. **(policy)**
32. **§7(a), undefined standard and process:** "Can be developed safely" has no standard. The NASEM report has no deadline or required contents, Congress has no timeline for acting on the joint resolution, and the bill does not say whether a narrow finding about one class of systems would suffice. Specify each.

**Section 8: Effective date**

33. **§8, 180-day gap:** The prohibition does not take effect for 180 days, which encourages developers to start runs during the gap. Pair this with the §3(b) fix, or make §3 effective on enactment and phase in only the registration requirements.
34. **§5(a) and §8 conflict:** §5(a) penalizes violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §3 takes effect 180 days later. It is unclear whether conduct in the gap is penalized. Align the two provisions.

**Cross-cutting issues**

35. **No severability clause:** If a court strikes one provision, such as §2(1)(B), the whole Act could fall. Add one.
36. **No preemption or savings clause:** The bill does not say whether it displaces state AI laws. Industry will argue the point either way, so the text should settle it. **(policy)**
37. **No rulemaking authority or appropriation:** Definitions such as chips, facilities, and FLOP counting depend on agency guidance the bill does not authorize. Add rulemaking authority, funding for enforcement, and a periodic review clause.
38. **Constitutional exposure:** Restrictions on publishing weights or code may draw First Amendment challenges. Vague criminal terms may draw due process challenges, and the §2(1)(B) determination may draw nondelegation or APA challenges. Consider legislative findings, narrow tailoring, and the severability clause above.
39. **No whistleblower protection:** Employees are the most likely source of information about covert training, so the bill should protect them from retaliation.
40. **Inconsistent terms:** The bill alternates between "model" and "system" and uses "artificial intelligence" without defining it. Use defined terms consistently.

**Highest-priority fixes for the markup**

- **Grandfather clause and §8 gap (items 14, 15, 33, 34):** These create a direct path for developers to keep running covered systems.
- **Registry has no penalty (items 21–22):** The reporting regime is unenforceable as written.
- **Aggregation and anti-evasion (items 1, 4, 9):** Without these, developers can avoid both the compute threshold and the facility threshold.
- **Undefined conduct and open-weight release (item 10):** The prohibition does not clearly reach releasing weights.
- **Sunset versus open-ended prohibition (item 31):** The conflict should be resolved before the bill moves.
- **Enforcement tools (items 26–27):** The bill lacks an enforcing agency and injunctive authority.

Before sending anything to the office, have Senate legislative counsel review the proposed language, since a few items depend on how the amendment was actually drafted.
