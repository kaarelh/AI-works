# Weak Spots in S. 2817 (Manager's Amendment, Oct. 6, 2026)

**Definitions and general structure**

1. **Sec. 2 (general):** The Act defines only three terms. "Train," "develop," "deploy," "person," "operator," "artificial intelligence," "safety evaluation," "advanced AI chips," and "Secretary" are undefined, which leaves the scope of the prohibition and registry open to dispute.
2. **Sec. 5 (general):** No agency is designated to enforce the civil penalty, bring suit, or investigate violations. The Act also gives no rulemaking authority, subpoena power, or injunctive relief.
3. **Whole Act:** There is no severability clause, judicial review provision, or savings clause. If a court strikes one provision, such as the Sec. 2(1)(B) capability test, it is unclear whether the rest survives.

**Covered AI systems (Sec. 2(1))**

4. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** The threshold counts compute in "a single training run." Developers could split training across multiple runs or stages, and the text does not address fine-tuning or post-training that pushes cumulative compute past 10^26.
5. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** "Integer or floating-point operations" has no counting standard. Precision, sparsity, and hardware-specific operations can change the total, so the threshold is open to dispute.
6. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** "Secretary" is not defined. It is unclear which official makes the capability determination.
7. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** "Substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no benchmark, no determination procedure, and no notice requirement. This invites a vagueness challenge and makes it hard for companies to know in advance whether they are covered.
8. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** The clause reaches "any artificial intelligence system," not only models, and it has no pre-deployment review. Companies cannot be sure whether they are prohibited until an unreviewable determination is made.

**Jurisdiction (Sec. 2(2))**

9. **Sec. 2(2):** "U.S. person" covers citizens and state-chartered corporations only. It excludes LLCs, partnerships, lawful permanent residents, and federally chartered entities, which narrows the "anywhere" reach of Sec. 3(a).
10. **Sec. 2(2):** A U.S. company could train through a foreign-incorporated subsidiary. The text does not say whether the U.S. parent is liable for that work.
11. **Sec. 2(2):** "State" is undefined and does not say whether D.C. or the territories are included.

**Facility registry (Sec. 2(3), Sec. 4)**

12. **Sec. 2(3):** "Advanced AI chips" is undefined, and "contains" does not address distributed, leased, cloud, or colocation capacity. Operators could split capacity across sites or rent it from third parties to stay under 50,000 chips.
13. **Sec. 2(3) and Sec. 2(1)(A):** The facility threshold is not linked to the training threshold. A 10^26-operation run can be completed on fewer than 50,000 chips over a longer period, so covered training could take place outside the registry.
14. **Sec. 4(a):** The Act sets no registration deadline and no start date for the duty to register.
15. **Sec. 4(b):** Reports cover "computing workloads" in general. Operators are not required to identify covered AI system training runs, the compute used, or whether any run exceeded 10^26 operations, so the reports may not help enforcement.
16. **Sec. 4 and Sec. 5(a):** Sec. 5 penalizes only violations of Sec. 3. Failing to register, filing late or false reports, and falsely certifying a report carry no stated penalty.
17. **Sec. 4 (general):** There are no confidentiality protections for trade secrets and no rules on public disclosure or information-sharing. This will likely draw industry objections and could affect how useful the data is.

**Prohibition scope (Sec. 3(a))**

18. **Sec. 3(a):** "Train, develop, or deploy" is undefined. The prohibition does not clearly reach open-weight release, licensing, sale, or providing compute to third parties, so the Act may not stop a covered model from spreading once it has been built.
19. **Sec. 3(a):** The Act does not say whether "in the United States" refers to where training occurs or where the person is located. This matters for work done abroad by non-U.S. persons.

**Grandfathering (Sec. 3(b))**

20. **Sec. 3(b):** The exemption applies to any system whose "training began" before enactment, and "training began" is undefined. Once a system is exempt, the text exempts all of its development and deployment, with no time limit.
21. **Sec. 3(b) and Sec. 8:** The grandfather cutoff is the enactment date, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. Runs begun in that gap are neither exempt under the text nor clearly prohibited when they start, and the text does not say whether they must stop on the effective date.
22. **Sec. 3(b):** The Act does not say whether continued training or fine-tuning of an exempt system stays exempt, or whether a successor model built on an exempt system's weights counts as the same "system."

**Safety evaluation exemption (Sec. 3(c))**

23. **Sec. 3(c):** The exemption turns on "the purpose of safety evaluation," which is undefined. It has no limits on scope, scale, or duration and no requirement for registration, notice, independent oversight, or disclosure of results. Intent is hard to prove, so the exemption could cover large training or deployment activities labeled as evaluation.

**Penalties (Sec. 5)**

24. **Sec. 5(a):** "Per day on which the violation occurs" does not define the unit of violation. It is unclear whether a violation is counted per model, per run, per deployment, or per user, and whether an ongoing training run counts as one violation per day.
25. **Sec. 5(a) and Sec. 8:** Sec. 5(a) applies penalties to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but Sec. 8 delays the Act's effect by 180 days. The text does not say which date controls.
26. **Sec. 5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined. The Act also does not address officer or director liability, corporate criminal liability, or the statute of limitations.
27. **Sec. 5 (general):** The Act has no whistleblower protections, no provision for collecting penalties from foreign persons, and no injunctive or cease-operations authority.

**International agreement (Sec. 6)**

28. **Sec. 6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no obligation to reach an agreement and no consequence if negotiations fail. "Equivalent" is undefined, and the section has no effect on the domestic prohibition.

**Duration and sunset (Sec. 7)**

29. **Sec. 7(a) and 7(b):** Sec. 7(a) keeps the prohibition in place indefinitely until Congress passes a joint resolution, while Sec. 7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years. The text does not say which governs, and if 7(b) controls, 7(a) has little effect.
30. **Sec. 7(a):** The NAS report has no deadline, no requirement that it be produced, and no standard for what "developed safely" means. The joint resolution has no expedited procedure, and the text does not say whether it goes to the President for signature.
31. **Sec. 7(b):** The sunset has no savings clause. The text does not address liability for violations before the sunset, pending enforcement actions, or retention of registry data. The 10-year period also runs from enactment, not from the effective date, so the prohibition would be in force for about nine and a half years.

**Points for the explainer**

- Sec. 3(b) (grandfathering), Sec. 3(c) (safety evaluation exemption), and the Sec. 4/Sec. 5 enforcement gap are probably the strongest items to lead with.
- Some of these may be deliberate policy choices. Grandfathering and safety exemptions are common in legislation, so the explainer should describe them as design tradeoffs and not as drafting errors.
- The constitutional points (vagueness in Sec. 2(1)(B) and Sec. 3(a), and any First Amendment questions about code or weight publication) are my reading only. Before publishing, have a lawyer or the subject matter experts you consult check them.
