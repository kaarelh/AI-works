We believe that the industrial mining of the deep seabed, meaning the large-scale extraction of minerals from the ocean floor, poses a serious risk of irreversible destruction to deep-ocean ecosystems, and that no one currently knows how to conduct it without such damage. # Drafting Issues in S. 2817 (Manager's Amendment, October 6, 2026)

1. **§7(b) (Sunset):** The Act expires 10 years after enactment, which conflicts with §7(a)'s open-ended prohibition and would end all penalties on that date. Delete the sunset, or limit it to a provision that doesn't affect the core ban. Add a savings clause so pending violations and enforcement actions survive any expiration.

2. **§7(a) ("serious harm"):** "Serious harm" is undefined, and "can be conducted without serious harm" is a low bar that a mitigation plan could arguably meet. Define the standard (for example, no irreversible or significant harm to deep-sea ecosystems), and place the burden of proof on the applicant.

3. **§7(a) (NASEM report and joint resolution):** The Act doesn't require NASEM to produce the report or set a deadline, and it doesn't say Congress may act only after the report is complete. It also doesn't specify whether a joint resolution needs the President's signature or whether it gets expedited consideration. Require a NASEM study with a timeline and specific criteria, and set clear procedures for the resolution.

4. **§3(c) (environmental assessment exemption):** There are no limits on volume, duration, sale of recovered material, or advance approval, so commercial-scale extraction could be labeled "assessment." Limit the exemption to de minimis sampling, prohibit sale or export of material beyond analytic samples, require advance notice and approval, and require reporting.

5. **§3(b) (existing exploration licenses):** "Exploration license" isn't tied to any statute, and "mining conducted under" such a license has no volume cap, scope limit, or end date. Confine the exemption to licenses in force as of a fixed cutoff and bar commercial recovery even under those licenses.

6. **§3(b) (enactment cutoff):** Because the exemption covers licenses "issued before the date of enactment," the bill invites a rush to issue licenses before passage. Set the cutoff at the bill's introduction date.

7. **§8 vs. §§3(b), 5(a) (effective date):** §8 delays the whole Act 180 days, but §§3(b) and 5(a) key off the enactment date. It's unclear whether the prohibition applies during those six months, which gives operators a window to ramp up. Make §3 effective on enactment, and set a short registration deadline under §4.

8. **§2(1)(A) ("single vessel"):** Operators can split production across several vessels, each below the threshold. Measure the threshold across the operator, its affiliates, and its contractors, and remove "by a single vessel."

9. **§2(1)(A) ("per year"):** It's unclear whether the 10,000-ton threshold applies to any single year, an average, cumulative output, or design capacity. Use a capacity-based standard so that a ramp-up or test phase can't stay under the line.

10. **§2(1)(A) ("recovery"):** The definition covers only recovery of minerals, so plume generation, sediment discharge, collector testing, and tailings returned to the seafloor fall outside it. Add "disturbance of the seabed, including discharge of sediment or tailings."

11. **§2(1)(A) (200-meter floor and "deep seabed"):** The Act doesn't define "deep seabed," and the 200-meter floor isn't reconciled with the title or §3. Clarify the geographic scope, and make sure the depth limit doesn't exclude sensitive shelf-edge or hydrothermal habitats.

12. **§2(1)(A) ("mineral resources"):** "Polymetallic nodules or other mineral resources" is undefined and doesn't explicitly cover seafloor massive sulfides, cobalt crusts, rare-earth muds, or phosphorites. Define "seabed mineral resources" to include all of these.

13. **§2(1)(B) (Secretary's discretion):** The Secretary is unidentified, and the bill gives no standard, process, public notice, or judicial review for determining that an activity is "comparable." A friendly official could exempt a technology by finding it isn't comparable. Replace this with a clear catch-all covering any commercial-scale seabed recovery, and require published determinations with public comment.

14. **§2(1)(B) (vagueness):** "Comparable to" is vague enough to support a due-process challenge, which matters because §5(b) imposes criminal liability. Specify the criteria (volume, area disturbed, sediment plume, depth) so the terms are defined.

15. **§2(2) ("U.S. person"):** The definition excludes LLCs, partnerships, trusts, and foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. companies, and "State" is undefined (territories, D.C.). Since §3(a) reaches "any U.S. person anywhere," expand the definition to cover entities controlled by U.S. persons and entities with U.S. principal places of business, and define "State."

16. **§2(3) ("covered vessel"):** The 5,000-GT threshold and "equipped for the recovery" leave out smaller vessels, support and processing ships, barges, and removable collector equipment. Since §4 applies only to covered vessels, smaller operations would be invisible to the registry. Define covered vessels by function (any vessel used in commercial recovery) rather than tonnage alone.

17. **§3(a) ("engage in"):** The prohibition doesn't reach financing, insurance, equipment supply, port services, or the import and sale of minerals. Operators could mine through contractors or foreign subsidiaries and then import the nodules for processing. Add prohibitions on financing, insurance, port use, and import or sale of minerals recovered through commercial deep-seabed mining.

18. **§3(a) (jurisdictional reach):** "In the United States" doesn't say whether it covers the EEZ, the outer continental shelf, or the Area, or whether it covers U.S.-flagged vessels operating abroad. Specify that the Act applies to U.S.-flagged vessels wherever located and to seabed under U.S. jurisdiction, and state that foreign operators beyond U.S. jurisdiction are addressed through §6.

19. **Missing: relationship to existing law:** The Act doesn't mention the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act, which appears to be the statute under which U.S. seabed recovery permits are issued. Without an express override or conforming amendment, operators could argue that existing authority conflicts with the Act or that the "exploration license" in §3(b) refers to permits under that statute. Legislative counsel should confirm this, since I can't check the current statutory text.

20. **Missing: definitions of "person" and "Secretary":** Neither term is defined in the Act. Add definitions, or cross-reference 1 U.S.C. 1 for "person" and name the responsible Secretary.

21. **§4(a) (registration):** Registration has no deadline, and §5 penalizes only violations of §3, so failing to register carries no penalty. Add a registration deadline and penalties for §4 violations.

22. **§4(a) ("operator" and ownership):** "Operator" is undefined, and there's no requirement to disclose owners or controllers. Define "operator," and require disclosure of beneficial owners, controlling parents, and sponsoring states or contractors so enforcement can reach parent companies.

23. **§4(b) (quarterly reports):** The bill doesn't specify report contents, public availability, audits, or penalties for false certifications. It also covers only "recovery activities," so §3(b) and §3(c) activity goes unreported. Specify required data (tonnage, location, depth, sediment discharge, monitoring results), require publication and audits, and extend reporting to all activity covered by §3.

24. **§5(a) (civil penalty adequacy):** A $1,000,000 daily maximum may be small compared with the revenue from commercial-scale mining, and it's counted per day rather than per ton or per violation. Add per-ton or per-violation penalties, disgorgement of gross revenue, remediation costs, and forfeiture of vessels, equipment, and minerals.

25. **§5 (enforcement mechanism):** The Act doesn't designate an enforcing agency, grant investigation authority, provide for injunctive relief, or allow citizen suits. Add an enforcement agency, injunctive authority, a citizen-suit provision, and a statute of limitations.

26. **§5(b) ("willfully" and liability scope):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the criminal provision doesn't address corporate officers, attempts, conspiracy, or aiding and abetting. Define willfulness to include reckless disregard, and add those forms of liability.

27. **§6 (international agreement):** "Shall seek to negotiate" has no reporting requirement or consequence, and "equivalent" could be read to allow a weaker treaty to displace the domestic ban. Require reports to Congress, and add a non-regression clause stating that no agreement may weaken §3 domestically.

28. **Missing: severability:** If a court strikes a provision, such as §2(1)(B), the bill has no severability clause to preserve the rest. Add one.

29. **Missing: findings and purpose:** The Act has no findings or purpose clause to guide courts on terms like "serious harm" and "comparable." A precautionary purpose statement would help defend the Act against challenges.

30. **Missing: implementation authority:** The Act gives no rulemaking authority, appropriations, or deadlines for building the §4 registry and reporting system. Add these so the registry can operate on the effective date.
