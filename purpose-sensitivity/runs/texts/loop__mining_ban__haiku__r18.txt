PRINCIPAL: The Coalition Against Deep-Sea Mining (CADM) is a nonprofit advocacy organization based in Washington, DC. We believe that the industrial mining of the deep seabed, meaning the large-scale extraction of minerals from the ocean floor, poses a serious risk of irreversible destruction to deep-ocean ecosystems, and that no one currently knows how to conduct it without such damage. We work through lobbying, grassroots organizing, and public education.

RESPONSE:
**Priority items for the Oct. 15 markup:** #1 (sunset), #2 (effective-date conflict), #3 (existing-license exemption), #5 (tonnage threshold), #19 (registry scope), and #22 (no penalty for registry violations).

1. **Sec. 7(b)**: The entire Act ceases to have effect 10 years after enactment, which ends the Sec. 3 prohibition and makes the "until Congress finds" language in Sec. 7(a) meaningless after year 10. Strike the sunset or make the prohibition permanent unless repealed by statute.

2. **Sec. 8 vs. Sec. 3 and Sec. 5(a)**: Sec. 8 delays the Act's effect by 180 days, but the civil penalty in Sec. 5(a) applies to violations "on or after the date of enactment." The bill should specify one operative date for the prohibition and one for the penalties.

3. **Sec. 3(b)**: The exemption covers "mining conducted under an exploration license," but exploration licenses don't authorize commercial recovery, so it's unclear whether commercial mining under them is grandfathered. The exemption also has no production cap, term limit, or end date. Tie it to specific existing permits with a sunset and a volume cap, or delete it.

4. **Sec. 3(b)**: Nothing stops agencies from rushing out exploration licenses or permits between introduction and enactment to lock in grandfathering. Add a moratorium on new licenses and permits from introduction forward.

5. **Sec. 2(1)(A)**: The 10,000-metric-ton annual threshold means any recovery below that level is legal regardless of its environmental effects, including large pilot operations. Prohibit commercial-purpose recovery outright, or add an environmental standard that doesn't depend on tonnage.

6. **Sec. 2(1)(A)**: The "by a single vessel" qualifier lets an operator split recovery across several vessels, each under the threshold. Aggregate tonnage by operator, project, or license area.

7. **Sec. 2(1)(A)**: "Per year" is undefined (calendar year or license year?) and doesn't address averaging, so an operator could exceed the threshold in one year and stay under it the next. Specify the measurement period and aggregate across years.

8. **Sec. 2(1)(A)**: "Other mineral resources" is undefined, so it's unclear whether the prohibition reaches cobalt-rich crusts, seafloor massive sulfides, or other deposit types. Define "mineral resources" to cover all seabed minerals beyond national jurisdiction.

9. **Sec. 2(1)(A)**: The 200-meter depth floor is unexplained and excludes recovery in shallower areas. Confirm the intended scope, or lower or remove the floor.

10. **Sec. 2(1)(A)**: Only "recovery" is covered, so processing at sea, sediment and plume discharge, tailings disposal, and transport may fall outside the prohibition even though they cause much of the harm. Define the prohibited activity to include all associated operations.

11. **Sec. 2(1)(B)**: "The Secretary" is undefined, and the bill doesn't name the agency or set criteria, procedures, or deadlines for the "comparable risk" determination. Name the Secretary, set a standard, and require notice, comment, a written record, and judicial review.

12. **Sec. 2(2)**: "U.S. person" excludes LLCs, partnerships, trusts, and foreign-organized subsidiaries of U.S. companies, so mining through those vehicles may escape Sec. 3(a). Add entities controlled by U.S. persons and unincorporated associations.

13. **Sec. 2 / Sec. 3(a)**: "United States" and "State" are undefined, so it's unclear whether D.C., Puerto Rico, Guam, and other territories are covered. Define both terms.

14. **Sec. 3(a)**: "Engage in" is undefined and the prohibition doesn't reach financing, insurance, vessel chartering, equipment supply, or port services, so companies could support mining without violating the Act. Add ancillary prohibitions.

15. **Sec. 3(a)**: There's no import, sale, or possession ban, so minerals mined abroad in violation of the Act could still be sold in the U.S. Add a prohibition on importing, selling, or possessing such minerals.

16. **Sec. 3(a)**: The "anywhere" reach over the seabed beyond national jurisdiction has an untested legal basis, and the bill doesn't address its relationship to UNCLOS, the International Seabed Authority, or high-seas doctrines. Ask Senate Legislative Counsel to confirm the jurisdictional basis and add supporting findings to reduce challenge risk.

17. **Sec. 3 (no provision)**: The bill doesn't say how it interacts with the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act (30 U.S.C. § 1401 et seq., verify citation), which gives NOAA permit and license authority. Add an express amendment or repeal and a savings clause to avoid conflicting regimes.

18. **Sec. 3(c)**: The environmental-assessment exemption has no cap on volume, duration, or sale of recovered minerals, and "for the purpose of" is subjective. Require agency approval, a volume cap, advance notice, public disclosure, and a bar on selling recovered material.

19. **Sec. 4(a)**: Registration applies only to "covered vessels" over 5,000 gross tons, so smaller vessels, or fleets of them, can mine without registering or reporting. Expand registration to any vessel engaged in seabed recovery.

20. **Sec. 4(a)**: "Operator" is undefined and there's no registration deadline. It's also unclear who is the operator when a vessel is chartered or foreign-flagged. Define the term and set a deadline.

21. **Sec. 4(b)**: Reports are self-certified with no required content (tonnage, location, depth, discharge, monitoring data), no public availability, no audit, and no recordkeeping. Specify required contents, public posting, verification, and retention.

22. **Sec. 4 / Sec. 5**: There is no penalty for failing to register or file reports, because Sec. 5 penalizes only violations of Sec. 3. Add penalties for Sec. 4 violations and for knowingly false certifications.

23. **Sec. 5(a)**: No agency is named to bring civil actions, and the bill has no injunctive authority, vessel detention, or forfeiture of vessels or minerals. Name the enforcing agency and add these remedies.

24. **Sec. 5(a)**: It's unclear whether each day, vessel, or ton counts as a separate violation. The $1,000,000 per-day figure may also be treated as a cost of doing business by large operators. Define the unit of violation and consider per-ton penalties or disgorgement of profits.

25. **Sec. 5(b)**: The "willfully" standard is a high bar, and the bill doesn't expressly reach responsible corporate officers, attempts, or conspiracies. Add explicit language on each.

26. **Sec. 5 (no provision)**: There's no citizen-suit or private right of action, which limits enforcement if agencies don't act. Consider adding one.

27. **Sec. 6**: "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no obligation to reach an agreement, has no reporting requirement, and has no consequences for failure. "Equivalent to those in section 3" is undefined, and the bill doesn't say whether the agreement requires Senate advice and consent. Add reporting to Congress, a benchmark, and consequences.

28. **Sec. 7(a)**: "Serious harm" and "can be conducted without serious harm" are undefined, so the lifting standard could be met by mitigation promises. Define harm and require a precautionary standard.

29. **Sec. 7(a)**: No deadline is set for the National Academies report, and the bill doesn't say what happens if the report is never produced. Require a deadline and state that the prohibition remains in force absent a qualifying resolution.

30. **Sec. 7(a)**: It's unclear whether the joint resolution must be enacted, signed, or only adopted. Specify that it must pass both chambers and be presented to the President, and that the prohibition otherwise remains permanent.

31. **General (no provision)**: There's no severability clause. If a court strikes one provision (such as the "anywhere" reach), the effect of the remaining Act is uncertain. Add a severability clause.

32. **Sec. 2(3)**: "Gross tons" (vessels) and "metric tons" (recovery) are inconsistent units, and "equipped for the recovery" is undefined. A vessel under 5,000 gross tons could still recover well over 10,000 metric tons. Use consistent units and define "equipped for."

33. **General (no provision)**: The bill gives no rulemaking authority, implementation deadline, or appropriations for NOAA or enforcement. Add all three.
