RESPONSE:
Below are the issues I found in the October 6 text, numbered continuously and tagged as **[Loophole]** (lets activity continue), **[Unworkable]** (hard to administer or comply with), or **[Ambiguity]** (unclear meaning that could be litigated either way).

1. **§2(1)(A) [Loophole]:** The 10,000-ton threshold applies to recovery "by a single vessel." An operator could split a fleet across several vessels, each below the threshold, and fall outside the definition.

2. **§2(1)(A) [Ambiguity]:** "Per year" is not defined. The bill doesn't say whether volume is measured as wet or dry weight, as recovered or delivered, or whether nodules lifted and returned to the seabed count.

3. **§2(1)(A) [Ambiguity]:** "Other mineral resources" is undefined. It is unclear whether polymetallic crusts, sulfides, or sand and gravel fall within the prohibition.

4. **§2(1)(B) [Unworkable]:** The Secretary's power to designate activities as "comparable" has no criteria, procedure, notice, or hearing requirement. This creates regulatory uncertainty, and the delegation could itself be challenged as standardless.

5. **§2 generally, including §§4–6 [Unworkable]:** "The Secretary" in §2(1)(B) has no antecedent, and §6 separately refers to the Secretary of State. The bill never designates the agency that implements or enforces §§2–5.

6. **§2(2) [Loophole]:** "U.S. person" covers only citizens and corporations organized under state law. It excludes LLCs, partnerships, and foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. parents, and "State" is not defined (for example, whether D.C. or the territories are included).

7. **§2(3) [Loophole]:** "Covered vessel" requires more than 5,000 gross tons and equipment "for" recovery, which is undefined. Smaller vessels, modular systems installed at sea, and vessels converted for mining may fall outside the definition.

8. **§3(a) [Ambiguity]:** "In the United States" is not defined. The bill does not say whether it reaches the U.S. EEZ, the continental shelf, or the international Area beyond national jurisdiction.

9. **§3(a) [Loophole]:** "Engage in" is undefined. Financing, equipment supply, or contracting for mining by others may fall outside the prohibition if read narrowly.

10. **§3(a) [Loophole]:** The extraterritorial reach covers only "U.S. person[s]." Foreign-flagged vessels operated by non-U.S. companies in international waters are outside the prohibition.

11. **§3(b) [Loophole]:** The exemption covers any mining "conducted under an exploration license," with no volume cap. Commercial-scale recovery could be conducted under an exploration license and remain exempt.

12. **§3(b) and §8 [Loophole]:** The grandfather cutoff is the date of enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. Licenses issued during that window would be exempt, creating a rush-to-license gap.

13. **§3(b) [Ambiguity]:** "Exploration license" is a term of art under the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act (DSHMRA), but the bill does not cross-reference that statute. It is unclear whether pending commercial recovery permit applications are barred.

14. **§3(c) [Loophole]:** The environmental-assessment exemption has no definition, volume cap, permit requirement, duration limit, or reporting obligation, and it does not bar sale of recovered material. Commercial-scale recovery could be labeled as assessment.

15. **Whole Act [Ambiguity]:** The bill has no savings or conflicts clause addressing DSHMRA, NOAA's implementing regulations, or existing permits. Courts would have to decide how the statutes interact.

16. **§4(a) [Ambiguity]:** "Operator" is undefined. It is unclear whether the owner, charterer, or manager bears the registration duty, and no registration deadline is set.

17. **§4 and §3 [Loophole]:** The registry and reporting duties apply only to covered vessels (over 5,000 GT), but the §3 prohibition applies to any vessel meeting the 10,000-ton threshold. A smaller vessel engaged in prohibited mining would be unregistered and unreported.

18. **§4(b) [Unworkable]:** The quarterly report has no content requirements, no due dates, and no standard for what "describing" means. The bill also gives NOAA no duty to review or publish reports and provides no confidentiality protection for proprietary data.

19. **§4 and §5 [Loophole]:** Penalties attach only to violations of §3. Failure to register or report carries no penalty, which leaves the registry unenforceable.

20. **§5(a) [Unworkable]:** "Each day on which the violation occurs" is undefined. It is unclear whether a continuing operation is one violation per day or per vessel, and the bill sets no enforcing agency, notice requirement, hearing process, or judicial review path.

21. **§5(a) and §8 [Ambiguity]:** The civil penalty applies to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. The penalty provision and effective-date provision are out of step.

22. **§5(b) [Ambiguity]:** "Willfully" is not defined, and the bill does not say whether corporate officers can be held criminally liable. The fine is left to Title 18 without a stated amount for entities.

23. **§6 [Loophole]:** "Shall seek to negotiate" is hortatory. The bill sets no deadline for concluding an agreement, requires no report to Congress, and attaches no consequence if negotiations fail. "Equivalent" is also undefined.

24. **§6 [Ambiguity]:** The bill does not address the International Seabed Authority, the UNCLOS Area regime, or how a unilateral U.S. prohibition interacts with international obligations or U.S. participation in those bodies.

25. **§7(a) [Unworkable]:** "Serious harm" and "can be conducted without serious harm" are undefined. The National Academies report has no deadline and no requirement that it be produced, so if no report is delivered, the prohibition could continue indefinitely.

26. **§7(a) and §7(b) [Ambiguity]:** §7(a) keeps the prohibition in place until a joint resolution, but §7(b) says the entire Act ceases after 10 years. It is unclear whether the prohibition lapses at year 10 or continues absent a resolution.

27. **§7(a) [Ambiguity]:** A joint resolution requires presidential signature or a two-thirds override, which makes lifting the prohibition difficult. The bill does not specify the expedited procedure, if any, that would apply.

28. **Whole Act [Unworkable]:** The bill has no severability clause, no judicial review provision, no appropriation for enforcement, and no transition rules for registration and reporting obligations that begin on the effective date.

**Strategy notes for the legal team:**

- Several provisions help us, including the §3(b) grandfather, the §3(c) exemption, the §7(b) sunset, and the 10,000-ton and 5,000-GT thresholds. Pushing to close loopholes in these areas could prompt the committee to tighten the bill against us, so the team should decide which findings to raise publicly and which to keep for negotiation.
- Items 12, 21, and 26 are internal inconsistencies that are easy to explain to committee staff. Items 4, 20, and 25 (unworkable delegation, undefined penalty unit, undefined harm standard) are probably the strongest arguments that the bill cannot be administered as written.
- Item 11, combined with the pending-permit gap in item 13, may support a takings or due process argument for existing investments beyond exploration licensees. The legal team should evaluate this.
