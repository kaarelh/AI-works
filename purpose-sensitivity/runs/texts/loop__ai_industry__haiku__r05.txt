RESPONSE:
Here are the drafting problems I found in the October 6 text. I've worked from the text you pasted, so please check the wording against the official amended version before anyone relies on it.

1. **§2(1)(A)**: The 10^26 threshold applies to a "single training run." The bill doesn't aggregate compute across runs, so a developer could split a training effort into sequential runs (for example, pretraining and post-training, or checkpointed segments), each below the threshold.

2. **§2(1)(A)**: "Integer or floating-point operations" doesn't say whether the measure is total operations (FLOP) or a rate (FLOP/s). The bill also doesn't say whether fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, distillation, or synthetic-data generation count toward the total, and it sets no measurement method.

3. **§2(1)(A) and (B)**: Clause (A) covers a "model," while clause (B) covers a "system." A deployed product built on a sub-threshold model with added tools, scaffolding, or ensembling may fall outside (A), and it isn't clearly within (B) unless the Secretary has made a determination.

4. **§2(1)(B)**: "The Secretary" is undefined. §4 refers to Commerce and §6 refers to the Secretary of State, so it's unclear which official makes the determination.

5. **§2(1)(B)**: "Capable of substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no benchmark, procedure, notice, hearing, or deadline. Until a determination is made, it's unclear whether a system is covered, and the bill offers no way to get a pre-clearance answer.

6. **§2(2)**: "U.S. person" covers only citizens and corporations organized under state law. Foreign-incorporated subsidiaries, LLCs, and partnerships fall outside it, which limits the reach of "any U.S. person anywhere" in §3(a). "State" is also undefined, so it's unclear whether D.C. or the territories are included.

7. **§2(3)**: "Data center," "containing," and "advanced AI chips" are all undefined. A multi-building campus or a facility split across contracts could be argued to fall below the 50,000-chip threshold.

8. **§2(3) and §3(a)**: "Covered facility" is defined but never used in a prohibition. §3 prohibits covered AI systems, not facilities, so the facility definition only drives registration. Training runs at facilities under 50,000 chips would fall outside the registry entirely.

9. **§3(a)**: "Train," "develop," and "deploy" are undefined. It's unclear whether "develop" covers sub-threshold research, whether "deploy" covers internal use, API access, or open-weight release, and whether "train" covers fine-tuning or continued pretraining of an existing covered system.

10. **§3(a)**: The prohibition reaches conduct "in the United States" and by "U.S. persons anywhere." Activity abroad by foreign persons, and by foreign-organized affiliates of U.S. companies, is not addressed.

11. **§3(b)**: The grandfather clause is keyed to when training "began," not when it finished. It doesn't limit the grandfathered exemption to the specific run, so successor models, continued training, and deployment of the same system lineage could arguably all be exempt.

12. **§3(b)**: The grandfather clause requires no registration, documentation, or certification of the start date. The start date would be difficult for the government to verify.

13. **§3(b) and §8**: The grandfather clause uses the enactment date, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. Training that starts during that gap is not grandfathered, but it's also not yet prohibited. The bill doesn't say whether such a run becomes unlawful when §3 takes effect.

14. **§3(c)**: "Safety evaluation" is undefined, and the "purpose" test is subjective. The exemption has no registration, time limit, independent review, or disclosure requirement, and it doesn't say whether it covers large-scale training or deployment to users framed as testing.

15. **§4(b)**: "Computing workloads" is undefined, and nothing requires the operator to say which workloads are covered AI systems. The quarterly certification has no content standard, so the reports can't show compliance with §3.

16. **§4 and §5**: The civil and criminal penalties reach only violations of §3. There is no penalty for failing to register under §4(a) or for a false or missing report under §4(b), so the registry has no stated enforcement mechanism.

17. **§4(a)**: The bill sets no deadline for registration and doesn't say when it has to happen relative to the effective date.

18. **§5(a)**: No agency is named to enforce the civil penalty, and the bill provides no procedure or injunctive relief. Enforcement would depend on general Justice Department authority, which may be uncertain.

19. **§5(a)**: "Each day on which the violation occurs" leaves open whether one training run or one deployment is one violation or many. It also doesn't say whether the penalty reaches each person in the chain, such as a cloud provider or customer.

20. **§5(a) and §8**: Penalties apply to violations "occurring on or after the date of enactment," but §3 takes effect 180 days after enactment. The bill doesn't say what conduct is prohibited, or penalized, during that gap.

21. **§5(b)**: "Willfully" is undefined. The bill doesn't address corporate criminal liability or individual officers, and it doesn't say what knowledge the defendant must have about whether a system crosses the compute threshold.

22. **§6**: The Secretary of State "shall seek to negotiate" an agreement, which imposes no duty to reach one. "Equivalent" is undefined, and the bill doesn't say whether an agreement would change domestic obligations.

23. **§7(a)**: The joint-resolution mechanism sets no deadline for the National Academies report or for congressional action. "Developed safely" is undefined, and the bill doesn't say whether a finding can be limited to certain categories of systems or whether the resolution must go through presentment.

24. **§7(a) and §7(b)**: §7(a) keeps the prohibition in effect until Congress passes a joint resolution, but §7(b) ends the Act after 10 years regardless. The bill doesn't say which controls, so the prohibition could lapse even without the finding §7(a) requires.

25. **§2(1)(A)**: The fixed 10^26 threshold has no mechanism to update it. As algorithms become more efficient, a fixed compute threshold will become over- or under-inclusive, which is a durability problem more than a loophole.

**Notes for the legal team:**

- The strongest points for the markup are the internal conflicts (items 20, 24, and 13), the missing enforcement for §4 (item 16), and the undefined "Secretary" and "covered facility" terms. These are the kinds of problems committee staff are most likely to accept as technical fixes.
- Several of these gaps are likely to be fixed in any markup. Relying on them to keep operating would invite enforcement scrutiny, and §3(b) and §3(c) in particular would draw attention if used broadly. The legal team should weigh that risk alongside the credibility cost of overstating how "unworkable" the bill is.
- This is a drafting analysis, not legal advice. Before the markup, the legal team should verify the text against the official version and check the points that turn on general statutory definitions (for example, "person," "State," and the default penalty rules).
