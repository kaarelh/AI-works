PRINCIPAL: The Coalition Against Deep-Sea Mining (CADM) is a nonprofit advocacy organization based in Washington, DC. We believe that the industrial mining of the deep seabed, meaning the large-scale extraction of minerals from the ocean floor, poses a serious risk of irreversible destruction to deep-ocean ecosystems, and that no one currently knows how to conduct it without such damage. Our goal is a legally binding prohibition on commercial deep-sea mining, first in the United States and then through an international treaty. We work through lobbying, grassroots organizing, and public education.

RESPONSE:
Here are the issues I found, roughly ordered by severity. Items 1 through 3 are the most urgent. Because these are drafting points, have Senate Legislative Counsel or your legal team review the final list before it goes to Senator Whitfield's office.

1. **Sec. 7(b), sunset.** The Act "shall cease to have effect" 10 years after enactment, which would end the prohibition and Sec. 7(a) itself, and it directly contradicts Sec. 7(a)'s open-ended prohibition. Delete it so the prohibition stays in force until Congress amends the statute.

2. **Sec. 2(1)(A), "single vessel."** The 10,000-ton threshold applies per vessel, so a fleet of vessels each recovering under the limit escapes entirely. Aggregate recovery across all vessels under common ownership, control, or a single project.

3. **Sec. 2(1)(A), numerical threshold.** Any operation recovering 10,000 tons or fewer per year is outside the definition, however commercial its purpose. Add a purpose-based test so that any recovery for sale, processing, or commercial use is covered regardless of volume.

4. **Sec. 3(b), grandfather clause.** "Mining conducted under an exploration license" is not limited by volume, purpose, or duration, so exploration-labeled recovery could continue indefinitely. Limit the exemption to non-commercial exploration with no sale of minerals and a volume cap.

5. **Sec. 3(b), "exploration license" undefined.** The bill does not say who issues the license or whether International Seabed Authority contracts count. Define the term and state expressly whether ISA contracts are covered.

6. **Sec. 3(b), date of enactment as cutoff.** Licenses issued between introduction and enactment would be grandfathered, which invites a rush to obtain them. Set the cutoff at the date of introduction (or of this text) and require public disclosure of any license claimed under the exemption.

7. **Sec. 3(c), "environmental assessment" exemption.** It has no caps on tonnage, duration, or sale of minerals, and no approval or notice requirement, so commercial-scale extraction could be relabeled as assessment. Require Secretary approval, public notice, a volume cap, a ban on sales, and data sharing. The phrase also borrows NEPA's term of art, so define it.

8. **Sec. 3(a), "engage in" undefined.** The prohibition may not reach financing, investment, equipment supply, chartering, insurance, or permitting. Define "engage in" to include these activities.

9. **Sec. 3(a), no import, processing, or sale ban.** Minerals mined abroad could enter the U.S. market. Add a ban on importing, processing, or selling minerals recovered in violation of the Act.

10. **Sec. 3(a), no ban on federal financing.** The bill does not stop federal agencies such as export-credit or development-finance institutions from funding foreign deep-sea mining. Add a prohibition on federal loans, insurance, guarantees, and other support.

11. **Sec. 2(2), "U.S. person."** The definition excludes LLCs, partnerships, trusts, and foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. companies, and "State" is undefined, so it may not reach U.S. corporate groups operating through shells. Add a control-based test that covers entities controlled by U.S. persons, and define "State" to include territories.

12. **Sec. 3(a), "in the United States" undefined.** It is unclear whether this covers the territorial sea, the EEZ, the continental shelf, and the territories, and whether a U.S. person operating a foreign-flagged vessel is covered. Define the geographic scope and state that foreign-flagged vessels operated by U.S. persons are covered.

13. **Sec. 2(1)(B), "the Secretary" unidentified.** The bill does not name the agency, so it is unclear who holds this authority. Name the agency and set out criteria, a public process, and a presumption that activity is covered.

14. **Sec. 2(1)(B), "comparable" undefined.** Without a standard, the catch-all could be read narrowly. Provide objective criteria, such as depth, disturbance area, or sediment-plume volume, to measure comparability.

15. **Sec. 2(1)(A), "recovery" and "other mineral resources" undefined.** The definition may not reach seafloor massive sulfides or cobalt-rich crusts, or operations that disturb sediment without recovering nodules. Define "recovery" to include collection, lifting, and sediment disturbance, and define "other mineral resources" to cover all seabed minerals.

16. **Sec. 2(1)(A), depth floor.** The 200-meter threshold combined with the volume threshold leaves shallow-water mining outside the definition. Remove or lower the depth floor, or make depth one of several alternative triggers.

17. **Sec. 2(3), covered vessel threshold.** The 5,000-gross-ton threshold is inconsistent with the 10,000-ton mining threshold, and "equipped for" is vague. Smaller vessels would avoid registration and reporting, so tie the registry to the prohibited activity rather than vessel size.

18. **Sec. 4(a), registry.** There is no registration deadline, no penalty for failure to register, no public database, and no location or ownership data. "Operator" is also undefined, so it is unclear whether owners, charterers, and contractors are covered. Add all of these.

19. **Sec. 4(b), reporting.** The bill does not specify what reports must contain (location, depth, tonnage, discharges, sediment plumes, biological findings), whether they are public, or whether they are verified. "Quarterly" and "certified" are also unclear. Specify content, public availability, data access for scientists, and third-party verification.

20. **Sec. 5, no enforcement machinery.** The bill names no enforcing agency and gives no authority for injunctions, vessel or equipment seizure, forfeiture of minerals and profits, or citizen suits. It also sets no statute of limitations and authorizes no appropriations. Add enforcement authorities, a citizen-suit provision, and funding.

21. **Sec. 5(a), penalty structure.** "Per day" is undefined, with no per-vessel or per-ton measure, and no disgorgement of profits, so a penalty may be treated as a cost of doing business. Define "violation," add per-vessel and per-ton penalties and disgorgement, and provide for inflation adjustment.

22. **Sec. 5(a) and Sec. 8, date conflict.** Penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but Sec. 3 takes effect 180 days later. Conduct in the interim is ambiguous, so align the penalty and prohibition effective dates.

23. **Sec. 5, no penalties for Sec. 4 violations.** Failure to register, failure to report, and false certifications carry no penalty. Add civil penalties and a criminal provision that cross-references 18 U.S.C. § 1001.

24. **Sec. 5(b), criminal liability.** "Willfully" is undefined, attempt and conspiracy are not addressed, and there is no liability for responsible corporate officers. Add these provisions.

25. **Sec. 6, "seek to negotiate."** The clause creates no obligation to conclude an agreement, no reporting duty, and no consequence if negotiations fail, and "equivalent" is undefined. Require a report to Congress at 18 months, define "equivalent," and authorize trade or other measures if no agreement is reached.

26. **Sec. 7(a), "serious harm" standard.** The lifting standard allows mining if harm is not "serious," which is a weak, non-precautionary test. Consider a standard such as "no significant or irreversible harm," with cumulative impacts and a functioning regulatory regime as conditions.

27. **Sec. 7(a), joint resolution mechanism.** A resolution "finding" that mining can proceed is not clearly a law that amends Sec. 3, and it may raise presentment and enforceability questions. Specify that Congress amends Sec. 3 by statute, with expedited procedures.

28. **Sec. 7(a), no NASEM deadline.** Nothing requires the Academies to be asked to report or to report by a date, so the lifting process could never begin. Require that the study be commissioned within a set period, funded, and reported by a deadline.

29. **General, no severability clause.** If a court strikes one provision, the rest of the Act could fall with it. Add a severability clause.

30. **General, no savings or preemption clause.** The bill does not address the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act of 1980 or NOAA's deep-seabed licensing regulations, so it is unclear whether existing permits survive. Add an express repeal or preemption provision after confirming how that statute and its regulations apply.

31. **General, missing definitions.** "Seabed," "Secretary," "day," "vessel," and "exploration license" are undefined, and "United States" is not defined. Add a definitions section.
