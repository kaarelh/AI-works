RESPONSE:
**Drafting and enforcement issues in S. 2817 (text as amended October 6, 2026)**

These are drafting observations from the text you provided. Some may be intentional, so the explainer should describe them as gaps or risks rather than assume bad intent.

1. **§2(1)(A):** The threshold applies "by a single vessel" and "per year," with no aggregation rule. An operator could run several vessels, each under 10,000 metric tons a year, and fall outside the definition entirely.

2. **§2(1)(A):** "Recovery" and "other mineral resources" are undefined. It is unclear whether the definition covers cobalt crusts or seafloor massive sulfides (not nodules), or whether lifting, onboard processing, and sediment discharge count as "recovery."

3. **§2(1)(A):** The 200-meter depth floor has no measurement rule, so it is unclear whether depth is measured from the surface, the vessel, or the seabed. The bill also doesn't address how it relates to other seabed mining regimes.

4. **§2(1)(B):** The Secretary's "comparable risk" determination has no standard, procedure, deadline, or judicial review provision. "Secretary" is never identified (Commerce, Interior, or State?), which invites nondelegation and vagueness challenges and leaves the bright-line test in (A) uncertain.

5. **§2(2):** "U.S. person" covers only natural citizens and state-chartered corporations. It excludes federal corporations, LLCs, partnerships, and trusts, so an operation held through an LLC may fall outside §3(a)'s "anywhere" prong.

6. **§2(2):** "State" is undefined. It is unclear whether the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, Guam, and other territories count.

7. **§2(3) and §4(a):** The 5,000 gross ton vessel threshold is unrelated to the 10,000 metric ton commercial threshold, and the two use different units. A vessel can recover more than 10,000 metric tons while under 5,000 gross tons and never be registered under §4.

8. **§2(3):** "Equipped for the recovery" is undefined. It is unclear whether this means designed, outfitted, or actually used for recovery, and owners could argue a vessel isn't "equipped" even when it is mining.

9. **§3(a):** "Engage in" is undefined. The bill doesn't say whether preparation, stockpiling, transport, or processing counts, and it doesn't prohibit financing, purchasing, or importing the resulting minerals.

10. **§3(a):** The bill doesn't state a jurisdictional basis for reaching conduct abroad, and "person" is undefined. Foreign-flagged vessels operated by foreign companies outside U.S. jurisdiction appear to be outside the prohibition, and challengers could invoke the presumption against extraterritoriality.

11. **§3(b):** "Exploration license" is undefined and doesn't name the issuing regime (such as NOAA licenses under the 1980 Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act or ISA contracts). The carve-out covers "mining conducted under" these licenses with no cap on volume, area, or duration, so exploration authority could support commercial-scale recovery.

12. **§3(b):** "Issued before the date of enactment" doesn't address renewals, amendments, transfers, or expansions after enactment, so grandfathered licenses could be extended indefinitely.

13. **§3(b):** The heading says "existing operations," but the text refers to licenses. The bill also doesn't say whether it supersedes or amends DSHMRA or what happens to pending permit applications, which creates a conflict-of-law question.

14. **§3(c):** "Environmental assessment" is undefined, and the exemption has no volume, duration, permit, reporting, or government-approval requirement. Commercial-scale recovery could be labeled as testing, making this the most significant loophole in the bill.

15. **§4(a):** Registration has no deadline or content requirements, and "operator" is undefined (owner, charterer, or manager?). Because registration applies only to covered vessels, mining by smaller vessels goes unregistered.

16. **§4(b):** Quarterly reports don't require tonnage, location, depth, or disposal data, have no submission deadline, and aren't required to be public. As written, the reports cannot be used to verify compliance with the 10,000-ton threshold.

17. **§4 and §5(a):** The civil penalty applies only to violations of §3, so failing to register or filing missing or false reports carries no clear penalty. The bill also doesn't say whether the certification is subject to false-statement liability, though general federal law may apply.

18. **§5(a):** The enforcement mechanics are missing: no enforcing agency, administrative process, hearing rights, penalty factors, injunctive or cease-and-desist authority, vessel or mineral forfeiture, or restoration remedies. It is also unclear how per-day penalties are counted for ongoing operations (per vessel or per operation?).

19. **§5(a) and §8:** The penalty applies to "any violation occurring on or after the date of enactment," but the prohibition doesn't take effect until 180 days later. The bill doesn't say when liability actually begins.

20. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill doesn't say whether corporate officers are liable or what fine applies beyond the general title 18 provisions. Proving knowledge that the conduct was unlawful can be a high bar in corporate cases.

21. **全 bill (no section):** The bill has no citizen-suit, private right of action, or whistleblower provision, so enforcement depends entirely on government agencies.

22. **§6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" doesn't require an agreement to be concluded, doesn't require a report to Congress, and sets no consequence if talks fail. "Equivalent" is undefined, and the bill doesn't address the ISA or the U.S. status as a non-party to UNCLOS.

23. **§7(a):** "Serious harm" is undefined. The bill sets no deadline for the NASEM report, doesn't require Congress to request it, and doesn't say whether "a report of" refers to any report or a specific one.

24. **§7(a):** The bill doesn't specify whether a joint resolution is presented to the President, which would allow a veto, and it doesn't say what legal effect enactment would have (repeal, suspension, or something else). No expedited procedures are provided.

25. **§7(a) and §7(b):** The 10-year sunset overrides the "until Congress finds" standard, so the prohibition ends after 10 years whether or not the harm finding is made. Section 7(a) is therefore effectively superseded.

26. **§7(b) and §8:** The sunset runs from enactment, but the prohibition begins 180 days later, so the prohibition would last about 9.5 years. The bill also has no savings clause for penalties accrued before the sunset, pending enforcement actions, or registry obligations.

27. **§8:** The 180-day delay leaves a window in which mining can continue or expand. The bill includes no moratorium on new exploration licenses or permits during that period, and the registry and reporting duties are also delayed.

28. **全 bill (no section):** The bill has no severability clause and no findings or purpose section. If a court strikes the discretionary determination in §2(1)(B) or the penalty provisions, the whole Act could fall, and the lack of findings leaves the constitutional basis for the extraterritorial reach unstated.
