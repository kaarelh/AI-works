RESPONSE:
**Drafting problems in S. 2817 (text as amended October 6, 2026)**

**Structural conflicts**

1. **§7(a) and §7(b).** Section 7(a) keeps the prohibition in effect until Congress lifts it by joint resolution, but §7(b) ends the entire Act 10 years after enactment regardless of any safety finding. Strike the sunset or make it conditional on a joint resolution, and add a savings clause so violations and pending enforcement actions survive expiration.

2. **§5(a), §8, and §3.** Section 5(a) penalizes violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §8 delays the Act's effect by 180 days. This leaves unclear whether training started in that window is lawful, and it creates an incentive to rush new runs before the prohibition takes effect. Make §3 effective on enactment, or freeze new covered training from the date of introduction.

3. **No severability clause.** If a court strikes one provision (for example, the open-ended Secretary determination in §2(1)(B)), the whole Act could fall with it. Add a severability clause.

**Scope and definitions**

4. **§2(1)(A).** The compute trigger applies to a "single training run," so a developer could split training into sequential phases or stages, or could build a covered model through fine-tuning or continued pretraining of an existing model. Measure cumulative compute per model lineage, including post-training.

5. **§2(1)(A).** "Integer or floating-point operations" is not a standard unit, and the bill does not specify how to count operations (precision, dense versus sparse, inference, synthetic data generation). Define "FLOP" and the counting method, and authorize the Secretary to adjust the threshold as algorithmic efficiency improves.

6. **§2(1)(B).** "Secretary" is undefined, and "substantially exceeding," "most cognitive tasks," and "human performance" have no measurable standard. This invites a vagueness challenge and inconsistent enforcement. Name the agency and define the test, for example by reference to a specified benchmark suite.

7. **§2(1)(B).** The bill sets no deadline, process, notice requirement, or default outcome for determinations. If the Secretary never acts, this prong never triggers. Require determinations within a fixed period after a request, provide for public notice, and make determinations judicially reviewable.

8. **§2(1)(A) scope (policy call).** At 10^26 FLOP, the first prong may reach current frontier models trained after enactment, which is far broader than the superintelligence focus stated in our materials. The office should decide whether that breadth is intended, since opponents will likely cite overbreadth.

9. **§2(2).** "U.S. person" covers only citizens and state-organized corporations. It excludes LLCs, partnerships, lawful permanent residents, and foreign-parented entities acting abroad through controlled affiliates, which weakens the "anywhere" reach in §3(a). Expand the definition to entities organized under U.S. law or controlled by U.S. persons, and define "State" to include D.C. and the territories.

10. **§2(3).** "Advanced AI chip" and "data center" are undefined. The 50,000-chip threshold is also poorly calibrated: at typical utilization, that many current-generation chips could produce a 10^26 FLOP run in roughly two to three months, and training can be spread across sites that each fall below the threshold. Set the threshold by aggregate or networked capacity and define the terms.

11. **§2 (missing definitions).** The bill does not define "operator," "safety evaluation," "State," or "Secretary." Each is needed for enforcement, and "operator" is especially important for leased or cloud compute.

**Prohibitions and exemptions**

12. **§3(a).** "Train," "develop," and "deploy" are undefined. The bill does not say whether releasing open weights, offering API access, or distributing models counts as deployment, which is the most likely route to evasion. Define each term and expressly cover weight release and remote access.

13. **§3(a).** The prohibition does not reach those who supply compute, chips, cloud capacity, financing, or technical help to someone else's covered training, so a provider could rent capacity to a prohibited developer without liability. Add aiding-and-abetting and facilitation liability, and require know-your-customer checks for covered-capacity providers.

14. **§3(b).** "Training began" is undefined, and the exemption is written as an exemption from all of §3, so grandfathered systems could be trained further and deployed indefinitely. Define the start of training, limit grandfathered systems to their existing compute and to no new successor training, and bar their deployment beyond a set period.

15. **§3(b).** Grandfathered systems have no registration requirement, so the government may not know they exist. Require registration within a fixed period after enactment.

16. **§3(c).** "Activities conducted for the purpose of safety evaluation" is undefined and subjective, and no limit applies to compute, duration, or deployment. A developer could label full training as evaluation and then release the model. Define safety evaluation, require notice to the Secretary, bar public deployment of evaluated systems, and limit the exemption to registered evaluators.

**Registry and reporting**

17. **§4(a).** The bill sets no registration deadline and does not say what registration must contain. Require registration within a fixed period after enactment, with specified contents.

18. **§4(b).** Quarterly reports must "describe computing workloads" with no required content, such as run identifiers, FLOP totals, model identities, or whether a covered system was involved. Specify the required data elements and the certification standard.

19. **§4(b).** Reports are self-certified with no verification, audit, or cross-check against chip or energy data. Add audit and verification authority.

20. **§4 and §5.** Failing to register, failing to report, or filing a false certification carries no penalty, because §5 applies only to violations of §3. Add penalties for each.

21. **Registry and prohibition are not linked.** The registry covers only facilities, not training runs, so a covered run can occur outside a registered facility or in a spread-out network of them. Require reporting of covered training runs themselves.

**Enforcement machinery**

22. **No enforcing agency.** Neither §5 nor any other section names who brings civil or criminal enforcement actions, so enforcement authority is unclear. Designate an agency and assign it to the Department of Justice or an agency with litigating authority.

23. **No investigative powers.** The bill grants no authority to subpoena records, inspect facilities, verify chip counts, or require access to training logs. Add these powers, along with rulemaking authority and an appropriation.

24. **§5(a) remedies.** The civil penalty is $1,000,000 per day per violation, with no injunctive relief, no authority to halt training or order compute shut down, no forfeiture of hardware or model weights, and no inflation adjustment. "Violation" and "day" are also undefined, so a continuing violation may be hard to count. Add injunctive relief and forfeiture, define the unit of violation, and consider penalties scaled to compute or revenue.

25. **§5(b).** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill does not address conspiracy, attempt, or responsible-officer liability, so individual executives may escape prosecution. Define willfulness and add these provisions.

26. **No whistleblower protections.** Employees who learn of covered training have no protected reporting channel or anti-retaliation rights. Add both.

**International agreement**

27. **§6.** The Secretary must only "seek to negotiate," with no obligation to conclude an agreement, no deadline for results, no report to Congress, and no linkage to export controls. "Equivalent" is undefined. Require a report to Congress, define equivalence, and authorize export restrictions on chips and weights to non-signatories.

**Lifting the prohibition**

28. **§7(a).** The NASEM report has no deadline, and the bill does not require NASEM to produce it. "Developed safely" has no standard. Set a deadline for the report and define what it must assess.

**Litigation risk**

29. **No findings or purpose section.** The bill has no congressional findings, which weakens deference arguments and the Commerce Clause and national-security basis if the Act is challenged. Add a findings section.

30. **No judicial review standard or savings clause.** The bill does not address judicial review of §2(1)(B) determinations or the interaction with state law. Add a review provision and a clear statement on state law.

---

**Top priorities for the office:** items 1, 2, 14, 16 (§3(c)), 13, and 22–24. Together these close the largest gaps: the sunset conflict, the effective-date gap, the grandfather and evaluation exemptions, the missing enforcement agency, and the lack of registry penalties.

**Caveats:** I worked from the bill text alone and did not check it against the Congressional Record, the manager's amendment's redline, or current law. The compute estimate in item 10 uses rough assumptions about chip performance and utilization. Legislative counsel should review the proposed language before anything goes to the office.
