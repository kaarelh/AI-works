RESPONSE:
Here are the issues I found in the October 6 text, roughly in order of severity. I reviewed the text alone, so Senate Legislative Counsel should check the proposed fixes against the rest of the U.S. Code and existing enforcement statutes.

**Most urgent**

1. **Sec. 3(b) with Sec. 8 (grandfathering).** The exemption turns on when training *began* before enactment, not the effective date. Because the Act takes effect 180 days after enactment, companies could start new frontier runs in that window and then deploy the resulting systems indefinitely. Fix: set a cutoff at introduction, require registration of grandfathered systems, and bar deployment absent review.

2. **Sec. 3(c) (safety evaluation exemption).** "Safety evaluation" is undefined and unlimited in scale, so developers could label capability training as evaluation. Fix: define the term, require advance notice to Commerce, bar deployment and any use of results to improve capabilities, and limit the exemption to government-approved protocols.

3. **Sec. 7(a) vs. Sec. 7(b) (duration).** Section 7(a) keeps the prohibition in force until Congress passes a joint resolution, but Section 7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years regardless. Fix: remove the sunset or make it conditional on an affirmative finding.

4. **Sec. 5(a) vs. Sec. 8 (effective date).** Civil penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but the Act as a whole takes effect 180 days later. The text doesn't say which controls during the gap. Fix: conform the penalty trigger to the effective date.

5. **Sec. 5 (no enforcement mechanism).** The bill names no enforcing agency, grants no injunctive or emergency authority, and specifies no venue. A civil penalty alone can't stop a training run in progress. Fix: authorize the Attorney General (or Commerce) to seek TROs and injunctions, and specify venue and procedures.

6. **Sec. 4 (registry has no penalty).** Section 5 penalizes only violations of Section 3, so failing to register, failing to report, or filing a false quarterly certification carries no penalty. The registry is effectively voluntary. Fix: add penalties for registration, reporting, and false-certification violations.

7. **Sec. 4(b) (reporting content).** "Computing workloads" is undefined, and the bill sets no deadline for initial registration, no required content, and no inspection or audit rights. Fix: specify what must be reported (training runs, compute used, model identifiers, status), set deadlines, and grant inspection authority.

**Scope and definitions**

8. **Sec. 2(1)(A) (single-run measurement).** Measuring compute "in a single training run" invites splitting one effort into several runs, or using continued pretraining, post-training, or fine-tuning to avoid the threshold. Fix: aggregate compute across related runs and models in the same development lineage, and cover post-training.

9. **Sec. 2(1)(A) (static threshold).** A fixed 10^26 figure will become less protective as algorithmic efficiency improves, and the bill gives no way to update it. Fix: authorize the Secretary to revise the threshold by rule, with a reporting requirement.

10. **Sec. 2(1)(B) (capability prong).** "Secretary" is undefined, and "substantially exceeding," "most," and "cognitive tasks" are open-ended. There is no procedure, notice, or standard for designation, so developers can't know in advance whether they are covered. Fix: name the Secretary of Commerce, specify benchmarks or a measurement process, and provide notice, a comment period, and an advisory-opinion mechanism.

11. **Sec. 2(2) (U.S. person).** Defining a U.S. person as a citizen or a corporation organized under state law excludes LLCs, partnerships, and U.S.-headquartered entities organized under federal or foreign law, and "State" is undefined. Fix: use "organized under U.S. law or with principal place of business in the United States."

12. **Sec. 3(a) (extraterritorial reach).** The prohibition reaches persons in the United States and U.S. persons abroad, but not foreign developers training abroad. Foreign subsidiaries of U.S. firms may also fall outside the definition. Fix: cover any person who uses U.S.-located compute or U.S.-origin chips, and make the reach of Section 6 explicit.

13. **Sec. 3(a) (no facilitation liability).** The bill doesn't address those who knowingly provide compute, cloud access, chips, or hosting to a covered project. Fix: add aiding, abetting, and facilitation liability for knowing provision of compute or hosting.

14. **Sec. 3(a) ("train, develop, or deploy").** None of these terms is defined. The bill doesn't clearly address publishing or releasing model weights, API access, or possessing weights. Fix: define "deploy" to include making a system available in any form, including open-weight release.

15. **Sec. 2(3) (covered facility).** "Advanced AI chip" is undefined, the threshold counts chips rather than compute, and a developer could split a run across facilities that each fall under 50,000 chips. "Containing" is also unclear for leased or cloud capacity. Fix: define chips by reference to existing export-control categories, aggregate across an operator's facilities, and address leased capacity.

16. **Sec. 2 (missing definitions).** "Artificial intelligence model," "artificial intelligence system," "training run," "computing power," "data center," and "safety evaluation" are all undefined, and each one is a point where a developer could argue it falls outside the prohibition. Fix: add definitions for each.

**Penalties**

17. **Sec. 5(a) (civil penalty).** It's unclear whether "each day" means per violation, per model, or per chip, and a $1 million daily maximum may be small relative to frontier-scale capital spending. The amount has no inflation adjustment and no disgorgement or compute-forfeiture provision. Fix: clarify the unit of violation, raise the cap, index it to inflation, and add a revenue-based or compute-forfeiture remedy.

18. **Sec. 5(b) (criminal penalty).** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill has no responsible-officer provision, no attempt or conspiracy provision, and no false-statement offense for registry filings. Fix: add these provisions and define willfulness.

**Implementation and treaty**

19. **Sec. 6 (international agreement).** "Shall seek to negotiate" carries no consequences, requires no report to Congress, and leaves "equivalent" undefined. Fix: require a report to Congress at 18 months and periodic updates thereafter, and specify minimum elements of an equivalent agreement.

20. **Sec. 7(a) (lifting the prohibition).** The standard "can be developed safely" is undefined, the NASEM report has no deadline or required contents, and the bill doesn't say what happens if no report is issued. Fix: set a report deadline and contents, provide an expedited joint-resolution procedure, and state that the prohibition continues absent a finding.

21. **No rulemaking or appropriations authority.** Commerce is given a registry and designation duties without rulemaking authority, funding, or staff. Fix: add rulemaking authority and authorize appropriations.

22. **No severability clause.** If a court strikes Section 2(1)(B) or the penalty provisions, the bill's status as a whole is unclear. Fix: add a severability clause that identifies the core prohibition as the provision that must survive.

23. **No preemption or savings clause.** The bill doesn't say whether it preempts state laws on the same subject or preserves them. Fix: add an express preemption or savings provision.

**Challenge risk**

24. **Constitutional exposure.** Several provisions are vulnerable to challenge: the open-ended designation in Section 2(1)(B) may face nondelegation and vagueness challenges, the criminal provision may face vagueness challenges, the compelled reporting in Section 4 may face First Amendment challenges, and restrictions on releasing weights may face speech-based challenges. Fix: use objective, measurable definitions, add legislative findings tying the registry to a compelling interest, and narrow compelled disclosures to what's necessary.

25. **No whistleblower protections.** Employees who would report unregistered training or misreported compute have no statutory protection. Fix: add anti-retaliation protections and a reporting channel.

**Suggested priorities for Thursday**

If the office can only take a few items, I'd prioritize #1 (grandfathering window), #2 (safety-evaluation exemption), #3 (the Section 7 conflict), #5 (enforcement authority), and #6 (no penalty for registry violations). Together these close the most significant ways for development to continue lawfully.
