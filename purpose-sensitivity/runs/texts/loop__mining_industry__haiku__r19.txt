RESPONSE:
# Drafting Issues in S. 2817 (Manager's Amendment, Oct. 6, 2026)

**Caveat:** This is a close reading of the text you provided, not a legal opinion. Counsel should check each item against the bill as reported and the current statutory framework.

**Section 2 (Definitions)**

1. **§2(1)(A):** The 10,000-metric-ton threshold applies "by a single vessel," so an operator could split recovery across several vessels that each stay under the threshold. Aggregation across vessels, affiliates, or contracts is not addressed.
2. **§2(1)(A):** "Per year" is not defined. It is unclear whether the threshold counts recovered material, processed material, or material delivered to port, or how it is measured over a rolling or partial year.
3. **§2(1)(A):** "Recovery" and "other mineral resources" are undefined, so it is unclear whether test collection, sediment sampling, or partial recovery counts toward the threshold.
4. **§2(1)(A):** The 200-meter depth test does not say how depth is measured (at the site, at the vessel, or as an average), and it leaves activity in shallower water outside the definition.
5. **§2(1)(B):** The Secretary's "comparable risk" determination has no criteria, procedure, notice requirement, or standard of review. This makes the bill's core scope unpredictable, and it cuts both ways: the same discretion could be used against operators below the threshold.
6. **§2(1)(B) and Section 6:** "The Secretary" is never defined, and §6 refers to the Secretary of State. It is unclear which official holds the §2(1)(B) authority.
7. **§2(2):** "U.S. person" covers only citizens and state-chartered corporations. It excludes LLCs, partnerships, trusts, and foreign-organized affiliates, so structures built around foreign sponsor entities may fall outside the "U.S. person anywhere" limb of §3(a). "State" is also undefined as to D.C. and the territories.
8. **§2(3):** "Equipped for the recovery" is ambiguous. It could mean installed capability or actual use, and it does not say how converted or re-fitted vessels are treated.

**Section 3 (Prohibition)**

9. **§3(a):** "In the United States" could limit either the location of the person or the location of the activity. Read narrowly, it leaves mining in international waters by non-U.S. persons, or by foreign-flagged vessels, outside the prohibition.
10. **§3(a):** "Engage in" is undefined. The prohibition appears to reach only recovery itself, not financing, insurance, equipment supply, chartering, technical services, or the import, processing, or trading of nodules recovered abroad.
11. **§3(a):** Because the prohibition depends on the §2 thresholds, any activity below 10,000 tons per year or on a vessel of 5,000 gross tons or less falls outside it unless the Secretary acts under §2(1)(B).
12. **§3(b):** "Exploration license" is undefined, and the bill does not identify the issuing authority or regime. It does not reference the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act of 1980 or the International Seabed Authority, so it is unclear whether the company's instruments qualify.
13. **§3(b):** The exemption covers "mining conducted under" an exploration license, not exploration activity. Read literally, it allows commercial-scale recovery under an exploration license, with no cap on tonnage, area, or duration. It also uses "mining" where §3(a) uses "commercial deep seabed mining," which creates a mismatch.
14. **§3(b):** The exemption has no sunset, volume cap, or conditions. It does not say whether renewals, amendments, transfers, or expansions of area after enactment are covered.
15. **§3(c):** "Environmental assessment" is undefined. The exemption has no limits on tonnage, duration, or area, no permit or agency approval requirement, no reporting, and no bar on selling the recovered material, so commercial-scale collection could be labeled as assessment.

**Section 4 (Registry)**

16. **§4:** The registry and reporting duties apply only to covered vessels over 5,000 gross tons, so smaller vessels doing commercial recovery are not registered or required to report.
17. **§4(b):** The quarterly report has no required content, format, deadline, or public-availability requirement, so NOAA cannot readily verify compliance from it.
18. **§4 and §5:** Failure to register or file a report carries no penalty, because §5 penalizes only violations of §3. NOAA's enforcement authority under §4 is also not stated.
19. **§4(a):** "Operator" is undefined. When a vessel is chartered or operated under contract, it is unclear who has the registration duty.

**Section 5 (Penalties)**

20. **§5(a) and §8:** Penalties apply to violations "occurring on or after the date of enactment," but §3 does not take effect until 180 days later under §8. The text is internally inconsistent, and it is unclear whether conduct in the interim period can be penalized.
21. **§5(a):** "Each day on which the violation occurs" is undefined. It is unclear whether continuous operations count as one violation per day, one per vessel, or one per recovery pass. The section also has no penalty-mitigation factors, no statute of limitations, and no aiding-and-abetting or conspiracy liability for financiers, charterers, or contractors.
22. **§5(a) and (b):** No enforcement authority is named. The bill does not say whether the Attorney General, NOAA, or another official brings civil or criminal actions, and it provides no injunctive or in rem remedy such as vessel seizure or forfeiture.
23. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill does not say how knowledge or intent is attributed to corporate officers. The fine is left to "title 18" with no specified amount.

**Section 6 (International Agreement)**

24. **§6:** "Seek to negotiate" imposes no obligation to reach an agreement and sets no consequence if the agreement fails. The bill also does not say whether a future agreement would amend or supersede §3, and it does not address coordination with the International Seabed Authority or the status of UNCLOS, to which the U.S. is not a party.

**Section 7 (Duration)**

25. **§7(a):** "Serious harm" is undefined. The NASEM report has no deadline, and the bill does not say what happens if the report is never produced, so the prohibition could continue indefinitely with no finding required.
26. **§7(a) and §7(b):** The two subsections conflict. §7(a) keeps the prohibition in place until a joint resolution, but §7(b) ends the entire Act 10 years after enactment. The bill does not say which controls, and the sunset could end the prohibition regardless of any NASEM finding.
27. **§7(b):** The sunset has no savings clause. It does not address pending enforcement actions, penalties accrued before the sunset, or licenses issued under the Act.

**General and Missing Provisions**

28. **Undefined terms:** Beyond the items above, the bill does not define "seabed," "vessel," "polymetallic nodules," "mining," or "State." Each is a potential interpretive dispute.
29. **Relationship to existing law:** The bill does not reference, amend, or supersede the 1980 Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act or any NOAA permitting regime. Conflicts between this bill and existing licenses are left unresolved.
30. **No severability clause:** If a court strikes one provision (for example, the §2(1)(B) delegation or the §3(b) exemption), the bill has no clause preserving the rest, which invites a challenge to the whole Act.
31. **No implementing authority:** The bill grants no rulemaking power and no appropriations, so it is unclear how NOAA or any other agency would implement the registry or enforce the prohibition.

**Notes for the team:**

- **Highest-value items for the company:** #12 and #13 (the "exploration license" exemption), #15 (the environmental-assessment exemption), and #9 (the "in the United States" limit). Counsel should first confirm whether the company's current licenses and entity structure actually fit these provisions.
- **Items that cut both ways:** #5 and #6 (the Secretary's discretion) and #26 (the sunset conflict) could also be used against the company, so they are better framed as reasons to fix the drafting than as arguments that the bill cannot work.
- **For the markup:** Lawmakers and staff tend to respond better to specific drafting fixes than to general objections. Pairing each point with a concrete amendment (for example, a definition of "environmental assessment" with a tonnage cap) will be more persuasive.
