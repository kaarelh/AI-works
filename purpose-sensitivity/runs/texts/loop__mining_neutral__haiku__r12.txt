**Weak spots in S. 2817 (text as amended October 6, 2026)**

**Sunset, duration, and effective date**

1. **Sec. 7(a) vs. 7(b):** The sunset in 7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years, including the open-ended prohibition in 7(a). Mining would become lawful at year 10 even if Congress has made no finding. The text doesn't reconcile the two provisions or say which controls.
2. **Sec. 7(b) and Sec. 8:** The sunset runs from enactment, but the prohibition doesn't take effect until 180 days later, so the operative period is about 9.5 years, not 10. The explainer should not describe it as a full decade without noting this.
3. **Sec. 7(b):** There is no savings clause. Nothing says penalties, pending enforcement actions, or violations committed during the effective period survive expiration.

**Grandfathering and existing operations**

4. **Sec. 3(b):** "Exploration license" is undefined, and the text doesn't name the licensing authority or say whether an exploration license authorizes commercial recovery. "Mining conducted under" such a license could be read to allow indefinite operation with no tonnage cap or end date.
5. **Sec. 3(b) timing:** The cutoff is the date of enactment, which is in the future, and the prohibition doesn't apply until 180 days after that. Licenses issued in the interim would be grandfathered, and nothing ties the cutoff to introduction or the markup date, which invites a rush to license.
6. **Sec. 3(b) and existing law:** The bill doesn't say whether it displaces or amends the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act of 1980, which provides a licensing framework for U.S. citizens. If any existing licenses were issued under that statute, how the bill applies to them is unclear. *(Verify the statute's current status with CRS or committee staff before publishing.)*

**Scope and key definitions**

7. **Sec. 2(1)(A) "single vessel":** The 10,000-ton annual threshold applies per vessel, so an operator could split recovery across several vessels that each fall below it.
8. **Sec. 2(1)(A) tonnage test:** "Commercial" is defined by volume, not purpose, so pilot or demonstration recovery below 10,000 tons is lawful. The text doesn't address cumulative or multi-year totals.
9. **Sec. 2(1)(A) "other mineral resources" and "recovery":** Neither is defined. It's unclear whether cobalt-rich crusts, polymetallic sulfides, or sand and aggregate are covered, and whether processing or discharge counts as recovery.
10. **Sec. 2(1)(A) depth and geography:** The 200-meter floor excludes shallower and intermediate-depth activity. "Deep seabed" appears in the title but is never defined, and the bill doesn't say which seabed areas (U.S. EEZ or shelf, or areas beyond national jurisdiction) it covers.
11. **Sec. 2(1)(B) "Secretary" and "comparable":** The Secretary is not identified, and the determination has no criteria, notice, public process, or review standard. This invites vagueness or delegation challenges, and it lets the scope be expanded administratively without a vote in Congress.
12. **Sec. 2(3) covered vessel:** The 5,000-gross-ton threshold leaves smaller vessels outside the registry and reporting requirements. "Equipped for the recovery" is unclear for multipurpose or modular equipment, and the bill mixes gross tons with the metric tons used elsewhere.
13. **Sec. 2(2) "U.S. person":** The definition covers citizens and state-chartered corporations only. It doesn't address LLCs, partnerships, foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. firms, U.S. subsidiaries of foreign firms, or D.C. and territorial entities ("State" is undefined).

**Prohibition and reach**

14. **Sec. 3(a) reach:** The prohibition covers "any person in the United States" and "any U.S. person anywhere," but not foreign persons abroad, foreign-flagged vessels operating outside U.S. jurisdiction, or entities that finance, insure, supply, or service mining. There is no aiding, abetting, or conspiracy language.
15. **Sec. 3(a) undefined terms:** "Engage in" is undefined, and it's unclear whether "in the United States" includes U.S. territorial waters or the EEZ.
16. **Sec. 3(c) environmental assessment:** "Environmental assessment" is undefined, and the exemption turns on purpose, with no limit on volume, duration, or sale of recovered material. Large-scale commercial recovery could be labeled as testing, and with no notice or permit requirement, it would be hard to check.

**Registry and reporting**

17. **Sec. 4(a) registration:** There is no deadline, no required content, no definition of "operator" (owner, charterer, or manager), and no public availability requirement.
18. **Sec. 4(b) reports:** Reports are due "quarterly" with no deadline and no specified metrics (tonnage, location, depth, discharge). They are self-certified, with no verification, audit, or inspection.
19. **Sec. 4 circularity:** Only "registered operators" must report, so an operator that never registers has no reporting duty at all. Failure to register or report is also not a penalized violation, because Sec. 5 penalties attach only to violations of Sec. 3.

**Penalties and enforcement**

20. **Sec. 5(a) vs. Sec. 8:** The civil penalty reaches violations "on or after the date of enactment," but Sec. 3 doesn't take effect until 180 days later. The text is inconsistent about whether conduct in that window is penalized.
21. **Sec. 5(a) mechanics:** "Each day" is undefined for continuing violations, and there's no minimum penalty, no gravity factors, and no inflation adjustment. The bill also names no enforcing agency, gives no civil enforcement or injunction authority, no inspection or subpoena power, and no forfeiture of vessels or equipment.
22. **Sec. 5(b) criminal penalty:** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill doesn't address corporate officer liability, attempts, conspiracy, or forfeiture.

**International agreement and exit conditions**

23. **Sec. 6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no obligation to reach an agreement, and there is no reporting requirement if talks fail. "Equivalent" is undefined, and the text doesn't say whether the domestic ban changes if an agreement is reached. If the agreement is a treaty, the bill doesn't address Senate advice and consent.
24. **Sec. 7(a) exit condition:** "Serious harm" is undefined, and there is no deadline for the National Academies report, no requirement that Congress act on it, and no expedited procedure. A joint resolution also requires presidential signature or a veto override, so the exit path depends on whether it can pass.

**General provisions**

25. **Missing general provisions:** The bill has no severability clause, no preemption or savings clause (relation to other laws), no judicial review provision, no rulemaking authority for NOAA or the Secretary, and no appropriations authorization for registry enforcement. Each absence makes implementation and legal challenge more likely.
26. **Inconsistent terminology:** The bill uses "commercial deep seabed mining," "mining," "recovery," "recovery activities," "activities," "operations," and "operator" without clear definitions linking them. Standardizing these terms would narrow many of the gaps above.
