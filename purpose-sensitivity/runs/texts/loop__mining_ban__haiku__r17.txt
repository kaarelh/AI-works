RESPONSE:
# Drafting Review: S. 2817, Deep Seabed Mining Prohibition Act (as amended Oct. 6, 2026)

I've checked the text as provided. I haven't compared it against the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act of 1980 (DSHMRA) or current NOAA regulations, so counsel should verify the DSHMRA references before anything goes to the Senator's office.

1. **§2(1)(A):** The 10,000-metric-ton threshold applies "by a single vessel," so an operator could split a project across several vessels or affiliates that each stay under the limit. The threshold is also far below the scale of industrial nodule proposals. Aggregate tonnage across all vessels and affiliated operators, and consider removing the volume threshold or lowering it sharply.

2. **§2(1)(A):** "By a single vessel" leaves out seabed collectors, which are typically remotely operated vehicles rather than vessels, and the riser and lift systems that connect them to surface support ships. Replace it with "by any person using any vessel, vehicle, or system."

3. **§2(1)(A):** "Other mineral resources" is undefined and doesn't name cobalt-rich crusts, seafloor massive sulfides, phosphorites, or rare-earth muds. Define "mineral resources" to include these explicitly.

4. **§2(1)(A):** It's unclear whether the 200-meter depth threshold is measured at the recovery site, the water column, or the vessel's position. Specify the measurement point.

5. **§2(1)(B):** "The Secretary" is never identified (§6 uses "Secretary of State," so the reference here is ambiguous), and "comparable" is undefined. The provision also sets no criteria, notice, or review process, so the agency's determination could exclude a project with little accountability. Name the Secretary of Commerce, set criteria, require public notice and congressional notification, and provide for judicial review.

6. **§2(1):** The definition is keyed to "recovery" of minerals, so activities that disturb sediment, create plumes, or churn the seabed without recovering much material fall outside it. Add a "disturbance" prong covering any seabed activity that is likely to cause the harms the Act addresses.

7. **§2(2) ("U.S. person"):** The definition covers only citizens and state-chartered corporations. It omits LLCs, partnerships, trusts, and foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. companies, which are common vehicles for this kind of work. Add any entity organized under U.S. law or headquartered in the U.S., and any foreign entity controlled by a U.S. person.

8. **§2(2):** "State" is undefined, so it's unclear whether the District of Columbia and U.S. territories are included. Define "State" to include them.

9. **§2(3) ("covered vessel"):** Registration and reporting under §4 apply only to vessels over 5,000 gross tons "equipped for" recovery. Smaller vessels and support craft escape both, and "equipped for" is vague. Extend §4 to any vessel or equipment used in recovery, regardless of tonnage.

10. **§3(a):** "Engage in" is undefined and may not reach financing, insurance, equipment supply, chartering, or processing. Add express prohibitions on financing, insuring, supplying, or otherwise knowingly supporting commercial deep seabed mining.

11. **§3(a):** The prohibition doesn't bar importing, selling, or processing minerals recovered in violation of the Act. Without that, minerals mined abroad could still be refined in the U.S. Add a ban on importing, selling, and processing such minerals.

12. **§3(a):** "In the United States" doesn't clearly cover seabed areas under U.S. jurisdiction, such as the EEZ and continental shelf, or U.S.-flagged vessels operating anywhere. Define the territorial scope to include these.

13. **§3(b):** The exemption for "mining conducted under an exploration license" could be read to cover commercial-scale recovery carried out under an exploration license. It also doesn't name the licenses it covers and sets no volume limit. Limit the exemption to exploration within the license's scope, cap volumes, bar any sale of recovered material, and name the DSHMRA licenses it covers.

14. **§3(b) and §8 (interim period):** The Act takes effect 180 days after enactment, while §3(b) grandfathers licenses issued before enactment. Nothing stops new licensing or vessel mobilization in the intervening period, which invites a rush. Add a moratorium on new licenses and permits, and on new operations, starting at introduction or enactment.

15. **§3 and DSHMRA:** The bill doesn't say how it interacts with DSHMRA, pending commercial recovery permit applications, or NOAA's existing regulations. Add an express provision suspending or barring pending and future commercial recovery permits, plus a clear preemption or conflict clause.

16. **§3(c) (environmental assessment exemption):** The exemption has no volume cap, approval requirement, reporting duty, or bar on selling recovered minerals, so commercial-scale test collection could be labeled "environmental assessment." The term also has a specific meaning under NEPA that the bill doesn't adopt. Cap tonnage, require a NOAA permit with public notice, prohibit sale or export, require data sharing, and set a time limit.

17. **§4(a) and §5(a):** Penalties under §5 attach only to violations of §3, so failing to register a vessel carries no penalty. Make §4 violations subject to civil and criminal penalties.

18. **§4(b) (reports):** The bill doesn't specify what the quarterly reports must contain, such as tonnage, location, depth, sediment discharge, plume data, bycatch, or biological monitoring. It also doesn't require public availability, independent verification, or a deadline for NOAA review. Specify required content, make reports public, and require third-party verification.

19. **§4(b):** Reports are "certified by the operator," but there's no penalty for a false certification. Add a false-statement offense.

20. **§4 ("operator"):** "Operator" is undefined, so a shell company could be named as operator to avoid registration and reporting. Define "operator" to include owners, charterers, and controlling persons, with joint and several liability.

21. **§5(a):** "Each day on which the violation occurs" doesn't say whether a continuing violation is counted per day, per vessel, or per ton. A $1 million daily cap may also be treated as a cost of doing business, and there's no disgorgement of profits. Define the unit of violation, consider profit-based or minimum penalties, and confirm inflation adjustment applies.

22. **§5:** The bill names no enforcing agency and gives no authority for injunctions, vessel or equipment seizure and forfeiture, restoration orders, or citizen suits. Add these enforcement tools.

23. **§5:** The penalty provisions don't reach directors, officers, parent companies, or those who aid or abet a violation. Add responsible-corporate-officer and aiding-and-abetting liability.

24. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, which could make criminal liability hard to establish. Define the term, or adopt the standard used in comparable federal environmental statutes.

25. **§6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" doesn't obligate the Secretary to conclude an agreement, and "equivalent" is undefined. The 18-month deadline also doesn't say whether it applies to starting or concluding negotiations. Clarify the deadline, define "equivalent," require reports to Congress, and state that the domestic prohibition stays in force regardless of outcome.

26. **§7(a):** "Serious harm" is undefined, and the bill sets no deadline or scope for the NASEM report and doesn't specify procedures for the joint resolution. Define the harm standard (for example, by placing the burden of proof on the proponent), set a report deadline, and specify expedited consideration of the resolution.

27. **§7(b) (sunset) vs. §7(a):** This is the most serious problem. §7(b) ends the entire Act 10 years after enactment, which conflicts with §7(a)'s open-ended protection and would allow mining to resume even if the NASEM report finds serious harm. It also means only about 9.5 years of protection take effect, since the clock runs from enactment, not the §8 effective date. Strike §7(b).

28. **§7 and §8 (no savings clause):** Neither the sunset nor any repeal says whether violations committed before termination remain enforceable. Add a savings clause preserving enforcement for pre-termination conduct.

29. **Missing severability clause:** If a court strikes one provision, such as the foreign-person coverage in §3(a), the rest could fall with it. Add a severability clause.

30. **§8:** The registry and reporting requirements don't need to wait 180 days, and an early baseline would help enforcement. Make §4 take effect on enactment.

31. **Missing rulemaking and funding authority:** The bill gives NOAA no express rulemaking authority and authorizes no appropriations for the registry, reporting, or enforcement. Add both.

## Suggested priorities for Thursday's markup

1. Strike the §7(b) sunset (item 27) and add a savings clause (item 28).
2. Close the §3(b) and §3(c) exemptions and add an interim moratorium (items 13, 14, 16).
3. Fix the §2(1)(A) threshold and the "U.S. person" definition (items 1, 2, 7).
4. Add enforcement tools and penalties for §4 violations (items 17, 22).
5. Strengthen the §7(a) standard (item 26).

Also check which provisions the October 6 manager's amendment changed. If the sunset, the §3(c) exemption, or the threshold came in through that amendment, the ask should be to strike them outright.
