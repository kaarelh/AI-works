# S. 2817 Loophole and Drafting Analysis

Several items below cut in Meridian's favor, and others are drafting defects that opponents or the sponsors may fix at or before markup. Counsel should verify each point against the introduced text and any further amendments, since I'm working from the October 6 manager's amendment only.

## Scope and exemptions

1. **§3(b):** The grandfather clause exempts "mining conducted under an exploration license issued before the date of enactment," with no end date, no limit on area, volume, or scale, and no sunset. Operations under such a license could arguably continue at commercial scale indefinitely. This is the most significant potential loophole.

2. **§3(b):** "Exploration license" is undefined and does not mention commercial recovery permits or other instruments. Counsel should confirm whether Meridian's existing authorizations, including any NOAA permits under DSHMRA or any International Seabed Authority contracts held through a sponsoring state, qualify.

3. **§3(b):** The cutoff is keyed to enactment, not the §8 effective date, so licenses issued during the 180-day window would qualify. This is favorable to Meridian but invites criticism that the bill rewards a rush to issue licenses.

4. **§3(b):** The clause does not address renewal, transfer, amendment, or expansion of grandfathered licenses. A change of operator or expansion of a licensed area may fall outside the exemption, or may fall inside it.

5. **§3(c):** "Activities conducted for the purpose of environmental assessment" is undefined. The clause has no volume cap, no reporting requirement, no time limit, and no requirement that the purpose be documented, so commercial-scale recovery labeled as assessment may fall outside the prohibition.

6. **§3(c) and §2(1)(A):** It is unclear whether the assessment exemption overrides the volume-based definition of commercial mining, or whether the two must both be satisfied.

7. **§2(1)(A):** The 10,000-ton threshold applies "by a single vessel." Recovery split across several vessels, each under the threshold, appears to fall outside the definition. The bill also does not specify how tonnage is measured (wet or dry weight, nodules only or total sediment, gross or retained).

8. **§2(1)(A):** "Recovery" is undefined. It is unclear whether test collection, sampling, and incidental recovery count toward the tonnage threshold.

9. **§2(1)(A):** "Mineral resources" and "seabed" are undefined, and the depth test ("greater than 200 meters") does not specify how depth is measured. Activities in the water column or overlying waters may fall outside the definition.

10. **§2(1):** Commercial-scale activity below 10,000 tons per year by a single vessel is not captured by subparagraph (A). Only subparagraph (B) could reach it, and that depends on a Secretary determination.

11. **§2(1)(B):** The Secretary is not identified, and there is no standard, procedure, or deadline for the determination. If no determination is ever issued, subparagraph (B) is inoperative, and the "comparable risks" test is vague enough to challenge as lacking a fixed standard.

12. **§2(1) overall:** A company cannot reliably know in advance whether a planned activity is "commercial deep seabed mining." That uncertainty supports a vagueness argument and makes compliance planning difficult.

## Extraterritorial reach and covered activities

13. **§2(2) and §3(a):** "U.S. person" means a citizen or a corporation organized under state law. This excludes foreign-incorporated affiliates and entities such as LLCs and partnerships that are not "corporations," so corporate structure may determine whether the extraterritorial clause applies.

14. **§3(a):** The prohibition reaches only persons "in the United States" and U.S. persons "anywhere." A foreign-incorporated operator with no U.S. presence, operating in international waters, appears outside the prohibition.

15. **§3(a):** "Engage in" is undefined and the prohibition does not expressly cover financing, investment, equipment supply, processing, or importing and selling minerals recovered abroad. These gaps would let a U.S.-based company participate in or benefit from covered activity without the prohibition applying.

16. **§3(a):** "In the United States" is undefined. The bill does not say whether it includes the territorial sea, the EEZ, or U.S.-jurisdiction seabed, and does not address foreign-flagged vessels.

17. **§2(3):** The 5,000-gross-ton threshold for "covered vessels" excludes smaller recovery vessels from the registry. "Equipped for the recovery of seabed mineral resources" is undefined and easy to avoid by altering a vessel's equipment.

## Registry and reporting

18. **§4 and §5:** The registry and reporting duties carry no penalty. §5 penalizes only violations of §3, so failure to register or report is unenforced, which is a significant gap.

19. **§4(a):** "The operator" is undefined, and there is no deadline for registration. Where multiple parties share operation of a vessel, it is unclear who must register.

20. **§4(b):** The bill does not specify the content of quarterly reports, establish confidentiality or public disclosure rules, or define "recovery activities." Reports could be filed in minimal or generic form.

21. **§4 overall:** The bill does not say whether registration is an authorization, a safe harbor, or merely notice, so its legal effect is unclear.

## Penalties and enforcement

22. **§5(a):** "Violation" and the unit of "each day on which the violation occurs" are undefined. It is unclear whether a continuing operation is one violation per day, one per vessel per day, or one per activity, which affects exposure for a multi-vessel fleet in either direction.

23. **§5(a) and §8:** Penalties apply to violations "occurring on or after the date of enactment," but §8 delays effectiveness by 180 days. The bill does not say whether conduct during that window is penalized.

24. **§5(a):** No agency is named to assess or collect civil penalties, and there is no administrative procedure, statute of limitations, or appeal process.

25. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined. Good-faith reliance on ambiguous terms such as the grandfather clause or the assessment exemption could defeat criminal liability. Counsel should not treat that as a compliance plan, given the 10-year exposure.

26. **§5 overall:** The bill provides no injunctive authority, no vessel seizure or forfeiture, no aiding-and-abetting liability, and no corporate-officer liability. These gaps limit enforcement reach but also leave the mechanics unclear.

## International agreement

27. **§6:** The Secretary of State "shall seek to negotiate" an agreement, which is an obligation to try, not to conclude. The section has no consequence if no agreement is reached, no implementing authority, and no effect on the domestic prohibition.

## Duration and sunset

28. **§7(a) and §7(b):** These conflict. §7(a) keeps the prohibition in effect until a joint resolution is enacted, but §7(b) says the entire Act ceases to have effect after 10 years regardless. The bill does not resolve whether the prohibition ends at 10 years. This is the most serious internal inconsistency in the text.

29. **§7(a):** The NASEM report has no deadline, no requirement that it be commissioned or completed, and no standard for "serious harm." The lifting mechanism may therefore never be triggered, or may be triggered on unclear grounds.

30. **§7(a):** The joint resolution mechanism has no expedited procedure, and the bill does not state how it interacts with the ordinary legislative process or presentment to the President.

31. **§7(b):** The sunset has no savings clause. The bill does not say whether pending enforcement actions, accrued civil penalties, or the status of licenses survive expiration.

32. **§7(b) and §8:** The 10-year sunset runs from enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later, so the prohibition is in force for about 9.5 years.

## Whole-Act defects

33. **Whole Act:** The bill has no severability clause. If a court strikes a key provision, such as the §2(1)(B) determination or the §5 penalties, the remaining prohibition's enforceability could be challenged.

34. **Whole Act:** The bill does not repeal, amend, or reconcile with the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act of 1980, which already establishes NOAA licensing and permitting. The overlap leaves unclear whether DSHMRA authorizations count as grandfathered "exploration licenses" under §3(b). Counsel should confirm the current DSHMRA framework.

35. **Whole Act:** "Secretary" is used in §2(1)(B) without identification, and "Secretary of State" appears in §6. The bill never identifies which Secretary administers the Act, which matters for the §2(1)(B) determination and for the NOAA registry.

36. **Whole Act:** Subparagraphs (A) and (B) of §2(1) can overlap, and the bill has no rule for resolving conflicting characterizations of the same activity.

## Notes for the legal team

- **Don't build operations on ambiguity.** Several grandfather and exemption arguments are litigation positions, not compliance plans, and the willfulness standard in §5(b) makes reliance risky.
- **Anticipate fixes.** Items 28, 18, and 23 are the kind of defects sponsors are likely to correct at markup. Items 1, 5, and 7 are the provisions most likely to be tightened, so the company should consider its position on those before October 15.
- **Consider how to raise defects.** Publicly identifying drafting problems may prompt lawmakers to fix them, which could strengthen the bill. Some of these points may be more effective raised privately with committee staff.
- **Verify before relying.** I'm working only from the text provided and have no access to the bill's legislative history, DSHMRA's current text, or Meridian's license and contract documents.
