# Drafting Problems and Loopholes in S. 2817 (Manager's Amendment, Oct. 6, 2026)

This review is based only on the text provided. Several fixes will need review by Senate Legislative Counsel, and the compute estimates below are rough.

## Definitions (Sec. 2)

1. **§2(1)(A), "single training run":** Developers can split one model's training into multiple runs or stages (pretraining, continued pretraining, RL, fine-tuning), each below 10^26. Fix: count cumulative compute across a model's full training lineage, including post-training.

2. **§2(1)(A), fixed 10^26 threshold:** Algorithmic efficiency gains mean a dangerous system could be trained below this number, and the bill has no mechanism to update it. Fix: give Commerce rulemaking authority to revise the threshold and specify how FLOP are counted (precision, total vs. per-second, inclusion of data processing).

3. **§2(1)(B), Secretary's capability determination:** "Secretary" is undefined, and the determination has no standard, evaluation method, deadline, notice requirement, or statement of whether it applies prospectively or retroactively. Fix: specify the agency, the evaluation criteria, a procedure, and a record requirement to reduce APA challenges and delay.

4. **§2(1)(B), "substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks":** These terms are undefined, which invites dispute over whether current systems qualify and supports vagueness challenges to the criminal penalties. Fix: define them with measurable benchmarks or a process for setting them.

5. **§2, "artificial intelligence" undefined:** Every prohibition depends on this term, which is never defined. Fix: adopt an existing statutory definition, such as the one in the National AI Initiative Act (15 U.S.C. 9401), after counsel confirms it.

6. **§2(2), "U.S. person":** The definition excludes foreign subsidiaries of U.S. companies, LLCs and partnerships, and permanent residents. Routing development through a foreign subsidiary is the most obvious evasion path. Fix: define "U.S. person" by control or beneficial ownership, or cover any entity controlled by a U.S. person.

7. **§2(2), "State" undefined:** It is unclear whether D.C., Puerto Rico, and other territories are included. Fix: define "State."

8. **§2(3), "covered facility":** "Advanced AI chips," "data center," and "contains" are undefined, and the definition does not clearly reach rented cloud capacity or compute spread across sites. Fix: define these terms and cover compute an entity controls or contracts for, whatever the location.

9. **§2(3) and §4, 50,000-chip threshold:** This threshold is far too high to capture a 10^26 training run. Roughly, at H100-class throughput (~10^15 FLOP/s), 10^26 FLOP is about 10^11 chip-seconds, so a 5,000-chip cluster at 40% utilization would need more than a year. Fix: lower the threshold, aggregate compute across sites an entity controls, and tie registration to the training threshold itself.

## Prohibition (Sec. 3)

10. **§3(a), extraterritorial gap:** Non-U.S. persons training or deploying abroad are not covered at all, and neither are foreign subsidiaries of U.S. companies. Section 6 is only an aspiration. Fix: cover any system deployed to U.S. users, and pair this with export-control or cloud-access restrictions.

11. **§3(a), undefined verbs:** "Train," "develop," and "deploy" are not defined, and it is unclear whether fine-tuning, distillation, API access, or research count. Fix: define each term; include making model weights available for download and providing API access within "deploy."

12. **§3(a), no facilitation or possession bar:** The prohibition does not cover supplying compute, chips, cloud services, funding, or model weights to a violator, and it does not bar possessing or distributing weights of a covered system. Fix: add aiding-and-abetting language, a compute-provision prohibition, and a weights-possession and distribution prohibition.

13. **§3(a), open-weight release:** Publishing model weights is not explicitly addressed, and courts may read "deploy" narrowly. Fix: state explicitly whether publishing weights is prohibited, and consider how this interacts with First Amendment arguments.

14. **§3(b), grandfathering:** "Training began" is undefined, and the exemption lets a pre-enactment run continue indefinitely and lets the resulting system be deployed indefinitely. Successor models built by continuing training from grandfathered checkpoints could also be argued to have "begun" earlier. Fix: limit the exemption to completed training, require registration by a fixed date, and bar continued training from exempt checkpoints.

15. **§3(b) and §8, the 180-day gap:** A run that starts during the gap is not grandfathered under §3(b), but the bill does not say whether it is covered when the prohibition takes effect. This also creates an incentive to accelerate runs before the effective date. Fix: state that any covered training in progress on the effective date is prohibited, or require registration by enactment.

16. **§3(c), safety-evaluation exemption:** This is the largest opening in the bill. "Safety evaluation" is undefined and turns on the developer's stated purpose, with no limits on scale, duration, location, or deployment afterward and no reporting requirement. Fix: exempt only evaluations under a government-approved protocol, with notice to Commerce, a time limit, independent evaluators, air-gapped systems, and a bar on deployment or capability uplift.

## Registry and Reporting (Sec. 4)

17. **§4(a), "operator" undefined, no deadline, and no penalty:** Nothing sets a registration deadline, and §5 penalizes only §3 violations, so failing to register carries no penalty. Fix: define "operator," set a deadline, and make failure to register a violation with its own penalty.

18. **§4(b), "computing workloads" undefined:** Reports need not identify training runs, FLOP counts, model identities, or safety-evaluation activities, so they may not show whether a covered system exists. Fix: specify required content, including each training run above a reporting threshold, cumulative FLOP, and the model trained.

19. **§4(b), self-certification only:** The bill has no audit, inspection, on-site access, or remote-monitoring authority, and false certification is not penalized. Fix: add inspection and monitoring powers and a penalty for false certification.

20. **§4, no confidentiality provisions:** Without trade-secret protections, industry will argue the reports are a competitive risk, and the bill says nothing about what Commerce may share with enforcement agencies. Fix: add a confidentiality clause with clear disclosure rules for enforcement.

21. **§4, cloud and chip suppliers not covered:** The registry does not require compute providers to verify customers or report large workloads. Fix: add know-your-customer obligations for cloud providers and chip sellers above a threshold.

## Penalties (Sec. 5)

22. **§5(a), penalty size:** At $1 million per day, a multi-month frontier training run could be treated as a cost of doing business for a well-funded developer. Fix: scale penalties to compute cost or revenue, and add disgorgement or forfeiture of compute and weights.

23. **§5, no enforcement mechanism:** The bill does not name who brings civil actions, grant investigative powers, or authorize injunctions to halt training, shut down facilities, or quarantine weights. Fix: name the enforcing agency and add injunctive relief, investigative subpoena power, and asset seizure.

24. **§5(a), date mismatch and "each day":** Penalties attach to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but the prohibition does not take effect until 180 days later, and it is unclear whether a continuing run is one violation or one per day. Fix: tie penalties to the effective date and define each day of covered training or deployment as a separate violation.

25. **§5(b), "willfully" undefined:** The criminal standard is unclear, and the bill does not address officer liability, attempt, or conspiracy. Fix: define "willfully" and add responsible-corporate-officer and attempt/conspiracy provisions.

26. **§5, no whistleblower protections:** Enforcement depends on information held inside developers, and the bill offers no protection or reward for reporting violations. Fix: add anti-retaliation protection and a reporting incentive.

## International Agreement (Sec. 6)

27. **§6, "seek to negotiate":** The deadline requires only that the Secretary seek negotiations, not conclude an agreement, and "equivalent" is undefined. There is also no requirement to report to Congress. Fix: require a status report to Congress, define "equivalent," and tie the agreement to export-control authority.

## Duration (Sec. 7)

28. **§7(a), lifting mechanism:** There is no timeline for the NASEM report, the phrase "developed safely" is undefined, and the bill does not say whether a joint resolution must be presented to the President or what legal effect it has. Fix: define the standard, set a timeline, and specify that lifting requires enacted legislation.

29. **§7(b) vs. §7(a), internal conflict and sunset:** Section 7(a) keeps the prohibition in force indefinitely, but §7(b) ends the entire Act 10 years after enactment, which is about 9.5 years after the prohibition takes effect. A developer could simply plan around the sunset. Fix: delete the sunset, or make it apply only if Congress has affirmatively lifted the prohibition.

30. **§7(b), no savings clause:** Violations during the Act's life may become unprosecutable once it sunsets, and the general savings statute (1 U.S.C. 109) may not apply to a sunset. Fix: add an explicit savings clause preserving liability for violations committed while the Act was in effect.

## Effective Date (Sec. 8)

31. **§8, mismatched dates:** Sections 3(b) and 5(a) run from enactment, while the prohibition runs from the effective date, and the registry's start date is not stated. Fix: make the registry effective at enactment so Commerce can see runs starting before the prohibition takes effect, and align all other dates.

## General

32. **No severability clause:** If §2(1)(B) or §3(c) is struck down, a court could void the entire Act. Fix: add a severability clause.

33. **No rulemaking authority:** Commerce cannot define terms, update thresholds, set reporting forms, or create evaluation standards. Fix: grant rulemaking authority with deadlines and appropriations.

34. **No congressional findings:** Courts often consider legislative findings when assessing commerce-power and First Amendment challenges, and the bill has none. Fix: add findings that tie the prohibition to interstate commerce and national security.

35. **Constitutional exposure:** Undefined terms in criminal provisions create vagueness risk, and restrictions on developing or publishing code and weights may draw First Amendment challenges. Fix: the definitional fixes above reduce both risks, and counsel should review the speech-related provisions.

## Highest Priority

If time is limited, the most consequential fixes are:

- **§3(c)**, the safety-evaluation exemption
- **§7(b)**, the 10-year sunset
- **§3(b)**, the grandfathering exemption
- **§2(2) and §3(a)**, the foreign-subsidiary and extraterritorial gaps
- **§5 and §4**, the missing enforcement mechanisms and the registry with no penalty
- **§2(1)(A) and §2(3)**, the single-run loophole and the registry threshold
