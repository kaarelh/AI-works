# Loopholes, Ambiguities, and Drafting Problems in S. 2817

I've tagged each item **[Loophole]** if it could let us keep operating under the bill as written, **[Unworkable]** if it creates compliance or enforcement problems we can cite, or **[Drafting]** for ambiguities and internal conflicts that cut in either direction. This is a drafting review, not a legal opinion. Counsel should verify the items flagged in the priorities section below.

## Section 2: Definitions

1. **§2(1)(A) [Loophole]:** The 10,000-metric-ton threshold applies to "a single vessel." A fleet of smaller vessels, each below the threshold, would fall outside the definition even if combined output is far larger.
2. **§2(1)(A) [Drafting]:** "Per year" is not tied to a measurement method. It doesn't say whether tonnage is wet or dry weight, measured at recovery or at delivery, or whether sediment and returned material count.
3. **§2(1)(A) [Drafting]:** "Recovery" is undefined. It's unclear whether lifting nodules for testing and returning them to the seabed counts as recovery.
4. **§2(1)(A) [Drafting]:** "Other mineral resources" is undefined, so the scope beyond polymetallic nodules (such as crusts or sulfides) is uncertain.
5. **§2(1)(B) [Drafting/Unworkable]:** The Secretary's "comparable risk" determination has no criteria, notice, process, or review standard. This gives the agency open-ended discretion, which is both a risk to us and a strong basis for an APA or vagueness challenge.
6. **§2 [Unworkable]:** "Secretary" is never defined, and the bill doesn't say which department administers the Act. §2(1)(B) uses "the Secretary" while §6 specifies the Secretary of State, so the two provisions may refer to different officials.
7. **§2(2) [Loophole]:** "U.S. person" covers only citizens and corporations organized under state law. It doesn't clearly cover LLCs, partnerships, or foreign-organized affiliates, so the entity structure of an operation could place it outside the definition. Counsel should check whether any part of our corporate structure falls into this gap.
8. **§2(2) [Drafting]:** "State" is undefined. It's unclear whether the District of Columbia and U.S. territories are included.
9. **§2(3) [Loophole]:** The "covered vessel" definition (over 5,000 gross tons, equipped for recovery) doesn't match the §3 prohibition's 10,000-ton, single-vessel test. A vessel can be prohibited under §3 without being a "covered vessel" for registry purposes.
10. **§2(3) [Loophole]:** "Equipped for the recovery of seabed mineral resources" is undefined. A vessel could be configured or retrofitted to avoid it, or recovery equipment could be deployed from a separate vessel.

## Section 3: Prohibition

11. **§3(a) [Loophole]:** The prohibition reaches conduct "in the United States" or by "any U.S. person anywhere," and does not reach non-U.S. persons operating outside U.S. jurisdiction. Most polymetallic nodule fields, such as the Clarion-Clipperton Zone, lie in the international Area and outside the U.S. EEZ, so foreign-flagged or foreign-owned contractors there may be outside the bill's reach. "In the United States" is also undefined, so it's unclear whether the EEZ or continental shelf is included.
12. **§3(a) [Loophole]:** "Engage in" is undefined. It's unclear whether the prohibition covers financing, chartering, supply, onshore processing, refining, or sale of recovered material, which could leave much of the supply chain outside the prohibition.
13. **§3(a) [Drafting]:** "Person" is undefined and §5 applies penalties to "any person" without distinguishing vessel operators, charterers, financiers, or crew.
14. **§3(a) [Drafting, major]:** The bill doesn't say how it relates to the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act of 1980 or to NOAA's existing licenses and permits under that law. It has no "notwithstanding" clause, repeal, or preemption language, so it's unclear whether existing permits stay valid or are overridden. Counsel should review this first.
15. **§3(b) [Loophole]:** The exemption covers only mining under an "exploration license" issued before enactment. If our rights rest on a commercial recovery permit, a contract with a sponsoring state, or another instrument, they may not be covered. We need to confirm the exact nature of each of our instruments.
16. **§3(b) [Loophole/Drafting]:** The exemption doesn't say whether it covers production beyond exploration scale, expanded areas, renewals, extensions, amendments, or transferred licenses. A broad reading protects expansion; a narrow reading does not.
17. **§3(b) [Drafting]:** The exemption applies to licenses issued "before the date of enactment," but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later under §8. Licenses issued in that gap are neither grandfathered nor subject to an operative prohibition, which creates an unaddressed transition period.
18. **§3(c) [Loophole, major]:** The "environmental assessment" exemption has no volume cap, duration limit, purpose verification, agency approval, or reporting requirement. Commercial-scale collection could be labeled as assessment. The term is also undefined and not tied to NEPA or any approved plan.
19. **§3 [Drafting]:** The bill has no good-faith reliance or safe harbor provision for operators acting under licenses or permits. Its absence weakens the position of operators who relied on prior government authorization.

## Section 4: Registry

20. **§4(a) [Drafting]:** "Operator" is undefined, so it's unclear whether the owner, charterer, master, or manager must register a vessel.
21. **§4(a) [Unworkable]:** The bill sets no registration deadline or effective date, and it doesn't authorize NOAA to issue forms, regulations, or procedures. NOAA has no implementing authority for this duty.
22. **§4 [Loophole]:** §5 penalizes only violations of §3. There's no penalty for failing to register or report, so the registry duties are unenforceable on their face.
23. **§4(b) [Unworkable]:** The content of quarterly reports is unspecified. The bill doesn't define "recovery activities," require volumes or locations, set due dates, or define "certified." Operators can't know what compliance looks like.
24. **§4(b) [Drafting, relevant to us]:** There's no confidentiality protection for commercially sensitive operational data in these reports, and no exemption from public disclosure. This creates a disclosure risk for proprietary information.
25. **§4 [Drafting]:** The bill doesn't say whether vessels operating under the §3(c) assessment exemption must report, which leaves the registry's reach uncertain.

## Section 5: Penalties

26. **§5(a) [Drafting, strong argument]:** §5(a) applies penalties to violations "occurring on or after the date of enactment," but §8 delays the entire Act's effective date by 180 days. We can argue that no penalty can attach before the effective date.
27. **§5(a) [Drafting]:** "Each day on which the violation occurs" is not defined. It's unclear whether violations are counted per vessel, per ton, or per day, and there's no aggregate cap, so exposure is hard to estimate.
28. **§5 [Unworkable]:** No agency is designated to enforce the civil penalties, issue administrative orders, or bring suit. The bill also has no venue, no statute of limitations, and no inflation adjustment.
29. **§5(b) [Drafting]:** "Willfully" is undefined, so the knowledge standard for criminal liability is unclear. The bill is also silent on corporate criminal liability and responsible-officer liability.
30. **§5(a) [Drafting]:** The civil penalty has no stated culpability standard, so it's unclear whether liability is strict or requires negligence or knowledge.

## Section 6: International Agreement

31. **§6 [Drafting]:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no obligation to reach an agreement and no consequence for failure. The bill also doesn't say how a negotiated agreement would take effect in U.S. law or whether it would modify §3.
32. **§6 [Loophole]:** The bill doesn't mention the International Seabed Authority or the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea. It can't reach contracts issued by the Authority or sponsored by other states, which limits the bill's practical reach outside U.S. waters.

## Section 7: Duration

33. **§7(a) [Drafting]:** "Serious harm" is undefined. The mechanism has no deadline for the NASEM report, no requirement that a report be produced, and no expedited procedure. If no report is issued, the prohibition may continue indefinitely.
34. **§7(a) [Drafting]:** The bill doesn't clarify how a "joint resolution" interacts with presentment to the President and the veto process.
35. **§7(a) vs. §7(b) [Drafting, major]:** §7(a) keeps the prohibition in effect until Congress acts, but §7(b) ends the entire Act 10 years after enactment regardless of whether any finding has been made. The two provisions conflict, and §7(a) is effectively overridden by §7(b).
36. **§7(b) [Drafting]:** The bill has no savings clause. It's unclear whether penalties for violations before the sunset, pending enforcement actions, or licenses issued under the Act survive the sunset.
37. **§7(b) [Drafting]:** The 10-year sunset runs from enactment, not from the effective date in §8, so the prohibition is in force for only about 9.5 years.

## Global Issues

38. **Whole Act [Unworkable]:** The bill has no general rulemaking authority, no appropriations, no severability clause, no judicial review provision, and no preemption or savings clause. Any of these omissions can be cited in markup as making the bill unworkable or litigation-prone.
39. **Whole Act [Drafting]:** The bill lacks a definitions section covering "person," "operator," "vessel," "exploration license," "environmental assessment," "recovery," "State," and "Secretary." Many of the items above trace back to these gaps.

In markup, it's safer to frame these points as requests for clarity and tighter definitions rather than as a roadmap for working around the prohibition.
