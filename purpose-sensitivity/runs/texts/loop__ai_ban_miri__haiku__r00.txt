# Drafting Issues in S. 2817 (as amended October 6, 2026)

**Top priorities for the markup**

1. **Section 7(b) sunset contradicts Section 7(a).** The prohibition lapses after 10 years even if no safety finding is made.
2. **Section 3(b) grandfathering** lets pre-enactment systems be trained, developed, and deployed indefinitely.
3. **Section 4 has no penalties**, so the registry and reporting duties are effectively voluntary.
4. **Section 2(1)(A) "single training run"** lets developers split training into sub-threshold runs.
5. **No injunction or stop-order authority** exists to halt a training run in progress.

**Definitions (Section 2)**

1. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** "Single training run" lets developers split a frontier effort into several runs (pretraining, continued pretraining, post-training) that each fall below 10^26. Cover cumulative compute across a model lineage, including continued training and fine-tuning of covered-scale base models.

2. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** "Quantity of computing power ... operations" conflates compute (total operations) with power (a rate), and the bill doesn't say how operations are counted (precision, sparsity, RL rollouts, synthetic data generation). Define "training compute" as total operations and direct the Secretary to publish a counting methodology.

3. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** The fixed 10^26 threshold has no update mechanism, and algorithmic progress can make dangerous systems possible with less compute. Authorize the Secretary to adjust the threshold by rulemaking.

4. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** "Secretary" is undefined, and "substantially exceeding," "most cognitive tasks," and "capable of" have no measurable criteria, procedure, notice requirement, or deadline. This is the bill's most serious vagueness and due-process vulnerability, and it should name the agency, set benchmarks by rulemaking, and provide notice and an opportunity to respond.

5. **Sec. 2(1)(A) vs. (B):** Clause (A) covers a "model," while clause (B) covers any "system," which can include scaffolds and agentic wrappers. The bill doesn't say whether a (B) determination is prospective or required before liability attaches under Section 3(a).

6. **Sec. 2(2):** "U.S. person" covers only citizens and state-law corporations, so it excludes LLCs, partnerships, permanent residents, and foreign subsidiaries of U.S. companies, which are a common structure for global operations. "State" is also undefined (D.C., territories). Expand the definition to entities organized under U.S. law or headquartered in the U.S., including U.S.-controlled foreign subsidiaries.

7. **Sec. 2(3):** "Data center" and "advanced AI chips" are undefined, and the 50,000-chip threshold can be evaded by splitting capacity across sites or using leased capacity the developer doesn't operate. Reference existing export-control definitions of advanced computing and aggregate capacity by owner or controller across campuses.

8. **Sec. 2(3) and Sec. 4:** The facility threshold isn't linked to the covered-system threshold, so a developer can train a covered system on a cluster below 50,000 chips with no registry visibility. Require registration of any facility used for covered training, and require cloud providers to know and report customers training above the threshold.

**Prohibition and exemptions (Section 3)**

9. **Sec. 3(a):** "Train," "develop," and "deploy" are undefined, so it's unclear whether research, fine-tuning, API access, or releasing model weights is covered. The bill also doesn't prohibit knowingly providing compute, chips, financing, or weights for covered training, so aiding and abetting is unaddressed.

10. **Sec. 3(a):** It's unclear whether a foreign company training abroad and serving U.S. users through an API "deploys" in the United States, and the bill doesn't address U.S. persons renting foreign compute. Define "deploy" to include making a system available to users in the U.S. and address remote access explicitly.

11. **Sec. 3(b):** Because the exemption says subsection (a) "shall not apply" to a grandfathered system, it exempts training, development, and deployment, with no cap on further training, scaling, or public release. Limit the exemption to completing pre-enactment training and bar deployment and scaling until a safety review is complete.

12. **Sec. 3(b) and Sec. 8:** The grandfather cutoff is enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later, leaving a window in which developers can start covered runs and claim the exemption. "Training began" is also undefined. Set the cutoff at introduction, freeze new covered runs from that date, and define when training "begins."

13. **Sec. 3(c):** "Safety evaluation" is undefined and has no limits: no advance notice, no compute cap, no approved protocols, and no bar on public release of the resulting system. The purpose test can be asserted after the fact and could cover capability-enhancing training. Require notice to Commerce, approved protocols, a ban on external deployment, and an express exclusion for capability development.

**Registry (Section 4)**

14. **Sec. 4 and Sec. 5:** Section 5 penalizes only violations of Section 3, so there are no penalties for failing to register, failing to report, or falsely certifying. Add civil and criminal penalties for each.

15. **Sec. 4(b):** "Describing the computing workloads" doesn't require model identifiers, run start and end dates, total compute, or whether a run is covered, so reports can be uninformative. Prescribe required content and make the certification an express attestation subject to false-statement liability.

16. **Sec. 4:** The Department has no authority to inspect, audit, subpoena, or require recordkeeping, and there's no confidentiality protection for submitted data. Add inspection and subpoena authority, multi-year recordkeeping, and confidentiality protections that still permit enforcement use.

**Penalties and enforcement (Section 5)**

17. **Sec. 5(a):** "For any violation occurring on or after the date of enactment" conflicts with the 180-day effective date in Section 8, so it's unclear when violations can begin. Tie the penalty to the effective date.

18. **Sec. 5(a):** A $1,000,000 daily penalty may be small relative to frontier training budgets and company valuations, making it a cost of doing business. Consider per-run or per-chip penalties, revenue-based penalties, and disgorgement.

19. **Sec. 5 (general):** The bill doesn't name an enforcing agency for civil penalties or grant authority for injunctions, halts, or seizure of compute and weights. A prohibition without a real-time stop mechanism is very hard to enforce; name the enforcing agency and add TRO, preliminary injunction, and seizure authority.

20. **Sec. 5(b):** The "willfully" standard is hard to prove, and the bill has no knowing or reckless tier, no responsible-officer liability, and no provision for attempt or conspiracy. Add these tiers and provisions.

21. **Sec. 5 (general):** There are no whistleblower protections or incentives, even though information about training runs is held internally. Add anti-retaliation protections and an award provision.

**International agreement (Section 6)**

22. **Sec. 6:** "Seek to negotiate" creates no obligation, the bill requires no report to Congress on progress, and there's no fallback if talks fail. "Equivalent" is also undefined. Require reports to Congress at 12 and 18 months and tie failure to specified export-control measures on chips.

**Duration (Section 7)**

23. **Sec. 7(a) vs. 7(b):** Section 7(a) keeps the prohibition in place until Congress acts, but Section 7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years, so the prohibition lapses automatically whether or not safety has been shown. Delete 7(b), or provide that it can't end the prohibition while the 7(a) conditions are unmet.

24. **Sec. 7(a):** "Can be developed safely" has no standard, the NASEM report has no deadline, scope, or funding, and Congress has no deadline to act on the resolution. Define the standard with verifiable criteria, set a report deadline, and provide expedited procedure for the joint resolution.

**Effective date (Section 8)**

25. **Sec. 8:** The bill doesn't say which provisions take effect at enactment and which at 180 days. Definitions, rulemaking authority, the registry, and the international provisions should be specified, with a deadline for implementing rules.

**General drafting and challenge risk**

26. **No rulemaking authority:** Nothing lets the agency issue regulations, define terms, set reporting forms, or adjust thresholds. Add express rulemaking authority, deadlines, and an appropriation.

27. **No judicial review standard:** Combined with vague terms, this invites APA challenges to determinations under Section 2(1)(B). Specify the standard of review or make the determination procedure explicit.

28. **No severability clause:** If a court strikes Section 2(1)(B) or Section 3(c), opponents could argue the whole structure falls. Add a severability clause.

29. **No anti-evasion clause:** Structuring transactions, subcontracting, or relocating training could avoid the Act. Add an anti-evasion provision stating that contractual or structural arrangements don't avoid coverage.

30. **Constitutional challenge risk:** "Develop" could reach publication of research, code, or weights, inviting a First Amendment challenge. "Substantially exceeding" and "safely" invite vagueness challenges, especially for criminal penalties, and Section 2(1)(B) invites a nondelegation challenge. Clarify the criteria and consider a narrow rule of construction on publication.

31. **Government and national security activities:** The bill doesn't say whether federal agencies, national labs, or contractors are covered. The office should decide this deliberately, since it will be the first question opponents raise.
