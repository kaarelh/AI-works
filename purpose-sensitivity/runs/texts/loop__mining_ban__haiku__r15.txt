RESPONSE:
**Priority items for Thursday:** (1) the §7(a)/§7(b) conflict, which lets the whole Act expire in 10 years; (2) the §4 registry and reporting duties, which carry no penalty; (3) the §3(b) and §3(c) exemptions, which could let commercial mining continue; (4) the §2(1)(A) threshold, which a fleet of smaller vessels could avoid; and (5) the §8 gap, which invites a rush to permit before enactment.

Please have Senate Legislative Counsel or our own counsel check these before we send them. Points about DSHMRA and the ISA reflect my general knowledge, so confirm current status.

**Definitions (§2)**

1. **§2(1)(A), single vessel:** The 10,000-ton threshold applies per vessel, so a fleet of vessels that each stay under it escapes coverage. It is also unclear whether the threshold is measured per calendar year, which would let operators split recovery across two years. *Fix:* aggregate recovery across all vessels controlled by the same person or affiliates, and use a rolling 12-month period.

2. **§2(1)(A), "commercial":** The operative definition never uses "commercial" as a standard, so an operator could argue that large-scale recovery for "testing" is not commercial. *Fix:* define commercial as any recovery whose material is sold, transferred, processed for sale, or otherwise used for profit, or measure coverage by volume alone.

3. **§2(1)(A) and §2(3), undefined terms:** "Recovery," "other mineral resources," and "seabed mineral resources" are undefined. It is unclear whether tonnage is wet or dry, whether it counts ore or contained metal, and whether discarded sediment counts. *Fix:* define "recovery" to include lifting material to the surface whether or not it is retained, and measure tonnage in wet metric tons.

4. **§2(1)(B), discretionary determination:** "The Secretary" is not identified (§6 refers to the Secretary of State), and the criteria ("risks," "comparable") are undefined. This gives an agency wide discretion to exempt activity and invites challenge. *Fix:* name the Secretary, set objective criteria, require a published determination with public comment, and make determinations reviewable.

5. **§2(1)(A), depth:** "Greater than 200 meters" does not say whether depth is measured at the water column or at the extraction site, and the bill does not say whether shallower mining is intentionally excluded. *Fix:* define the measurement and state the intent.

6. **§2(2), "U.S. person":** The definition excludes LLCs, partnerships, and entities organized under federal law, and "State" is undefined. *Fix:* cover any entity organized under U.S. federal or state law, including LLCs and partnerships, and define "State" to include the District of Columbia and the territories.

7. **§2(3), covered vessel:** The 5,000-gross-ton threshold controls the §4 registry, so smaller or retrofitted vessels escape reporting. "Equipped for" and "gross tons" are also undefined, and gross tonnage is not the same as deadweight. *Fix:* tie registration to any vessel engaged in covered activity, and define "equipped for."

**Prohibition (§3)**

8. **§3(a), "in the United States":** The phrase does not say whether it includes the U.S. exclusive economic zone and extended continental shelf, where most nodule-mining activity would occur. *Fix:* define "United States" to include the EEZ and continental shelf, and expressly reach U.S. persons operating in the Area beyond national jurisdiction.

9. **§3(a), "engage in":** The prohibition does not reach financing, insuring, investing in, chartering vessels for, or supplying equipment to commercial seabed mining. *Fix:* add prohibitions on financing, insuring, and procuring services for covered activity, and on aiding or abetting it.

10. **§3(a), no import or sales ban:** The bill does not prohibit importing, buying, processing, or selling minerals recovered through commercial seabed mining, so a foreign operator could mine abroad and sell in the U.S. market. *Fix:* prohibit the import, sale, and processing in the U.S. of minerals recovered through commercial deep seabed mining, regardless of where recovery occurred.

11. **§3(a), no attempt or preparatory-activity coverage:** The bill does not reach attempts, conspiracy, or preparatory steps such as site staging or deploying collection equipment. *Fix:* add coverage for attempts, conspiracy, and preparatory activity.

12. **§3(a), relationship to DSHMRA:** The bill does not amend the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act (30 U.S.C. §1401 et seq.), under which NOAA issues seabed mining permits, so conflict with that statute could be argued and permit processing could continue. *Fix:* add "notwithstanding any other law," bar NOAA from issuing or processing permits for covered activity, and amend or repeal the relevant DSHMRA permitting provisions.

13. **§3(a), extraterritorial reach:** The prohibition on U.S. persons abroad reaches only entities that qualify as U.S. persons, so foreign-owned operations sponsored by other states are outside its reach. *Fix:* state the nationality basis for jurisdiction to pre-empt challenge, and rely on the import and sales ban (item 10) as a backstop.

**Exemptions (§3(b)–(c))**

14. **§3(b), exploration licenses:** Exploration licenses do not authorize commercial recovery, but "mining conducted under" an exploration license could be read to cover commercial volumes recovered incidentally. *Fix:* exempt only exploration-scale activity below a set tonnage cap, and bar sale or processing of recovered material.

15. **§3(b), grandfather cutoff:** Tying the exemption to licenses "issued before the date of enactment" invites a rush to issue licenses before passage. The bill also does not address pending applications or contracts, such as ISA contracts or sponsoring-state authorizations. *Fix:* set the cutoff at the bill's introduction date, exclude pending applications, and state whether ISA contracts count as licenses.

16. **§3(b), no limit on grandfathered activity:** The exemption has no time limit, area limit, or volume cap, so grandfathered operations could continue indefinitely and expand. *Fix:* sunset the exemption within a fixed period, limit it to the licensed area, and bar volume increases.

17. **§3(c), environmental-assessment exemption:** The exemption has no volume cap, duration limit, sales prohibition, or reporting requirement, so commercial recovery could be relabeled as "environmental assessment." *Fix:* cap tonnage, prohibit sale, export, or processing of recovered material, require advance approval and public reporting, and subject these activities to §4 reports.

**Registry and reporting (§4)**

18. **§4, no penalty for registry violations:** §5 penalizes only violations of §3, so failing to register a vessel or file a report carries no penalty, which makes these duties unenforceable. *Fix:* extend §5 civil and criminal penalties to §4 violations, and add a penalty for false certifications.

19. **§4(a)–(b), "operator" undefined:** The reporting duty runs only from the "registered operator," so an unregistered operator has no duty to report, and the bill does not require disclosure of owners or controllers. *Fix:* define "operator," and require disclosure of beneficial owners and controllers above a set ownership threshold.

20. **§4(b), report content:** The bill does not specify what reports must contain (location, depth, tonnage, discharge, sediment plumes, incidents), sets no filing deadline, and does not require public posting or audit. *Fix:* specify required contents, require public posting, and give NOAA audit or third-party verification authority.

**Penalties (§5)**

21. **§5(a) and §8, conflicting effective dates:** §5(a) reaches violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §3 takes effect 180 days later under §8, so it is unclear whether conduct during that window is penalized. *Fix:* align the penalty start date with the §3 effective date.

22. **§5(a), "each day":** "Day" is undefined for continuing violations, and there is no per-ton or per-vessel alternative, no inflation adjustment, no profit disgorgement, and no forfeiture of vessels or equipment. *Fix:* add per-ton and per-vessel measures, forfeiture, disgorgement, and inflation adjustment.

23. **§5, no enforcing agency or remedies:** The bill does not say who brings civil actions, who may seek injunctions, or whether administrative penalties or seizures are available. *Fix:* designate the enforcing agency, and add injunctive authority and an administrative penalty process.

24. **§5, no citizen suit or whistleblower provisions:** Without a citizen-suit provision, enforcement depends entirely on federal action, and there are no whistleblower protections for employees who report violations. *Fix:* add a citizen-suit provision with notice requirements, and add whistleblower protections. (This is a strategic ask as well as a drafting fix.)

25. **§5(b), criminal liability:** "Willfully" is undefined, there is no responsible-corporate-officer liability, and conspiracy and attempt are not covered. *Fix:* define willfulness, add responsible-corporate-officer liability, and cover conspiracy and attempt.

**International agreement (§6)**

26. **§6, no obligation to conclude:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no duty to reach agreement, and there is no reporting requirement. "Equivalent" is undefined, so a weaker ISA regulatory regime could be treated as satisfying the requirement. *Fix:* require reports to Congress at 18 months and annually, require any agreement to be no weaker than §3, and direct State to raise the prohibition at the ISA.

27. **§6, no non-derogation clause:** Nothing says a future agreement cannot narrow the domestic prohibition. *Fix:* state that the domestic prohibition remains in force regardless of any international agreement.

**Duration (§7)**

28. **§7(a) vs. §7(b), direct conflict:** §7(a) keeps the prohibition in force until Congress makes a finding, but §7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years regardless of any finding. *Fix:* delete the §7(b) sunset, or make it conditional on a §7(a) finding.

29. **§7(a), "serious harm" undefined:** Without a definition, industry can argue that proposed mitigation meets the standard. *Fix:* define the standard (for example, "irreversible or serious harm to deep-sea ecosystems, including loss of species or habitat not recoverable on human timescales") and place the burden on proponents.

30. **§7(a), joint resolution mechanics:** A joint resolution requires presidential signature or a two-thirds override, and the bill specifies no expedited procedure. The lifting mechanism could stall, or a simple majority could be used to end the prohibition without a full review. *Fix:* specify the findings required, the voting procedure, and expedited consideration.

31. **§7(a), NASEM report:** The bill sets no deadline for the report, requires no congressional action on it, and provides no funding. *Fix:* set a deadline, provide funding, require public release, and require a vote within a set period.

32. **§7(b), savings clause:** On sunset, the bill says nothing about pending enforcement actions or violations that occurred before the sunset date. *Fix:* add a savings clause preserving liability for pre-sunset conduct.

**Effective date (§8)**

33. **§8, race to permit:** The 180-day delay gives operators time to accelerate operations or secure permits before the prohibition takes effect, and the bill includes no moratorium on new permits. *Fix:* impose an immediate moratorium on new permits, leases, and licenses on enactment, and shorten the effective date.

**General provisions (missing)**

34. **No severability clause:** If a court strikes one provision (such as the §3(b) exemption), the rest of the structure could fall. *Fix:* add a severability clause.

35. **No rule-of-construction or savings clause:** The bill does not say how it interacts with other federal laws (such as the Marine Mammal Protection Act or the Clean Water Act), and does not clarify its effect on scientific research or fishing. *Fix:* add a clause preserving other laws and clarifying the scope of the prohibition.

36. **No rulemaking or appropriations authority:** The bill gives no agency authority to issue implementing regulations and no funding for enforcement. *Fix:* add rulemaking authority and an authorization of appropriations.
