# Drafting Problems and Loopholes in S. 2817 (as amended October 6, 2026)

I worked from the text you pasted. I did not check it against the official bill text or the amendment, so please verify section references before publishing.

**Core definitions**

1. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** "Single training run" does not address cumulative compute across sequential or staged training, continued pretraining, or fine-tuning. A developer could split a large effort into runs that each fall under 10^26 operations, or fine-tune a model past the threshold.

2. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** The bill does not say how compute is measured, who measures it, or whether integer and floating-point operations are summed. Without a methodology, the threshold is hard to apply and easy to dispute.

3. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** "The Secretary" is never defined. The text does not say which Secretary (presumably Commerce), which affects enforcement, the registry, and the determination power.

4. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** The Secretary's determination has no criteria, notice, hearing, or review standard. "Substantially exceeding," "most," and "cognitive tasks" are undefined. This is both a discretion problem and a likely nondelegation and due-process challenge, and the clause could carry much of the bill's reach.

5. **Sec. 2(1)(B) and Sec. 3(b):** It is unclear whether a Secretarial determination applies to systems already in existence, and whether a system designated under (B) can claim the grandfather exemption, which keys on training start date.

**Scope of persons and geography**

6. **Sec. 2(2):** "U.S. person" covers citizens and state-chartered corporations only. It excludes LLCs, partnerships, and foreign-incorporated subsidiaries, so a company could operate through an entity organized abroad and fall outside "U.S. person anywhere."

7. **Sec. 2(2):** "State" is undefined. It is unclear whether the District of Columbia and U.S. territories are included.

8. **Sec. 3(a):** "In the United States" is undefined. The text does not address cloud compute physically located abroad, remote training by U.S.-based staff, or non-U.S. entities whose systems serve U.S. users.

9. **Sec. 3(a) and Sec. 6:** The prohibition does not reach non-U.S. persons acting outside the United States. Sec. 6's treaty provision is the only mechanism for that gap, and it is non-binding in practice (see item 29).

**Core prohibition terms**

10. **Sec. 3(a):** "Train," "develop," and "deploy" are undefined. It is unclear whether "develop" covers research, whether "deploy" covers API access, and whether releasing model weights counts. These gaps matter for enforcement and for possible First Amendment challenges involving publication.

11. **Sec. 3(a):** "Person" is not defined in the Act. It likely takes its meaning from the general definitions in 1 U.S.C. § 1, but the bill should say so.

**Grandfathering and transition**

12. **Sec. 3(b):** The exemption applies to the covered system as a whole. Read literally, it lets a pre-enactment system be trained, deployed, and offered to the public indefinitely, because it does not limit the exemption to training.

13. **Sec. 3(b):** "Training began" is undefined. The text does not address ongoing multi-month runs, checkpoints from earlier runs, or whether additional compute can be added to an exempt system.

14. **Sec. 3(b) and Sec. 4:** Grandfathered systems have no registration or reporting duty. The registry covers facilities, not systems, so these systems may operate with no federal visibility. Fine-tuning or modifying a grandfathered model is also not addressed.

15. **Sec. 3(b) and Sec. 8:** The exemption keys on the enactment date, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. A training run that starts during those 180 days is neither grandfathered nor yet prohibited, so its status is unclear.

**Safety evaluation exemption**

16. **Sec. 3(c):** "Safety evaluation" is undefined. The "for the purpose of" test is subjective and hard to prove. The exemption has no requirements for registration, qualified evaluators, containment, duration limits, or a bar on commercial deployment, so a developer could label commercial training as safety evaluation.

17. **Sec. 3(c):** The bill does not require evaluation results to be reported to Commerce or to the National Academies, and it does not say whether the exemption covers deployment to users.

**Covered facilities and the registry**

18. **Sec. 2(3):** "Data center" and "advanced AI chips" are undefined. The 50,000-chip threshold can be avoided by spreading chips across multiple sites, and the bill has no aggregation rule for affiliated facilities.

19. **Sec. 2(3) and Sec. 4(a):** The bill sets no geographic limit, so it is unclear whether a U.S.-owned facility abroad must register. The registry is also not linked to Sec. 3: a facility can be registered and still run prohibited training.

20. **Sec. 4 and Sec. 5:** Sec. 5 penalizes only violations of Sec. 3. Failing to register or filing a false quarterly certification carries no penalty under this Act. (False statements may be covered by 18 U.S.C. § 1001, but this Act does not reference it.)

21. **Sec. 4(a):** The bill sets no deadline for initial registration. "Operator" is undefined, which leaves open whether it means the owner, the cloud provider, or the tenant.

22. **Sec. 4(b):** "Computing workloads" is undefined, and the bill does not say what a report must contain. Reports need not identify which workloads involve covered systems, and the bill does not require Commerce to review, publish, or audit them or to protect confidential business information.

**Penalties and enforcement**

23. **Sec. 5(a):** "Violation" is not defined. It is unclear whether the $1,000,000 penalty applies per day, per model, per training run, or per deployment, and the "each day" language invites disputes over continuing violations.

24. **Sec. 5(a):** The bill does not name an enforcing agency or procedure. It does not say whether the Attorney General brings civil actions or an agency imposes administrative penalties, and it does not specify a mental state, so it is unclear whether liability is strict.

25. **Sec. 5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined. The bill does not address corporate criminal liability, officer liability, or aiding-and-abetting and conspiracy liability.

26. **Sec. 5(a) and Sec. 8:** Penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but the prohibition takes effect 180 days after enactment. The two provisions conflict on when liability begins.

27. **Whole Act:** The bill has no rulemaking authority, appropriation, whistleblower protection, savings clause, severability clause, or preemption clause. It does not address interaction with existing state AI laws, and it has no mechanism to update the 10^26 threshold as compute costs change.

**Duration, sunset, and treaty**

28. **Sec. 7(a):** "Safely" is undefined. The National Academies report has no deadline and no required contents, and the bill does not say whether it is a precondition for a joint resolution or only informs one. The bill also does not say what happens if no report is produced. The joint resolution is an unusual mechanism for lifting a statutory prohibition, and it raises questions about legislative findings that bind future conduct.

29. **Sec. 7(a) and Sec. 7(b):** The prohibition lasts "until" Congress acts, but the entire Act ceases to have effect 10 years after enactment regardless. The text is internally inconsistent about whether the prohibition is permanent or capped at 10 years.

30. **Sec. 7(b):** The sunset has no savings clause for pending investigations or prosecutions. It is unclear whether violations committed during the Act's effective period remain prosecutable afterward. The general savings statute (1 U.S.C. § 109) may apply, but that should be confirmed.

31. **Sec. 6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no obligation to reach an agreement. The bill has no reporting requirement, no consequence if talks fail, and no definition of "equivalent." It also does not say whether the domestic prohibition changes if an agreement is reached.

**Notes for the explainer:**

- **Drafting errors versus policy choices.** Items 12 through 15 (grandfathering) and 16 (safety evaluation) may be deliberate policy choices rather than mistakes. Frame them as "as drafted" and confirm the sponsors' intent with committee staff before calling them loopholes.
- **Most significant conflicts.** Items 26 (penalty start date versus effective date), 29 (permanent versus 10-year duration), and 20 (registry with no penalty) are internal contradictions or enforcement gaps that are easy to verify and likely to matter to readers.
