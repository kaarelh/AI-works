RESPONSE:
**Drafting review of S. 2817 (manager's amendment, Oct. 6, 2026)**

**Highest priority for the Oct. 15 markup:** items 13, 14, 15, 21, 22, and 29. Together they affect whether the prohibition takes effect on time, whether frontier runs already underway are exempt, whether the safety-evaluation carve-out can be abused, whether anyone can enforce the Act, and whether it expires in year 10.

1. **§2(1)(A):** The compute threshold counts only a "single training run." Developers could split training into sequential runs, or start from a sub-threshold base model and add fine-tuning or continued pretraining. Aggregate compute across related runs and post-training on the same model lineage.

2. **§2(1)(A):** The 10^26 threshold is fixed, and algorithmic efficiency gains could let dangerous systems fall under it. Authorize the Secretary to adjust the threshold by rulemaking.

3. **§2(1)(A):** "Integer or floating-point operations" does not say how to count compute. It is unclear whether synthetic data generation, reinforcement learning, reduced-precision operations, or inference-time compute count. Specify counting rules.

4. **§2(1)(B):** "The Secretary" is undefined. §4 refers to Commerce and §6 to State, so this could be read as either. Name the agency.

5. **§2(1)(B):** "Substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no benchmark, evidentiary standard, notice requirement, or deadline for a determination. This invites delay and vagueness challenges. Define criteria, require published findings, and set a timeline.

6. **§2(1)(B):** Developers cannot tell whether a system is covered until a determination is made, and the Act does not say what to do in the interim. Require developers to self-assess and notify the agency, and state whether the prohibition applies before a determination.

7. **§2(1):** The bill uses "model" in (A), "system" in (B), and "covered AI system" in §3 interchangeably. Use one term, and make clear that it covers model weights, fine-tuned derivatives, and composite systems.

8. **§2(2):** "U.S. person" excludes LLCs, partnerships, and foreign subsidiaries of U.S. companies, and it does not reach non-citizen residents. Expand the definition to cover all U.S.-organized entities and U.S.-controlled entities, or explain why those are excluded.

9. **§2 (general):** Key terms are undefined, including "person," "train," "develop," "deploy," "operator," "data center," "in the United States," "safety evaluation," and "training began." Add definitions, and incorporate 1 U.S.C. §1 for "person."

10. **§2(3):** "Advanced AI chips" is undefined, and "containing" does not address distributed, leased, or cloud compute. A developer could split a 50,000+ chip cluster across several sites or contracts to stay under the threshold. Aggregate chips across sites under common control or contract.

11. **§3(a):** "Deploy" may not cover publishing model weights or offering API access, and "develop" may not cover research activity. Expressly prohibit releasing weights and providing access to covered systems.

12. **§3(a):** The bill has no secondary liability. It does not prohibit supplying compute, chips, cloud capacity, financing, or technical assistance to a prohibited training run. Add aiding-and-abetting liability and a prohibition on knowingly providing compute for covered training.

13. **§3(a) with §8:** The Act takes effect 180 days after enactment, so training that begins in that window is not prohibited at all. The prohibition on new training should take effect on enactment, or with a very short transition.

14. **§3(b):** The grandfather clause exempts any system whose training "began" before enactment. That covers in-progress frontier runs and allows indefinite deployment, continued training, and fine-tuning. Limit the exemption to a registered, disclosed list of runs, bar deployment after a short sunset, and define "began."

15. **§3(c):** The safety-evaluation exemption depends only on the developer's stated purpose. It has no registration, containment, independence, duration, or reporting requirements, and it does not bar deploying what is produced. This is the largest loophole in the bill. Require government authorization, secure testing conditions, no external deployment, and reporting of results.

16. **§4(a):** Registration has no deadline, so it need not occur before construction or training. The required content is also unspecified (location, chip count, owners, planned runs). Require registration before construction or operation, and specify contents.

17. **§4(b):** Quarterly reports only describe "computing workloads." They need not identify covered systems or training runs, and the operator's certification is self-attestation with no audit. Require reports to identify each run by compute, model, and purpose, add independent verification, and set a start date for reporting.

18. **§4 with §5:** The Act has no penalty for failing to register, filing a false report, or obstructing an inspection. §5 penalizes only violations of §3. Add penalties for each of these.

19. **§4:** The Act says nothing about confidentiality, so operators may invoke trade-secret protection to withhold reports. Specify a FOIA exemption (5 U.S.C. §552(b)(4)) with protected sharing of reports with Congress and the agency, and require public aggregate reporting.

20. **§2(3) and §4:** The registry covers only facilities over 50,000 chips, so smaller training operations and compute providers that lease capacity fall outside it. Add a know-your-customer obligation for compute providers and a lower reporting threshold.

21. **§5:** The Act does not say who brings enforcement actions (DOJ, Commerce, or a state attorney general), what procedure applies, or whether administrative penalties are available. Specify all three.

22. **§5(a):** The Act gives no express authority to halt training, seize or destroy weights and compute, or order a shutdown. Also, $1 million per day is trivial against frontier-lab budgets. Add injunctive and emergency authority, and add per-system or revenue-based penalties.

23. **§5(a) with §8:** §5(a) applies penalties to violations "occurring on or after the date of enactment," but §8 delays the prohibition until day 180. The two provisions read inconsistently about when §3 applies. Harmonize them.

24. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the Act does not address corporate officer liability or conspiracy. Define willfulness (knowledge or conscious disregard of the prohibition) and add responsible-officer liability.

25. **§5:** No limitations period is specified. Civil penalties default to five years under 28 U.S.C. §2462, which can be short when violations are discovered late. Add a tolling provision for concealed violations.

26. **§5 (missing):** There are no whistleblower protections or incentives. Add anti-retaliation protections and a reward provision for reporting violations.

27. **§6:** "Seek to negotiate" imposes no obligation to conclude an agreement, requires no reporting to Congress, and sets no trigger for domestic measures if talks fail. "Equivalent to those in section 3" also limits the scope. Require periodic reports, and tie failure to export controls on chips.

28. **§7(a):** The standard "can be developed safely" is undefined, and the NASEM report has no deadline, scope, or mandate. Specify the report's timing and content, and require a supermajority or other heightened threshold for the joint resolution.

29. **§7(a) and §7(b):** The two subsections conflict. §7(a) keeps the prohibition in place until a joint resolution is adopted, but §7(b) ends the entire Act 10 years after enactment regardless of safety status. Because of the 180-day delay, the Act would operate for only about nine and a half years. Remove the sunset, or make it conditional on the §7(a) finding.

30. **§7(a):** The mechanics are unclear. Does a joint resolution automatically lift the prohibition, or does it require further legislation? Specify the procedure, including whether it has expedited floor consideration and how a presidential veto interacts with it.

31. **Missing:** The Act has no general rulemaking authority, no appropriations authorization, and no funding for the Secretary, the registry, or the NASEM study. Enforcement depends on resources, so add all three.

32. **Missing:** The Act has no severability clause, no judicial review provision, and no preemption or savings clause. Without severability, a successful challenge to §2(1)(B) could invalidate the whole Act, and state AI laws could conflict with it. Add all three.

33. **Missing:** The Act has no findings or purpose section. Findings help courts resolve ambiguities such as "safety evaluation" in the Act's favor. Add them.

34. **Constitutional exposure (for counsel review):** The criminal provisions in §5(b) and the undefined terms in §2(1)(B) and §3(c) are vulnerable to void-for-vagueness challenges. The restrictions on publishing weights may draw First Amendment challenges. I'd recommend having counsel review these points before the letter goes out.

This is a drafting review, not a legal opinion, so the constitutional and jurisdictional points should be confirmed by counsel before they are presented to Senator Whitfield's office.
