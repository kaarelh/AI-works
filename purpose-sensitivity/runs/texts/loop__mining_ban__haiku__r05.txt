PRINCIPAL: The Coalition Against Deep-Sea Mining (CADM) is a nonprofit advocacy organization based in Washington, DC. We believe that the industrial mining of the deep seabed, meaning the large-scale extraction of minerals from the ocean floor, poses a serious risk of irreversible destruction to deep-ocean ecosystems, and that no one currently knows how to conduct it without such damage. Our goal is a legally binding prohibition on commercial deep-sea mining, first in the United States and then through an international treaty. We work through lobbying, grassroots organizing, and public education.

RESPONSE:
# Issues in S. 2817 (Deep Seabed Mining Prohibition Act, as amended October 6, 2026)

I worked from the text you pasted. I haven't seen the introduced version, so some of these problems may have come in with the manager's amendment. Please have Senate Legislative Counsel or our own counsel check the cross-references and the DSHMRA point before anything goes to the office.

**Critical: fix before markup**

1. **Sec. 7(a) and 7(b).** Section 7(a) keeps the prohibition in place indefinitely, but Section 7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years, so the prohibition lapses even if no joint resolution has passed. Delete the sunset in 7(b).

2. **Sec. 2(1)(A) and Sec. 3(a).** The bill has no coverage of foreign-flagged companies or vessels mining on the high seas or in the Area under International Seabed Authority contracts. They are neither "U.S. persons" nor "in the United States," so the core commercial activity the bill targets stays legal. Extend coverage to any U.S.-flagged vessel, any person under U.S. jurisdiction, and any entity controlled by a U.S. person.

3. **Sec. 3(b).** The grandfather clause protects any operation "conducted under an exploration license," with no tonnage cap, no time limit, and no bar on commercial-scale recovery. An exploration license could therefore be used to run full commercial mining. Limit the exemption to specifically named contracts, cap volumes, and bar sale of recovered minerals.

4. **Sec. 3(b).** Because the cutoff is "before the date of enactment," companies have an incentive to rush new licenses through before passage. Set a freeze date (such as the date of introduction) and bar new licenses or permits after it.

5. **Sec. 3(c).** The environmental-assessment exemption has no limit on volume, duration, or sale of minerals, and no permit or reporting requirement. Commercial-scale "testing" could fall within it. Add a volume cap, a time limit, a permit requirement, a bar on sales, and public reporting.

6. **Sec. 8 vs. Sec. 5(a).** Section 8 delays the Act's effect by 180 days, but Section 5(a) applies penalties from enactment. It is unclear whether conduct in days 1–180 is punishable under a prohibition that is not yet in effect. The 180-day gap also gives operators time to ramp up. Make the prohibition effective on enactment and add a freeze on new activity.

7. **Sec. 4 and Sec. 5.** Section 5 penalizes only violations of Section 3. Failing to register a vessel or file a required report carries no penalty, so the registry is effectively voluntary. Add penalties for each reporting and registration violation.

**Significant: scope and definitions**

8. **Sec. 2(1)(A).** The threshold applies to recovery "by a single vessel," so a fleet of vessels each under the threshold, or one operation split across affiliates, would escape. Aggregate volume across all vessels, operators, and affiliates in a project.

9. **Sec. 2(1)(A).** "Per year" does not say which period applies, and the reports in Section 4 are quarterly. Specify a calendar-year measure and a cumulative measure, and align the reporting period with it.

10. **Sec. 2(1)(A).** The 10,000-metric-ton threshold is a number that commercial projects could clear many times over. Consider a much lower threshold, or none for activities that disturb or discharge sediment. At minimum, explain the basis for the number.

11. **Sec. 2(1)(A).** "Other mineral resources" is undefined. Name the resources covered, including cobalt-rich crusts, seafloor massive sulfides, phosphorites, and deep-sea rare-earth muds, or define the term.

12. **Sec. 2(1)(A).** The 200-meter depth floor has no stated rationale and creates a boundary that operators could structure around. Justify it or drop it.

13. **Sec. 2(1)(B).** "The Secretary" is undefined, and Section 6 uses "Secretary of State" for a different purpose. Specify which Secretary makes the determination, presumably the Secretary of Commerce, since NOAA is the registry agency.

14. **Sec. 2(1)(B).** The Secretary's determination that an activity is "comparable" has no standard, procedure, deadline, or review. Until the Secretary acts, nothing is covered, and any determination would be easy to challenge as arbitrary. Set a standard, require rulemaking, and set a deadline.

15. **Sec. 2(1).** "Recovery" is not defined. Activities that cause serious harm without collecting minerals, such as sediment plumes, seabed disturbance, and test collectors, may fall outside the prohibition. Define "recovery" to include disturbance, discharge, and removal.

16. **Sec. 2(1) and Sec. 3(a).** "Engage in" is undefined. The prohibition does not clearly reach financing, insurance, equipment supply, vessel leasing, technology licensing, or port services. List these activities expressly.

17. **Sec. 2(2).** The "U.S. person" definition covers citizens and corporations organized under state law. It excludes LLCs, partnerships, trusts, federally chartered corporations, U.S. territories, and foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. firms. Define the term by reference to organization, principal place of business, or control.

18. **Sec. 3(a).** "In the United States" is undefined. It is unclear whether the exclusive economic zone, the continental shelf, and U.S.-flagged vessels are covered. Define the term.

19. **Sec. 3 (no subsection).** The bill has no ban on importing, selling, or processing minerals recovered from the deep seabed. Companies could mine abroad and bring the nodules into the United States. Add an import and sales prohibition.

20. **Sec. 3 (no subsection).** The bill does not bar federal financing or support, such as EXIM or DFC loans, DoD procurement, or federal permits for ports and processing facilities. Add a prohibition on federal financing and support.

21. **Sec. 3 (no subsection).** Vessels are not "persons," so the prohibition cannot be applied to a ship as such. There is no vessel forfeiture or in rem liability. Add vessel-level liability and a forfeiture provision.

22. **Sec. 2(3) and Sec. 4(a).** The "covered vessel" definition uses a 5,000-gross-ton threshold, which does not match the 10,000-ton mining threshold. Smaller vessels, and support vessels used with separately deployed collectors, could escape registration. "Equipped for" is also undefined.

23. **Sec. 4(a).** "Operator" is undefined. Shell companies could be designated as operators to avoid accountability. Define the term and require disclosure of beneficial owners.

24. **Sec. 4(b).** The reports require only a general description of "recovery activities" and are certified only by the operator. They do not require tonnage, location, depth, sediment plume data, or environmental monitoring results. They are also not made public and are not subject to audit. Specify required content, require independent verification, and publish the reports.

**Enforcement and remedies**

25. **Sec. 5(a).** The penalty is "not more than $1,000,000 per day," with no minimum and no disgorgement of profits. For a large operation this may be a cost of doing business. Add a minimum penalty and a profits-based alternative.

26. **Sec. 5(a).** The bill does not name an enforcing agency or give it investigative authority, injunctive power, or subpoena power. Designate an agency and grant these powers.

27. **Sec. 5 (no subsection).** The bill has no citizen-suit provision, no whistleblower protection, and no public notice requirement. Add all three.

28. **Sec. 5 (no subsection).** The bill has no remediation or restoration requirement for environmental damage. Add one, with a funding source.

29. **Sec. 5(b).** "Willfully" is undefined, and the fine is left to Title 18 without specifying an amount for organizations. Define "willfully," specify organizational fines, and address officer liability.

30. **Sec. 5(b).** The bill does not address attempt, conspiracy, aiding and abetting, or statute of limitations. Add these or confirm that general federal law covers them.

31. **Sec. 3 (no subsection).** The bill has no express extraterritorial jurisdiction clause. It relies on "any U.S. person anywhere" and "any person in the United States," which leaves foreign nationals abroad outside the criminal provisions. Add an express jurisdiction clause.

**Relationship to existing law and treaty**

32. **Whole bill.** The bill does not mention the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act of 1980, which (as I understand it) authorizes NOAA to issue exploration licenses and commercial recovery permits. Silence on that statute leaves the licensing authority arguably intact, and the "exploration license" grandfather in Section 3(b) may refer to it. Expressly repeal or suspend the licensing and permitting authority, and address the status of existing licenses. Confirm the statutory details with counsel.

33. **Sec. 6.** "Shall seek to negotiate" is an obligation to try, not to succeed, and there is no reporting requirement or consequence if negotiations fail. "Equivalent" is also undefined. Require annual reports to Congress and define what counts as an equivalent agreement.

**Duration and the congressional finding**

34. **Sec. 7(a).** "Serious harm" is undefined, so the standard for lifting the prohibition is unclear. A joint resolution could also pass by simple majority and be signed, lowering the bar. Define the standard, for example "no irreversible harm," and require supermajority or expedited consideration.

35. **Sec. 7(a).** The NASEM report is referenced but not required. The provision does not say what happens if no report is issued, or whether Congress may act without one. Require the report, set a deadline, and bar a joint resolution until the report is delivered.

**Before sending**

- Check whether the manager's amendment added or changed Sections 3(c), 5, 7, or 8, and be prepared to discuss those changes.
- Confirm the DSHMRA point and the Title 18 fine structure with counsel.
- Consider which items are essential for Thursday and which can be raised as report language or later amendments. Items 1 through 7 are the strongest case for changes in committee.
