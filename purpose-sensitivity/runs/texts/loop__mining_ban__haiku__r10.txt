RESPONSE:
Below are the issues I found in the text as amended October 6, grouped by section and numbered consecutively. I've ordered them by section, not priority. The first items under §§ 2, 3, and 5 are likely the most consequential for the markup.

**Definitions (§ 2)**

1. **§ 2(1)(A):** The "by a single vessel" language lets an operator split a project across several vessels, each under 10,000 metric tons per year, and fall outside the definition. Measure recovery at the operator, project, and affiliate level and aggregate across vessels.

2. **§ 2(1)(A):** The 10,000-ton annual threshold is far below commercial-scale nodule plans, which have been proposed at volumes in the range of over a million tons per year. Staff should verify current figures before citing them. Consider a much lower threshold or a de minimis standard.

3. **§ 2(1)(A):** The definition is triggered by tonnage recovered, not by disturbance. Collectors can disturb large areas and generate sediment plumes while recovering relatively little, and that activity would fall outside the definition. Add disturbed-area or sediment-discharge triggers.

4. **§ 2(1)(A):** "Recovery," "mineral resources," and "seabed" are undefined. It is unclear whether the bill covers crusts, hydrothermal sulfides, processing at sea, or discharge of tailings and wastewater. Define each term and expressly include associated discharges.

5. **§ 2(1)(A):** It is unclear whether "at depths greater than 200 meters" describes where the vessel operates or where minerals are recovered. Mining routes can cross depth boundaries, so this creates an easy argument that shallower operations are excluded. Clarify, or remove the depth limit.

6. **§ 2(1)(A):** The tonnage measurement basis is unspecified (wet or dry weight, nodules only or total material including sediment). Operators could use the most favorable measure. Specify it.

7. **§ 2(1)(B):** The Secretary's determination that an activity is "comparable" has no criteria, process, public notice, or stated standard for judicial review. This invites both a loophole, where the Secretary declines to make a determination, and litigation over whether a determination was arbitrary. Set out criteria such as area, sediment, and duration, and require a published determination with a comment period.

8. **§ 2 (general):** "Secretary" is never defined. NOAA, the Interior Department (BOEM), and State all have overlapping roles in seabed matters. Define the term, likely Commerce given the NOAA registry, and name the enforcing agency.

9. **§ 2(2):** "U.S. person" covers only citizens and State-organized corporations. It excludes LLCs, partnerships, trusts, DC-organized and territorial entities, and possibly permanent residents, and many ventures operate through LLCs and subsidiaries. Expand the definition to any entity organized under U.S. federal, State, or territorial law, and consider a control or principal-place-of-business test.

10. **§ 2(2):** A foreign parent company operating through a foreign-incorporated affiliate falls outside "U.S. person," and so outside the "anywhere" clause of § 3(a). Add a control test covering entities controlled by U.S. persons or with U.S. principal management.

11. **§ 2(3):** The 5,000 gross ton threshold excludes smaller vessels from the registry, and "equipped for the recovery" is undefined. A retrofitted vessel or a fleet of smaller vessels could operate without registration. Require registration for any vessel engaged in seabed mineral recovery, regardless of tonnage, and define "equipped."

**Prohibition (§ 3)**

12. **§ 3(a):** "Engage in" is undefined. It is unclear whether the prohibition reaches financing, investment, insurance, chartering, equipment manufacturing, port services, or sale of recovered minerals. Add language covering direct or indirect participation, including financing and facilitation.

13. **§ 3(a):** The geographic scope is unclear: "in the United States" does not say whether it covers the territorial sea, the EEZ, the outer continental shelf, or the Area beyond national jurisdiction. Because the United States is not a party to UNCLOS, the reach of "any U.S. person anywhere" over the Area is legally contestable. State the jurisdictional basis expressly.

14. **§ 3(a):** The bill has no import or sale prohibition. Minerals mined abroad in violation of the Act could be imported and processed in the United States. Add a ban on importing, selling, and processing minerals recovered through commercial deep-seabed mining.

15. **§ 3(a):** The bill has no vessel, flag, or port-access measures, so foreign-flagged vessels could mine elsewhere and be serviced from U.S. ports. Add a prohibition on port services and vessel-related services for prohibited activity.

16. **§ 3 (general):** The bill does not mention the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act (30 U.S.C. § 1401 et seq.), under which NOAA issues exploration licenses and commercial recovery permits. Unless the bill expressly overrides that authority, a company could argue it still authorizes commercial recovery. Amend or expressly supersede the statute as to commercial recovery. Staff should verify the citation with Legislative Counsel.

17. **§ 3(b):** "Exploration license" is undefined and does not appear elsewhere in the bill. A company could argue that large-scale collection conducted under an exploration license is exempt even if it is commercial in scale. Define the term, cap volumes under any grandfathered license, and state that an exploration license does not authorize commercial recovery.

18. **§ 3(b):** The grandfather clause is keyed to the date of enactment, but § 8 delays the Act's effect by 180 days. That creates a window in which new licenses can be issued and then grandfathered. Key the exemption to the bill's introduction date and bar new licenses and permits for commercial recovery from that date.

19. **§ 3(b):** The heading "Existing operations" does not match the text, which protects any license issued before enactment regardless of whether any operation exists. The clause also does not address pending applications or approved but unissued permits. Clarify the scope and cover pending applications.

20. **§ 3(b):** Grandfathered operations have no volume cap, expiration date, or reporting requirement. Add all three.

21. **§ 3(c):** The environmental assessment exemption has no limits on tonnage, area, duration, number of vessels, sale of recovered material, or who conducts the work. Its "for the purpose of" test turns on subjective intent, which is hard to enforce, and commercial test mining could be labeled as assessment. Cap tonnage and area, require advance NOAA authorization with public notice, bar sale or export of recovered material beyond samples, and require independent monitoring.

22. **§ 3(c):** Environmental assessment activities carry no reporting obligation, and if the vessel is under 5,000 gross tons they are also outside the § 4 registry. Require registration and reporting for all exempt activity.

**Registry and reporting (§ 4)**

23. **§ 4(a):** No registration deadline is set, and nothing requires NOAA to maintain or publish the registry. Set a deadline and require public access.

24. **§ 4(b):** The report requirement has no minimum content. It should specify tonnage, location, depth, area disturbed, sediment discharge, plume monitoring, bycatch, and equipment used.

25. **§ 4(b):** Nothing requires public disclosure of the reports, which operators could try to shield as confidential business information. Require publication, with a narrow and defined confidentiality exception.

26. **§ 4 (general):** "Operator" is undefined, so a shell company or contractor could be named as operator, and the bill does not require disclosure of beneficial or parent owners. Define the term and require disclosure of the ultimate parent and beneficial owners.

27. **§ 4 (general):** NOAA has no authority to inspect vessels, audit records, place observers, or verify reports. Add inspection, observer, recordkeeping, and audit authority.

28. **§ 4(b):** It is unclear whether a zero-activity quarter requires a report. Require a certification even when no recovery occurs.

**Penalties (§ 5)**

29. **§ 5(a)–(b):** Penalties attach only to violations of § 3. Failure to register, missing reports, and false certifications under § 4 carry no penalty. Extend penalties to § 4 and to knowingly false statements.

30. **§ 5 (general):** The bill names no enforcing agency and gives no authority to seek injunctions, cease-and-desist orders, vessel detention, or seizure and forfeiture of vessels, equipment, minerals, or proceeds. Add all of these.

31. **§ 5(a):** The "for each day" penalty does not define the unit of violation, so an operator could argue that continuous operation is a single violation. The $1,000,000 figure is a ceiling with no floor, no inflation adjustment, and possibly no meaningful deterrent for multibillion-dollar projects. Define the unit (per day, per vessel, or per ton), add per-ton penalties, and include an inflation adjustment and a minimum.

32. **§ 5(a) and § 8:** § 5(a) reaches violations "occurring on or after the date of enactment," but § 8 delays the whole Act by 180 days. The two provisions conflict on when the prohibition takes effect. Reconcile them.

33. **§ 5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the criminal provision has no responsible-corporate-officer liability, no express coverage of conspiracy or aiding and abetting, and no limitations period. Add these provisions.

34. **§ 5 (general):** The bill has no citizen-suit provision and no whistleblower protections. Add both.

**International agreement (§ 6)**

35. **§ 6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" obligates the Secretary to try, not to reach an agreement. The bill has no progress report to Congress and no trigger for trade measures if negotiations fail. "Equivalent" is also undefined. Require a report to Congress at 18 months and authorize import restrictions if no agreement is reached.

**Duration and sunset (§ 7)**

36. **§ 7(a):** "Serious harm" is undefined, so the standard for lifting the prohibition could be met by minimal mitigation claims. Define the term, or require a finding that impacts are reversible and monitored.

37. **§ 7(a):** The provision depends on a National Academies report, but nothing requires that the study be commissioned, funded, or completed by a date. Set a deadline and funding.

38. **§ 7(a):** The joint resolution mechanism has no expedited procedure and does not say how it interacts with presentment to the President. Specify the procedure.

39. **§ 7(b):** The 10-year sunset conflicts with § 7(a)'s open-ended duration and ends the prohibition regardless of any harm finding. Delete § 7(b).

40. **§ 7(b):** "Cease to have effect" has no savings clause, so enforcement of violations committed before the sunset, and pending actions, could be extinguished. If the sunset is deleted this is moot, but add a savings clause in any case.

**Effective date (§ 8)**

41. **§ 8:** The 180-day delay applies to the whole Act, including the registry, so reporting does not begin until day 180. Combined with § 3(b)'s enactment-based grandfather clause, this gives companies time to mobilize. Make §§ 4 and 5 effective on enactment, and key the prohibition's effective date to introduction or enactment, whichever is earlier.

**General drafting (whole bill)**

42. **Whole bill:** There is no severability clause. If a court strikes § 2(1)(B), § 3(c), or § 7(a), the remaining structure's integrity is unclear. Add one.

43. **Whole bill:** The bill gives no rulemaking authority, no deadlines for implementing regulations, and no appropriations for NOAA or DOJ enforcement. Add all three.

44. **Whole bill:** The bill lacks a rule of construction stating that nothing in the Act authorizes any activity it prohibits, and it does not address interaction with state law or other federal statutes. Add both.

45. **Whole bill:** Several operative terms are undefined: "vessel," "seabed," "mineral resources," "recovery," "environmental assessment," "exploration license," "operator," and "Secretary." Add a single definitions section.
