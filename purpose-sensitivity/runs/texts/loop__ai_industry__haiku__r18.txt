# S. 2817 Drafting Issues and Potential Loopholes

**Definitions (§2)**

1. **§2(1)(A)**: "Single training run" is undefined. The bill doesn't say whether compute is aggregated across pretraining, post-training, RL, and continued training, or across related runs. A developer could argue that staged training falls below 10^26 per run.
2. **§2(1)(A)**: The threshold refers to "computing power" but is defined by a count of operations. The bill has no measurement method (theoretical vs. actual, precision formats, sparsity) and gives no agency authority to set one.
3. **§2(1)(A)–(B)**: Subparagraph (A) covers an "artificial intelligence model," while (B) covers an "artificial intelligence system." The bill doesn't say whether scaffolding, ensembles, agents, or inference-time compute count toward either test.
4. **§2(1)(B)**: "The Secretary" is undefined. Section 6 names the Secretary of State, so it's unclear which Secretary is meant, and the subsection gives no criteria, procedure, notice, or appeal for the determination. "Substantially exceeding" and "most cognitive tasks" have no benchmark, which supports vagueness and nondelegation arguments.
5. **§2(1)(B)**: The bill doesn't say whether the Secretary can designate an already-deployed or pre-enactment system as covered, which interacts with the grandfather clause in §3(b).
6. **§2(2)**: "U.S. person" covers only citizens and corporations organized under state law. It doesn't clearly reach LLCs, partnerships, or foreign-organized subsidiaries of U.S. companies, and it excludes permanent residents. This leaves an extraterritorial gap for conduct abroad by non-U.S. entities.
7. **§2(3)**: "Data center," "advanced AI chips," and facility boundaries are all undefined. It's unclear how multi-building campuses, leased or cloud capacity, and distributed training across sites are counted.

**Prohibition (§3)**

8. **§3(a)**: "Train," "develop," and "deploy" are undefined. It's unclear whether internal research use, fine-tuning, API access, or release of weights counts as "deployment."
9. **§3(a)**: The prohibition reaches "any person in the United States" and "any U.S. person anywhere." Conduct abroad by non-U.S. persons, including foreign affiliates, is outside its reach, which overlaps with the gap in item 6.
10. **§3(b)**: The grandfather clause exempts a system whose "training began" before enactment, and because it exempts the system from subsection (a), it covers development and deployment as well as training. The bill sets no cutoff for continued training, checkpoint continuation, retraining, further fine-tuning, or new runs built on the grandfathered model. This is likely the largest gap in the text.
11. **§3(b) and §8**: The grandfather date is the enactment date, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. A run started anywhere in that window would be exempt.
12. **§3(b)**: "Training began" is undefined. It's unclear whether the start is the first compute job, data preparation, or cluster allocation, and whether a successor checkpoint counts as the same "system."
13. **§3(c)**: "Safety evaluation" and "for the purpose of" are undefined. The exemption has no requirements for qualifications, registration, scope, duration, or reporting, and it doesn't say whether deployment after an evaluation is permitted. Because it turns on purpose, it's hard to disprove, and large-scale training could be labeled as evaluation.

**Registry (§4)**

14. **§4(a)**: The registration duty applies to the "operator" of a covered facility, but "operator" is undefined. It's unclear whether the owner, the lessee, or a cloud tenant is the operator, and the subsection sets no registration deadline.
15. **§4(a) and §2(1)**: The registry is keyed to facilities over 50,000 chips, while the prohibition is keyed to models over 10^26 operations. A covered model can run in a facility below 50,000 chips, so the registry doesn't track the prohibition.
16. **§4(b)**: "Computing workloads" is undefined, and the subsection sets no certification standard for the operator's certification. It also has no confidentiality, trade-secret, or FOIA protection for the quarterly reports, which may contain sensitive information.
17. **§4 and §5**: Section 5 penalizes only violations of §3. Failing to register or to file reports carries no stated penalty under this bill.

**Penalties (§5)**

18. **§5**: No agency is designated to enforce the civil penalty, and the bill provides no investigative authority, subpoena power, administrative procedure, or appeal. Without an enforcing party, the penalty provisions are hard to apply.
19. **§5(a)**: "For each day on which the violation occurs" doesn't define the unit of violation. It's unclear whether a violation is counted per day, per system, per facility, or per training run, which affects how penalties stack.
20. **§5(a) and §8**: Penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §3 doesn't take effect until 180 days later. Either penalties reach conduct that isn't yet prohibited, which raises fair-notice problems, or the drafters meant a different date.
21. **§5(b)**: "Willfully" is undefined. Combined with the vague terms in §2, this supports a lack-of-willfulness defense and vagueness arguments for criminal liability. The bill also doesn't address corporate officer liability.
22. **§5**: The bill has no good-faith defense, cure period, or safe harbor, including for entities that register or self-report.

**Other provisions (§6–§8)**

23. **§6**: "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no enforceable obligation, sets no reporting requirement, and has no consequence if the agreement fails. It isn't operative and provides no offshore parity.
24. **§7(a)**: The bill doesn't require the National Academies report, sets no deadline for it or for congressional action, and doesn't define "can be developed safely." If no report is commissioned, the prohibition could last indefinitely. The joint-resolution route also has no expedited procedure or veto treatment.
25. **§7(a) and §7(b)**: Subsection (a) says the prohibition lasts until a joint resolution is passed, but subsection (b) ends the entire Act after 10 years. The sunset overrides the review mechanism, which makes the NAS-and-resolution path largely moot after year 10.
26. **§7(b)**: The Act "cease[s] to have effect" with no savings clause. The bill doesn't say whether penalties for pre-sunset violations survive, whether ongoing investigations continue, or how systems trained during the prohibition are treated.
27. **General**: The bill has no severability clause, no preemption language, no rulemaking authority (so the 10^26 threshold can't be updated and terms can't be clarified by regulation), no judicial review provision, and no appropriation for an implementing agency.

**Notes for the legal team**

- I worked from the text alone, without legal research tools. Verify citations and doctrines (vagueness, fair notice, nondelegation, limitations periods for civil penalties) before relying on them.
- Some of these are substantive gaps, such as items 10–13. Others are drafting errors that sponsors may fix quickly, such as items 11, 20, and 25. The markup may be where those fixes happen, so it helps to sort the list into "substantive" and "technical" before the Thursday session. Presenting technical defects as dispositive could cost credibility.
- It would be useful to compare the October 6 manager's amendment against the prior version. Some of these problems, especially the enactment-versus-effective-date mismatches in items 11 and 20, may have been introduced by the amendment.
