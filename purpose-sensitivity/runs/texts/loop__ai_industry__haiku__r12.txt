# Loophole and drafting analysis: S. 2817 (as amended October 6, 2026)

1. **§2(1)(A):** The threshold mixes units. It sets a "quantity of computing power" but measures it in "operations," and power is a rate while operations are a count. The bill also doesn't specify numeric precision (FP8 vs. FP32), which changes the count substantially.

2. **§2(1)(A):** "Single training run" is undefined. Pretraining, continued pretraining, fine-tuning, RL post-training, and distillation could be argued to be separate runs, and the bill doesn't aggregate compute across runs.

3. **§2(1)(A):** The 10^26 threshold is static with no update mechanism. Algorithmic efficiency gains could bring frontier-level capability below the line, so only §2(1)(B) would reach those systems.

4. **§2(1)(B):** The Secretary's capability determination has no criteria, procedure, notice requirement, hearing, or review standard. "Substantially exceeding," "most cognitive tasks," and "capable" are all undefined, which supports arbitrary-and-capricious and due process arguments, though it also makes coverage unpredictable for us.

5. **§2(1)(B) and §6:** "The Secretary" is never identified. §4 names the Department of Commerce and §6 names the Secretary of State, so the official making the determination in §2 is unclear.

6. **§2(1)(B) and §3(b):** The bill doesn't say whether a (B) determination applies to systems already trained or deployed, or only to systems whose training begins after enactment. That affects how the §3(b) grandfather clause applies.

7. **§2(2):** "U.S. person" excludes foreign-organized subsidiaries, LLCs (arguably not "corporations"), partnerships, and permanent residents. Also, "State" is undefined, so it's unclear whether D.C. and territories are included.

8. **§2(3):** "Data center," "advanced AI chips," and the boundary of a "facility" are all undefined. Chip counts across multiple sites, leased cloud capacity, or colocated clusters are therefore hard to count.

9. **§2(3) and §4:** Registration is keyed to facility size, not to covered systems. A covered system could be trained on far fewer than 50,000 chips over a longer period (very roughly, on the order of 10,000 chips running for most of a year, depending on per-chip throughput and utilization), so the registry may not capture it.

10. **§3(a):** "Develop" and "deploy" are undefined. It's unclear whether internal use, API access, research previews, and open-weight release count as deployment, and whether fine-tuning a non-covered model into a covered one is "training."

11. **§3(a):** The clause "any person in the United States, or any U.S. person anywhere" leaves a jurisdictional gap for non-U.S. persons training abroad. It's also unclear whether a U.S.-based person who directs a foreign-located training run is covered.

12. **§3(a) and §2(1):** The definition doesn't address systems composed of several models (agentic or ensemble systems) where no single model exceeds the threshold.

13. **§3(b):** "Training began" is undefined (e.g., does data collection or compute reservation count?). The clause also doesn't say whether continued training, fine-tuning, or successor runs of a grandfathered system keep the exemption.

14. **§3(c):** The safety evaluation exception is purpose-based, with no definition of "safety evaluation" and no requirements on who conducts it, documentation, qualifications, reporting, scope, or registration. Because evaluations are interleaved with development, this is the broadest potential loophole, and it's also the most likely point of dispute.

15. **§4(a):** "Operator" is undefined. For cloud-hosted facilities, it's unclear whether the facility owner or the workload customer must register. The bill also sets no registration deadline relative to the effective date.

16. **§4(b):** "Describing the computing workloads" has no content, format, or granularity requirement, and "certified by the operator" has no defined standard, so compliance is hard to verify.

17. **§4:** The reports have no trade secret protection or confidentiality designation, and no FOIA exemption is provided. Disclosure of workload information could expose proprietary material, which is a legitimate markup concern.

18. **§4 and §5:** The penalties in §5 attach only to violations of §3. Failing to register or to file reports carries no civil or criminal penalty under the bill, which is a significant enforcement gap.

19. **§5 (general):** No agency is designated to enforce civil penalties, and the bill sets no assessment procedure, venue, injunctive authority, or statute of limitations. Because of this, the bill can be argued to be unenforceable as written absent an implementing authority.

20. **§5(a) and §8:** The civil penalty applies to violations "occurring on or after the date of enactment," but §8 delays the Act's effective date by 180 days. This creates an unresolved question about whether conduct during the 180-day window is penalized.

21. **§5(a):** The $1,000,000 per-day penalty has no cap, no mens rea standard (so strict liability could be argued), and no mitigating factors or good-faith defense. For a continuing training run, aggregate exposure could be extreme and disproportionate, which supports a proportionality argument.

22. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill doesn't clarify whether officers or directors can be liable without personal knowledge of a violation.

23. **§6:** The Secretary of State is only required to "seek to negotiate" an agreement, with no deadline consequence, reporting requirement, or trigger tied to the prohibition. "Equivalent" is undefined. The provision has no operative effect, which is worth noting in the markup.

24. **§7(a) and §7(b):** These provisions directly conflict. §7(a) keeps the §3 prohibition in effect until Congress passes a joint resolution, but §7(b) says the entire Act ceases to have effect after 10 years regardless. It's unclear whether the prohibition lapses at year 10 absent a resolution. This is probably the most important ambiguity in the bill.

25. **§7(a):** "Can be developed safely" is an undefined standard. The bill also sets no deadline for the National Academies report and doesn't say whether the joint resolution is presented to the President, so the procedure for lifting the prohibition is unclear.

26. **§7(b):** The bill has no savings clause. Upon sunset, it's unclear whether pending enforcement actions, accrued penalties, and registration obligations for pre-sunset conduct survive.

27. **§7(b) and §8:** The 10-year sunset runs from enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later, so the operative prohibition lasts about 9.5 years. This is a minor inconsistency but illustrates the drafting problem.

28. **General omissions:** The bill has no severability clause, no definition of "artificial intelligence," "training," or "safety evaluation," no rulemaking authority, no appropriations for the registry, no judicial review provision, and no notice-and-hearing process. Several of these gaps interact with items 4, 19, and 24.

## Highest-impact items for the markup

- **#24** (§7(a) vs. §7(b) conflict)
- **#19** (no enforcing agency)
- **#18** (no penalty for §4 violations)
- **#14** (safety evaluation exception)
- **#4** (undefined Secretary determination)
- **#2** (undefined "single training run")
- **#9** (facility threshold doesn't track covered systems)
- **#20** (enactment vs. effective-date conflict)

## Caveats for the legal team

- **Some of these cut both ways.** The vagueness in §2(1)(B) and §3(c) also creates uncertainty for us. Arguments that the bill is unworkable should be stated precisely, since opposing counsel and staff will test them.
- **Ambiguities are not reliable compliance strategies.** Agency guidance or courts could narrow nearly any of these readings, and relying on a narrow interpretation in operations carries legal and reputational risk.
- **I analyzed only the text provided.** I haven't seen the base bill, the amendment's redline, legislative history, or any CRS or committee analysis, so counsel should check which issues are new in the October 6 amendment and confirm the analysis against the official text.
