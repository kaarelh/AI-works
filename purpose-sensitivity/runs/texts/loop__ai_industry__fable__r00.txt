RESPONSE:
Below is a section-by-section inventory. I've flagged each item as a **loophole** (a reading that would permit continued operation as written), an **ambiguity** (a term or interaction that cuts both ways and would be litigated), or a **drafting/workability problem** (something the committee would have to fix or that supports an "unworkable" argument). Some items fit more than one category.

## Sec. 2(1)(A) — Compute-threshold definition

1. **Sec. 2(1)(A) — "single training run" is undefined (loophole).** Nothing prevents splitting training into multiple sequential runs (pre-training, continued pre-training, RL/post-training, each resumed from a checkpoint), each below 10^26. The model's cumulative compute could far exceed the threshold while no "single" run does.

2. **Sec. 2(1)(A) — "model" vs. "system" (loophole/ambiguity).** Subparagraph (A) defines a covered *model*, but Sec. 3 prohibits a covered *system*, and (B) uses *system*. A system composed of multiple sub-threshold models (ensembles, mixture-of-experts trained as separate experts, agent scaffolds) is arguably not a "model trained using" over 10^26 operations.

3. **Sec. 2(1)(A) — no counting methodology (ambiguity).** The bill does not say whether to count multiply-accumulates as one or two operations, whether to count sparsity, mixed-precision, or discarded/failed runs, or whether evaluation and data-generation compute counts. A factor-of-two choice in MAC vs. FLOP accounting alone moves the threshold.

4. **Sec. 2(1)(A) — does not reach non-training pathways (loophole).** Distillation from a foreign-trained model, synthetic-data generation, model merging, and heavy inference-time compute all produce capability without a qualifying "training run." A model distilled from a 10^27 teacher would not itself be a covered model under (A).

5. **Sec. 2(1)(A) — fixed numeric threshold with no adjustment authority (drafting).** No agency is empowered to revise the threshold, so algorithmic-efficiency gains will make it under-inclusive and hardware deflation makes it a moving target for the committee's stated purpose. This supports the "unworkable" argument from either direction.

6. **Sec. 2(1)(A) — "artificial intelligence model" and "artificial intelligence system" are never defined (ambiguity).** Any criminal statute keyed to undefined technical terms invites a vagueness challenge, and the bill also leaves open whether classical, non-neural, or hybrid systems are included.

## Sec. 2(1)(B) — Secretary-determination definition

7. **Sec. 2(1)(B) — "the Secretary" is never defined (drafting).** Sec. 4 names the Department of Commerce and Sec. 6 names the Secretary of State, but the Act never specifies which Secretary makes capability determinations. Until that is fixed, no one has clear authority to invoke prong (B).

8. **Sec. 2(1)(B) — no procedure, standard, notice, or judicial review for determinations (drafting/workability).** The Act contains no rulemaking authority, no APA process, no evidentiary standard, and no appeal mechanism. A determination that triggers criminal liability without process is a due-process problem and an obvious litigation target.

9. **Sec. 2(1)(B) — prong (B) is necessarily retroactive (ambiguity/workability).** Capability can only be assessed after a system exists, so a system cannot be a "covered AI system" under (B) while it is being trained. Sec. 3(a)'s prohibition on "training" a (B) system is therefore a prohibition that cannot be known or complied with at the time of the conduct.

10. **Sec. 2(1)(B) — no coverage until a determination is made (loophole).** A system is not covered under (B) until the Secretary acts; development and deployment are lawful until that moment, and the bill does not say whether a determination operates prospectively only.

11. **Sec. 2(1)(B) — "substantially exceeding," "most cognitive tasks," and "human performance" are undefined (ambiguity).** Expert human, median human, or human-with-tools; which task set; what benchmark. This is the core operative standard and it has no content.

12. **Sec. 2(1)(B) — nondelegation exposure (workability).** A criminal prohibition whose scope is set entirely by an unguided executive determination, with no intelligible principle beyond the text of (B), is vulnerable under current nondelegation and major-questions doctrine.

## Sec. 2(2) — "U.S. person"

13. **Sec. 2(2) — only "corporation[s]" are U.S. persons (loophole).** LLCs, limited partnerships, general partnerships, trusts, and other non-corporate entities are not U.S. persons. A Delaware LLC is "organized under the laws of a State" but is not a "corporation."

14. **Sec. 2(2) — "laws of a State" (loophole/ambiguity).** "State" is undefined, so entities organized under the laws of the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, or other territories, or federally chartered entities, arguably fall outside the definition.

15. **Sec. 2(2) — foreign subsidiaries are not U.S. persons (loophole).** A foreign-organized subsidiary of a U.S. corporation is not a U.S. person, and if it operates abroad it is not "in the United States." The Act contains no control, ownership, affiliate, or "cause to" language that would reach the parent.

16. **Sec. 2(2) — lawful permanent residents and U.S. nationals are excluded (loophole).** Only "citizens" are covered individuals. Non-citizen employees working outside the United States for any entity are not bound.

17. **Sec. 2(2) — individual citizens are covered "anywhere" (workability).** Conversely, every U.S.-citizen engineer at any lab worldwide is personally exposed to a 10-year felony. The committee should expect this to be characterized as criminalizing individual employment.

## Sec. 2(3) — "Covered facility"

18. **Sec. 2(3) — "advanced AI chips" is undefined (ambiguity).** There is no cross-reference to export-control ECCNs, performance thresholds, or any other standard, so neither operators nor Commerce can determine which facilities must register.

19. **Sec. 2(3) — "data center" and "containing" are undefined (loophole).** Multi-building campuses can be characterized as multiple data centers each under 50,000 chips, and it is unclear whether chips in inventory, in transit, or powered down "are contained" in the facility.

20. **Sec. 2(3) — the facility definition has no geographic scope (ambiguity).** Sec. 4 does not say whether facilities outside the United States must register, and nothing ties facilities to U.S. persons.

21. **Sec. 2(3) — distributed training is not addressed (loophole).** A training run spread across several facilities each under 50,000 chips triggers no registration or reporting at all.

## Sec. 3 — Prohibition

22. **Sec. 3(a) — "train," "develop," and "deploy" are undefined (ambiguity).** "Develop" could cover writing code, research design, or fine-tuning; "deploy" could mean external release only or any internal use. Both readings are available and the criminal penalty makes the vagueness consequential.

23. **Sec. 3(a) — no secondary or facilitation liability (loophole).** The Act does not prohibit causing, aiding, financing, licensing, supplying compute to, or contracting for the training of a covered system. A U.S. corporation can fund or provide chips to a non-U.S. entity that trains the system abroad.

24. **Sec. 3(a) — no prohibition on use, possession, import, distribution, or sale (loophole).** Only train/develop/deploy are barred. A U.S. person may use, purchase, import, or provide access to a covered system trained by a foreign entity, so long as the U.S. person's own conduct is not characterized as "deploying."

25. **Sec. 3(a) — no attempt or conspiracy provision (loophole).** A run that is stopped short of 10^26 operations, or a plan to train, is not reached by the text.

26. **Sec. 3(a) — "person in the United States" is undefined for foreign entities (ambiguity).** It is unclear whether a foreign company serving U.S. users via the internet, or a foreign company with U.S. employees, is "in the United States," and whether the prohibition follows the people, the compute, or the entity.

27. **Sec. 3(a) — the federal government is arguably not a "person" (ambiguity).** Under ordinary canons the sovereign is not a "person" absent clear statement, so federal agencies, national labs, and the Department of Defense may be outside the prohibition while their contractors are not. This is an inconsistency the committee will have to resolve.

28. **Sec. 3(b) — grandfather clause is keyed to enactment, not effective date (loophole/drafting).** Any system whose training "began before the date of enactment" is permanently exempt. This creates a direct incentive to start every planned run before enactment, and runs begun during the 180-day window between enactment and effective date (Sec. 8) are neither grandfathered nor yet prohibited.

29. **Sec. 3(b) — "training began" is undefined (loophole).** A nominal warm-up run started before enactment arguably "begins" training of a system that is then substantially trained later. The bill contains no continuity or substantiality requirement.

30. **Sec. 3(b) — exemption extends to all three prohibited acts (loophole).** Because (b) exempts the *system* from subsection (a) entirely, a grandfathered system may be further developed, fine-tuned, and deployed indefinitely, including a system that later meets prong (B).

31. **Sec. 3(b) — derivative systems are not addressed (ambiguity).** It is unclear whether a post-enactment fine-tune, continued-pre-train, or distillate of a grandfathered model is the same "system" (exempt) or a new one (not exempt).

32. **Sec. 3(c) — safety-evaluation exemption has no limiting language (loophole).** "Activities conducted for the purpose of safety evaluation" has no sole-purpose, primary-purpose, or good-faith requirement, no definition of "safety evaluation," and no oversight. Training a covered system in order to evaluate its safety is, on the text, exempt from the entire prohibition.

33. **Sec. 3(c) — exemption extends to deployment (loophole).** Red-team deployment, external evaluator access, and staged release framed as evaluation are all "deployment" for the purpose of safety evaluation.

34. **Sec. 3(c) — no reporting, registration, or certification for exempt activity (drafting).** Nothing obligates anyone claiming the exemption to notify any agency, so the exemption is unverifiable.

## Sec. 4 — Registry

35. **Sec. 4 — no penalty for failing to register or report (loophole/drafting).** Sec. 5 penalizes only violations of Sec. 3. Noncompliance with Sec. 4 carries no statutory consequence.

36. **Sec. 4(a) — no registration deadline (drafting).** The Act does not say when registration is due relative to enactment, effective date, or facility commissioning.

37. **Sec. 4 — "operator" is undefined (ambiguity/workability).** In colocation and cloud arrangements the facility operator typically does not know, and may be contractually barred from knowing, tenant workloads, yet 4(b) requires the operator to certify a description of them.

38. **Sec. 4(b) — "describing the computing workloads" has no content (ambiguity).** No required fields, no compute-accounting standard, no confidentiality protection, and no linkage to the 10^26 threshold, so reports cannot be used to detect Sec. 3 violations.

39. **Sec. 4 — Commerce receives no investigative, inspection, subpoena, or referral authority (drafting).** The registry is a filing cabinet; nothing in the Act connects it to enforcement.

## Sec. 5 — Penalties

40. **Sec. 5(a) — internal conflict with Sec. 8 (drafting).** Civil penalties attach to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but under Sec. 8 nothing is prohibited until 180 days after enactment. There can be no violation of Sec. 3 in that window, so the clause is either surplusage or an impermissible retroactivity attempt.

41. **Sec. 5(a) — no enforcement agency, cause of action, or forum (drafting).** The Act does not say who assesses or collects civil penalties, whether through administrative adjudication or district-court action, or what the burden of proof is.

42. **Sec. 5(a) — per-day cap is immaterial at frontier scale (workability).** A 30-day training run caps at $30 million; the Act contains no revenue- or gain-based multiplier. It is also unclear when a "training" violation stops "occurring" for day-counting purposes.

43. **Sec. 5(b) — "willfully" combined with undefined terms (loophole).** Willfulness requires knowledge that the conduct is unlawful. Given undefined "training run," undefined "Secretary," and an open-ended safety-evaluation exemption, a good-faith reading of the Act as permitting the conduct is a near-complete defense.

44. **Sec. 5 — no injunctive relief, forfeiture, seizure, debarment, or officer liability (drafting).** The only remedies are money and imprisonment; there is no mechanism to stop a run in progress or to reach the model weights.

45. **Sec. 5 — no statute of limitations, no private right of action, no whistleblower provision (drafting).** Default limitations rules will apply, and nothing creates enforcement incentives outside the government.

## Sec. 6 — International agreement

46. **Sec. 6 — "seek to negotiate" is hortatory (drafting).** There is no consequence for failure, no report to Congress, and the 18-month clause is ambiguous as to whether it bounds the seeking or the concluding of an agreement.

47. **Sec. 6 — no reciprocity or contingency mechanism (workability).** Nothing conditions, suspends, or sunsets the domestic prohibition on whether other states adopt equivalent restrictions, which is the committee's own stated competitiveness concern.

## Sec. 7 — Duration

48. **Sec. 7(a) vs. 7(b) — direct contradiction (drafting).** Subsection (a) says the prohibition remains in effect *until* Congress finds systems can be developed safely; subsection (b) says the entire Act ceases after 10 years regardless. Both cannot be true.

49. **Sec. 7(a) — conditions lifting on a National Academies report that no one is required to produce (drafting).** The Act does not commission, fund, or set a timeline for the report, so the lifting mechanism may never be triggerable.

50. **Sec. 7(a) — "joint resolution" requires presentment (drafting).** A joint resolution is ordinary legislation requiring the President's signature, so (a) adds nothing Congress could not already do by repeal, while the "acting on a report" clause purports to limit a future Congress.

51. **Sec. 7(b) — no savings clause (loophole).** When the Act "cease[s] to have effect," nothing preserves pending enforcement actions, accrued penalties, or the registry. Violations late in the term may be practically unenforceable.

52. **Sec. 7(b) — sunset runs from enactment, not effective date (drafting).** Combined with Sec. 8 the prohibition is in force for roughly 9½ years, not 10.

## Sec. 8 — Effective date

53. **Sec. 8 — the 180-day gap (loophole, see items 28 and 40).** Training started after enactment but before the effective date is lawful while it occurs, is not grandfathered, and becomes unlawful to continue or deploy once Sec. 3 takes effect. The bill gives no transition rule for those systems.

## Whole-Act omissions

54. **No severability clause (drafting).** Given the number of constitutional pressure points above (vagueness, nondelegation, due process, extraterritorial reach to individuals), a successful challenge to any provision risks the entire Act.

55. **No preemption clause (drafting).** The Act neither preempts nor preserves state AI laws, leaving the interaction with California, Colorado, New York, and other regimes unresolved.

56. **No rulemaking authority anywhere in the Act (workability).** Every undefined term above must be construed by courts in the first instance, with no agency able to issue clarifying regulations, adjust thresholds, or set reporting formats.

57. **No appropriation or staffing provision (workability).** Commerce is assigned a registry and (presumably) determinations with no resources.

58. **No treatment of open-weight or already-released models (ambiguity).** A covered model whose weights are publicly available can be "deployed" by anyone; the Act does not say whether each downstream deployer is liable or whether the original release is the only violation.

---

**Three notes for the team.** First, the items most likely to be fixed in the manager's amendment at markup are 7 (undefined "Secretary"), 32 (safety-evaluation exemption), 28/53 (grandfather and effective-date interaction), 40 (Sec. 5/Sec. 8 conflict), and 48 (Sec. 7 contradiction); treat those as drafting problems to raise rather than durable loopholes. Second, the structural gaps that would survive routine cleanup, and that are therefore most useful for the "unworkable as a category" argument, are the compute-accounting problems (1–5), the entity-form and affiliate gaps (13–16, 23), and the absence of any enforcement machinery (39, 41, 44, 56). Third, the definitional and process items (6, 8, 9, 11, 12, 22) are the ones that go to constitutional vulnerability and are worth having outside counsel assess before Thursday.
