# Weak Spots in S. 2817 (Manager's Amendment, Oct. 6, 2026)

**Section 2 (Definitions)**

1. **§2(1)(A):** The 10^26 threshold applies to a "single training run," so compute spread across multiple runs, phases, or checkpoints may fall outside it. The bill also doesn't say how FLOPs are counted, which invites disputes over methodology.
2. **§2(1)(A):** It's unclear whether fine-tuning, post-training, or distillation counts toward the threshold. A model could be built on a base model trained elsewhere and cross the line in ways the text doesn't address.
3. **§2(1)(A):** A fixed compute threshold doesn't adjust for algorithmic efficiency, so a model below 10^26 could still reach the capability the bill targets. The backstop in (1)(B) is undefined (see items 4–5).
4. **§2(1)(B):** "The Secretary" is never identified. The text doesn't say which Secretary makes the determination or under what procedure.
5. **§2(1)(B):** "Substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no metric, benchmark, notice, hearing, or appeal process. This is a vagueness problem that matters most if the provision supports criminal liability.
6. **§2(1)(B):** The text doesn't say whether a determination applies to systems already deployed or only to future training, so the reach of a designation is unclear.
7. **§2(1)(A) vs. (B) vs. §3:** The bill switches between "model" and "system" and never defines either precisely. A product that combines several sub-threshold models could escape both prongs.
8. **§2(2) "U.S. person":** The definition covers only citizens and corporations organized under state law. Foreign-incorporated affiliates, LLCs, and partnerships fall outside it, and "State" is undefined (it's unclear whether D.C. or territories count).
9. **§2(3) "covered facility":** "Advanced AI chips" and "data center" are undefined. A developer can split capacity across sites or lease it from cloud providers to stay under 50,000 chips per facility.
10. **§2 (general):** "Person" and "operator" are undefined. It's unclear whether a cloud provider or its tenant is the registrant under §4.

**Section 3 (Prohibition)**

11. **§3(a):** "Train," "develop," and "deploy" are undefined. "Deploy" doesn't clearly address open-weight releases or U.S. users accessing a model hosted abroad, which bears directly on whether "in the United States" applies.
12. **§3(a):** The prohibition doesn't reach providing compute, chips, cloud access, financing, or other facilitation, and it has no aiding-and-abetting provision. Companies could supply the means without being covered.
13. **§3(b):** The exemption is keyed to when training "began," not when it ended or when the system was deployed. It has no compute cap, so a grandfathered run could continue indefinitely and the resulting system could stay in use.
14. **§3(b) with §8:** The exemption date is enactment, but §3 takes effect 180 days later. Training started during that gap is neither grandfathered nor clearly prohibited, and its status after the effective date is unresolved.
15. **§3(c):** "Activities conducted for the purpose of safety evaluation" is undefined and self-certifying. The text sets no scale limit, registration requirement, or independent-evaluator requirement, so large training runs or deployments could be labeled as evaluation.

**Section 4 (Registry)**

16. **§4(a)–(b):** The registry tracks facilities, not training runs or covered AI systems, so it can't show whether a covered model exists. Reports need only describe "computing workloads," with no required detail on training compute or run identity.
17. **§4 with §5:** Failing to register or report carries no penalty, because §5 penalizes only violations of §3. The bill also sets no deadline for initial registration.
18. **§4(b):** The bill doesn't define the certification standard, gives no authority to inspect, audit, or verify, and provides no penalty for false certifications beyond general law.

**Section 5 (Penalties)**

19. **§5(a):** "Violation" is undefined, and it's unclear whether the $1,000,000 applies per violation or per day. The text sets no aggregate cap and no inflation adjustment, and the amount may be small relative to large training budgets.
20. **§5(a):** The bill doesn't say which agency or party brings civil actions, and it gives no authority to seek injunctions or halt training or deployment. Nothing addresses seizing or disabling compute.
21. **§5(a) with §8:** The penalty covers violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §3 isn't effective until 180 days later. This conflicts with §8 and raises a retroactivity challenge or leaves the provision with no clear operation.
22. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the text doesn't say how liability attaches to corporations or to officers. Combined with the vague terms in §2, this creates a due-process and lenity problem for criminal enforcement.

**Sections 6–8 (International, Duration, Effective Date)**

23. **§6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no binding obligation, reporting requirement, or consequence for failure. "Equivalent" is undefined, and the bill doesn't address whether a treaty would need Senate ratification.
24. **§7(a):** No deadline requires the National Academies to report, and the text doesn't say what happens if no report is issued. "Developed safely" is undefined, and the bill doesn't explain how a joint resolution would interact with presentment to the President.
25. **§7(a) vs. §7(b):** The indefinite prohibition in (a) conflicts with the 10-year sunset in (b), and the text doesn't say which controls. The sunset runs from enactment rather than the effective date, so the prohibition would be in force for only about nine and a half years.
26. **§8:** The registry (§4) has no separate effective date, so it takes effect with the rest of the Act 180 days after enactment. The gap before §3 applies gives developers time to start runs before the prohibition begins.

**Structural gaps**

27. **Missing severability clause:** If a court strikes a provision such as §2(1)(B), the text doesn't say whether the rest survives. This invites facial challenges to the whole Act.
28. **Missing enforcement and procedure provisions:** The bill has no rulemaking authority, no appropriation, no judicial review provision, no whistleblower protection, and no preemption clause. Each gap makes implementation harder and leaves more room for litigation.
29. **Speech and research challenges (flag for legal input):** A ban on "developing" models could be challenged on First Amendment grounds if it reaches publishing code, weights, or academic research. I'd treat this as a litigation risk to verify with constitutional-law experts before stating it in the explainer.
