# Drafting Problems and Loopholes in S. 2817 (Manager's Amendment, Oct. 6, 2026)

**Definitions and Scope Triggers**

1. **§2(1)(A):** "Single training run" is undefined, so developers could split a run across several jobs, or push compute into fine-tuning, distillation, or synthetic-data generation, to stay under 10^26 operations. The bill also sets no counting method (precision, sparsity, or estimation).

2. **§2(1)(A), §4(b):** Nothing requires developers to disclose or verify training compute. Commerce cannot check threshold compliance because the only reporting duty (§4(b)) covers "computing workloads" in general.

3. **§2(1)(B):** "Substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no benchmarks, evaluation method, or burden of proof. Given criminal exposure under §5(b), this invites a vagueness challenge.

4. **§2(1)(B):** "Secretary" is never identified (presumably Commerce), and the bill sets no procedure for notice, response, or appeal of a determination. Review would fall to default administrative-law principles, which is a weak basis for a prohibition this broad.

5. **§2(1)(A) vs. §2(1)(B):** The bill creates two inconsistent triggers. One is a fixed compute threshold and the other is a discretionary capability finding that applies to "any" system, with no stated relationship between them.

6. **§2 (generally), §3(a):** "Model" and "system" are used interchangeably, and neither is defined. It is unclear whether agents, scaffolds, or products built on a covered model are themselves covered.

7. **§2(2):** "U.S. person" excludes permanent residents, foreign subsidiaries organized abroad, and unincorporated entities such as partnerships and possibly LLCs. "State" is also undefined, leaving open whether D.C. and the territories are included.

8. **§2(3), §2(1)(A):** "Advanced AI chips" and "data center" are undefined, and the 50,000-chip facility threshold has no stated link to the 10^26 compute threshold. A developer could train a covered system in a smaller facility or spread a run across several, avoiding registration altogether.

**Prohibition Scope (§3)**

9. **§3(a):** "Train," "develop," and "deploy" are undefined. It is unclear whether releasing open weights, offering API access, or internal use counts as "deploy," and whether "develop" reaches research short of a full training run.

10. **§3(a):** The prohibition reaches "any person in the United States" but only U.S. persons abroad. Foreign companies training outside the United States are outside the statute's direct reach and depend on the treaty in §6.

11. **§3(a), §5(a):** No pre-clearance, advisory opinion, or safe harbor exists for developers to learn whether a system is covered. Combined with the undefined capability trigger, companies face uncertain exposure with no clear way to resolve it.

12. **§3(b):** The grandfather clause turns on when training "began," not on whether it continues. A developer could keep training or further pretraining a pre-enactment run, and "began" is undefined, so a placeholder run could start the clock.

13. **§3(b):** The clause protects training only. It does not limit deployment of pre-enactment systems and does not say whether derivative or successor models inherit the exemption.

14. **§3(c):** "Safety evaluation" is undefined. The bill requires no registration, scope limit, independent evaluator, or documentation, so frontier training or customer deployment could be recast as "evaluation."

**Effective Date and Timing (§§3(b), 5(a), 8)**

15. **§3(b), §8:** The grandfather date (enactment) and the effective date (180 days later) differ. Training that begins during that 180-day window is neither grandfathered nor clearly prohibited until the effective date, and the bill does not say whether such a run may be completed.

16. **§5(a), §8:** §5(a) penalizes violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §3 does not take effect until 180 days later. Read literally, this conflicts with §8, and the explainer should not assume a single clear start date for liability.

**Registry (§4)**

17. **§4(a):** "Operator" is undefined. The bill does not say whether the cloud provider, the tenant, or a colocation firm must register, and it sets no registration deadline.

18. **§4(b):** Reports must describe "computing workloads" but need not state whether any workload is a covered AI system or report its training compute. The certification has no content requirements, verification, audit right, or confidentiality protection.

19. **§4, §5:** No penalty attaches to failing to register, failing to file, or filing a false certification, since §5 reaches only violations of §3. False-statement liability under other federal law may apply, but the bill does not say so.

20. **§4:** Commerce receives no stated rulemaking, enforcement, information-sharing, or funding authority to run the registry.

**Penalties (§5)**

21. **§5(a):** The bill does not say who brings civil enforcement actions (DOJ, Commerce, or a private party). It also gives no investigative or subpoena power, injunctive relief, or whistleblower protection.

22. **§5(a):** "Violation" is undefined, so it is unclear whether penalties run per model, per deployment, per person, or per day. The bill also lacks an inflation adjustment and an aggregation rule.

23. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill does not address corporate officer liability or attempt and conspiracy. The fine amount defers to title 18 general provisions (verify the applicable figures).

24. **§5, general:** The bill has no severability clause and no savings clause. If the §2(1)(B) capability trigger is struck down, it is unclear what survives, and the sunset in §7(b) could affect pending penalties.

**International Agreement (§6)**

25. **§6:** "Seek to negotiate" imposes no obligation and no consequence if negotiations fail. "Equivalent" is undefined, and nothing ties a failed agreement to any domestic reassessment.

**Duration and Sunset (§7)**

26. **§7(a) vs. §7(b):** §7(a) keeps the prohibition in force until a joint resolution is enacted, but §7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years. The prohibition could lapse at year 10 regardless of any safety finding, and the bill does not say which provision controls.

27. **§7(a):** The bill does not say whether a "joint resolution" must be presented to the President, which affects whether a veto is possible. It also sets no deadline for the NAS report, does not require the NAS to produce one, and does not define "developed safely."

28. **§7(b):** A sunset is not a repeal, and the bill has no savings clause. Whether pending civil or criminal liability survives expiration is unclear, and the general savings statute (1 U.S.C. §109) may not reach an expiration. This should be verified.
