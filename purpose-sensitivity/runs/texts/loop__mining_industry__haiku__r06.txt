# Drafting Issues in S. 2817 (Manager's Amendment, Oct. 6, 2026)

This list covers the text you provided. Some items depend on facts I don't have, such as Meridian's license status, pending applications, and entity structure, so legal should confirm those before relying on them. This is analysis to support the team's review, not a legal opinion.

**Bottom line:** The core prohibition on large-scale recovery by U.S. persons is clear. The strongest arguments are in the exemptions (§3(b), §3(c)), the internal conflict over duration (§7), the enforcement gaps (§4, §5), and the silence about existing DSHMRA licensing (not addressed in the text).

## A. Exemptions and thresholds that could allow continued operations

1. **§3(b):** The exploration-license exemption has no volume cap, no end date, and no limit to exploration-only activity. The text exempts "mining conducted under" a pre-enactment license, so it may cover commercial-scale recovery under that license. Legal should check the terms of any existing license.

2. **§3(b) and §8:** The grandfather clause keys to the *date of enactment*, but the prohibition doesn't take effect until 180 days later. Licenses issued in that gap would fall outside the prohibition.

3. **§3(b):** The text doesn't address pending applications, renewals, amendments, or transfers of licenses. Whether an application filed but not yet granted is protected is unclear.

4. **§3(c):** The environmental-assessment exemption has no definition, volume limit, duration limit, or reporting requirement. Commercial-scale recovery could be labeled as assessment activity.

5. **§2(1)(A):** The 10,000-metric-ton threshold applies "by a single vessel," so a fleet of vessels that each stay under the threshold appears to fall outside the definition. The text doesn't say whether volumes are aggregated by vessel, operator, or affiliate group.

6. **§2(1)(A):** "Recovery" and "metric tons" are not measured. The text doesn't specify wet or dry weight, nodules or contained metals, or how "per year" is calculated across periods. Test or pilot collection is not addressed.

7. **§2(3) and §4:** The covered-vessel threshold (over 5,000 gross tons) doesn't match the prohibition's 10,000-ton threshold. A smaller vessel could exceed 10,000 tons without being registered or reporting.

8. **§2(3):** "Equipped for the recovery of seabed mineral resources" is undefined. A multipurpose vessel could argue it isn't "equipped for" recovery, and the text doesn't say whether gross tonnage is measured under any particular standard.

## B. Scope of the prohibition

9. **§3(a):** The prohibition covers only "engage in commercial deep seabed mining." It doesn't reach processing, transport, financing, sale, or import of minerals recovered elsewhere, and it has no aiding-and-abetting or conspiracy language. (This also weakens the bill's effectiveness, which opponents may raise.)

10. **§2(2) and §3(a):** Extraterritorial reach depends on the "U.S. person" definition. A foreign-organized subsidiary, a foreign-flagged vessel, or a non-U.S. operator would fall outside it. Legal should confirm how Meridian's entities and vessels fit the definition.

11. **§2(2):** The definition covers only citizens and corporations "organized under the laws of a State." It doesn't clearly cover federally chartered corporations, partnerships, LLCs, or trusts, and it excludes permanent residents.

12. **§2 and §3(a):** "Person," "the United States," and "State" are undefined. The geographic reach of "any person in the United States" is unclear, including whether it covers territorial waters or the Exclusive Economic Zone.

13. **Scope generally:** The bill doesn't clearly state whether it applies to activities in the International Seabed Area, which is beyond national jurisdiction. Enforcement there is limited, and the extraterritorial reach may raise international-law questions.

## C. Enforcement, reporting, and penalties

14. **§4(a) and §5:** Failure to register or report is not penalized. Section 5 penalizes only violations of §3, so the registry is effectively voluntary.

15. **§4(b):** The required content of quarterly reports is undefined. There is no tonnage requirement, no inspection or audit authority, and no penalty for a false certification.

16. **§5(a):** The bill doesn't name an enforcing agency or set out procedures for notice, hearing, or assessment. It also doesn't define the penalty unit for "each day" or list factors for setting the penalty.

17. **§5(a) and §8:** The penalty applies to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. Penalties for conduct in that window are ambiguous.

18. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the text doesn't address corporate criminal liability or officer liability. The fine is set by reference to Title 18 without a separate organizational fine amount.

19. **§6:** The duty to "seek to negotiate" an agreement is not binding, "equivalent" is undefined, and the text has no consequence for failure. The provision doesn't affect the domestic prohibition, but it may be used to argue the bill is unworkable.

## D. Internal conflicts in duration

20. **§7(a) and §7(b):** This is the most significant conflict. Section 7(a) keeps the prohibition in effect until Congress passes a joint resolution, but §7(b) says the entire Act ceases after 10 years. It's unclear whether the prohibition ends at year 10 regardless of any finding.

21. **§7(a):** "Serious harm" is undefined. The text doesn't require the NASEM report to be commissioned, sets no deadline, and doesn't say whether a joint resolution needs presidential signature to take effect.

22. **§7(b):** There's no savings clause for violations or penalties that accrue before the sunset. It's unclear whether pre-sunset violations remain enforceable, and legal should check whether the general savings statute (1 U.S.C. § 109) applies to a sunset.

23. **§4 and §7(b):** The registry and reporting obligations are not addressed after sunset, so they may be orphaned.

## E. Undefined terms and discretionary authority

24. **§2 (general):** "Secretary" is undefined. The text doesn't say whether it means the Secretary of Commerce, the Interior, or another official, which matters for §2(1)(B).

25. **§2(1)(B):** The Secretary can extend the definition to any activity judged "comparable," with no standard for "comparable," no notice, no procedure, and no stated review. This creates uncertainty for planning, and it may also support a challenge that the determination is arbitrary.

26. **§2 (general):** "Exploration license," "operator," and "environmental assessment" are undefined, and each affects scope.

## F. Relationship to existing law

27. **Whole Act:** The bill doesn't mention the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act (1980) or NOAA's existing licensing and permitting regime. It doesn't say whether the Act amends, supersedes, or runs alongside that regime, or what happens to permits already issued. Legal should check the current DSHMRA regulations, which I believe are at 15 C.F.R. Part 970, though this should be verified.

28. **Whole Act:** The bill has no conforming amendments, no savings clause for existing contracts or investments, and no provision on compensation or reliance interests. Legal should evaluate whether those gaps support contract or constitutional arguments, though these are not textual loopholes.

## Priorities for the markup

If the team wants to focus, the highest-value points are:
- **§7(a) vs. §7(b)** (item 20): The sunset conflict is the clearest internal inconsistency.
- **§3(b) and §8** (items 1–2): The grandfather clause is the most direct path to continued operations.
- **§3(c)** (item 4): The environmental-assessment exemption is open-ended.
- **§2(1)(A)** (item 5): The single-vessel threshold is easy to explain.
- **DSHMRA silence** (item 27): The bill doesn't say how it relates to the existing licensing regime.
- **§5(a) and §8** (item 17): The effective-date conflict in the penalty provision is a clear drafting error.

**Caveats for the team:** Some of these points cut both ways. Items 9 and 13 could be cited by opponents as evidence the bill is ineffective, and the committee may fix several drafting problems with amendments. Also, this analysis is based only on the text provided. Please confirm it against the introduced version and the manager's amendment, and verify the statutory citations before they go into any filing or testimony.
