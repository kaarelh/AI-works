# Loopholes, Ambiguities, and Drafting Problems in S. 2817

**Sec. 2(1)(A) (compute threshold)**

1. Sec. 2(1)(A): The threshold applies to "a single training run," so developers can split compute across several runs, continue training from a checkpoint as a "new" run, or push capability gains through post-training, fine-tuning, or RL to stay under 10^26. Define covered compute as cumulative compute used to produce or materially advance a model, across related runs and post-training.

2. Sec. 2(1)(A): "Computing power greater than 10^26 integer or floating-point operations" conflates power (a rate) with a count of operations, and it doesn't say how operations are counted across precisions. Use "total training compute, measured in floating-point operations," with a stated counting method.

3. Sec. 2(1)(A): A fixed FLOP threshold will become less protective as algorithmic efficiency improves, and the bill gives no mechanism to update it. Add Commerce authority to revise the threshold by rule.

**Sec. 2(1)(B) (capability trigger)**

4. Sec. 2(1)(B): "The Secretary" is undefined, and the bill sets no process for designation, including notice, evidence, a hearing, or appeal. This invites both administrative and constitutional challenge.

5. Sec. 2(1)(B): "Capable of substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no measurement standard, benchmark, or evaluation method, so developers can argue any system falls outside it.

6. Sec. 2(1)(B): This clause is the only route for systems below 10^26, but it depends on a determination the Secretary is never required to make, and no deadline or developer-initiated process exists. Require determinations on a set schedule and allow developers to request them.

7. Sec. 2(1): Clause (A) covers a "model" and clause (B) covers a "system," and the bill doesn't reconcile the two. A deployed product that combines several smaller models with tools may fall outside (A) while not clearly being designated under (B). Use one defined term, and specify whether a system's aggregate compute counts.

**Sec. 2(2) (U.S. person)**

8. Sec. 2(2): "U.S. person" omits LLCs, partnerships, and other unincorporated entities, and "State" is undefined (it doesn't say whether DC and territories count). A U.S.-controlled company could train abroad through a non-corporate vehicle outside the "U.S. person anywhere" reach. Expand the definition to any entity organized under U.S. federal or state law or controlled by U.S. persons, and define "State."

**Sec. 2(3) (covered facility)**

9. Sec. 2(3): "Advanced AI chips" is undefined, so the threshold can be reduced by reclassifying hardware. Define the term by performance characteristics and allow Commerce to update it.

10. Sec. 2(3): "Data center" and "containing" are undefined, so a single training cluster can be split across buildings, sites, or cloud accounts to stay under 50,000. Define the unit by interconnected or aggregate compute.

11. Sec. 2(3): The facility threshold doesn't track the training threshold. A cluster well under 50,000 chips can reach 10^26 operations over several months, so many relevant runs fall outside the registry. The definition also appears only in Sec. 4 and isn't linked to the prohibition in Sec. 3.

**Sec. 3(a) (prohibition)**

12. Sec. 3(a): "Train," "develop," and "deploy" overlap and are undefined. It's unclear whether serving API access counts as deployment, and whether training intermediate checkpoints or conducting research runs counts as "develop." Define each term.

13. Sec. 3(a): The prohibited acts omit publishing or releasing model weights, selling or licensing a covered system, and providing compute or cloud services for covered training. Add them, or the bill can be routed around through these channels.

14. Sec. 3(a): A foreign-organized subsidiary of a U.S. company that trains abroad isn't clearly covered by either "any person in the United States" or "any U.S. person anywhere." Add parent-control language.

15. Sec. 3(a): A foreign company that trains abroad and serves U.S. users isn't clearly covered, because "deploy" isn't tied to location. Specify that making a covered system available to users in the U.S. is deployment in the U.S.

**Sec. 3(b) (existing systems)**

16. Sec. 3(b): The exemption covers any run that "began" before enactment, and it exempts all of subsection (a), including deployment and further development. A grandfathered run could continue scaling for years, and it's unclear whether new training on grandfathered weights counts as the same system. Limit the exemption to registered systems, bar further scale-up or capability-enhancing training, and set a deployment deadline.

17. Sec. 3(b) with Sec. 8: Companies have a 180-day window between enactment and the effective date in which they could start new covered runs and then claim the grandfather exemption. Key the cutoff to the bill's introduction date or the effective date, and require registration of in-progress runs.

**Sec. 3(c) (safety evaluation)**

18. Sec. 3(c): "Activities conducted for the purpose of safety evaluation" is undefined and has no requirement for independence, notice, registration, duration, or limits. A developer could label capability-enhancing training or public deployment as "evaluation." Require advance notice to Commerce, bar public deployment and capability-enhancing training under this exemption, and require reporting.

**Sec. 4 (registry)**

19. Sec. 4: Sec. 5 penalizes only violations of Sec. 3, so failing to register or to file quarterly reports carries no penalty. Add penalties and a deadline for initial registration.

20. Sec. 4: "Operator" is undefined. It's unclear whether this means the owner, the lessee, a cloud provider, or the entity actually running a given workload, which could leave the actual trainer outside the reporting requirement. Define it as whoever controls the compute for a given workload.

21. Sec. 4(b): Reports describe "computing workloads" without requiring model identifiers, start and end dates, compute totals, or the purpose of each run. This makes it impossible to verify Sec. 3 compliance or grandfathering claims. Specify required fields.

22. Sec. 4(b): The operator certifies the reports, but the bill gives Commerce no audit or inspection authority, no express false-statement penalty, and no confidentiality protection for proprietary information. Add all three.

**Sec. 5 (penalties and enforcement)**

23. Sec. 5: The bill names no enforcing agency, forum, or procedure. It's unclear whether DOJ or Commerce brings civil actions, and the bill grants no injunctive or emergency authority to halt an ongoing run. Specify the enforcing agency and add injunctive relief.

24. Sec. 5(a): "Each day on which the violation occurs" is unclear for a continuing training run lasting months. Also, $1,000,000 per day is small relative to frontier training budgets and could be treated as a cost of doing business. Clarify how days are counted, and consider a per-run penalty or a percentage of compute cost. Add a statute of limitations.

25. Sec. 5(b): "Willfully" is undefined. A company could argue it didn't know its run crossed the threshold or was covered. Define willfulness to include knowing or reckless disregard of covered status, add responsible-corporate-officer liability for imprisonment, and add attempt, conspiracy, and aiding-and-abetting provisions.

26. Sec. 5(a) with Sec. 8: Sec. 5(a) applies to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but Sec. 8 delays the Act's effective date by 180 days. Resolve the conflict so that the prohibition and the penalties start on the same date.

**Sec. 6 (international agreement)**

27. Sec. 6: "Seek to negotiate" creates no reporting requirement, no deadline consequence, and no definition of "equivalent" prohibitions. Require an annual report to Congress on negotiations and define the minimum elements of an equivalent agreement.

**Sec. 7 (duration)**

28. Sec. 7(a) with Sec. 7(b): Sec. 7(a) keeps the prohibition in effect until Congress acts, but Sec. 7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years regardless of any finding. Remove the sunset, or make it conditional on a joint resolution. This is the most serious internal conflict in the bill.

29. Sec. 7(b): The sunset has no savings clause for violations committed before it takes effect or for enforcement actions still pending. Add one.

30. Sec. 7(a): The bill sets no deadline for the National Academies report, doesn't require the report to be produced, doesn't define "developed safely," and doesn't specify how the joint resolution would move through Congress. Clarify each, or Congress may never act.

**Sec. 8 (effective date) and general gaps**

31. Sec. 8: The bill grants no rulemaking authority, but the definitions, registry, and thresholds all need regulations. Add rulemaking authority with a deadline, and make sure the effective date allows time for the rules.

32. General: The bill has no severability clause. If a court strikes Sec. 2(1)(B) as vague or Sec. 2(2) as overbroad, the whole Act could fall. Add one.

33. General: The bill has no savings or preemption clause, so it's unclear whether state AI laws are preempted and how the Act interacts with export controls. Add express language.

34. General: Key terms are undefined, including "artificial intelligence," "training run," "safety evaluation," "deploy," "State," "Secretary," "operator," and "data center." Add a definitions section or the regulatory authority to define them.

35. General: The bill appropriates no funds for the registry or enforcement. Add an authorization or appropriation.

36. General: Several provisions are exposed to constitutional challenge: vagueness in Sec. 2(1)(B) and Sec. 3(c), nondelegation concerns in the Secretary's determination, and possible First Amendment challenges to restrictions on releasing weights or code. These should go to Senate Legal Counsel or outside counsel for review. I'm not a lawyer, and this list is a drafting review, not a legal opinion.

**Suggested priorities for the Thursday markup (if the list must be shortened):**

- **Sunset conflict (Sec. 7(b))**: Remove it or make it conditional.
- **Grandfathering and effective-date gap (Sec. 3(b), Sec. 8)**: Close the 180-day race window and resolve the enactment-versus-effective-date conflict.
- **Single-run and facility-threshold loopholes (Sec. 2(1)(A), Sec. 2(3))**: Count cumulative compute and align the facility threshold with the training threshold.
- **Safety-evaluation exemption (Sec. 3(c))**: Add notice and limits.
- **Enforcement gaps (Sec. 4, Sec. 5)**: Name the enforcing agency, add penalties for registry violations, and add injunctive authority.
- **Missing prohibited acts (Sec. 3(a))**: Cover weight release and compute provision.
