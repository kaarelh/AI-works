# Weak Spots in S. 2817 (Text as Amended October 6, 2026)

These issues come from reading the text you provided. I haven't checked them against the official bill, CRS analysis, or current NOAA regulations, so verify the agency and licensing references before publishing.

## Internal Conflicts (Highest Priority)

1. **§5(a) vs. §8:** Section 8 delays the Act's effect by 180 days, but §5(a) applies civil penalties to violations "on or after the date of enactment." It is unclear whether conduct during the 180-day gap is penalizable.

2. **§7(a) vs. §7(b):** Section 7(a) keeps the §3 prohibition in force until Congress passes a joint resolution, but §7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years. It is unclear whether mining becomes lawful at year 10 without a joint resolution, and the joint-resolution mechanism may be effectively moot.

3. **Sunset timing (§7(b) and §8):** The 10-year sunset runs from enactment, not from the effective date, so the prohibition would be in force for only about nine and a half years.

4. **No savings clause:** The bill does not say whether pending investigations, enforcement actions, or violations committed before a sunset or repeal remain actionable.

## Scope and Definitions

5. **§2(1)(A), "by a single vessel":** An operator could split recovery across several vessels, each under 10,000 metric tons per year, and fall outside the definition. The bill also does not say how the annual tonnage is measured.

6. **§2(1)(A), "other mineral resources" and "recovery":** Neither term is defined. It is unclear whether the definition covers sand, gravel, hydrothermal sulfides, or cobalt crusts, or whether processing and discharge count as "recovery."

7. **§2(1)(A), 200-meter depth threshold:** Activity at 200 meters or shallower falls outside the definition, and the bill does not explain why. It also does not address how this interacts with existing Outer Continental Shelf authorities.

8. **§2(1)(B), "the Secretary":** The bill never identifies which Secretary decides, and it sets no standard, procedure, notice requirement, or review process for the determination. Terms like "comparable" and "deep-sea ecosystems" are also undefined, which makes any determination easy to challenge as arbitrary.

9. **§2(2), "U.S. person":** The definition covers citizens and state-chartered corporations only. It does not address LLCs, partnerships, trusts, federally chartered entities, or foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. companies.

10. **§2(3) vs. §2(1)(A):** The covered-vessel threshold (5,000 gross tons) differs from the production threshold (10,000 metric tons) and uses different units. Because registration applies only to covered vessels, a smaller vessel could exceed the production threshold without being registered.

11. **§2(3), "equipped for the recovery":** "Equipped" is undefined. A vessel built for other purposes but capable of recovery may fall outside the definition, and the bill does not say who decides.

## Reach of the Prohibition

12. **§3(a), "any person in the United States, or any U.S. person anywhere":** Non-U.S. persons acting abroad are outside the prohibition, including foreign-flagged vessels on the high seas or in other countries' exclusive economic zones, and foreign-incorporated contractors. "In the United States" is also undefined; it is unclear whether it covers the territorial sea, the exclusive economic zone, or the outer continental shelf.

13. **§3(a), "engage in":** The bill does not say whether financing, insuring, equipping, importing, processing, or marketing minerals recovered elsewhere counts. This leaves the supply chain largely outside the prohibition and hard to enforce against.

14. **§3(b), "exploration license":** The bill does not name the issuing agency or the licensing regime that issues such licenses. It also treats "mining conducted under an exploration license" as exempt, but exploration licenses may not authorize commercial-scale recovery, so the exemption could cover commercial activity labeled as exploration. (Verify against the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act framework.)

15. **§3(b), grandfathering:** The exemption has no sunset, no volume or area cap, and no requirement that the license be in good standing. Renewals could extend it indefinitely, and pending applications are not addressed.

16. **§3(c), "environmental assessment":** The bill does not define the purpose or limit the volume, duration, or vessels involved. It requires no approval or reporting, and §4 reporting does not cover this activity, so commercial recovery could be relabeled as assessment with little oversight. This is probably the largest exemption risk in the bill.

## Registry and Reporting

17. **§4(a), "operator":** The term is undefined. It is unclear whether the registration duty falls on the vessel owner, the charterer, or the party with operational control, and a charter arrangement could shift responsibility.

18. **§4(b), quarterly reports:** The bill sets no content requirements (tonnage, location, depth, byproducts), no verification standard, no deadline for NOAA review, and no public disclosure requirement. It also does not require reporting on §3(c) assessment activities.

19. **§4 has no penalty provision:** Section 5 penalizes only violations of §3, so failure to register, failure to file, or filing a false report carries no stated penalty. The "certified" language in §4(b) also adds no separate false-statement penalty.

20. **§4, purpose of registration:** The bill does not say whether registration authorizes any activity or is only a notification. Without that, registration could be read as implicit permission.

## Penalties and Enforcement

21. **§5(a), enforcement authority:** The bill names no agency or official to bring civil actions. It provides no administrative penalty process, no injunctive authority, no statute of limitations, and no vessel forfeiture or disgorgement of profits.

22. **§5(a), "violation" and "each day":** The bill does not say whether a violation is counted per vessel, per ton, per person, or per day, or whether a continuous operation is one violation or many. It also does not specify which officers or directors are personally liable.

23. **§5(b), "willfully":** The standard is undefined, and the bill includes no aiding-and-abetting or conspiracy provision. The fine is set by reference to title 18 without specifying the corporate fine level.

24. **No rulemaking authority:** The Act does not authorize regulations, so details such as how tonnage is calculated, how exploration licenses work, and how the Secretary makes determinations would depend on agency action the bill doesn't require.

25. **No severability or judicial review clause:** If a court strikes one provision, such as the §2(1)(B) determination authority, the bill does not say whether the rest of the Act survives. It also sets no standard for judicial review of agency determinations.

## International Provisions

26. **§6, "shall seek to negotiate":** The bill sets no deadline for concluding an agreement, requires no report to Congress on the outcome, and attaches no consequence to failure. "Equivalent to those in section 3" is also undefined.

27. **§6, no reference to the International Seabed Authority or UNCLOS:** The bill does not address how the Act interacts with contracts issued by the International Seabed Authority to non-U.S.-sponsored contractors. The United States is not a party to UNCLOS, which the explainer should note.

## Duration and Congressional Review

28. **§7(a), "serious harm":** The standard for lifting the prohibition is undefined, so the finding required for a joint resolution is open to interpretation.

29. **§7(a), National Academies report:** The bill sets no deadline or content requirements for the report. The report may never be produced, and the mechanism depends on actions Congress does not fully control.

30. **§7(a), joint resolution procedure:** The bill does not say whether the joint resolution follows the ordinary presentment process, so it is unclear whether the President could veto it. This affects whether the finding has the force of law.

**Suggested priorities for the explainer:** Lead with the §3(c) assessment exemption (item 16), the §7(a)/§7(b) conflict (item 2), the §5(a)/§8 timing conflict (item 1), the "single vessel" threshold (item 5), and the lack of enforcement authority (item 21). These are the points most likely to matter to the committee on October 15, and they are drafting problems, not policy positions, which fits our nonpartisan framing.
