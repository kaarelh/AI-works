# Drafting Issues in S. 2817 (Manager's Amendment, Oct. 6, 2026)

## Section 2 (Definitions)

1. **§2(1)(A), "single training run":** Developers can split training into several runs, or count post-training, RL, fine-tuning, and continued pretraining separately, and stay under 10^26. Count cumulative compute across each model lineage, including all post-training and fine-tuning.

2. **§2(1)(A), no measurement method:** The bill doesn't say how FLOPs are counted, at what precision, or who certifies the count, so the threshold can't be verified. Require a defined counting method and a developer certification, and let the Secretary update the method by rule.

3. **§2(1)(A), fixed threshold:** A static 10^26 figure will become obsolete as algorithms become more efficient, so systems with dangerous capabilities could be built below it. Give the Secretary authority to lower the threshold by rulemaking.

4. **§2(1), compound systems:** The definition covers a "model" or "system" individually, so an agentic architecture that combines several sub-threshold models may fall outside it. Define a covered system to include any combination of models whose aggregate compute or capability meets the threshold.

5. **§2(1)(B), "Secretary" is undefined:** No agency is named, so it's unclear who makes the determination or enforces it. Name the Secretary of Commerce or a specific agency.

6. **§2(1)(B), vague capability standard:** "Substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no benchmark, measurement method, or procedure, which makes any determination easy to challenge as arbitrary. Specify an evaluation suite, a process, and notice-and-comment rulemaking.

7. **§2(1)(B), no submission or timing requirement:** Nothing requires developers to submit systems for assessment, and nothing bars deployment before a determination, so a determination could come after release. Require pre-deployment submission and a bar on deployment until assessment is complete.

8. **§2 generally, undefined and inconsistent terms:** "Artificial intelligence" is never defined, and the bill alternates between "model" (§2(1)(A)) and "system" (§2(1)(B)). Define both terms and use them consistently.

9. **§2(2), "U.S. person" is too narrow:** It excludes LLCs, partnerships, unincorporated entities, and foreign-incorporated companies with U.S. operations. "State" is also undefined, so it's unclear whether D.C. and the territories are included. Define U.S. person by place of organization or principal place of business, and define "State" to include D.C. and the territories.

10. **§2(3), "advanced AI chips" is undefined:** The term has no performance or export-control reference, so the facility threshold is open to interpretation. Define it by reference to chip performance categories.

11. **§2(3), "containing" is ambiguous:** The 50,000-chip threshold doesn't say whether it applies to a single site or to networked, leased, or colocated capacity. Aggregate networked and contracted capacity across sites and lower the threshold.

## Section 3 (Prohibition)

12. **§3(a), "person in the United States" and "deploy":** Foreign developers training abroad for U.S. users may fall outside the bill, and it's unclear where API inference counts as "deployment." Cover any covered system made available to U.S. users or run on U.S. infrastructure, regardless of the developer's nationality.

13. **§3(a), "train, develop, or deploy" is undefined:** It's unclear whether the bill reaches publishing model weights, licensing, or API access, which are the main routes for open-weight release. Define "deploy" to include publication, weight release, and API access, and separately prohibit distributing covered weights.

14. **§3(a), no prohibition on support:** The bill doesn't bar providing compute, chips, cloud services, data, or technical assistance for covered systems. Add aiding-and-abetting liability and a bar on providing compute, with customer verification duties for cloud providers.

15. **§3(b), grandfather clause is open-ended:** Any system whose training "began" before enactment is exempt, with no limit on further training, deployment, fine-tuning, or derivative models. "Began" is undefined, so a run could be started nominally before enactment and continued indefinitely. Limit the exemption to models already deployed, require registration within a fixed period, bar further scaling, and define "began" as the first compute use.

16. **§3 and §8, the enactment-to-effective-date gap:** The bill is silent on new training runs started during the 180-day window before §3 takes effect. Those runs could continue past the effective date and remain unaddressed. Add a moratorium on starting new covered training runs that takes effect on enactment.

17. **§3(c), the safety-evaluation exemption is a major loophole:** "Activities conducted for the purpose of safety evaluation" is undefined, requires no registration or approval, sets no compute limit, and doesn't bar deployment. Because "purpose" is subjective, any developer can label a run an evaluation. Require advance registration, limit the exemption to government-approved or independent evaluators, bar deployment, and require disclosure of results.

## Section 4 (Registry and Reporting)

18. **§4(a), the registry is disconnected from the prohibition:** Registration covers only facilities over 50,000 chips, while §3 applies to covered systems regardless of facility size. Organizations training covered systems on smaller or distributed clusters would never register. Require registration of any entity training a covered system, with a lower facility threshold.

19. **§4(a), no registration deadline or content requirements:** The bill sets no deadline and specifies no information beyond the facility's existence. Set a deadline and require identification of training runs and their compute totals.

20. **§4(b), reports are self-certified and unverifiable:** Quarterly reports only need to describe "computing workloads," with no requirement to identify covered systems or report FLOP counts, and no audit mechanism. Require identification of each covered run, its compute total, and the model it produced, and add audit authority.

21. **§4, no penalty for noncompliance:** §5 penalizes only violations of §3, so failing to register or report carries no consequence. Add civil penalties for reporting failures and criminal penalties for false certifications.

22. **§4(b), no confidentiality or whistleblower protections:** Reports could be exposed or withheld without a clear statutory basis, and the bill doesn't authorize sharing with enforcement agencies. Add a FOIA exemption, authorize sharing with DOJ and the enforcing agency, and protect employees who report violations.

## Section 5 (Penalties and Enforcement)

23. **Section 5 generally, no enforcing authority:** No agency or official is authorized to investigate, sue, or seek relief. Designate the enforcing agency and the Attorney General to seek injunctions, subpoenas, and inspections, and authorize orders to halt training or seize weights and compute.

24. **§5(a), the penalty unit is undefined:** "Each day on which the violation occurs" doesn't say whether a violation is a training run, a deployment, or a model. A flat $1 million per day may also be treated as a cost of doing business by large firms. Define violations per run or deployment and add penalties tied to revenue or compute value, along with disgorgement.

25. **§5(a) vs. §8, the start date conflicts:** §5(a) applies penalties to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §8 delays §3 until 180 days later. Align the penalty start date with the effective date.

26. **§5(b), "willfully" is undefined:** Without a definition, prosecutors may have to prove knowledge of the law, which is hard to establish against technical staff. Define willfulness to include knowing or reckless conduct, and add responsible-officer liability.

## Section 6 (International Agreement)

27. **§6, "seek to negotiate" has no teeth:** The bill requires only an effort, with no reporting to Congress and no consequence for failure, and "equivalent" is undefined. Require a report to Congress at 12 months, define the required content of an agreement, and state that the domestic prohibition doesn't depend on an agreement.

## Section 7 (Duration)

28. **§7(a) vs. §7(b), the bill contradicts itself:** §7(a) keeps the prohibition in place until Congress finds covered systems can be developed safely, but §7(b) ends the Act after 10 years regardless. Delete §7(b), which is consistent with the bill's premise that the prohibition should last until safety is shown.

29. **§7(a), the "developed safely" standard is undefined:** The bill doesn't set a standard for safe development, a deadline or scope for the NAS report, or what the resolution must cover. The resolution could also pass on a narrow finding about a single class of systems. Specify the NAS deliverable, deadline, and standard, and require any resolution to address the covered systems specifically. Consider expedited procedures for the resolution.

## Missing Provisions

30. **No severability clause:** If a court strikes one provision, the rest of the Act could fail. Add a severability clause.

31. **No judicial review provisions:** Determinations under §2(1)(B) and any regulations will likely face APA challenges. Specify the review standard, and provide that determinations remain in effect pending review.

32. **No rulemaking or funding authority:** The bill grants no rulemaking power and authorizes no appropriations, so the agency can't implement or enforce the Act. Add rulemaking authority and an authorization of appropriations.

33. **No whistleblower protections:** Employees are the most likely source of information about violations, and the bill doesn't protect them. Add anti-retaliation protections.

## Priorities for Thursday

If the office can take only a few changes, I'd prioritize:

- **Items 1–2 and 18–20:** a counting method and reporting regime that cover all covered training, not just large facilities.
- **Item 13:** an explicit bar on releasing weights.
- **Item 15:** a narrower grandfather clause that doesn't allow indefinite continuation.
- **Item 16:** a moratorium on new covered training from enactment.
- **Item 17:** tighter limits on the safety-evaluation exemption.
- **Item 28:** deleting the 10-year sunset.
- **Item 23:** named enforcement authority with injunctive power.
