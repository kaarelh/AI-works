RESPONSE:
**Highest priority (fix before Thursday):** items 1, 2, 3, 9, 12, 17, and 22. Items 1 and 22 could leave the bill without any working prohibition or enforcement.

1. **Sec. 7(a) vs. Sec. 7(b):** Section 7(a) keeps the prohibition in force until Congress lifts it, but Section 7(b) says the entire Act ceases to have effect 10 years after enactment, which would end the prohibition, registry, and penalties together. Delete the sunset or limit it to the lifting mechanism, and state that the prohibition persists absent a joint resolution.

2. **Sec. 3(c):** The "safety evaluation" exemption has no definition and no limits on compute, scale, duration, containment, or who conducts the work, and it requires no notice or reporting. Any training run can be labeled as safety evaluation. Define the term, require notice to Commerce, cap compute, and limit the exemption to sandboxed work that is never deployed.

3. **Sec. 3(b):** The grandfather clause exempts any system whose training "began" before enactment, but "began" is undefined, and the clause does not say whether continued training, fine-tuning, or deployment of that system is also exempt. A developer could start a placeholder run before enactment and then keep scaling and deploying indefinitely. Limit the exemption to models completed by a fixed date, freeze their compute, and bar further scaling.

4. **Sec. 3(b) and Sec. 8:** The grandfather clause keys off enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later, leaving unclear whether runs started during that window are covered. This gap invites companies to race into large training runs before the prohibition starts. Key all dates to the effective date and bar new covered training from enactment onward.

5. **Sec. 5(a) and Sec. 8:** Civil penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," while Section 3 does not take effect until 180 days later. The text does not say whether conduct in that interval is penalized. Align the two provisions.

6. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** The compute threshold does not say how to count operations. It leaves open whether low-precision operations are normalized, whether FLOP and FLOPS are distinguished, and whether inference, synthetic-data generation, reinforcement learning, and distillation count toward the total. Define total compute across all training stages and specify a measurement method.

7. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** "Single training run" lets developers split one effort into several nominal runs, each under 10^26. Aggregate compute across related runs, continued pretraining, and post-training of the same base model within a defined window.

8. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** The threshold is hardcoded, and algorithmic efficiency gains could let a system with dangerous capabilities be trained below 10^26. Give Commerce authority to lower the threshold, and make the capability test in (B) work as a backstop.

9. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** "Substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no benchmark, evaluation method, procedure, deadline, notice requirement, or judicial-review standard. The vagueness invites a due process challenge, and the prohibition is unenforceable until the Secretary acts. Specify evaluation criteria and a process with deadlines, and make clear that a determination is not a precondition for the prohibition.

10. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** "The Secretary" is undefined, and the bill names the Department of Commerce in Section 4 and the Secretary of State in Section 6. Specify which Secretary makes the determination.

11. **Sec. 2(2):** The "U.S. person" definition excludes permanent residents, unincorporated entities such as LLCs and partnerships, and companies with substantial U.S. operations that are organized abroad. "State" is also undefined, leaving open whether D.C. and the territories are included. Define the term to cover entities with a principal place of business or substantial operations in the U.S., and cover residents.

12. **Sec. 3(a):** Extraterritorial reach extends only to "U.S. persons," so foreign subsidiaries, affiliates, and foreign-incorporated companies training abroad fall outside the Act. Extend coverage to entities controlled by or acting on behalf of U.S. persons, and to any person who makes covered systems available to U.S. users.

13. **Sec. 3(a):** "Train, develop, or deploy" is undefined and omits key activities, including releasing or distributing model weights, providing compute or chips, hosting, financing, and selling access. Define the verbs and add "release, transfer, provide compute for, host, fund, or knowingly facilitate."

14. **Sec. 3(a):** "Person" is undefined in the Act and relies on the default definition in 1 U.S.C. §1, which is unclear about some entity types and does not clearly cover individual employees. Define the term expressly.

15. **Sec. 3 (general):** The Act has no aiding-and-abetting, conspiracy, or attempt language, so cloud and chip providers who knowingly support covered training may escape liability. Add explicit secondary liability.

16. **Sec. 4(a):** The registry threshold is 50,000 "advanced AI chips," but "advanced AI chip" and "data center" are undefined, and the threshold is not tied to the 10^26 prohibition. A cluster below 50,000 chips can exceed 10^26 given enough time, and distributed training across sites can evade the count. Define the terms, register by compute capacity and covered training, and include multi-site and cloud arrangements.

17. **Sec. 4 and Sec. 5:** Section 5 penalizes only violations of Section 3, so failing to register or report carries no penalty, and the registry is effectively voluntary. Add civil and criminal penalties for Section 4 violations and for false certifications.

18. **Sec. 4(b):** Quarterly reports "describing computing workloads" have no required content, such as whether a workload is a covered system or the compute used. There is no deadline and no advance notice requirement. Require pre-notification of planned covered runs and specify the report contents.

19. **Sec. 4(a)–(b):** "Operator" is undefined, which leaves open whether the data center owner, the cloud provider, or the tenant running the workload must register and certify. Define "operator" to include whoever controls the compute.

20. **Sec. 4 (general):** The Act gives Commerce no authority to inspect facilities, audit reports, subpoena records, or require recordkeeping. Add these powers.

21. **Sec. 5(a):** A $1 million daily maximum is a small cost for large developers, and "each day on which the violation occurs" does not say whether a months-long training run is one violation or one per day. Consider penalties tied to revenue or compute spend, and define the unit of violation.

22. **Sec. 5 (general):** No agency is designated to enforce the Act, and the Act does not expressly authorize injunctions, emergency orders, or the seizure or disabling of compute and weights. It also does not address venue or limitations periods. Designate the enforcing agency, authorize injunctive relief, and give enforcers power to halt covered runs.

23. **Sec. 5 (general):** The Act has no citizen-suit provision and no whistleblower protections, although insiders are often the only source of information about covert training runs. Add both.

24. **Sec. 5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the Act does not address liability for corporate officers. Define the willfulness standard and add responsible-officer liability.

25. **Sec. 5 and general:** The Act has no severability or savings clause. If a court strikes Section 2(1)(B) or part of Section 3, the whole Act could fall. Add a severability clause.

26. **Sec. 6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no duty to reach an agreement, requires no report to Congress on failure, and does not authorize export controls or sanctions against noncompliant countries. "Equivalent" is undefined. Require a report on progress, define equivalence, and authorize specific measures if negotiations fail.

27. **Sec. 7(a):** The lifting standard is ambiguous. "Developed safely" is undefined, the NAS report has no deadline, and the bill does not require an affirmative NAS finding. It also does not say whether the joint resolution is presented to the President for signature or veto, which invites a dispute over whether a lift is effective. Specify all of these.

28. **Sec. 2 (general):** Key terms are undefined, including "artificial intelligence," "develop," "safety evaluation," "advanced AI chip," and "data center." Add a complete definitions section.

29. **Sec. 4 through Sec. 7 (general):** The Act gives Commerce no rulemaking authority to set reporting formats, update thresholds, or implement the Act, and it authorizes no appropriations for enforcement. Add both.

30. **Sec. 1 (general):** The Act has no savings clause. It does not say whether it preempts stricter state laws or leaves them in place, which invites litigation. Add an express statement on state law.

This is a drafting and policy review based only on the text provided. Before sending anything to Senator Whitfield's office, have the Coalition's counsel or the Senate Legislative Counsel check the constitutional questions, particularly vagueness in item 9, nondelegation in items 9 and 27, and the enforcement design in items 21 through 25.
