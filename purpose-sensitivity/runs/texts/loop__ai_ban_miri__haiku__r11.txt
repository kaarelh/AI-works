RESPONSE:
**Priority items for Thursday**

1. **§7(b) vs. §7(a):** The 10-year sunset ends the whole Act, while §7(a) says the prohibition lasts until Congress acts. The office needs to know which is intended.
2. **§3(b):** The grandfather clause exempts any system whose training began before enactment, including its deployment.
3. **§3(c):** The "safety evaluation" exemption has no definition, notice requirement, or limits.
4. **§2(1)(A)–(B):** The single-run compute test and the undefined Secretary determination leave the core scope open to evasion.
5. **§5 and §8:** Enforcement authority is missing, and the penalty start date conflicts with the effective date.

**Full list**

**Definitions and scope (§2)**

1. **§2(1)(A), "single training run":** Counting only a single run lets developers split a project across several runs, continued pretraining, or post-training stages to stay under 10^26. Count cumulative compute for a model and its successors and derivatives, including fine-tuning and RL.
2. **§2(1)(A), fixed threshold:** Algorithmic efficiency gains will allow dangerous systems to be trained below 10^26, and the bill has no way to adjust. Give the Secretary authority, after notice, to lower the threshold by rule.
3. **§2(1)(A), measurement:** "Trained" and the FLOP counting method are undefined, and it's unclear whether synthetic data generation, distillation, or inference-time compute count. Specify a measurement method and inclusion rules.
4. **§2(1)(A) vs. (B), "model" vs. "system":** The two subparagraphs use different terms, and §3 uses a third. Developers could argue that scaffolds, agents, or model-plus-tooling bundles fall outside the definition. Use one defined term that covers any model, system, or combination.
5. **§2(1)(B), no determination process:** The Secretary is unnamed, and there are no criteria for "substantially exceeding" or "most cognitive tasks," no evaluation method, no notice, and no deadline. Since (B) is the only backstop for systems below the compute line, this is the weakest point in the bill. Name the agency, set evaluation criteria, and require notice, a record, and a deadline.
6. **§2(1)(B), vagueness:** The open-ended standard invites due-process challenges to the criminal penalty in §5(b) and APA challenges to determinations. Tighten the standard and state the judicial review posture.
7. **§2(1)(B), timing:** It's unclear whether a determination applies prospectively, reaches systems already in training, or can be sought in advance. Specify.
8. **§2(2), "U.S. person":** The definition excludes foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. companies, LLCs and partnerships, and lawful permanent residents, and "State" is undefined (D.C. and territories are unclear). Consider covering entities controlled by U.S. persons or doing business in the U.S., and define "State."
9. **§2(3), "covered facility":** "Advanced AI chips" is undefined, and the 50,000-chip threshold applies per data center. Operators can split training across sites below the threshold or rent cloud capacity. Define the chip term (by reference to export-control parameters if possible) and aggregate chips across an operator's sites.
10. **§2, undefined terms:** "Artificial intelligence," "data center," "operator," "train," "develop," "deploy," "safety evaluation," and "Secretary" are not defined. Add a definitions provision.

**Prohibition (§3)**

11. **§3(a), enabling activity:** The prohibition doesn't reach providing chips, cloud access, financing, data, or weights to someone else's covered system. Add a ban on knowingly providing material support for covered systems.
12. **§3(a), open weights:** "Deploy" may not cover publishing or transferring weights, so a covered system could be released for download. Expressly prohibit making covered weights available and address possession.
13. **§3(a), foreign developers:** The prohibition reaches persons "in the United States" and U.S. persons abroad, but a foreign developer that trains abroad and doesn't deploy in the U.S. is outside it. Consider covering any developer whose system is offered in the U.S., and rely on §6 for the rest.
14. **§3(b), grandfathering:** Systems whose training began before enactment are exempt from the whole prohibition, including deployment, with no conditions. A developer could start a very large run before enactment and deploy it indefinitely. Limit the exemption to systems already deployed, bar further scaling, and require registration and a safety case before any deployment.
15. **§3(b), "began" and derivative runs:** "Began" is undefined, and it's unclear whether continued pretraining or scaling from a grandfathered model counts as a new run. State that any new training run after enactment is covered, including runs initialized from grandfathered weights.
16. **§3(c), safety-evaluation exemption:** "Purpose of safety evaluation" is undefined and subjective, so it could cover training a superintelligence-scale model and deploying it under a safety label. Require advance notice to Commerce, a defined protocol, containment, no deployment, no capability-improvement purpose, and disclosure of results.

**Registry and reporting (§4)**

17. **§4(a), registration:** There is no deadline, no content requirements (chip count, location, owner, cloud tenants), and no requirement to register before operating. Specify contents, a deadline, and registration as a precondition to operation.
18. **§4(b), "computing workloads":** The term is undefined and not tied to covered systems, so reports need not identify which runs are covered, their compute totals, or any safety-evaluation designation. Require per-run fields: compute used, model identity, purpose, and any safety-evaluation designation.
19. **§4(b), self-certification:** Reports are certified by the operator, with no audit, inspection, or verification power, and quarterly reporting is slow. Add inspection and audit authority, and require advance notice of large runs.
20. **§4, no penalties for registry violations:** §5 penalizes only violations of §3, so failing to register or filing a false report carries no specific penalty. Add penalties and state that false certifications are separately punishable.
21. **§4, confidentiality:** The bill doesn't say whether reports are public, exempt from FOIA, or shared with national security agencies. Specify.
22. **§4, "operator":** The term is undefined, so cloud providers and tenants could each claim the other is the operator and leave a facility unregistered. Define "operator" to cover owners, lessors, and anyone with control over the compute, with joint obligations.

**Penalties and enforcement (§5)**

23. **§5, no enforcing agency or remedies:** No agency is designated, and the bill grants no authority for injunctions, cease-and-desist orders, or seizure of chips and weights. Designate an agency, authorize orders to halt training, and authorize seizure and forfeiture.
24. **§5(a), penalty size:** $1,000,000 per day may be small relative to the cost of a frontier training run or company revenue, making it a cost of doing business. Scale the penalty to revenue or compute spending, or set a per-run penalty.
25. **§5(a), unit of violation:** "Each day on which the violation occurs" doesn't say whether a violation is counted per run, per model, per chip, or per day of deployment. Define the unit.
26. **§5(a) vs. §8, start date:** Penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §3 doesn't take effect until 180 days later. Read literally, this penalizes conduct that isn't yet prohibited, or leaves the 180-day delay meaningless for penalties. Reconcile the two.
27. **§5(b), willfulness:** Criminal liability requires willfulness, and a developer could argue it didn't know a system crossed the thresholds given the vague definitions. Consider a knowing or reckless standard, and add conspiracy and aiding-and-abetting language.
28. **§5, individual and successor liability:** The bill doesn't reach officers or directors who direct violations, and it has no anti-evasion provision for reorganizing or moving entities. Add both.

**Duration, international, and general provisions (§§6–8)**

29. **§7(b), sunset:** The Act ends 10 years after enactment, which ends the prohibition, the registry, and the penalties whether or not the risk is resolved. This conflicts with §7(a), which keeps the prohibition in place until Congress acts. Delete the sunset or make it apply only after a joint resolution is enacted.
30. **§7(a), lifting mechanism:** The bill sets no deadline for the NAS report, provides no funding for it, does not define "developed safely," and sets no expedited procedure for the joint resolution. Specify each.
31. **§6, international agreement:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no obligation to conclude an agreement, no report to Congress, and no consequence for failure. It also doesn't say whether an agreement would replace the domestic ban. Add a reporting requirement and clarify the relationship.
32. **§8, effective date:** The 180-day delay, combined with the grandfather clause (item 14), gives developers a window to start large runs before the prohibition applies. Make the prohibition apply to any run starting after enactment, or shorten the delay.
33. **Whole Act, severability:** There is no severability clause, so if a court strikes §2(1)(B) or §3(c), the rest may fall with it. Add one.
34. **Whole Act, rulemaking:** Commerce has no express authority to issue regulations for registry contents, threshold measurement, chip definitions, or determinations. Add it.
35. **Whole Act, relation to other law:** The bill has no savings or preemption clause and doesn't address existing export controls or state laws, which invites conflict claims. Add a clarifying provision.
36. **Whole Act, speech challenges:** Restrictions on publishing or releasing code and weights may draw First Amendment challenges. Either address publication expressly with narrow language or note the issue for counsel.
37. **Whole Act, whistleblowers:** There are no whistleblower protections or incentives, though insiders are likely the main source for detecting covert training. Add protections.

**Caveats:** I'm working only from the amended text you provided, so I can't tell which problems the October 6 manager's amendment introduced. Several items (the sunset, the grandfather clause, and the penalty level) are policy choices for MIRI and the Senator's office, not only drafting fixes. Before sending anything, have the office's legislative counsel review the definitions and the §7 conflict.
