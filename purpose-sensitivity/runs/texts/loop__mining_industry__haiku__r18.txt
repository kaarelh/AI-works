# Loopholes, Ambiguities, and Drafting Problems in S. 2817

This review covers the text as amended October 6, 2026, as provided. I did not compare it against the prior version or check outside references such as the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act (DSHMRA), so counsel should verify those.

**Scope and definitions**

1. **§2(1)(A):** The threshold applies only to activity "by a single vessel." A fleet of vessels each under 10,000 tons per year, or one operation split across vessels, falls outside subparagraph (A) even if aggregate output is far higher. Only subparagraph (B) could reach it, and that depends on a Secretary's determination.
2. **§2(1)(A):** The measurement basis is undefined (wet or dry weight, nodules only or total material including sediment), and "per year" is not defined (calendar year or rolling period). Output could be managed to stay just under 10,000 tons, for example 9,500 tons in one year and 9,500 the next.
3. **§2(1)(A):** "Recovery" is undefined. It is unclear whether test collection, sediment discharge, or material recovered and then returned to the seabed counts.
4. **§2(1)(A):** "Other mineral resources" is undefined, so it is unclear whether byproducts, sediment, or non-nodule deposits count toward the threshold.
5. **§2(1)(A):** The text does not say whether the 200-meter depth is measured at the point of recovery or at the vessel, and it does not address territorial scope. Its relationship to the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act in the U.S. EEZ is unaddressed.
6. **§2(1)(B):** "Risks … comparable" has no criteria, procedure, notice requirement, record requirement, or review standard. This invites a vagueness challenge and gives the Secretary discretion that could cut either way.
7. **§2(1)(B):** The text does not say whether a determination must precede activity, whether it applies to ongoing operations, or how operators receive notice.
8. **§2 (general):** "Secretary" is undefined. §2(1)(B) uses "Secretary," §4 uses NOAA, and §6 uses the Secretary of State, so it is unclear who makes the subparagraph (B) determination.
9. **§2(2):** "Corporation organized under the laws of a State" arguably excludes LLCs, partnerships, and other state-law entities, creating doubt about whether they are covered by §3(a)'s "any U.S. person anywhere."
10. **§2(2):** A foreign-incorporated subsidiary of a U.S. company is not a "U.S. person," and the definition has no control or beneficial-ownership test. This leaves room to structure operations through foreign affiliates outside the "anywhere" reach.
11. **§2(2):** "State" is undefined. It is unclear whether the District of Columbia and the territories are included.
12. **§2(3):** The 5,000 gross ton threshold exempts smaller vessels from the §4 registry entirely. "Gross tons" is also unspecified (gross tonnage or gross register tonnage under international convention).
13. **§2(3):** "Equipped for the recovery" is undefined. Support vessels, vessels with detachable collection systems, or vessels reconfigured between campaigns could argue they are not covered.
14. **§2(3):** "Vessel" is undefined. Collection systems towed or operated by remotely operated or autonomous vehicles may not clearly fall within it.
15. **§2(3) and §3(a):** "Covered vessel" is used only in §4. The §3 prohibition is not tied to covered vessels, so a smaller vessel engaged in commercial mining is prohibited but never registered or reported, which leaves the government unable to identify violators.

**Prohibition**

16. **§3(a):** "Engage in" is undefined. The text does not say whether financing, contracting, equipment supply, processing, or sale of mineral resources is covered.
17. **§3(a):** There is no prohibition on importing, processing, or selling minerals recovered from the deep seabed. Output recovered by a foreign-flagged or foreign-affiliated operation could be brought into U.S. commerce without violating the Act.
18. **§3(a):** "In the United States" is undefined as applied to the territorial sea, EEZ, and U.S.-flagged vessels on the high seas. The bill also does not address conduct by non-U.S. persons outside U.S. jurisdiction, or how "any U.S. person anywhere" would be enforced against foreign-flagged vessels.
19. **§3 (general):** The bill does not amend, repeal, or reference DSHMRA's licensing regime. It is unclear whether existing DSHMRA permits or NOAA licenses are "exploration licenses" for §3(b), or whether commercial-recovery authorizations under that regime are affected.
20. **§3 (general):** There is no savings, supremacy, or conflict clause. The bill does not say whether it overrides DSHMRA, OCSLA, or international obligations, and there is no rule of construction for existing law.

**Existing operations exemption**

21. **§3(b):** The exemption covers "mining conducted under an exploration license," but an exploration license does not authorize commercial recovery. Operators can argue that commercial-volume extraction under an exploration authorization is exempt, and the government can argue it is not.
22. **§3(b):** The exemption does not say whether it covers pending applications, renewals, extensions, amendments, expanded production rates, new vessels, transfers to successors or affiliates, or non-license instruments such as contracts.
23. **§3(b) and §8:** Licenses are protected only if issued "before the date of enactment," but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. Licenses issued during that window are neither protected nor, until the effective date, prohibited. Activity during the window is not prohibited at all.
24. **§3(b):** "License" is undefined. The text does not identify the issuing agency or say whether foreign-government or International Seabed Authority-sponsored exploration authorizations count, or whether a "permit" is a license.
25. **§3(c):** The "environmental assessment" exemption has no limits on volume, duration, location, or sale of recovered material. Commercial-scale recovery labeled as assessment could fall outside the prohibition, which is likely the largest loophole in the bill.
26. **§3(c):** The exemption has no approval, certification, notice, or reporting requirement, so there is no way to verify the stated purpose.
27. **§3(c):** The purpose test is undefined for mixed-purpose activity. The text does not say whether environmental assessment must be the primary purpose or merely one purpose.

**Registry and reporting**

28. **§4 and §5(a):** Registration and reporting are not themselves prohibited by §3, and §5 penalties attach only to §3 violations. Failure to register or report carries no penalty, so the registry is effectively voluntary.
29. **§4(a):** "Operator" is undefined. The text does not say who is the operator of a chartered, leased, or foreign-owned vessel, or how responsibility is allocated among multiple parties.
30. **§4(b):** The report has no content requirements (volumes, locations, depths, retained or discharged material). It also has no public-disclosure or confidentiality provision, so it is unclear whether NOAA publishes reports or how trade secrets are protected.
31. **§4(b):** "Certified by the operator" has no standard for the certification and no penalty for a false certification.
32. **§4:** The bill grants no rulemaking authority, so the registration form, reporting content, and deadlines cannot be set administratively. There is also no first reporting deadline and no link between registration and the start of operations.

**Penalties**

33. **§5(a) and §8:** Civil penalties apply to violations "occurring on or after the date of enactment," but §3 does not take effect until 180 days later. It is unclear whether earlier conduct can be penalized, which invites a retroactivity argument. The language may be surplus.
34. **§5(a):** The bill does not identify the enforcing agency or procedure. It does not say whether DOJ, NOAA, or the Coast Guard brings suit, whether there is administrative assessment or a hearing, whether judicial review applies, or what statute of limitations governs.
35. **§5(a):** "Per day" has no counting rule. It does not say whether violations are counted per vessel, per operation, per day of production, or per day of presence, and there is no inflation adjustment.
36. **§5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined. The text does not address corporate and officer liability beyond general criminal law, or aiding and financing of prohibited activity beyond general conspiracy principles.
37. **General:** "Person" is used throughout but not defined in the Act. Its reach (for example, whether it includes vessels or individual officers) depends on the Dictionary Act (1 U.S.C. §1), which should be confirmed.

**International agreement**

38. **§6:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no obligation to conclude an agreement, no reporting requirement, and no consequence for failure. The text also does not say whether an agreement would automatically change domestic law or require implementing legislation.

**Duration and sunset**

39. **§7(a):** The bill has no procedure for the joint resolution (expedited consideration, presentment, veto override), no deadline for the National Academies report, and no requirement that the report be requested. "Serious harm" is undefined, so the threshold for lifting the prohibition is unclear.
40. **§7(a) and §7(b):** The two subsections conflict. §7(a) keeps the prohibition in effect until a joint resolution is adopted, but §7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years. The prohibition therefore expires at 10 years regardless of §7(a), and it is unclear whether a joint resolution would repeal the Act or only lift the prohibition.
41. **§7(b):** There is no savings clause. The text does not say whether penalties for pre-sunset violations, pending enforcement actions, or exemptions under §3(b) survive expiration.
42. **§7(b) and §8:** The 10-year sunset runs from enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days after enactment. The prohibition's effective life is therefore about 9.5 years, not 10.

**Missing provisions**

43. **General:** There is no severability clause. If one provision is struck down, for example the §2(1)(B) discretion, it is unclear whether the rest of the Act survives.
44. **General:** "Deep seabed" is undefined, and the Act does not say how it relates to the continental shelf or the EEZ.
45. **General:** There is no appropriation or funding provision for enforcement, registry administration, or the §6 negotiations.

**Notes for legal**

- **Choose the argument.** Some of these items support "the bill is unworkable": undefined enforcement, vagueness in §2(1)(B), the §5(a)/§8 mismatch, the §7 conflict, and the registry with no penalty. Others support "the bill is easy to evade": fleet-splitting, §3(c), and the import gap. Arguing both at once weakens the case. The unworkability argument is probably more persuasive to the committee.
- **Some items favor Meridian.** The sunset in §7(b), the unenforced registry, and the §3(b) grandfathering all work in the company's favor. Publicizing them may prompt amendments that close them. Decide what to raise before the markup.
- **Some loopholes carry legal and reputational risk.** Deliberately structuring around a threshold or labeling commercial recovery as environmental assessment could be framed as bad faith. That framing could also strengthen support for the bill's most restrictive version.
- **Verification needed.** Counsel should confirm the DSHMRA, OCSLA, and Dictionary Act references, and check the October 6 amendment's changes against the prior text, since some of these issues may already have been addressed or introduced by the amendment.
