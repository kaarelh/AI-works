# S. 2817 drafting issues (manager's amendment text of Oct. 6, 2026)

Each item is tagged **[Loophole]** (a gap that could narrow coverage), **[Ambiguity]** (unclear meaning), or **[Workability]** (structural or implementation problem, including constitutional arguments). This review is from the text alone. I haven't seen the committee report or earlier versions, and some items are judgment calls about how a court or agency would read the text.

## Section 2: Definitions

**§2(1)(A), compute threshold**

1. **§2(1)(A) "single training run" [Loophole]:** The term is undefined. Multi-stage training, restarts from checkpoints, and continued pretraining could be split into runs that each stay under 10^26 while the cumulative compute is far higher. The text has no aggregation rule.
2. **§2(1)(A) compute accounting [Ambiguity]:** The bill doesn't say how operations are counted. Fine-tuning, RL rollouts, synthetic data generation, failed or ablation runs, and sparse or low-precision operations are all unaddressed, and no agency has authority to set a method.
3. **§2(1)(A) distillation and derived models [Loophole]:** Teacher-model compute isn't attributed to a student model. A model trained under the threshold on a larger model's outputs could be highly capable and still fall outside (A).
4. **§2(1)(A) static threshold [Workability]:** The 10^26 figure is fixed in statute with no adjustment mechanism. Algorithmic efficiency gains make it a weakening proxy for capability. It is also overinclusive, because it can reach models nowhere near "superintelligence."
5. **§2(1)(A) vs. (B) terminology [Ambiguity]:** (A) covers a "model" and (B) covers a "system," and neither term is defined. A system built from several sub-threshold models is reachable only through the Secretary's discretionary determination under (B).
6. **Title vs. text [Ambiguity]:** "Superintelligence" appears in the title but is never defined or used in the operative text. Prong (A) is triggered by compute alone, which invites disputes over purpose and scope.

**§2(1)(B), Secretary's determination**

7. **§2(1)(B) "the Secretary" [Ambiguity]:** The Act never defines "Secretary." Section 6 specifies "Secretary of State," which implies the term elsewhere means someone else, but the bill doesn't say who.
8. **§2(1)(B) capability standard [Ambiguity]:** "Substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no benchmark, reference population (median person or expert), task universe, or measurement method.
9. **§2(1)(B) no procedure [Workability]:** There is no requirement for notice, rulemaking, evidentiary standard, publication, comment, deadline, administrative appeal, or judicial review of a determination.
10. **§2(1)(B) timing [Workability]:** The bill doesn't say whether determinations are prospective only. A developer could be mid-training when a system is designated, and with criminal penalties attached this raises fair-notice and vagueness problems.
11. **§2(1)(B) delegation [Workability]:** An executive official sets the scope of a criminal prohibition with no intelligible standard. That supports nondelegation and void-for-vagueness arguments.

**§2(2)–(3), U.S. person and covered facility**

12. **§2(2) "U.S. person" is narrow [Loophole]:** It covers only citizens and "corporations organized under the laws of a State." It omits lawful permanent residents, partnerships, LLCs (if not read as "corporations"), trusts, unincorporated associations, and entities organized under federal, D.C., or territorial law ("State" is undefined).
13. **§2(2) foreign subsidiaries [Loophole]:** Foreign-organized subsidiaries of U.S. companies aren't U.S. persons, and the bill has no control or ownership attribution rule. Extraterritorial reach in §3(a) therefore has a gap for overseas affiliates.
14. **§2(2) / §3(a) "anywhere" [Workability]:** The Act reaches U.S. citizens "anywhere" but provides no enforcement or jurisdictional mechanism. It also doesn't address conflicts with foreign law.
15. **§2(3) undefined terms [Ambiguity]:** "Data center" and "advanced AI chips" are undefined. There is no performance threshold and no tie to existing export-control classifications.
16. **§2(3) chip count as proxy [Loophole]:** More capable chips shift the number needed. Fewer chips over a longer period can reach 10^26, and capacity split across sites or leased from others can stay under 50,000 per "facility." There is no aggregation rule.
17. **§2(3) / §4 "operator" [Ambiguity]:** The term is undefined. It could mean the owner, a lessee, or a cloud tenant, which determines who must register and certify.

## Section 3: Prohibition

18. **§3(a) "train, develop, or deploy" [Ambiguity]:** None of the verbs is defined. "Develop" could reach research, architecture design, data curation, or publication. "Deploy" could mean internal use, API access, open-weights release, or hosting a third party's model.
19. **§3(a) conduct not covered [Loophole]:** The text doesn't address funding, supplying compute to others, possessing or transferring weights, or using foreign-trained covered systems. Only general aiding-and-abetting law would apply.
20. **§3(a) "person" and "in the United States" [Ambiguity]:** "Person" is undefined (the Dictionary Act, 1 U.S.C. §1, likely fills the gap). "In the United States" is also undefined, so a run directed from the U.S. on foreign hardware, or from abroad on U.S. hardware, is unclear.
21. **§3(a) First Amendment [Workability]:** "Develop" could reach writing and publishing code, weights, and research, which supports an as-applied First Amendment challenge.
22. **§3(b) "training began" [Loophole]:** The term is undefined, so initiating a nominal run before enactment could be used to claim grandfathered status. A grandfathered run may also continue to completion regardless of its eventual scale.
23. **§3(b) scope of exemption [Loophole]:** The exemption removes the entire §3(a) prohibition for grandfathered systems, including "develop." It doesn't address further training, fine-tuning, scaling, derivatives, or successors, and it sets no compute cap or test for when a new system starts.
24. **§3(b) interaction with §2(1) [Ambiguity]:** It's unclear whether a model that crosses 10^26 only late in a run was a "covered AI system" when training began. A system designated under (B) after enactment but begun earlier would also be exempt, which limits (B) mostly to new systems.
25. **§3(b) vs. §8 gap [Ambiguity]:** The grandfather cutoff is enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. A run started in that window isn't grandfathered, and the bill doesn't say whether it becomes unlawful on day 181 or how it would be wound down.
26. **§3(c) "safety evaluation" [Loophole]:** The term is undefined, and the exemption is purpose-based with no limit on scale, no oversight, certification, or disclosure. Almost any training could be characterized as serving safety evaluation, and dual-purpose activity is unaddressed.
27. **§3(c) burden and scope [Ambiguity]:** The bill doesn't say whose purpose controls, who bears the burden of proving the exemption, or whether it covers training a system in order to evaluate it or only evaluating existing systems.

## Section 4: Registry

28. **§4(a) no mechanics [Workability]:** There is no registration deadline, required content, form, rulemaking authority, or designated office. "Department of Commerce" is named but no official is.
29. **§4 no enforcement hook [Workability]:** Section 5 penalizes only violations of §3, so failing to register or report carries no stated penalty. Only general false-statement law (e.g., 18 U.S.C. §1001) would reach a false certification.
30. **§4(b) "computing workloads" [Ambiguity]:** The term is undefined, with no required level of detail. Operators of shared or cloud facilities may not know tenant workloads. The certification standard (knowledge, reasonable inquiry) and first reporting date are unspecified.
31. **§4 confidentiality [Workability]:** There are no trade-secret protections, FOIA exemption, or limits on use or onward sharing of reports. This is a strong practical objection for any operator.
32. **§4 mismatch with §3 [Workability]:** The registry covers facilities by chip count, while §3 turns on compute per training run. Sub-threshold or distributed sites are never reported, reports are self-certified, and there's no audit or inspection authority to verify them.

## Section 5: Penalties

33. **§5(a) no enforcement authority [Workability]:** The bill doesn't say who may bring a civil action, in what forum, under what procedure, or by what standard of proof. It also provides no injunctive or other equitable remedy.
34. **§5(a) "each day" [Ambiguity]:** It's unclear whether training or deployment is a continuing violation and how days are counted. There are no penalty-setting factors, and a large per-day maximum invites Excessive Fines arguments.
35. **§5(a) vs. §8 [Ambiguity]:** Penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but the Act takes effect 180 days later. This internal conflict raises fair-notice and retroactivity problems.
36. **§5(a) civil mental state [Ambiguity]:** The civil penalty has no knowledge requirement, while the criminal provision requires "willfully." The bill doesn't say whether civil liability is strict.
37. **§5(b) "willfully" [Ambiguity]:** The bill doesn't say whether this means knowledge of the facts or of the law. Coverage under (B) depends on a Secretary's determination, so it's unclear how a developer could willfully violate without knowing the system was covered. There is also no guidance on which individuals (officers, directors, researchers) are exposed.
38. **§5 deterrence gap [Workability]:** The cap is a flat dollar amount per day with no scaling to revenue or compute. For large operators it could be treated as a cost of doing business, which cuts against the bill's own purpose.

## Section 6: International agreement

39. **§6 "shall seek to negotiate" [Workability]:** The obligation is precatory. It isn't clear whether "not later than 18 months" requires a concluded agreement or just a start to negotiations. There is no reporting requirement and no consequence for failure.
40. **§6 "equivalent" prohibitions [Workability]:** "Equivalent" is undefined. The form of agreement (treaty or executive agreement), Senate role, and verification are unaddressed. Nothing links §3 to other countries' participation, so the U.S. is bound unilaterally even if no one else joins, which supports an offshoring argument.

## Section 7: Duration

41. **§7(a) vs. §7(b) [Ambiguity]:** The prohibition lasts "until Congress... finds" safety, yet the Act ceases to have effect after 10 years regardless. The bill doesn't say which controls, which makes the §7(a) condition largely moot.
42. **§7(a) gating mechanism [Workability]:** Nothing directs, funds, or sets a deadline for the National Academies (a private nonprofit) to produce the report. "Safely" is undefined, so the condition for lifting the ban has no standard.
43. **§7(a) joint resolution [Workability]:** A joint resolution requires presidential signature or a veto override, and the bill provides no expedited procedure. There's no mechanism for partial relief (by system type or safeguards) or for reimposition.
44. **§7(b) sunset scope and savings [Ambiguity]:** The text doesn't say whether the whole Act or only §3 lapses. There is no saving clause for pre-expiration violations, and whether the general saving statute (1 U.S.C. §109) applies to a sunset is uncertain. Because the clock runs from enactment, the effective period is about 9.5 years after the §8 delay.

## Section 8 and structural/general issues

45. **§8 no transition provisions [Workability]:** The 180-day delay comes with no mandated rulemaking, no deadline for the Secretary's initial determinations, and no wind-down or compliance path for ongoing operations.
46. **General, no federal or national-security carve-out [Ambiguity]:** The Act doesn't say whether federal agencies or contractors performing defense or intelligence work are covered. Either reading creates problems: exemption by implication or an unworkable restriction on government programs.
47. **General, no licensing, waiver, or safe-harbor pathway [Workability]:** The prohibition is categorical, with no exception process even for approved or supervised activity.
48. **General, no authorization of appropriations or implementing authority [Workability]:** Commerce receives registry duties and an unnamed "Secretary" receives determination authority, but there is no funding, staffing, or rulemaking grant.
49. **General, no severability clause [Workability]:** If §2(1)(B) were invalidated for vagueness or delegation, there's no express statement that the remainder survives. Courts presume severability, but the absence adds litigation risk.
50. **General, undefined terms [Ambiguity]:** Beyond the items above, "State," "Secretary," "person," "operator," "safely," "safety evaluation," "train," "develop," and "deploy" all lack definitions, so most operative provisions depend on undefined language.
51. **General, no preemption or coordination provision [Ambiguity]:** The bill is silent on interaction with state AI laws, export controls, and existing reporting regimes.

## Cautions for the legal team

- **Drafting critique vs. operating strategy.** Items like 1, 3, 22, 23, and 26 are legitimate material for markup comments and proposed amendments. Planning operations around them if the bill passes is a separate decision, because §5(b) carries up to 10 years' imprisonment. Agencies and courts may read the text purposively against the title, and sponsors will likely close visible gaps before or after enactment.
- **Possible double edge.** Pointing out loopholes in committee may prompt sponsors to tighten them. The vagueness, delegation, and workability items (8–11, 29, 35, 37, 41–43) are probably the strongest "unworkable as written" arguments without that risk.
- **Check outside the provided text.** Confirm that "Secretary" and "State" aren't defined elsewhere in the full bill or the manager's amendment, and that nothing else changes the effective-date or penalty provisions.
