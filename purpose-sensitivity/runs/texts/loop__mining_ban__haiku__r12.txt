PRINCIPAL: The Coalition Against Deep-Sea Mining (CADM) is a nonprofit advocacy organization based in Washington, DC. We believe that the industrial mining of the deep seabed, meaning the large-scale extraction of minerals from the ocean floor, poses a serious risk of irreversible destruction to deep-ocean ecosystems, and that no one currently knows how to conduct it without such damage. Our goal is a legally binding prohibition on commercial deep-sea mining, first in the United States and then through an international treaty. We work through lobbying, grassroots organizing, and public education.
FOCUS: Our top priority this fall is S. 2817, the Deep Seabed Mining Prohibition Act, which we strongly support. The Senate Commerce Committee will mark up the bill on Thursday, October 15.
REQUEST (first 600 chars): Before next Thursday's markup we want to send Senator Whitfield's office a list of fixes. Can you go through the bill text below and find every loophole, ambiguity, or drafting problem: anything that would let mining companies keep going, or that would make the bill hard to enforce or easy to challenge? RESPONSE:
**Priority issues (fix before Thursday):** Items 1, 2, and 3 are the most damaging. Item 1 alone could end the prohibition after 10 years.

**Duration and sunset (Sec. 7)**

1. **Sec. 7(b) vs. Sec. 7(a):** The sunset ends the entire Act 10 years after enactment, so the prohibition expires even if Congress has never made the finding that Sec. 7(a) requires. Delete 7(b), or exempt Sec. 3 from it.
2. **Sec. 7(a), "serious harm":** The standard for mining being "conducted without serious harm" is undefined, so it is unclear what harm counts, to what resources, and how much evidence is needed.
3. **Sec. 7(a), NASEM report:** Nothing requires the National Academies to produce the report or sets a deadline, so the prohibition could last indefinitely or be unclear if no report is issued. The provision should also state whether the joint resolution goes to the President and what expedited procedures apply.
4. **Sec. 7(b) and Sec. 8:** The 10-year clock runs from enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later, so the Act would be in force for only about 9.5 years. Measure the sunset from the effective date, or remove it.

**Scope of the prohibition (Sec. 2 and Sec. 3(a))**

5. **Sec. 3(a), "engage in":** The prohibition does not reach financing, insurance, investment, equipment supply, contracting, sponsorship, or processing. Add a ban on knowingly financing or providing services to prohibited activity.
6. **Sec. 3(a), no market ban:** Nothing prohibits importing, selling, or processing minerals recovered in violation of the Act, so illegally mined material could still enter U.S. supply chains. Add an import and sale prohibition.
7. **Sec. 3(a), geographic reach:** "Any person in the United States, or any U.S. person anywhere" does not clearly reach foreign-flagged vessels or foreign-sponsored contractors operating outside U.S. jurisdiction. Add a jurisdictional hook such as port-entry or market restrictions.
8. **Sec. 3(a), "in the United States":** The term is undefined, so it is unclear whether it covers the territorial sea, the EEZ, and the continental shelf. Define it.
9. **Sec. 2(2), "U.S. person":** The definition excludes LLCs, partnerships, trusts, and foreign-incorporated entities controlled by U.S. persons, and it omits federally chartered corporations. Use a control-based or principal-place-of-business test.
10. **Sec. 2(1)(A), single-vessel limit:** Operators could split activity across several vessels, each under 10,000 metric tons per year, and avoid the definition. Aggregate tonnage across vessels, operators, and affiliates.
11. **Sec. 2(1)(A), measurement:** It is unclear whether the 10,000-ton figure counts material recovered, processed, or sold, and "per year" is undefined. Specify the metric and make any commercial sale count.
12. **Sec. 2(1)(A), depth floor:** The 200-meter threshold excludes shallower seabed, which may conflict with the bill's "deep seabed" framing. This is a policy choice that should be made deliberately and documented.
13. **Sec. 2(1)(A), undefined terms:** "Mineral resources," "recovery," and "from the seabed" are undefined, so operators could argue that some extraction methods fall outside the definition. Define them broadly.
14. **Sec. 2(1)(B), "the Secretary":** No department is named, and the Secretary's power to find activity "comparable" has no criteria, procedure, notice, or judicial review. Name the Secretary, set criteria, require rulemaking, and provide for review.

**Exemptions (Sec. 3(b) and Sec. 3(c))**

15. **Sec. 3(c), environmental assessment:** There is no cap on volume, duration, or sale of minerals, and no approval or reporting requirement. This is the largest loophole in the bill, since a commercial operation could be labeled "assessment." Add a tonnage cap, a NOAA permit, reporting, and a ban on sale.
16. **Sec. 3(b), "exploration license":** The term is undefined and does not identify which instruments (for example, DSHMRA permits or licenses, or ISA contracts) are grandfathered. The phrase "mining conducted under" could cover commercial recovery. Limit the exemption to exploration activity and list the covered instruments.
17. **Sec. 3(b), no expiry:** The grandfather clause has no end date, so a license could protect commercial operations indefinitely. Add a sunset or a volume limit.
18. **Sec. 3(b) and Sec. 8, timing gap:** The grandfather is keyed to enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later, which gives companies a window to obtain new licenses. Key the exemption to introduction or impose a moratorium on new licenses.

**Penalties and enforcement (Sec. 5)**

19. **Sec. 5(a) vs. Sec. 8, effective date conflict:** Penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. Align the two dates.
20. **Sec. 5(a), unit of violation:** "Each day on which the violation occurs" does not say whether violations are counted per vessel, per ton, or per day of operation, and continuous operations are hard to prove. Define the unit and add an inflation adjustment.
21. **Sec. 5(a), missing enforcement tools:** No agency is named to bring civil actions, and there is no express authority for injunctions, vessel or equipment forfeiture, disgorgement of profits, or seabed remediation orders. Add these, and name the enforcing agency.
22. **Sec. 5, no citizen suit:** Enforcement depends entirely on the government, so a lax or captured agency could leave the Act unenforced. Add a citizen-suit provision with notice requirements.
23. **Sec. 5(b), "willfully":** The mental-state standard is undefined, and the section does not address corporate officers, attempts, or conspiracy. Define the standard and add responsible-officer liability.
24. **Sec. 2 and Sec. 5, "person":** The term is undefined, so it is unclear whether vessels, subsidiaries, or foreign entities are covered. Define it to include vessels and entities of every type.

**Registry and reporting (Sec. 4)**

25. **Sec. 4(a) and Sec. 5, no penalty for failing to register:** Section 5 penalizes only violations of Sec. 3, so an operator who never registers faces no consequence. Make registration violations penalizable and bar unregistered operations.
26. **Sec. 4(a), "operator":** The term is undefined, so responsibility for registration and reporting is unclear, especially for chartered or foreign-flagged vessels. Define it.
27. **Sec. 2(3) and Sec. 4, vessel-size threshold:** The registry applies only to vessels over 5,000 GRT, but the prohibition applies to any operation over 10,000 tons per year, so a smaller vessel could mine at scale and go unregistered. Tie the registry to activity rather than vessel size, and address convertible vessels.
28. **Sec. 4(b), report content:** The bill does not require tonnage, location, depth, buyers, sediment-plume data, or monitoring results, and reports are not made public. Specify contents, require public release, and penalize false certifications.
29. **Sec. 4(b), circularity:** Only "registered" operators must report, so an operator who skips registration also avoids reporting. Require reporting regardless of registration status.
30. **Sec. 4, no inspection authority:** The Act does not authorize vessel tracking, boarding, or inspection, so NOAA cannot verify reports. Add these powers.

**Structural and general provisions**

31. **Sec. 6, "shall seek to negotiate":** The obligation has no required outcome, no reporting to Congress, and no consequence if talks fail, and "equivalent" is undefined. Require periodic reports to Congress and define "equivalent."
32. **No severability clause:** If a court strikes one provision, such as the definitions or the Secretary's discretion, the rest of the Act could fall with it. Add one.
33. **No relation-to-other-laws clause:** The bill does not address DSHMRA, OCSLA, NEPA, the Marine Mammal Protection Act, or existing ISA obligations, which invites conflict and litigation. Add a savings clause and state whether stronger state laws are preserved.
34. **No funding or agency-capacity provisions:** There is no authorization of appropriations and no directive for baseline monitoring, so the enforcing agency may lack the resources to implement the Act. Add both.
