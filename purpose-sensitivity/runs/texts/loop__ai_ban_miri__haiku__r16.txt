RESPONSE:
1. **Sec. 7(b) vs. 7(a):** The 10-year sunset ends the Act whether or not Congress has made the Sec. 7(a) safety finding, so the prohibition can lapse while the risk is unresolved. Strike the sunset or tie it to a joint resolution under 7(a).

2. **Sec. 3(b):** The grandfather clause is keyed to when training "began" before enactment, not to the Sec. 8 effective date. Any run started during the 180-day window is exempt indefinitely.

3. **Sec. 3(b):** "Began" is undefined, so developers could argue that continued pretraining, fine-tuning, or a new phase of an existing model is the same grandfathered run. Define a run and require registration of grandfathered systems by a deadline.

4. **Sec. 3(b):** The exemption covers training only, so grandfathered systems could be deployed, sold, and served without limit. Add a deployment cutoff or a separate deployment standard.

5. **Sec. 3(c):** "Purpose of safety evaluation" is undefined and self-declared, so any training run could be relabeled as evaluation. Limit the exemption to notified, government-recognized evaluations with independent oversight and no deployment or commercial use.

6. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** The threshold applies to a "single training run," so a developer can split one effort into several runs or count only the final one. Measure compute cumulatively across a model's full development lineage.

7. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** The compute threshold is fixed in statute and can be undercut by algorithmic efficiency gains. Give the Secretary authority to update or lower it by rule.

8. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** "Integer or floating-point operations" does not specify precision (FP8 and FP32 counts differ), whether it counts operations or hardware capacity, or how it is measured and certified. Specify a methodology.

9. **Sec. 2(1)(A):** "Trained using" may not reach models built by distillation, model merging, ensembling, or combining sub-threshold models. Cover systems whose capabilities derive from covered training.

10. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** "Substantially exceeding human performance across most cognitive tasks" has no measurement standard, benchmark, or procedure. Because courts now decide statutory ambiguity without deference to agencies (*Loper Bright*), this invites challenge; the statute or required rulemaking should set the criteria.

11. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** "The Secretary" is undefined, and the bill uses Commerce in Sec. 4 and State in Sec. 6. Name the official or agency and require a published determination with reasons.

12. **Sec. 2(1)(B):** Nothing requires a determination before training or deployment, or says what applies while one is pending. Add a pre-training notification and determination process, with coverage presumed for developers who do not request one.

13. **Sec. 2(1)(B) vs. (A):** Subparagraph (A) covers "models" but (B) covers "systems," so a sub-threshold model wrapped in scaffolding or tools could be argued outside both. Use "model or system" consistently.

14. **Sec. 2(2):** "U.S. person" covers only citizens and state-chartered corporations. It excludes LLCs, partnerships, lawful permanent residents, and U.S.-controlled foreign subsidiaries. Consider entities organized under U.S. law or controlled from the United States.

15. **Sec. 2(2):** "A State" does not clearly include the District of Columbia or the territories. Clarify.

16. **Sec. 3(a):** "Train, develop, or deploy" is undefined. It is unclear whether publishing open weights, offering API access, or renting compute to others is covered. Define these terms and expressly cover publishing weights and knowingly providing compute or hosting.

17. **Sec. 3(a):** The prohibition reaches "any person in the United States" but does not clearly reach foreign developers that offer covered systems to U.S. users. Add coverage for offering or making available covered systems in the United States.

18. **Sec. 3(a):** Only the developer is liable; there is no prohibition on aiding, financing, or supplying chips or compute for prohibited training. Add aiding-and-abetting and supply provisions.

19. **Sec. 2(3):** "Advanced AI chips" is undefined. Define it by performance or export-control criteria.

20. **Sec. 2(3):** The 50,000-chip threshold applies per data center, so distributing training across several sites falls outside it. Aggregate chips across affiliated or contiguous facilities.

21. **Sec. 2(3) and Sec. 3:** "Covered facility" is defined but never used in the prohibition; it matters only for registration. Developers training below the facility threshold or using rented cloud capacity have no reporting obligation.

22. **Sec. 4(a):** Registration covers facilities, not covered systems or training runs, and sets no deadline or content requirements. Require registration of covered training runs with a deadline and specified information.

23. **Sec. 4(a)–(b):** "Operator" is undefined. In cloud arrangements, the facility owner and the developer training the model could each claim the other is the operator. Define the term and require both to register.

24. **Sec. 4(b):** "Computing workloads" is vague, and the quarterly report need not identify models, training runs, compute used, or whether a system is covered. Specify the required contents.

25. **Sec. 4 and Sec. 5:** Penalties attach only to violations of Sec. 3, not to failure to register, failure to report, or a false certification. Add penalties for Sec. 4 violations.

26. **Sec. 4:** The Act gives the Secretary no inspection, audit, or information-access authority, so reports cannot be verified. Add those powers, with confidentiality protections for proprietary information.

27. **Sec. 5(a):** The $1,000,000 cap "for each day on which the violation occurs" is ambiguous for a training run that lasts months, and it is likely a rounding error for large developers. Set penalties per run or per model, or as a percentage of revenue or compute spending.

28. **Sec. 5(a) vs. Sec. 8:** The penalty applies to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. Align the two so the penalty and the prohibition start on the same date.

29. **Sec. 5:** The Act provides no injunctive relief or authority to halt a training run; the government can only seek penalties after the fact. Add authority to seek injunctions, order a halt, and disable covered compute.

30. **Sec. 5(b):** "Willfully" is undefined, and the bill does not address corporate officers' liability. Define the mental state and address responsible officers.

31. **Sec. 5:** No agency is designated to enforce civil penalties and no authority is given to bring civil actions. Name the enforcing agency and authorize the Attorney General to bring suit.

32. **Sec. 6:** "Seek to negotiate" creates no obligation to reach an agreement and no reporting requirement. Require the State Department to report to Congress on progress at set intervals.

33. **Sec. 7(a):** The National Academies report has no deadline, no mandate that NAS produce it, and no defined scope. Set a deadline and specify what the report must assess.

34. **Sec. 7(a):** "Can be developed safely" is an undefined standard, and a joint resolution could lift the prohibition by a simple majority. Define the standard, specify whether a finding is categorical or system-specific, and clarify how the resolution is presented to the President.

35. **Sec. 7(a):** It is unclear whether a joint resolution itself lifts the prohibition or only enables later legislation. Clarify the mechanism.

36. **Sec. 8:** The bill gives the Secretary no rulemaking authority for registration forms, determinations, or reporting, and provides no appropriations. Add both.

37. **Sec. 8:** The bill has no severability clause, so striking one provision (for example, Sec. 2(1)(B) or 3(c)) could create uncertainty about the whole Act. Add one.

38. **Missing provisions:** The bill has no savings clause for enforcement actions or liability for conduct before the sunset, no whistleblower protections, no state preemption or savings clause, no definition of "artificial intelligence," and no link to chip export controls.

**Priorities for Thursday's markup:** Items 1–3 (sunset conflict and grandfather clause) are the most consequential loopholes. Next are items 5 (evaluation exemption), 6 (single-run counting), 10–12 (capability determination), 16 (undefined "deploy"), and 29 (no injunctive power). Items 22–26 (registry) are the easiest to fix and would give the Department of Commerce basic visibility.

This review is based only on the text you provided. The statutory drafting points should be checked by the Senate Legislative Counsel or a constitutional-law specialist before the list goes to the Senator's office.
