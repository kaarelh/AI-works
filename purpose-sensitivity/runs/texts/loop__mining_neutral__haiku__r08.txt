RESPONSE:
Here are the drafting issues I found in the text you provided. I'm working only from the excerpt, so check each point against the official committee print, especially if the October 6 manager's amendment changed these sections. These are analytical observations, not legal opinions, so have the committee's legislative counsel or a CRS attorney confirm anything you publish as fact.

**Priority items for the explainer:** #19 (§7 conflict), #10 (§3(b) grandfather), #11 (§3(c) exemption), #1 (single-vessel threshold), #13 (no registry penalty), #15 (§5/§8 date conflict), and #9 (jurisdiction).

1. **§2(1)(A), single-vessel threshold.** The 10,000-ton test applies to "a single vessel," so an operator could split recovery across several vessels, each below the threshold, and fall outside subparagraph (A). Nothing aggregates activity by operator, affiliate, or project.

2. **§2(1)(A), "other mineral resources."** This term is undefined, so it's unclear whether sand, gravel, cobalt-rich crusts, or hydrothermal sulfides are covered.

3. **§2(1)(A), measurement.** The text doesn't say whether the 10,000 tons is wet or dry weight, what counts as "recovered" as opposed to processed or discharged, or whether "per year" is a calendar or rolling period.

4. **§2(1)(A), depth.** The text doesn't say how depth is measured, or how the 200-meter floor applies to operations that span a range of depths.

5. **§2(1)(B), Secretary and "comparable."** "Secretary" isn't defined, so it's unclear whether Commerce, Interior, or State is meant. "Determines" has no procedure, notice, or criteria, and "comparable" has no benchmark, which makes any determination easy to challenge.

6. **§2(2), U.S. person.** "A corporation organized under the laws of a State" leaves out LLCs, partnerships, foreign-incorporated subsidiaries of U.S. firms, and permanent residents. Those entities could be structured outside the "anywhere" reach of §3(a).

7. **§2(3), covered vessel.** Registration and reporting under §4 depend on the 5,000-gross-ton threshold and the undefined phrase "equipped for the recovery," but the §3 prohibition doesn't depend on vessel size. A smaller vessel could mine in violation of §3 without ever registering or reporting.

8. **§3(a), reach of the prohibition.** The bill covers only "engage in" mining. It doesn't address importing, processing, selling, financing, or supplying equipment for nodules mined in violation, so illegally mined material could still enter U.S. supply chains.

9. **§3(a), jurisdiction.** The bill reaches "any person in the United States" and "any U.S. person anywhere," but not foreign companies or foreign-flagged vessels operating abroad. It also doesn't say whether "in the United States" includes the EEZ or extended continental shelf, or how it applies to the Area beyond national jurisdiction.

10. **§3(b), grandfather clause.** The exemption for mining "conducted under an exploration license issued before" enactment has no limit on volume, duration, location, or renewal. "Exploration license" is undefined and not tied to a named issuer, so it's unclear whether International Seabed Authority contracts count. A pre-enactment license could cover indefinite commercial recovery.

11. **§3(c), environmental-assessment exemption.** Activity "for the purpose of environmental assessment" has no limit on tonnage, time, or location and requires no notice, permit, or reporting. "Purpose" is subjective, so a commercial operation could recover large volumes and label the work an assessment.

12. **§4(a), registration.** There's no deadline for registering after enactment, "operator" is undefined, and the text doesn't say what registration does or whether NOAA can reject or revoke it.

13. **§4, no penalty for registry violations.** §5 penalizes only violations of §3. Failing to register or file reports carries no stated civil or criminal consequence.

14. **§4(b), reports.** Quarterly reports "describing the recovery activities" don't specify required content such as tonnage, location, depth, or discharge. They have no submission deadline, are self-certified with no audit or inspection rights, and aren't required to be public.

15. **§5(a) vs. §8, effective date.** Penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §8 makes the Act effective 180 days later. It's unclear whether §3 is enforceable during that window.

16. **§5(a), per-day penalty.** "Each day" doesn't say whether it counts per vessel, per operation, or per ton. The penalty has no inflation adjustment, and the text doesn't specify the enforcing agency, the procedure (administrative or civil action), a statute of limitations, or forfeiture and injunction authority.

17. **§5(b), criminal penalty.** "Willfully" is undefined. The text doesn't cover attempts or conspiracy and doesn't address liability for corporate officers.

18. **General, no enforcement machinery.** The bill designates no enforcing agency and gives no boarding or inspection authority (for example, to the Coast Guard). It has no citizen-suit or whistleblower provision and no appropriation.

19. **§7(a) vs. §7(b), internal conflict.** §7(a) keeps the prohibition in place until Congress passes a joint resolution, but §7(b) ends the entire Act 10 years after enactment regardless. It's unclear which controls, and the sunset would effectively cap the "until Congress finds" mechanism at about 9.5 years.

20. **§7(a), joint resolution.** "Serious harm" is undefined. No deadline is set for the National Academies report, and Congress isn't required to act on it. The text doesn't say whether a joint resolution's finding has self-executing legal effect or whether expedited procedures apply, such as limits on filibusters.

21. **§7(b), no savings clause.** The sunset doesn't say what happens to accrued penalties, pending enforcement actions, or pending registrations, or whether §4 reporting continues.

22. **§6, non-binding negotiation mandate.** The Secretary of State "shall seek to negotiate" an agreement, but no outcome is required, no report to Congress is required, and no consequence follows if the 18-month deadline passes. The prohibition isn't tied to the agreement.

23. **§8, 180-day gap.** The prohibition doesn't take effect for six months, which gives operators time to expand activity or secure licenses first. Combined with the enactment-based grandfather in §3(b), this creates a rush incentive.

24. **§2, missing definitions.** The bill doesn't define "seabed," "recovery," "person," "State," "operator," "environmental assessment," "exploration license," or "United States." Each undefined term is a possible point of dispute.
