RESPONSE:
# Drafting Issues in S. 2817 (as amended October 6, 2026)

These are ordered by section. Each item identifies the problem and suggests a fix for the Senator's office. Before sending, have Senate Legislative Counsel or CRS review the proposed language, and confirm the text against the official amended version.

1. **§2(1)(A), "single training run":** Developers can split one model's training across several runs or stages (pretraining, continued pretraining, RL, fine-tuning) so that no single run crosses 10^26. **Fix:** Measure cumulative compute across the full training lineage of a model, including post-training and fine-tuning.

2. **§2(1)(A), "integer or floating-point operations":** The bill doesn't specify how operations are counted. Precision (FP8 vs. FP32) and sparsity can change totals by large factors. **Fix:** Require a precision-normalized counting method and direct the Secretary to issue measurement rules.

3. **§2(1)(A), fixed threshold:** Algorithmic efficiency gains mean systems with covered-level capabilities may be trained below 10^26. **Fix:** Authorize periodic review of the threshold and pair it with the capability prong.

4. **§2(1)(B), "substantially exceeding," "most," "cognitive tasks":** These terms are undefined and unmeasurable, and the bill sets no benchmark, procedure, or deadline for the Secretary's determination. This invites an arbitrary-and-capricious challenge under the APA. **Fix:** Tie the determination to a specified benchmark suite, require notice, public comment, and written reasoning.

5. **§2(1)(B) and throughout, "the Secretary":** The bill never defines which Secretary is meant. §4 points to Commerce and §6 to State, while §2(1)(B) is unclear. **Fix:** Define "Secretary" as the Secretary of Commerce, or name the responsible official in each section.

6. **§2(1)(A) vs. (B), "model" vs. "system":** Prong (A) covers models, but prong (B) covers systems. Developers could argue that agents, scaffolds, or multi-model products built on a covered model are outside the definition. **Fix:** Define "covered AI system" to include any system incorporating a covered model, and any model derived from one by fine-tuning or distillation.

7. **§2(2), "U.S. person":** Limiting this to citizens and state-law corporations excludes LLCs, partnerships, foreign-organized subsidiaries of U.S. firms, and U.S.-based permanent residents working abroad. **Fix:** Expand the definition to include any entity organized under U.S. federal or state law, or controlled by U.S. persons.

8. **§3(a), territorial reach:** It is unclear whether "in the United States" modifies "train, develop, or deploy." Foreign developers that train abroad and serve U.S. users remotely may fall outside the prohibition. **Fix:** Add an express clause covering any covered system made available to users in the United States, regardless of where training occurs.

9. **§3(a), undefined verbs:** "Train," "develop," and "deploy" are undefined. "Deploy" may not reach API access, open-weight releases, or internal use, and "develop" could be read to reach research or publication, which would invite First Amendment challenges. **Fix:** Define "deploy" to include third-party access, API access, and weight release. Define "train" to include fine-tuning and RL. Clarify the scope of "develop" and address the publication question directly.

10. **§3(a), no supply-side restriction:** The prohibition doesn't reach anyone who knowingly provides compute, cloud services, or weights to a prohibited developer. **Fix:** Add a prohibition on knowingly enabling prohibited activity, along with know-your-customer obligations for cloud and compute providers.

11. **§3(b), grandfather clause:** Any system whose training "began" before enactment is exempt, regardless of how long training continues, whether training is still ongoing, or whether the system is later deployed. **Fix:** Limit the exemption to systems completed and registered by a date certain. Require registration and bar deployment of grandfathered systems above a threshold. Define "training began" and treat new post-enactment training as new training.

12. **§3(b) and §8, the 180-day gap:** The grandfather cutoff is keyed to enactment, but the prohibition takes effect 180 days later. Developers could start very large runs during that window and then claim exemption or argue they were outside the prohibition. **Fix:** Bar new covered training from the date of enactment, or key the cutoff to introduction or a fixed date.

13. **§3(c), "for the purpose of safety evaluation":** This purpose-based exemption is undefined, unverifiable, and unlimited in scale, duration, or deployment. Any training run can be relabeled as evaluation. **Fix:** Narrow the exemption to government-authorized evaluation under a permitting process, with a compute cap, reporting to Commerce, no commercial or public deployment, and security requirements.

14. **§4(a), registry scope:** Registration is tied to covered facilities (more than 50,000 chips), not to covered training runs or systems. Developers who spread training across sub-threshold sites or rent compute can avoid registration entirely. **Fix:** Require registration of each covered training run and system regardless of facility size, and aggregate compute across affiliated operators.

15. **§2(3), "covered facility" and "advanced AI chips":** "Advanced AI chips" is undefined, and "containing" allows distributed training to escape the threshold. **Fix:** Define the chips by performance or memory, measure aggregate compute capacity rather than chip count, and aggregate across affiliated facilities.

16. **§4(b), "describing the computing workloads":** The bill doesn't specify what reports must contain, when they are due beyond "quarterly," or whether they are public. **Fix:** Specify required content, including run identifiers, compute used, model identity, and any safety-evaluation designation, along with deadlines and a public summary.

17. **§4 and §5, no penalty for registry violations:** Penalties in §5 attach only to §3 violations. Failing to register or filing a false certification carries no penalty. **Fix:** Add civil and criminal penalties for registry violations, require officer-level certification, and reference 18 U.S.C. §1001.

18. **§4, no verification authority:** Commerce has no express power to audit, inspect, subpoena, or verify reports. **Fix:** Add audit, inspection, subpoena, and telemetry-access authority, along with whistleblower protections.

19. **§5(a), inconsistent date:** Penalties apply to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §3 doesn't take effect until §8's effective date. **Fix:** Change the reference to the effective date and conform the two sections.

20. **§5(a), no enforcing authority:** The bill doesn't say who brings civil actions, in what court, or through what process. **Fix:** Designate the enforcing agency or the Attorney General, specify venue, and add an administrative penalty process with judicial review.

21. **§5(a), penalty size and unit:** A $1 million per-day penalty is small relative to the revenues of large AI firms, and "per day" is ambiguous for continuing violations. **Fix:** Add per-training-run or compute-based penalties, revenue-based penalties, and disgorgement, and define the unit of violation.

22. **§5, no injunctive or emergency authority:** Nothing authorizes an order to halt training, quarantine or seize weights, or require destruction. **Fix:** Add express injunction, seizure, and destruction authority with expedited procedures.

23. **§5(b), "willfully" undefined:** Developers could argue a good-faith belief that a run qualifies for the evaluation exemption or the grandfather clause. **Fix:** Define "willfully" to include reckless disregard, and limit good-faith reliance as a defense to cases where the developer was registered and had filed required reports.

24. **§5, no individual or secondary liability:** The bill doesn't reach officers who direct violations, or aiders and abettors. Entities could pay fines while responsible individuals avoid liability. **Fix:** Add responsible-officer liability and aiding-and-abetting or conspiracy liability.

25. **§7(a) vs. §7(b), conflicting duration:** §7(a) keeps the prohibition in effect until Congress acts, but §7(b) ends the Act after 10 years regardless. Absent congressional action, the prohibition expires. **Fix:** Remove the sunset, or make the prohibition indefinite and end it only through the joint-resolution process.

26. **§7(a), "can be developed safely" and the NASEM report:** The standard is undefined, and the bill sets no deadline for the NASEM report and no mechanism if it is never issued. NASEM is also a private nonprofit, not a federal agency. **Fix:** Require the study within a set period, fund it, define the criteria, and specify that the prohibition remains in force if no report is issued.

27. **§7(a), joint resolution mechanics:** A joint resolution is presented to the President and is subject to veto. The bill doesn't say so or provide expedited procedures. **Fix:** State the presentment and veto process explicitly, and consider expedited consideration provisions.

28. **§6, "seek to negotiate":** This is an effort requirement with no reporting, no consequence for failure, and an undefined "equivalent" standard. **Fix:** Require semiannual reports to Congress, define minimum elements, and coordinate with chip and weight export controls.

29. **Missing: export controls.** The bill doesn't restrict exporting compute, chips, or weights to foreign developers, so training could move offshore. **Fix:** Direct Commerce to issue corresponding export controls.

30. **Missing: severability, rulemaking, and funding.** There is no severability clause, so if a challenge strikes §2(1)(B), the whole Act may fall. The Secretary also lacks express rulemaking authority, and no enforcement funding is authorized. **Fix:** Add a severability clause, general rulemaking authority, and an appropriation for enforcement.

## Highest-priority fixes for the Senator's office

1. **§7(b) sunset** conflicts with §7(a) and could end the prohibition after 10 years.
2. **§3(b) grandfather clause** and the **§8 gap** could let large training runs proceed.
3. **§3(c) safety-evaluation exemption** is open-ended and unverifiable.
4. **§4 registry scope and penalties** (items 14, 17, 18) leave the registry unenforced and easy to avoid.
5. **§5 enforcement authority and injunctive relief** (items 20, 22) mean there is no clear way to stop violations.
6. **§2(1)(A) compute counting** (items 1, 2, 15) lets developers stay below the threshold.
