# Drafting Issues in S. 2817 (Oct. 6 Manager's Amendment Text)

1. **§2(1)(A), "by a single vessel":** An operator could split a project across several vessels, each below 10,000 metric tons per year, and fall outside the definition. The threshold should measure aggregate recovery by a person, its affiliates, and any joint project. The measurement period ("per year") also needs a definition.

2. **§2(1)(A), "polymetallic nodules or other mineral resources":** "Mineral resources" and "recovery" are undefined. This creates uncertainty about whether cobalt-rich crusts, seafloor massive sulfides, or sediment-based extraction are covered.

3. **§2(1)(A), tonnage basis:** The bill doesn't say whether tonnage is wet or dry, or whether it counts ore only or total material moved. Operators could argue that their measured tonnage falls below the threshold.

4. **§2(1), defined term "commercial":** The definition is based entirely on tonnage, so a commercial operation below 10,000 tons per year falls outside the prohibition despite its name. Consider adding a commercial-purpose trigger, such as sale, export, or commercial use of recovered minerals.

5. **§2(1)(A), depth and geography:** "Seabed" and "deep seabed" are undefined, and the bill doesn't say where the 200-meter depth is measured. It also doesn't state whether the prohibition covers U.S. waters, the continental shelf, or the Area beyond national jurisdiction.

6. **§2(1)(B), "Secretary" and catch-all standard:** "Secretary" is never identified, and the bill sets no process, deadline, notice requirement, or criteria for determining "comparable" risk. Because subparagraph (B) cross-references (A), it also inherits the single-vessel limit. The determination could be delayed or challenged as arbitrary.

7. **§2(2), "U.S. person":** The definition excludes LLCs, partnerships, trusts, and foreign-organized entities controlled by U.S. persons. "State" is also undefined, so it's unclear whether D.C. and the territories are included. Consider covering any entity organized under U.S. law, controlled by U.S. persons, or headquartered in the U.S.

8. **§2(3), "covered vessel":** "Equipped for" is undefined, and the vessel threshold is in gross tons while the mining threshold is in metric tons. An operation could use barges, platforms, or smaller vessels to fall outside both the definition and the registry.

9. **§3(a), "engage in":** The term is undefined and doesn't reach financing, insurance, equipment supply, vessel chartering, processing, or transport of minerals. The bill also has no ban on importing or selling seabed minerals mined abroad, which leaves a major gap in deterrence.

10. **§3(a), jurisdictional reach:** The prohibition covers "any person in the United States" and "any U.S. person anywhere," but foreign nationals mining beyond U.S. jurisdiction are not covered. The bill has no port-access, flag-state, or import measures to address this.

11. **§3(b), "exploration license":** The term is undefined. The provision allows mining "conducted under" an exploration license, which could permit commercial-scale recovery with no volume cap, duration limit, or good-standing requirement. This is the most likely route for existing projects to continue.

12. **§3(b), scope of "issued":** The grandfather clause covers only licenses "issued" before enactment. It's unclear whether renewals, extensions, transfers, or expanded scopes of existing licenses are covered, and it doesn't address licenses held through foreign subsidiaries or sponsoring-state arrangements.

13. **§3(b), incentive to rush:** A cutoff tied to enactment invites a rush to issue or expand licenses before the bill passes. Consider a fixed cutoff date, such as the date of introduction, and a bar on new or expanded licenses after that date.

14. **§3(c), "environmental assessment" exemption:** The term is undefined, and the exemption has no limit on tonnage, area, duration, or sales. It could cover full-scale commercial recovery relabeled as testing. Consider capping volume and area, requiring prior NOAA approval and public notice, and excluding any sale of recovered minerals from the exemption.

15. **§4(a), registration:** The bill sets no deadline and no content requirements for registration. It's also unclear whether vessels already operating must register.

16. **§4(b), quarterly reports:** "Recovery activities" is not defined, so reports have no required minimum content such as tonnage, location, depth, sediment-plume data, or biodiversity monitoring. The reports are self-certified, with no audit, inspection rights, public disclosure, or NOAA duty to review them. Consider a required data set, third-party monitoring, and public posting.

17. **§5, penalties don't cover §4 violations:** Penalties apply only to violations of §3. There is no penalty for failing to register, failing to file reports, or submitting a false certified report.

18. **§5(a), civil penalty mechanics:** "Each day on which the violation occurs" doesn't define a continuing violation. The bill also doesn't name an enforcing agency or set out administrative penalty procedures, injunctive relief, vessel or equipment seizure, forfeiture of minerals, inflation adjustment, or a citizen-suit provision. Consider a penalty of the greater of a fixed amount or the value of the minerals recovered.

19. **§5(b), criminal penalty:** "Willfully" is undefined. The provision also doesn't reach aiding, abetting, or conspiracy, or responsible corporate officers, and it has no forfeiture provision for proceeds or assets.

20. **§5(a) and §8, timing conflict:** §5(a) applies penalties to violations "on or after the date of enactment," but §8 delays the §3 prohibition until 180 days after enactment. It's therefore unclear whether mining during that window is unlawful. The bill should use one date for both.

21. **§6, international agreement:** "Shall seek to negotiate" creates no binding duty. The bill has no reporting requirement to Congress, no consequence if no agreement is reached within 18 months, and "equivalent to those in section 3" could be read to accept a weaker agreement. It also doesn't address trade measures against non-party mining.

22. **§7(a), exit standard:** "Serious harm" is undefined, and the bill doesn't require that the NASEM report be produced or set a deadline for it. The joint-resolution procedure is also unspecified (for example, whether it requires presidential signature or has expedited consideration). The standard should match the bill's own premise, such as "without irreversible harm," and be tied to a demonstrated scientific standard.

23. **§7(a) and §7(b), direct conflict:** §7(a) keeps the prohibition in place until a joint resolution is adopted, but §7(b) ends the entire Act after 10 years. It's unclear which controls, and the sunset could be read to end the prohibition regardless of any NASEM findings. Strike §7(b).

24. **Missing general provisions:** The bill has no severability clause, judicial review or citizen-suit clause, savings clause for other federal and state law, appropriations for enforcing agencies, rulemaking authority, or definition of "Secretary" or "Administrator." Counsel should also consider whether to address how the Act interacts with international seabed contracts, since the U.S. is not a party to UNCLOS.
