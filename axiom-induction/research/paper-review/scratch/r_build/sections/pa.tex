\section{The axioms we actually have: PA and ZF}\label{sec:pa}

Which axioms does the posterior pick up for arithmetic and set theory? Sources: track pa; track experiments for E2, E3(b), E6 and Props X9--X11; AS \citep{claude2026axiomschemas} is cited by its own numbering. Units are bits; proofs and full tables are in \cref{app:pa}. Three caveats hold throughout. (i) Track pa computes with the two-part code $\Lsch$ or with $\Lzero$ under a learned grammar, never with the generative $\Lone$ of \cref{sec:model}; its numbers are not $\Lone$ results. (ii) Candidate sets are hand-picked and finite. (iii) Derivation costs are those of explicit derivations, hence upper bounds. ``Proved; computed'' means a proof plus a derivation checked line by line by track pa's checker \texttt{nd.py} (\cref{app:pa:checker}).

\subsection{Setting}\label{sec:pa:setting}

\begin{definition}[The two-part derivation code $\Lsch$]\pabrk\src{pa Defs 0.2, 0.3}\label{def:pa:lsch}
Derivations are in a sound and complete natural-deduction calculus $\CND$ (\cref{tab:model:calculi}). An instance $f(\varphi)$ of a schema $f(P)$ is derived schematically in $P$ and costs $\mathrm{bits}(\pi)+L_\Qg(\varphi)$, the motive being coded once by the grammar. Per line, $\mathrm{bits}(\pi)$ charges $\log_210$ for the rule, $\log_2|T|$ per axiom citation, $\log_2(\text{line index})$ per premise reference and $\beta$ per \emph{written symbol}. The \emph{decodable} variant also codes the number of lines, the premise counts of tc lines and the variables of subst and $\exists$I lines. $\Lnaive$ writes every formula out at the instance. On a finite candidate set the prior is $\pi(T)\propto2^{-\beta\sum_{\tau\in T}|\tau|}$: $\beta=\log_223\approx4.52$ bits per symbol of \texttt{nd.size} (a quantifier and its variable count $2$) in the derivation computations, $\beta=5$ per symbol of the \texttt{dtlib} size (a de Bruijn $\forall$ counts $1$) in those built on AS's code u7. $\CL(T;D)$ is the total code length, $\ell_{\mathrm{cite}}$ the cost of a one-line citation.
\end{definition}

\noindent The decodable variant adds 4.8--7.3\% to derivation costs and is a prefix code, so Kraft holds (proved): $\Lsch$ is the two-part form of a sub-probability likelihood, of the $\Lsig$ kind, not of plain $\Lone$ (\cref{prop:time:codelength}). The grammar $\Qg$ is \emph{learned}: each metavariable body is coded at its read-off position by sequential KT in the context (sort, depth, parent, child index), over 9 formula symbols ($=,\neg,\wedge,\vee,\to,\forall,\exists,\top,\bot$) and 14 term symbols. Sequential KT is a Dirichlet($\frac12$) marginal \citep{krichevsky1981performance}, as are the weights, so $\CL$ differences in the $\Lzero$ computations are exact $\log_2$ posterior odds, except where templates overlap (\cref{rem:pa:overlap}).

\begin{definition}[Refutation rule $\paR d$]\pabrk\src{pa Def 0.4}\label{def:pa:refute}
With data $D$ and negative data $N$, $\paR d$ sets the likelihood of $T$ to $0$ if $T\vdash_{\le d}\neg s'$ for some $s'\in D$ or $T\vdash_{\le d}s$ for some $s\in N$ ($\Th_d$ in derivation size; under $\Lzero$, $d=0$ and ``derives'' means ``has as an instance''). $\paR\infty$ is not computable.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Equivalent axiomatisations: usage decides}\label{sec:pa:equiv}

Let $\Dlt:=\forall u\forall v\,(u<v\leftrightarrow\exists z\,(u+Sz=v))$. For a motive $\varphi$ in $x$ (parameters allowed), $\Ind(\varphi):=\varphi(0)\wedge\forall x(\varphi\to\varphi(Sx))\to\forall x\varphi$; $\CVI(\varphi):=\forall x(\theta_\varphi\to\varphi)\to\forall x\varphi$ with $\theta_\varphi:=\forall y(y<x\to\varphi(y))$; $\LNP(\varphi):=\exists x\varphi\to\exists x(\varphi\wedge\forall y(y<x\to\neg\varphi(y)))$. For nonempty $S\subseteq\{\Ind,\CVI,\LNP\}$ let $\PA_S:=\Q+\Dlt+\{f(P):f\in S\}$, $\PA_f:=\PA_{\{f\}}$, and $\PA_{\mathrm{all}}$ with all three.

\begin{proposition}[Three forms of induction]\status{proved; computed}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 1.1}\label{prop:pa:equiv}
With $B:=\{\mathrm{Q1},\dots,\mathrm{Q5},\Dlt\}$, for every motive $\varphi$: (A) $B\vdash\CVI(\varphi)\to\Ind(\varphi)$; (B) $B\vdash\Ind(\theta_\varphi)\to\CVI(\varphi)$; (C1) $\vdash\CVI(\neg\varphi)\to\LNP(\varphi)$; (C2) $\vdash\LNP(\neg\varphi)\to\CVI(\varphi)$. So $\PA_{\Ind}$, $\PA_{\CVI}$, $\PA_{\LNP}$ have the same theorems.
\end{proposition}

\noindent The derivations were checked schematically in $P$, replayed on 200 random motives, and re-checked by the referee's independent checker (\cref{app:pa:equiv}).

\begin{remark}[Patterns]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Rem 1.2}\label{rem:pa:pat}
$\CVI(P),\LNP(P)\in\PAT$, while $\TInd\in\DT\setminus\PAT$ ($P(0)$, $P(Sx)$). So $\PA$ is $\Q$, $\Dlt$ and one higher-order pattern (compare AS Prop 5.18: $\Q$ plus the first-order pattern $\IndEq$).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Usage.} Let the data be i.i.d.\ uses: a form $f$ with probability $p_f$ and a motive $\varphi\sim\Qg$, the datum being $f(\varphi)$. In $\PA_S$ a use costs $c_S(f)+L_\Qg(\varphi)$, $c_S(f)$ the cost of the cheapest derivation of $f(P)$ in a fixed library of checked derivations (a citation if $f\in S$; \cref{tab:pa:costs}).

\begin{proposition}[Usage limit]\status{proved, given the library constants}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 1.3}\label{prop:pa:usage}
$\frac1n(\CL(\PA_S;D_n)-\CL(\PA_{S'};D_n))\to\sum_fp_f(c_S(f)-c_{S'}(f))$ a.s.; the posterior concentrates on the $S$ minimising $\Ex_p[c_S(f)]$ if it is unique.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Lower bound]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 1.4}\label{prop:pa:lower}
Every $\Lsch$-derivation of $\CVI(P)$ from $\PA_{\Ind}$ costs at least $\log_210+11\beta\approx53$ bits more than citing it in $\PA_{\CVI}$; every derivation of $\Ind(P)$ from $\PA_{\CVI}$ at least $\log_210+9\beta\approx44$ bits more than citing it in $\PA_{\Ind}$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent The first version's argument (``at least two lines'') was wrong, since a citation also writes $P(x)$; the proof in \cref{app:pa:equiv} follows the premises back to a line that writes the form's skeleton or antecedent.

\begin{remark}[The usage-closed axiomatisation]\status{computed}\pabrk\src{pa \S1.2}\label{rem:pa:usage}
For $\Lsch$ with this library: (i) $\PA_{\Ind}$ beats $\PA_{\CVI}$ iff $p_{\CVI}/p_{\Ind}<0.600$, and $\PA_{\LNP}$ iff $p_{\LNP}/p_{\Ind}<0.757$ ($0.609$, $0.765$ under the decodable code; under $\Lnaive$ the first ratio is $0.671$--$1.052$, so the ratios are code-specific). (ii) $\PA_{\mathrm{all}}$ pays $81+86$ prior bits and $0.29$ bits per citation more than $\PA_{\Ind}$, so it is ahead after about one use of a non-primitive form (in expectation after 1--12 schema uses for the mixes tried). (iii) So the posterior concentrates on the \emph{usage-closed} axiomatisation: every form used at a rate above about $5\cdot10^{-4}$ per use is primitive. All candidates are $\PA$-equivalent, so the theorems are right whichever wins (compare \cref{prop:ident:sep}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Recursion conventions]\status{proved; computed}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 1.5}\label{prop:pa:recursion}
$\Q+\TInd$ and the theory with Q4, Q5 replaced by $0+x=x$, $Sx+y=S(x+y)$ prove each other's recursion laws with one induction each, so they are equivalent; the per-use overheads are 213.8 and 593.4 bits one way, 213.8 and 545.1 the other. The posterior adopts the convention the data use.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[ZF]\status{proved; computed; known; conjecture (reverse overheads)}\pabrk\src{pa \S1.4}\label{rem:pa:zf}
Separation from Replacement, Replacement from Collection and Foundation from $\in$-induction are checked, at 574--1432 bits per use against template priors of 72--185 bits (\cref{tab:pa:zf}). Collection from Replacement holds over $\ZF$, fails without Power Set \citep{zarach1996replacement,gitman2016zfc} (known) and is open to us without Foundation; $\EInd$ from $\Found$ needs transitive closures (known; \citealp{kaye2007interpretations}). The reverse overheads were not formalised (conjecture: much larger). So the textbook ZF axioms win exactly when they are used directly.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[E6: commutativity]\status{computed}\pabrk\src{experiments E6}\label{rem:pa:e6}
Among eight forms of commutativity (\cref{prop:exp:comm}), the chain $\Lone$ identifies the generating form by $n=32$ in every run, both among logically equivalent forms and among instance-equivalent but weaker ones; under $\Lselc$ five closed-guard forms tie, and the mass on provers of $\forall x\forall y\,x+y=y+x$ tends to their prior share $0.088$ (\cref{tab:exp:e6}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The MDL finding in PA}\label{sec:pa:mdl}

AS found that ``MDL tracks the statistics of usage, not the logical boundaries of schemas'' (AS \S6.10, Props E.16, E.17, Table 12); its codes PC and DPC are sequential KT, so its table already gives $\log_2$ posterior odds. Track pa re-ran AS's code u7 (reproducing AS Table 12 at $n=1000$) on $T^*:=\Q+\TInd$ against the root split $H_{\mathrm{root}}$ (7 templates $T_f$) and the split $H_{\mathrm{used}}$ into the roots $F$ that occur, adding the grammars SDPC (one learned positional grammar shared by all templates), RDPC ($T^*$'s grammar may condition on the motive's root) and CF (one nonterminal per sort), under usage laws G1 (u7's natural law, 7 roots), G2 and G3 (4 and 3 roots).

\begin{proposition}[Chain rule]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 2.1}\label{prop:pa:chain}
With fixed parameters, if $T^*$ has weight $w$ on $\TInd$ and body law $\Qg(\varphi)=\Qg(f)\Qg(\varphi\mid f)$, $f$ the root, then $H_F$ ($F\supseteq$ the roots in the support) with weights $w\Qg(f)$ on $T_f$ and body laws $\Qg(\cdot\mid f)$ has $P_{H_F}=P_{T^*}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Occam identity]\status{(a), (c) proved; (b) proved under its growth condition; computed}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 2.3}\label{prop:pa:occam}
Use $\Lzero$ with a Dirichlet($\frac12$) citation index and a shared learned positional grammar (KT per context $c$, alphabet $A_c$); $\KT(k;A)$ is $-\log_2$ of the Dirichlet($\frac12$) marginal of counts $k$ over $A$ symbols. Suppose every datum is covered by exactly one template of $H$ and one of $H'$, and, for a set $C$ of contexts, $H'$ codes datum by datum exactly the (context, symbol) pairs that $H$ codes outside $C$.
(a) $\CL(H')-\CL(H)=\Delta\mathrm{prior}+\KT(n';|H'|)-\KT(n;|H|)-\sum_{c\in C}\KT(m_c;|A_c|)$ ($n,n'$ citation counts, $m_c$ symbol counts in $c$).
(b) If all nonzero counts grow linearly, $\KT(k;A)=N\hat H(k)+\frac{A-1}2\log_2N+O(1)$, $N=\sum k$.
(c) If $C=\{c\}$, the data visiting $c$ are those of one template of $H$, and $H'$'s template and the pair ($H$'s template, symbol in $c$) determine each other, then $\CL(H')-\CL(H)=\Delta\mathrm{prior}+\frac{(|H'|-|H|)-(|A_c|-1)}2\log_2n+O(1)$; for a split of $\TInd$ into roots $F$ under SDPC, $\Delta\mathrm{prior}+\frac{|F|-9}2\log_2n+O(1)$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent The identity equals the computed SDPC code length to $0.1$ bit at every $n$ and law (slopes $-1$, $-2.5$, $-3$ bits per doubling for $|F|=7,4,3$). It reconciles the signs of the Occam drift: with a fixed $\Qg$ only the split pays Occam terms (\cref{prop:ident:splitlzero}; E2: about 4 bits per doubling); with a learned grammar the schema also pays $\frac12\log_2n$ per formula symbol never used at the root.

\begin{remark}[Refuted: a constant margin]\status{refuted}\pabrk\src{pa \S2.2; referee m2}
The first version called the SDPC margin of $H_{\mathrm{root}}$ constant ($+770\to+762$ bits for $n=10^3\to2.56\cdot10^5$). It falls by 1 bit per doubling (\cref{prop:pa:occam}(c), $|F|=7$); extrapolated crossover $n\approx2^{780}$.
\end{remark}

\noindent On G1 at $n=256000$ the split leads by $274656$, $94089$, $163028$ and $17242$ bits under NAIVE, PC, DPC and CF, and trails by $762$ (SDPC) and $5986$ (RDPC) bits (\cref{tab:pa:mdl}, \cref{app:pa:mdl}). So the finding is about the grammar, not the likelihood family: a split pays off linearly whenever $\Qg$ ignores a feature that the split conditions on and that usage depends on; SDPC and RDPC hold it at the prior margin up to Occam terms. A derivation likelihood changes the consequences:

\begin{proposition}[Detours]\status{(a) proved; computed; (b) known}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 2.2}\label{prop:pa:detour}
(a) A split $H_F$ derives every induction instance if $F$ contains a non-atomic connective, through the wrappers $\neg\neg P$, $P\wedge P$, $P\vee P$, $(0=0)\to P$, $\forall zP$, $\exists zP$ (six checked derivations; under $\Lsch$ they cost 369.2--488.9 bits more than a citation). (b) If $F=\{=\}$, $\Q+H_F\subseteq\IOpen$, which does not prove the irrationality of $\sqrt2$ \citep{shepherdson1964nonstandard} (secondary sources only), while $\PA$ does.
\end{proposition}

\noindent So under $\Lone$ MDL's incompleteness for schemas (AS App.~E.7) disappears once a split has a non-atomic root; the usage effect and the unsoundness error (\cref{rem:pa:lumps}) remain (qualification (i) of \cref{rem:ident:mdl}).

\subsection{Theorem data: statements given without proof}\label{sec:pa:theorems}

H\"anni proposed that ``statements given without proof in our data set should be fairly easily derived from axioms''. Then the data are theorems. A theory $T$ can derive a datum $s$, at cost $D_T(s)$ (cheapest library derivation under $\Lsch$), or adopt it as a ground axiom.

\begin{proposition}[Memorisation threshold]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 3.1}\label{prop:pa:memo}
If $s$ occurs $r$ times and the other data have the same best derivations under $T$ and $T\cup\{s\}$, then $\CL(T\cup\{s\})-\CL(T)=\beta|s|+\Delta I-r(D_T(s)-\ell_{\mathrm{cite}})$, with $\Delta I$ the index cost change ($\log_2\frac{k+1}k$ per citation over $k$ axioms). Up to $\Delta I$, memorising at the first occurrence is cheaper iff $\beta<\beta^*(s):=(D_T(s)-\ell_{\mathrm{cite}})/|s|$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Lemmas, compressible data, theorem streams]\status{computed; known}\pabrk\src{pa \S\S3.3--3.5}\label{rem:pa:streams}
(i) Commutativity costs 1358.6 bits with its two lemmas derived in line and 504.7 with them adopted (prior 90.5): lemmas used often become axioms (compare \cref{prop:univ:quant}(c)). (ii) A schema compresses its direct instances about fourfold; for a non-primitive form, adopting $f(P)$ beats deriving and memorising after 1--2 uses. (iii) On streams of fresh induction uses (probability $u$) and library theorems, for $u=0$ the MAP is $\Q$ plus the library from the first datum, a finite subtheory of $\PA$ and so strictly weaker (known; \citealp{rylln1952axiomatizability}); for $u=0.1,0.5$ it is $\Q+\TInd$ plus the library, with $\PA$'s theorems.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Gibbs]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 3.2}\label{prop:pa:gibbs}
If $\sum_s2^{-\ell(s)}\le1$ (e.g.\ $\ell(s)=\beta|s|$) and $P$ has finite entropy, then $\Ex_P[-\log_2P]\le\Ex_P[\ell]$. So on data from $P_{T^*}$ the generator's code length for a new datum is in expectation at most the prior cost of memorising it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Well specified, the generator wins]\status{proved, given \cref{thm:ident:doob} and known facts; proof sketch (last part)}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 3.3}\label{prop:pa:wellspec}
Let the data be i.i.d.\ from $\PA$'s $\Lone$ generator in setting W (\cref{def:ident:W}), weights positive and parameters admissible ($\operatorname{supp}P_{\PA}=\Th(\PA)$, \cref{lem:model:lone}), in a countable class with $\pi(\PA)>0$. Then a.s.\ every $\Q\cup S$ ($S$ a finite set of $\PA$-theorems) has posterior $0$ eventually, and $\pi_n(\Cstar)\to1$. Theories $\PA\cup S$ (generic weights) decay polynomially, like spare slots.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[``Must prove'' with refutation]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 3.4}\label{prop:pa:must}
Under $\Sprove$ with bounded derivability and $\paR d$: (a) the posterior is the prior restricted to the score-1 theories; (b) $\Q+\TInd$ beats $\Q\cup\{s_1,\dots,s_m\}$ once $\beta\sum|s_i|>72$ bits (two library theorems suffice), if $d$ covers its derivations; (c) any unrefuted theory with smaller prior deriving all data within $d$ beats both, e.g.\ the bare formula metavariable $F_0$, or an inconsistent theory whose shortest refutation exceeds $d$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Answer.} A derivation-length likelihood finds $\Q+\TInd$ from theorem data (1) when the data come from its own derivation process (\cref{prop:pa:gibbs,prop:pa:wellspec}), which human theorems, selected for short statements and interest, do not; (2) when they contain direct uses of the schema; (3) when the axiom prior is much steeper than the proof code ($\beta\ge\beta^*$); (4) under the 0/1 constraint with refutation, at the price of the size principle. Otherwise $\Lsch$ memorises short theorems, and the MAP is strictly weaker than $\PA$. $\Leq$ (\cref{ex:ident:lonedq}) and the fragment $\{\mathrm{Q4},\mathrm{Q5}\}$ (\cref{prop:univ:eqfrag}) show the same pattern: a derivation likelihood favours the theory that makes the data cheap to state. Not computed: theorem data under plain $\Lone$ or the full sum. A time bound on computing the likelihood sums over fewer derivations and so pushes further towards memorisation (\cref{rem:time:links}).

\subsection{Data from \texorpdfstring{$\Th(\N)$}{Th(N)}, the I\texorpdfstring{$\Sigma_n$}{Sigma-n} chain and narrow practice}\label{sec:pa:thn}

\begin{proposition}[No consistent r.e.\ theory survives]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 4.1}\label{prop:pa:thn}
If $P_T(s)>0$ iff $T\vdash s$ (e.g.\ $\Lone$, weights positive, parameters admissible) and the data are i.i.d.\ from a law with full support on $\Th(\N)$, every consistent r.e.\ theory has posterior $0$ eventually, a.s.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Refutation]\status{proved; known}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 4.2}\label{prop:pa:refute}
Without a refutation rule inconsistent theories are never refuted; $\paR\infty$ refutes them at once but is not computable; $\paR{d_n}$ with $d_n\to\infty$ refutes each eventually; no computable rule does better uniformly, since consistency of r.e.\ theories is $\Pi_1$-complete.
\end{proposition}

\noindent Along $\PA$, $\PA+\Con(\PA)$, \dots\ the posterior drifts; a toy model with logarithmic drift is in \cref{rem:pa:tower}. In $\DT$ each step must add a ground sentence:

\begin{proposition}[Reflection is not a template]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 4.3}\label{prop:pa:refl}
Every $\DT$ template whose instances lie in $\Refl_T:=\{\Prv_T(\gn\varphi)\to\varphi\}$ (unary numerals) is ground. The proof is that of \cref{prop:time:notemplate} with $\Prv_T$ for $\Accf f$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Regret against a theory plus exceptions]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 4.4}\label{prop:pa:regret}
Under $\Leps$, for every $T$ and $D_n$, with $E$ the data $T$ neither derives nor refutes: $-\log_2P_{\mathrm{mix}}(D_n)\le-\log_2\pi(T)+\sum_{s\notin E}-\log_2((1-\varepsilon)P_T(s))+\sum_{s\in E}-\log_2(\varepsilon\mu_0(s))$. With 0/1 support the same holds for $T\cup E$, the exceptions memorised at $\beta|e|$ prior bits each.
\end{proposition}

\noindent Pointwise limits are trivial: under 0/1 support memorisation alone eventually decides every sentence correctly (\cref{rem:pa:pointwise}). So for $\Th(\N)$ what can be promised is regret against every theory plus its exceptions and depth-relative soundness, not identification. In Gold's model the chain $\ISigma1\subset\ISigma2\subset\cdots$ with union $\PA$ is a limit point.

\begin{proposition}[The $\ISigma n$ chain]\status{(a) proved, given known facts; (b) proof sketch; (c) refuted}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 4.6; referee M4, m7}\label{prop:pa:isigma}
(a) For i.i.d.\ theorems from $\PA$'s $\Lone$ generator (setting W, parameters admissible), each $\ISigma n=\Q+\sigma_n$ has posterior $0$ eventually and $\pi_n(\Cstar_{\PA})\to1$ a.s.\ in any countable class with $\pi(\PA)>0$; the waiting times are presumably astronomical (a heuristic). (b) Before that, $\sigma_n$ pays at each induction use for a Tarski biconditional that grows with the motive, so the schema wins linearly on induction-use data. (c) \emph{Refuted:} ``if the community uses only $\Sigma_n$ motives, every $\DT$ theory covering its practice contains $\PA$-strength induction, so the posterior lands on the $L_\infty$ side''.
\end{proposition}

\noindent (a) uses $\ISigma{n+1}\vdash\Con(\ISigma n)$ (known; \citealp{hajek1993metamathematics}, location not verified), G\"odel's second theorem and \cref{thm:ident:doob}. (c) fails because AS Thm 5.16 concerns a single covering template and the wrappers of \cref{prop:pa:detour} need a formula metavariable (AS Thm 5.16 does hold with parameters, contrary to a remark of the referee). What is true, using that equivalent motives give equivalent induction instances (\cref{lem:pa:motive}):

\begin{proposition}[Read-once induction templates]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 4.8}\label{prop:pa:readonce}
Let $\tau=\TInd[P:=\lambda x.C]$ with $C$ read-once: built by connectives and quantifiers from atoms $A(x,\bar z)=B(x,\bar z)$ ($A,B$ term metavariables) and $F(x,\bar z)$ ($F$ a formula metavariable), each metavariable occurring once. (a) If $C$ has a formula metavariable, $\mathrm{Q1}+\inst(\tau)\vdash\Ind(\psi)$ for every $\psi$. (b) Otherwise every instance has $C$'s formula skeleton, so $\Q+\inst(\tau)\subseteq\ISigma k$ for some $k$, and $\ISigma k\subsetneq\PA$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent The read-once condition is sufficient, not necessary (\cref{rem:pa:shift}); which other templates give full induction is open.

\begin{example}[Narrow practice ends on the weaker side]\status{computed}\pabrk\src{pa \S4.3 (1); referee r8}\label{ex:pa:narrow}
Practice: $\Q$ plus induction on motives $\exists z(s=t)$ and $\neg(s=t)$. $H_{\mathrm{narrow}}:=\Q+\Ind(\lambda x.\exists z\,A(x,z)=B(x,z))+\Ind(\lambda x.\neg C(x)=D(x))$ (term metavariables) covers it and lies in $\ISigma1\subsetneq\PA$. $\CL(H_{\mathrm{narrow}})-\CL(T^*)=+230.8,+212.5,+192.5,+174.6$ bits at $n=100,300,1000,3000$, matching \cref{prop:pa:occam}(a) to 0.1 bit; slope $\frac{9-8}2-3\cdot\frac{9-1}2=-11.5$ bits per doubling (three fixed formula contexts), crossover $n\approx2^{26.7}$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[G1 keeps $\PA$]\status{computed; proof sketch (slope)}\pabrk\src{pa \S4.3 (2)}\label{ex:pa:skel}
u7's law G1 has 157 motive skeletons, each equivalent to a $\Sigma_2$ formula, so a term-only skeleton theory covers G1 inside $\ISigma2$; coverage does not force $\PA$. But it is $42838.5$ bits behind at $n=3000$ (prior part $43040$), with asymptotic slope $\frac{164-8}2-19\cdot\frac{9-1}2=+2.0$ bits per doubling (19 contexts; Wilks's theorem, from memory).
\end{example}

\noindent So deductive strength beyond usage is set by an Occam balance: a term-only theory gains $\frac{|A_c|-1}2$ bits per doubling for each context it fixes and pays $\frac12$ per extra template. Narrow practice lands on the weaker side, broad practice on $\PA$'s; this is the Bayesian $L_\infty$-versus-$L_5$ choice (\cref{prop:ident:gold}).

\begin{conjecture}[Broad practice]\status{conjecture}\pabrk\src{pa Conj 4.10}\label{conj:pa:broad}
If the motives have unbounded depth and use every connective at every depth with positive probability, the mass on theories deriving all of $\PA$ tends to $1$. (Open because covering theories may use non-read-once templates.)
\end{conjecture}

\noindent Track experiments (fixed PCFG, $\Lzero$, causal pools; language with $<$ and nine motive roots) finds the narrow effect with atomic motives. Let $T_f$ be $\TInd$ with motive root $f$; for a connective root, $\Q+T_f$ has the same consequences as $\Q+\TInd$ (\cref{prop:pa:fragments}), and Q3 is redundant given $\TInd$ (\cref{prop:pa:redundant}).

\begin{proposition}[Atomic fragments are weaker]\status{proved, given two known facts}\pabrk\src{experiments Prop X10}\label{prop:pa:atomic}
$T^*:=\mathrm{Q1..Q7}+\TInd$ contains $\PA$ and proves $\sigma:=\neg\exists x\exists y(\neg y=0\wedge x\cdot x=(SS0\cdot y)\cdot y)$; $\Q+\{T_=,T_<\}\nvdash\sigma$ (known facts: $\PA\vdash\sigma$; Shepherdson's model, \citealp{shepherdson1964nonstandard}, secondary sources only).
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[E3(b)]\status{computed}\pabrk\src{experiments E3(b)}\label{rem:pa:e3b}
$\Lzero$ data from $T^*$ with misspecified motive laws (5 seeds, $n\le2048$). Three connective-rich laws keep the theorems (mass $0.998$--$1.000$ on $T^*$-equivalents at $n=2048$); under two of them the MAP leaves $T^*$ for $T^*+T_\exists$ or an observed-root split. Atomic motives: $T^*$ has mass $0.988$ at $n=64$ in 4 of 5 seeds, then $\Q+\{T_=,T_<\}$ has mass $1.000$ in 5 of 5 seeds from $n=1024$ ($T^*$-equivalents: $2\cdot10^{-178}$ at $n=2048$): the posterior moves to a strictly weaker theory (\cref{tab:exp:e3b}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Unlabelled mixtures: a Bayesian DTRC}\label{sec:pa:dtrc}

Hypotheses: finite sets of $\DT$ templates, prior 5 bits per symbol, Dirichlet($\frac12$) weights, one shared positional grammar, negative data under $\paR0$. Data: G1 practice (Q1--Q7 as ground sentences, induction with probability $\frac12$), one seed; hand-picked candidates (\cref{tab:pa:dtrc}).

\begin{proposition}[Spare slot]\status{proved; computed}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 5.1}\label{prop:pa:spare}
A never-used template $\tau$ added to $K$ templates costs $\beta|\tau|+\frac12\log_2n+\log_2\frac{\Gamma(K/2)}{\Gamma((K+1)/2)}+o(1)$ bits (\cref{prop:ident:spare}(a)); $19.03$ and $19.82$ bits at $n=1000,3000$ for $K=8$, $\tau=F\to F$, against the computed $19.0$, $19.8$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Unsound lumps]\status{computed}\pabrk\src{pa \S5.4; experiments E2}\label{rem:pa:lumps}
At $n=10,30$ the unsound lump $F_0+\TInd$ leads by 174.7 and 42.0 bits ($F_0$ codes the rarely seen Q axioms through the grammar for less than seven ground templates cost), and $\Ver\delta0$ accepts the false $0=S0$ for every $\delta\ge3\cdot10^{-13}$; the negative $0=S0$ refutes the lump. This is AS's ``errs towards unsoundness for rarely used ground axioms'' (AS App.~E.7) in Bayesian form, and no contradiction with \cref{thm:sound:avg,thm:sound:shrink} ($\pi(\Q+\TInd)=2^{-330}$ here; u7's data are not from a model generator). One run in a hand-picked set; for the frequency see E2.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[E2]\status{computed}\pabrk\src{experiments E2}\label{rem:pa:e2}
$\Lzero$ citations from $T^*$ at fixed weights, 25 seeds, $n\le512$, causal pools. $T^*$ takes the mass once Q1, Q2, Q4--Q7 have each been cited (median datum 55); before that the sound but weaker $\SeenQ(D_n)$ leads, a competitor pa did not score (data laws and candidate sets differ). Mean mass of $T^*$: $0.751$ at $n=64$, $0.996$ at $512$. A $\delta=0.05$ verifier accepts a false probe in 5 of 25 seeds, at $n\le16$ (\cref{rem:sound:lumps}). The complete root split stays about 700 bits behind; the false spare $T^*+\{0=S0\}$ keeps $0.004$ at $n=512$ (\cref{tab:exp:e2}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[$\forall x\varphi$ as a sound merge]\status{computed; identity proved}\pabrk\src{pa \S5.5; referee M5}\label{rem:pa:merge}
On data $t+0=t$ ($t$ random closed terms) the schema $z+0=z$ wins (AS Prop 6.13). The sound split by the head of $t$ covers every closed instance but does not entail $\forall x(x+0=x)$; it closes in at 5 bits per doubling (\cref{prop:pa:occam}(c), $\frac{3-13}2$), crossover near $n=10^8$: a Bayesian $\omega$-gap (\cref{thm:univ:omega}), slower than in \cref{prop:univ:open} because the parameter probability is learned. $\forall x(x+0=x)$ itself loses at a rate set by the rule code (\cref{tab:pa:rulecode}): $\log_210$ bits per datum under the fixed code (the first version's ``$2^{-3.3n}$, as H4 predicts'' holds only for it), $\frac{R-1}2\log_2n+O(1)$ under a learned depth-indexed code, $h(f)$ bits per datum in a practice with a fraction $f$ of $\varphi$-instances ($h$ the binary entropy). The direction holds under every code (\cref{thm:univ:B}; universal's learned shared $c$: \cref{prop:univ:robust}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[``Does not contradict'' learns nothing]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 5.2}\label{prop:pa:nc}
If the likelihood is $\mu_0(s)[T\nvdash\neg s]$ with $\mu_0$ independent of $T$, the posterior is the prior restricted to compatible theories.
\end{proposition}

\noindent $\Leps$ is the minimal model that credits derivations without killing gaps, as H\"anni asks (\cref{prop:model:whichsize}(b)).

\begin{proposition}[Inconsistency is invisible to the size principle]\status{proved}\pabrk\src{pa Prop 5.3}\label{prop:pa:incons}
Under $\Lmax$ with citation weights, adding $\sigma$ with fixed weight fraction $w$ gives $P_{T\cup\{\sigma\}}(s)\ge(1-w)^{c(s)}P_T(s)$, $c(s)$ the citations in $s$'s best $T$-derivation; with Dirichlet weights an unused $\sigma$ costs its prior plus about $\frac12\log_2n$ bits. Consistency must be enforced by $\paR d$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[Euler's polynomial (F4)]\status{computed; proof sketch (covering, eventual behaviour)}\pabrk\src{pa \S6 F4; referee m9}\label{ex:pa:euler}
Data: $\mathrm{Prime}(k\cdot k+k+41)$, $k$ geometric($0.9$) conditioned on primality (false $k$: $40,41,44,\dots$). \subsubsection{The overall answer}\label{sec:pa:answer}

The inducer does \emph{not} robustly pick up the textbook axioms; it picks up the axioms that are \emph{used}. Within the hand-picked candidate sets, and for data that are direct uses of axioms, it puts nearly all mass on theories deductively equivalent to $\PA$; in three of the four cases this is automatic, since every candidate is $\PA$-equivalent (usage mixes, recursion conventions, root splits), and in the \DTRC{} run $\PA$-equivalents get mass $\ge1-10^{-96}$ from $n=100$. It fails robustly on theorem data, narrow practice, absent counterexamples, small $n$ and inconsistency (\cref{tab:pa:failures}). Not scored: all finite template unions, non-read-once templates, stronger true theories such as $\PA+\Con(\PA)$ (spare slots), further ZF candidates. The first version's ``it robustly gets a theory deductively equivalent to $\PA$'' is refuted as stated (referee M2--M4).
