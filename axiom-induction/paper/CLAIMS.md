# Answers to the question

Hänni asked whether there is a good Solomonoff-style axiom inducer (template prior, derivation-length likelihood, time penalty), what it does for ∀xφ from instances φ(t), and whether it robustly picks up the actual axioms, a deductively equivalent system, or at least puts much posterior mass on them. The intro and discussion are written from these bullets; each cites the ledger ids behind it.

* **A1. A good version exists, in a precise and limited sense.** Theories are finite sets of DT° templates with a proper prefix-code prior and Dirichlet weights; the likelihood is a generative derivation grammar whose normaliser does not depend on the data (`def:model:prior`, `lem:model:kraft`, `def:model:grammar`, `lem:model:grammar`, `def:model:lone`, `lem:model:lone`). Normalisation gives the size principle (`lem:model:size`, `prop:model:whichsize`(a)); Hänni's 0/1 variants lack it (`prop:model:whichsize`(b), `prop:pa:nc`), and his graded score, normalised, is a two-part code that charges written derivation length (`rem:model:graded`). The likelihood is a computable real but whether it is 0 is undecidable (`prop:model:compute`), and the two-part shortcut can change posterior ties (`prop:model:max`). For ∀xφ the version should also model which data get reported (`\Lsel` or a prior over filters) and include noise (`thm:univ:omega`(e), `prop:univ:robust`, `rem:univ:B2cit`).

* **A2. ∀xφ from closed instances: the data never favour ∀xφ over its instance schema.** Mass leaves over-general templates exponentially (`thm:univ:size`), the memoriser class only quasi-polynomially (`prop:univ:memo`), and over-specific templates gain until an anchor appears (`prop:univ:overspec`). Between $\Hall$ and $\Hsch$ the odds stay at the prior odds (citation with closure, selection-aware likelihood, Hänni's scores) or fall by a factor c per datum (derivation likelihood in $\Cmin$) (`thm:univ:odds`, `thm:univ:B`). This survives noise, any penalty monotone in derivation length or time, and a learned c (`prop:univ:robust`); the exceptions are non-logical and bounded (`prop:univ:B2`) or calculus-specific (`rem:univ:detour`). Experiments reproduce the factor exactly (`tab:exp:e1`).

* **A3. "All future data are instances" is confirmed; "the axioms prove ∀xφ" is not — a Bayesian ω-gap.** The first gets posterior probability → 1 (`thm:univ:confirm`). The second tends to a prior share strictly between 0 and 1 (0.03–0.48 depending on the code), to 0, or to a class-dependent limit under misspecification (`thm:univ:omega`, `tab:univ:share`, `prop:univ:noguard`, `prop:univ:sentences`). The same gap appears for an axiom we actually have (`prop:univ:q4`) and under citation likelihoods on the axiom's own theorems (`ex:ident:weaker`). What licenses ∀xφ: one instance at a fresh parameter (`prop:univ:open`(a)); quantified data (`prop:univ:quant`); a selection-aware likelihood without a guard (`prop:univ:noguard`(c)); truth (not derivability) under a Gaifman-type credence, safely in ℕ (`prop:univ:gaifman`). Without a guard and with unmodelled selection the step is not taken (`rem:univ:openrefuted`).

* **A4. Well specified, the posterior identifies the generator: the theorem set, not the axioms.** It concentrates on the generator class and is the prior inside it (`thm:ident:doob`, `cor:ident:prior`); deductive beliefs converge to the class's vote, which is the true theorem set under $\Lone$ with parameters admissible and the instance union under $\Lzero$ (`cor:ident:deductive`, `thm:ident:limit`), with no bound on the number of axioms and around Gold's theorem except on a null set of texts (`prop:ident:gold`). So a deductively equivalent system is found; the actual axioms get asymptotic mass π(T*)/π(C*), never forced to 1. Under $\Lone$ deductively equivalent axiomatisations generally have different laws, so the posterior picks the one that generated the data (`prop:ident:sep`, `rem:pa:e6`) — in human terms, the usage-closed one (`prop:pa:usage`, `rem:pa:usage`). Splits of a schema tie at matched weights and lose only at Occam rates with learned weights; as far as proved a derivation likelihood does not change the MDL finding (`prop:ident:splits`, `prop:ident:splitlzero`, `prop:ident:splitlone`, `rem:ident:mdl`, `prop:pa:occam`). Spare slots are never refuted, only attrited polynomially (`prop:ident:spare`).

* **A5. Robustly picking up the actual axioms: no.** Human data are not drawn from the model. The posterior then goes to the best-fitting generator, which can be strictly weaker (`ex:ident:weaker`, `rem:ident:e3a`, `rem:pa:e3b` with `prop:pa:atomic`, `ex:pa:narrow`, `rem:pa:streams`) or unsound (`ex:ident:escape`, `ex:ident:lonedq`, `rem:ident:fullclass`, `ex:pa:euler`, `rem:pa:lumps`) (`tab:ident:misspec`). A lot of posterior mass on PA-equivalents is obtained for axiom-instance data within the candidate sets and pools scored (`tab:pa:dtrc`, `rem:pa:e2`, `sec:pa:answer`), not over the full class.

* **A6. The thresholded verifier is sound only under well-specification, and with Dirichlet weights only on average or with a shrinking threshold.** Fixed weights: no adaptive prover ever gets a non-theorem accepted, with probability ≥ 1 − δ/π(C*_d), uniformly in time (`thm:sound:fixed`, `rem:sound:tight`). Dirichlet weights: averaged over the weight prior (`thm:sound:avg`), or at each weight vector with δ_n ≍ n^{−(K−1)/2} (`thm:sound:shrink`); a constant threshold fails (`ex:sound:constant`). Misspecified: a waiting prover succeeds with probability 1 (`prop:sound:misspec`; `rem:sound:e4`). The δ → 0 limit is the cautious verifier read on theorems (`prop:sound:cautious`). Hänni's trichotomy is a belief/plausibility pair; both point estimates he considers are incoherent in general (`prop:sound:belief`, `prop:sound:bracket`, `ex:sound:renorm`, `prop:sound:fiftyfifty`).

* **A7. A time penalty on checking or generating axioms does not stop Hänni's collapse; derivation size in written symbols does price it.** His equivalence holds within a constant via Craig (`thm:time:equiv`), though his own schema can be inconsistent in single-sorted arithmetic (`prop:time:single`). Membership in the collapse sets is polynomial (`prop:time:cheap`; brief H6 refuted). Templates block his schema (`prop:time:notemplate`) but one ground reflection sentence over PA reproduces any sound assigner (`prop:time:collapse`). Every theory consistent with a hard assigner needs long derivations in symbols (`thm:time:ntime`, `cor:time:hard`), which $\Lsig$ and the graded score charge polynomially (`prop:time:sigma`); plain $\Lone$ charges only logarithmically as far as proved (`prop:time:lonesize`, `cor:time:log`, `conj:time:poly`).

* **A8. "Statements given without proof should be easily derivable" does not by itself find the axioms.** Under the computable two-part code, memorising short theorems is 6–24 times cheaper than deriving them, and on theorem data the MAP is strictly weaker than PA (`prop:pa:memo`, `tab:pa:theorems`, `rem:pa:streams`). The generator wins when the data come from its own derivation process (`prop:pa:gibbs`, `prop:pa:wellspec`) or contain direct uses of the schema; a 0/1 "must prove" constraint removes memorisation only by giving up the size principle (`prop:pa:must`). Frequently used lemmas become axioms (`prop:univ:quant`(c), `rem:pa:streams`), and a time bound on computing the likelihood pushes further towards memorisation (`rem:time:links`).

* **A9. Positive data suffice in the limit, but some errors are never corrected by them.** Stochastic positive data plus the size principle identify both ends of Gold's limit point (`prop:ident:gold`, `tab:exp:e5`). But a spare slot holding a false sentence decays only like n^{−1/2} (`prop:ident:spare`, `rem:ident:sparetotal`), inconsistency is invisible to the size principle (`prop:pa:incons`), and only refutation at growing depth removes it (`prop:pa:refute`). For data from all of Th(ℕ) no theory is well specified; what remains is regret against every theory plus its exceptions (`prop:pa:thn`, `prop:pa:regret`, `rem:pa:tower`).
