\subsection{The two inductors}\label{app:time:inducers}

\begin{proposition}[Why consistent assigners]\status{proved}\src{model Prop 6.0}\label{prop:time:fiall}
Let $R$ be a unary predicate, $w_1,w_2,\dots$ distinct closed terms, and $\varphi_i$ be $R(w_i)$ or $\neg R(w_i)$ by independent fair coins, all labelled true (a consistent labelling). $\FIall$'s log loss is at most a constant (the program ``always accept''); the expected log loss of $\AI$ and of $\FIcons$ is at least $n$ bits.
\end{proposition}

\noindent This confirms H\"anni's informal separation, and is why the fair comparison is with $\FIcons$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:time:fiall}]
$\FIall$: the program ``always accept'' has some length $c$ and labels every $\varphi_i$ true, so $W_{\FIall}(D_n)\ge2^{-c}$; with $W_{\FIall}(\emptyset)\le1$ (Kraft, for a prefix machine) the log loss $-\log_2W(D_n)+\log_2W(\emptyset)$ is at most $c$.
$\AI$ and $\FIcons$: let $D$ be the data so far and $\psi:=R(w_{i})$ for the next index. A hypothesis compatible with $D+(\psi,1)$ and with $D+(\neg\psi,1)$ would prove, or accept, both $\psi$ and $\neg\psi$, against consistency. So the two sets of compatible hypotheses are disjoint subsets of those compatible with $D$, and the predictive probabilities $a:=W(D+(\psi,1))/W(D)$ and $a':=W(D+(\neg\psi,1))/W(D)$ satisfy $a+a'\le1$. The coin shows $\psi$ or $\neg\psi$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each, independently of $D$, so the expected step loss is $\tfrac12(-\log_2a-\log_2a')\ge-\log_2\frac{a+a'}2\ge1$ bit, by convexity of $-\log_2$. Summing over the $n$ steps gives at least $n$ bits.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:time:equiv}]
\emph{$W_{\mathrm{FI}}\ge2^{-c'}W_{\AI}$.} For a decider $p$ let $f_p$ be the program: on input $\varphi$, dovetail searches for proofs of $\varphi$ and of $\neg\varphi$ from $B\cup A_p$ (both sets are decidable, so the theorems are recursively enumerable), and output $\mathrm{acc}$ or $\mathrm{rej}$ for the first proof found. Its code is a fixed wrapper followed by $p$, so $|f_p|\le|p|+c'$ and $p\mapsto f_p$ is injective. Let $p$ be compatible with $D$. Then $B\cup A_p$ is consistent, so at most one of $\varphi,\neg\varphi$ is provable, and $f_p$ labels each $\varphi_i$ with $b_i$. Also $\Gamma_{f_p}\subseteq\Th(B\cup A_p)$, so $B\cup\Gamma_{f_p}$ is consistent and $f_p$ is a compatible $\FIcons$ hypothesis. Summing $2^{-|f_p|}\ge2^{-c'}2^{-|p|}$ over compatible $p$ gives the claim.

\emph{$W_{\AI}\ge2^{-c''}W_{\mathrm{FI}}$ (Craig's trick).} For an $\FIcons$ hypothesis $f$ define $A^C_f$ as in \cref{thm:time:equiv}. Its decider $p_f$: given $\chi$, try each of the at most $|\chi|$ decompositions $\chi=\psi^{\wedge m}$; for each, run $f$ for $m-1$ steps on $\psi$ and check whether it accepts in exactly $m-1$ steps, and, if $\psi=\neg\varphi$, run $f$ on $\varphi$ and check whether it rejects in exactly $m-1$ steps. Every run is bounded, so $p_f$ halts on every input, even when $f$ is partial. Each member $\varphi^{\wedge(k+1)}$ is logically equivalent to $\varphi\in\Gamma_f$ (likewise for $\neg\varphi$), and each member of $\Gamma_f$ has such a power in $A^C_f$, with $k$ the halting time. So $\Cn(B\cup A^C_f)=\Cn(B\cup\Gamma_f)$, which is consistent, and $B\cup A^C_f$ decides every $\varphi_i$ as $f$ does. Hence $p_f$ is compatible whenever $f$ is. Its code is a fixed wrapper followed by $f$: $|p_f|\le|f|+c''$, and $f\mapsto p_f$ is injective.

\emph{Losses.} Put $c:=\max(c',c'')$. The semimeasure log loss of an inducer on $D_n$ is $-\log_2W(D_n)+\log_2W(\emptyset)$; each of the two terms differs by at most $c$ between $\AI$ and $\FIcons$, and both depend on $D_n$ only as a set. If one weight is $0$, so is the other, and both losses are infinite.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The convention, and ``what is a sequence'']\status{proved}\src{model \S6.1}\label{rem:time:convention}
Renormalising at each step, $P(b\mid\varphi,D):=W(D+(\varphi,b))/\sum_{b'}W(D+(\varphi,b'))$, the argument of \cref{thm:time:equiv} gives only $2c$ bits \emph{per step}. The constant equivalence is proved only in the semimeasure convention, which answers H\"anni's ``wtf is a sequence here?'' (any reveal order, any labelling). His ``same spec length as the axiom system plus a const'' holds in both directions, and $B$ needs no arithmetic. His route through the schema does need arithmetic; it works with two sorts, or in one sort for sound assigners, and \cref{prop:time:single} shows that it fails for some consistent unsound ones (\cref{sec:time:schema}).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Per-step renormalisation.} With $P_X(b\mid\varphi,D):=W_X(D+(\varphi,b))/(W_X(D+(\varphi,0))+W_X(D+(\varphi,1)))$, the numerator and the denominator each change by a factor in $[2^{-c},2^c]$ between the inducers, so each step's ratio lies in $[2^{-2c},2^{2c}]$; over $n$ steps the bound is $2cn$ bits, and the argument gives nothing better.

\subsection{H\"anni's schema}\label{app:time:schema}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:time:twosorted}]
\emph{Consistency.} Take the structure whose arithmetic sort is the standard $\N$ and whose $L$-sort is a model $M$ of $\Gamma_f$. In $\N$, $\Accf{f}(\gn{\varphi})$ is true iff $f$ accepts $\varphi$, and then $\varphi\in\Gamma_f$ holds in $M$; likewise for rejections. So every member of $A_f$ holds, and so does $\Q$.
\emph{$\supseteq$.} If $f(\varphi)=\mathrm{acc}$, then $\Accf{f}(\gn{\varphi})$ is a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence, hence provable in $\Q$ ($\Sigma_1$-completeness for $<$-free $\Sigma_1$ sentences, which applies as $\Accf{f}$ is written without $<$; known, e.g.\ \citealp{boolos2007computability}, chapter on $\Q$, not checked); MP with its member of $A_f$ gives $\varphi$. Rejections are similar. So $\Gamma_f\subseteq\Cn(B\cup A_f)$.
\emph{$\subseteq$.} An $L$-sentence provable from $B\cup A_f$ holds in every structure $\N\oplus M$ with $M\models\Gamma_f$; its truth there depends only on $M$. So it holds in every model of $\Gamma_f$, and by completeness it lies in $\Cn(\Gamma_f)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:time:single}]
\emph{$\Gamma_f$.} Since $\Con(\PA)$ holds, the search on $\gn{\Con(\PA)}$ never ends, so $\Gamma_f=\{\neg\Con(\PA)\}$. $\PA+\neg\Con(\PA)$ is consistent by Gödel's second incompleteness theorem.
\emph{$\neg\Con(\PA)$.} $\Accf{f}(\gn{\neg\Con(\PA)})$ is a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence, so $\PA$ proves it; with its member of $A_f$, $\PA\cup A_f\vdash\neg\Con(\PA)$.
\emph{$\Con(\PA)$.} $\PA\vdash\neg\Con(\PA)\to\Accf{f}(\gn{\Con(\PA)})$: if a proof of $\bot$ exists there is a least one, and $\PA$ proves that the search halts on it with output $\mathrm{acc}$. This formalisation step is standard and already provable in $\ISigma{1}$. With the member $\Accf{f}(\gn{\Con(\PA)})\to\Con(\PA)$ of $A_f$, $\PA\cup A_f\vdash\neg\Con(\PA)\to\Con(\PA)$, so $\PA\cup A_f\vdash\Con(\PA)$. Both together are a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Templates}\label{app:time:templates}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:time:notemplate}]
Let $\tau\in\DT$ with $\inst(\tau)\subseteq C_f$, and suppose $\tau$ has a metavariable $M$ with an occurrence at position $q$. Constant bodies exist for every type with two different root symbols: $\lambda\bar z.0$ and $\lambda\bar z.(0+0)$ for terms, $\lambda\bar z.(0=0)$ and $\lambda\bar z.\neg(0=0)$ for formulas. By the preservation lemma (AS Lemma~2.4), for a constant body the subtree of $\tau\theta$ at $q$ is that constant, whatever the occurrence's arguments, and the rigid symbols of $\tau$ above $q$ are the same in every instance. Every member of $C_f$ has the form $\Accf{f}(N)\to\varphi$, where $N$ is the numeral $\gn{\varphi}$ substituted for the free variable of the fixed formula $\Accf{f}(x)$. Since $\inst(\tau)\ne\emptyset$, the path from the root to $q$ is a path of such a member.

\emph{Case 1: $q$ is the root or lies in the antecedent.}
(i) If $q$ lies inside an occurrence of the numeral $N$ (its root included), every member of $C_f$ carries $S$ or $0$ at $q$. Put $\theta(M):=\lambda\bar z.(0+0)$ and any bodies elsewhere. Then $\tau\theta$ has $+$ at $q$. If $\tau\theta$ were in $C_f$, $q$ would again lie inside a numeral, since the path to $q$ is the same; so $\tau\theta\notin C_f$.
(ii) Otherwise $q$ is the root or a position of the fixed skeleton of $\Accf{f}(x)$. All members of $C_f$ carry the same symbol there ($\to$ at the root). Choose the constant body of the right type whose root symbol differs from it; then $\tau\theta\notin C_f$.
Both contradict $\inst(\tau)\subseteq C_f$.

\emph{Case 2: no metavariable occurs at the root or in the antecedent.} Then the antecedent of every instance is one fixed sentence $\Accf{f}(N_0)$, and the only member of $C_f$ with that antecedent is $\Accf{f}(\gn{\varphi_0})\to\varphi_0$ for the single $\varphi_0$ with $\gn{\varphi_0}=N_0$. So $\tau$ has at most one instance. But a template with a metavariable has infinitely many instances: each type has infinitely many bodies, and $\theta\mapsto\tau\theta$ is injective (AS Thm~2.5(a)). Contradiction.

So $\tau$ is ground. A ground template has one instance; $C_f$ is infinite, so no finite set of $\DT$ templates has instance union $C_f$, and such a set with instances in $C_f$ covers at most as many members as it has templates.

\emph{Reflection schema} (pa Prop~4.3). With $\Prv_T(x)$ in place of $\Accf{f}(x)$ the members are $\Prv_T(\gn{\varphi})\to\varphi$, with unary numerals, and the proof goes through word for word.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Implicit in the notes.} Case~1(i) uses that ``inside the numeral'' is determined by the path to $q$, which is rigid in $\tau$; we have made this explicit. The notes, and the statement, treat the acceptance part $C_f$. The rejection part, and templates whose instances mix the two parts, are not treated: at a skeleton position where $\Accf{f}$ and $\Rejf{f}$ carry different symbols, Case~1(ii) would need a constant body whose root differs from both (\cref{app:ver:open}).

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:time:collapse}]
(a) Let $f(\varphi)=\mathrm{acc}$ with $\varphi\in\Sigma_n$. Then $\PA\vdash\Accf{f}(\gn{\varphi})$ (a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence) and $\PA\vdash\Sent_{\Sigma_n}(\gn{\varphi})$ (a true $\Delta_1$ fact). So $\PA+\rho_{f,n}\vdash\Tr_n(\gn{\varphi})$, and $\PA\vdash\Tr_n(\gn{\varphi})\to\varphi$. For rejections, $\PA+\rho_{f,n}\vdash\neg\Tr_n(\gn{\varphi})$ and $\PA\vdash\varphi\to\Tr_n(\gn{\varphi})$, so $\neg\varphi$ follows.
(b) In $\N$, $\Accf{f}$ and $\Rejf{f}$ hold exactly of $f$'s actual decisions, and $\Tr_n$ is $\Sigma_n$-truth, so both conjuncts of $\rho_{f,n}$ are true when $f$ is $\Sigma_n$-sound.
\emph{Length.} $f$ enters $\rho_{f,n}$ only through its index, written as a binary numeral of length $O(|f|)$ inside $\Accf{f}$ and $\Rejf{f}$; the rest of the sentence depends on $n$ only.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Time penalties}\label{app:time:penalties}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:time:cheap}]
(a) Parsing $\chi$ as an implication with antecedent $\Accf{f}(t)$ or $\Rejf{f}(t)$ is a comparison with a fixed formula, linear in $|\chi|$. Computing the Gödel number of the consequent (or of the formula under its negation) is polynomial in $|\chi|$, and so is writing it as a binary numeral and comparing it with $t$. With unary numerals, $t$ itself has length $\gn{\varphi}\le|\chi|$, so writing out the numeral is again polynomial in $|\chi|$. No step runs $f$.
(b) $\chi=\psi^{\wedge m}$ forces $m\le|\chi|$, and there are at most $|\chi|$ candidate decompositions. For each, $f$ is simulated for $m-1<|\chi|$ steps on $\psi$ (and on $\varphi$ if $\psi=\neg\varphi$), in time polynomial in $|\chi|$ for fixed $f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:time:ntime}]
\emph{Algorithm.} Given $w$ of length $m$, compute $\varphi_w$ and the time-constructible bound $\ell(m)$, guess a $\CK$-derivation of symbol size at most $\ell(m)$ whose parameter numbers have at most $K+1+\lceil\log_2(\ell(m)+1)\rceil$ bits, $K=O(\ell(m)^e)$ the membership-time bound at size $\ell(m)$ (if any derivation of size at most $\ell(m)$ exists, such a one does, by \cref{lem:time:truncate}; a parameter counts one symbol whatever its number, so without this bound the guess could be arbitrarily long), and check it: each line is an instance of a logical axiom (A1--A3, A5 and the equality axioms by matching; A4 by first-order matching, which is polynomial), a member of $T$ (time $O(\ell^e)$), or follows by MP or Gen. Accept iff the derivation is valid and ends in $\varphi_w$. All checks are polynomial in $\ell(m)$, of a degree that depends only on $e$ and on the degree $d_{\CK}$ of $\CK$'s line checks; so the machine runs in time $O(\ell(m)^{c_e})$.
\emph{Completeness.} If $w\in X$, such a derivation exists by hypothesis.
\emph{Soundness.} If $w\notin X$, then $\neg\varphi_w\in\Gamma_{f_X}$, so $T\vdash\varphi_w$ would make $T\cup\Gamma_{f_X}$ inconsistent. No derivation exists, and the machine rejects.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:time:hard}]
\emph{Uniformity.} The constant $c_e$ of \cref{thm:time:ntime} depends on $T$ only through $e$. Membership in a finite $\DT$ theory is matching against each template, in time $O(|T|\cdot|\chi|)$ (AS Thm~2.5), so $e=1$ for all of them, with a $T$-dependent constant factor that does not change the class.
\emph{The hierarchy theorem} (known; exact form not checked against \citealp{cook1972hierarchy,seiferas1978separating,zak1983turing}): if $t_2$ is time-constructible and $t_1(m+1)=o(t_2(m))$, then $\NTIME(t_2)\setminus\NTIME(t_1)\ne\emptyset$.
(a) Apply it with $t_1:=s^{c_1}$ and $t_2:=s^{c_1+1}$ to get a decidable $X\in\NTIME(s^{c_1+1})\setminus\NTIME(s^{c_1})$. Suppose some finite $\DT$ theory $T$ with $T\cup\Gamma_{f_X}$ consistent had, for all but finitely many $m$, a derivation of size at most $s(m)$ of every $\varphi_w$ with $w\in X$, $|w|=m$. Put the finitely many words of the exceptional lengths in a table. The machine of \cref{thm:time:ntime} with $\ell=s$, plus the table, decides $X$ in $\NTIME(s^{c_1})$: a contradiction.
(b) The same with $c_e$ in place of $c_1$, for each $e$.
\emph{A single $X$ for all $e$} (proof sketch). Take $X\in\NTIME(s^{\log_2 s})\setminus\NTIME(s^{(\log_2 s)/2})$, when these bounds are time-constructible and satisfy the gap condition. Every $s^{c_e}$ is eventually below $s^{(\log_2 s)/2}$, so the argument of (a) applies to every $e$. The time-constructibility and gap conditions for these bounds are not checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Upper bounds]\status{proof sketch ($A^C_f$, two-sorted $A_f$); not established for $\rho_{f,n}$}\src{model \S6.4}\label{rem:time:upper}
The collapse constructions through $A^C_f$ and the two-sorted $A_f$ pay at most a polynomial of the assigner's \emph{deterministic} running time $k$ in derivation size: cite $\varphi^{\wedge(k+1)}$ and eliminate $\wedge$; or prove the true $\Sigma_1$ sentence $\Accf{f}(\gn{\varphi})$ in $\Q$, in size polynomial in the computation (recalled from \citealp{pudlak1998lengths}, not checked), then apply MP. For $\rho_{f,n}$ the same route also needs a $\PA$-proof of the Tarski biconditional for $\varphi$, whose size is not bounded here.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Upper bounds (\cref{rem:time:upper}); proof sketch.} From $A^C_f$, a derivation of $\varphi$ cites $\varphi^{\wedge(k+1)}$, $k=\mathrm{time}_f(\varphi)$, and applies $\wedge$-elimination, in size polynomial in $k|\varphi|$. From $A_f$ in two sorts, a derivation is a $\Q$-proof of the true $\Sigma_1$ sentence $\Accf{f}(\gn{\varphi})$ followed by MP; the size of such a $\Q$-proof is polynomial in the length of the computation (recalled from \citealp{pudlak1998lengths}, not checked). From $\rho_{f,n}$, the same plus a $\PA$-proof of the Tarski biconditional $\Tr_n(\gn{\varphi})\to\varphi$, whose size is not bounded in the notes.

\subsection{What the derivation likelihoods charge}\label{app:time:lik}

\paragraph{The counterexample to the pre-referee claim (\cref{prop:time:lonesize}; referee M2).} Let $\varphi^{(0)}:=\forall x\,(x=x)$, the logical axiom of reflexivity (size 4). Round $j=1,\dots,k$: apply $\forall$E with the term $p+p$ ($p$ the first parameter), then Gen on $p$; this doubles the number of occurrences of the bound variable and keeps one $\forall$ at the root. A final $\forall$E with $p+p$ gives $\varphi_k$, an equation whose two sides are balanced sums of $2^{k+1}$ copies of $p$; its size is $2(2^{k+2}-1)+1=2^{k+3}-1$. The tree has one citation node; each $\forall$E contributes its own node and the $5$ grammar nodes of $p+p$ (the $+$ node and two parameters, each with a one-node index chain), each Gen its node and a one-node chain: $\nu=1+6+8k=7+8k$. In the referee's grammar ($\alpha_{\mathrm{ax}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{lg}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{MP}}=0.2$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{Gen}}=\alpha_{\forall\mathrm E}=0.1$, so $m=0.6$; seven logical schemas cited uniformly; terms $0$:\,.4, $S$:\,.2, $+$:\,.1, $\cdot$:\,.1, parameter:\,.2; parameter index geometric with ratio $\tfrac12$) a round costs $-\ln(0.1\cdot0.1\cdot(0.2\cdot0.5)^2)-\ln(0.1\cdot0.5)=12.206$ nats and the citation plus final $\forall$E $12.77$ nats. Every derivation of $\varphi_k$ contains $\varphi_k$, so $\ell_{\min}(\varphi_k)\ge2^{k+3}-1$, and $-\ln P_T(\varphi_k)\le-\ln\Pr(\text{tree})$ because $Z_T\le1$. For every $\kappa>0$ and $C$ the claimed inequality fails for large $k$; \cref{tab:time:c12} gives the numbers.

\emph{One-node example.} For $B_m(x):=(x+\dots+x=0)$ with $m$ summands and $t:=S^m0$, the A4 instance $\forall xB_m(x)\to B_m(t)$ has size quadratic in $m$ but probability $e^{-O(m)}$: the referee's script gives sizes $145$, $1765$, $6725$ symbols at costs at most $99.4$, $369.0$, $728.5$ nats for $m=10,40,80$ (\texttt{r2\_l1\_size.out}).

\begin{lemma}[Symbol size against grammar choices]\status{proved; computed}\src{model Lemma 6.10}\label{lem:time:symexp}
Let $\pi$ be a valid $\Lone$ tree from a finite $T$ with $\nu$ grammar nodes, $a_T\ge2$ a bound on the sizes of the templates of $T$ and of $\CK$'s logical templates, and $c_T:=2a_T^2$. Every conclusion in $\pi$ has symbol size at most $(c_T+1)\nu^2e^{\nu/e}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:time:symexp}]
For a node $v$ let $G(v)$ be the number of Gen nodes in its subtree and $\Pi(v):=\prod_u|t_u|$ over the $\forall$E nodes $u$ in its subtree, $t_u$ the substituted term ($\Pi=1$ if there are none). We show $|\mathrm{concl}(v)|\le(c_T\nu^2+G(v))\,\Pi(v)$ by induction on the tree.
\emph{Citation of a $\DT$ template} (of $T$, or A1--A3, A5, equality; substitution $\theta$). An occurrence $M(\bar t)$ becomes $\theta(M)[\bar t/\bar z]$. The body $\theta(M)$ has at most $\nu$ nodes and each argument $t_i$, a subterm of the template, has size at most $a_T$, so the piece has size at most $\nu a_T$. There are at most $a_T$ occurrences and at most $a_T$ rigid symbols, so $|\tau\theta|\le a_T+a_T^2\nu\le c_T\nu\le c_T\nu^2$.
\emph{Citation of A4} ($\forall xB\to B[t/x]$, not a template; \cref{lem:model:a4}). $B$ and $t$ have at most $\nu$ nodes each, so the size is at most $1+(|B|+1)+|B|\,|t|\le3\nu^2\le c_T\nu^2$ (such a tree has $\nu\ge3$).
\emph{$\forall$E with term $t$.} The conclusion $\psi[t/x]$ replaces each occurrence of the bound variable by $t$, which contains no bound indices, so no index shifting is needed: $|\psi[t/x]|\le|\psi|\,|t|\le|\mathrm{premise}|\,|t|$, and $\Pi$ gains the factor $|t|$.
\emph{Gen.} Replacing a parameter by an index keeps the size and the new $\forall$ adds one; $G$ grows by one and $\Pi\ge1$.
\emph{MP.} The conclusion is a proper subformula of the major premise, and $G$ and $\Pi$ only grow from a child to its parent.
Finally $G(v)\le\nu$, and the substituted terms of different $\forall$E nodes are disjoint sets of grammar nodes, so $\sum_u|t_u|\le\nu$. For $k$ terms, AM--GM gives $\prod_u|t_u|\le(\nu/k)^k\le\sup_{y>0}(\nu/y)^y=e^{\nu/e}$. Hence $|\mathrm{concl}(v)|\le(c_T\nu^2+\nu)e^{\nu/e}\le(c_T+1)\nu^2e^{\nu/e}$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Computed} (\texttt{c12\_l1\_size.out}, Part~B). Chains without A4 citations satisfy the stronger bound $(c_T+1)\nu e^{\nu/e}$ (the citation case gives $c_T\nu$). This is what the script tests: on 9865 conclusions of 3000 random valid $\forall$E/Gen chains, with terms from a PCFG and cited sentences with up to three quantifiers and repeated variables, neither the inductive bound nor the final bound was violated; the largest $\ln(\text{size})/\nu$ was $0.220$. The first version of the lemma had $\nu^{\nu+1}$ in place of $e^{\nu/e}$, and $\nu$ in place of $\nu^2$; the A4 case (the referee's one-node example) forced $\nu^2$ (model \S12.4).

\begin{proposition}[$\Lone$ charges its own code length exponentially]\status{proved; computed}\src{model Prop 6.11}\label{prop:time:codelength}
Assume $\Lone$ with $m<1$, $\Qg$ uniformly subcritical (\cref{def:model:grammar}), $T$ finite and a geometric parameter law. There are $\zeta,C>0$, depending on the grammar and on $T$, with $P_T(\varphi)\le C\,e^{-\zeta\,\nu_{\min}(\varphi)}/Z_T$ for every $\varphi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:time:codelength}]
The whole tree (derivation nodes, $\Qg$ nodes, parameter-chain nodes) is a multi-type branching process with boundedly many children per node, each node's children drawn independently given its type. Give derivation nodes weight $1$, $\Qg$ nodes weight $\eta h_{\Qg}$ ($h_{\Qg}$ from \cref{def:model:grammar}) and chain nodes weight $\eta$. A derivation node has expected weighted offspring at most $m+\eta(v_Th_{\Qg,\max}+1)$, with $v_T$ bounding the number of metavariables of a template of $T$ or of $\CK$; this is below $1$ for small $\eta$, since $m<1$. $\Qg$ nodes satisfy the condition with $\rho_{\Qg}$, and a chain node continues with probability $g_{\mathrm{par}}<1$. So the uniform condition holds with $\rho_{\mathrm{br}}:=\max(m+\eta(v_Th_{\Qg,\max}+1),\rho_{\Qg},g_{\mathrm{par}})<1$, and \cref{lem:model:grammar}(b),(d) gives $\Ex[e^{\zeta\nu}]\le C$ for some $\zeta>0$. A valid tree concluding $\varphi$ has $\nu\ge\nu_{\min}(\varphi)$, so $P_T(\varphi)=\mu_T(\varphi)/Z_T\le\Pr[\nu\ge\nu_{\min}(\varphi)]/Z_T\le Ce^{-\zeta\nu_{\min}(\varphi)}/Z_T$ by Markov's inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Code length bounds the assigner's time, logarithmically]\status{proved}\src{model Thm 6.12}\label{thm:time:log}
In the setting of \cref{thm:time:ntime}, let $T$ be a finite $\DT$ theory with $T\cup\Gamma_{f_X}$ consistent, and let every $\varphi_w$, $w\in X$, have a valid $\Lone$ tree from $T$ with at most $\ell(|w|)$ grammar nodes, $\ell(m)\ge m$ time-constructible. Then $X\in\NTIME(2^{(d+1)\ell(m)})$, where $d$, the polynomial degree of the syntactic operations used in checking, depends neither on $T$ nor on $\ell$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:time:log}]
On input $w$, put $\ell:=\ell(|w|)$ and guess a tree with at most $\ell$ grammar nodes. Each node is a choice from a finite list (rule, template of $T$, logical schema, production of $\Qg$) or one of at most $\ell+O(1)$ variables, so the guess has $O(\ell\ln\ell)$ bits. Compute every node's conclusion explicitly, check validity (MP needs equality of formulas, $\forall$E a root $\forall$) and compare the root's conclusion with $\varphi_w$. By \cref{lem:time:symexp} every formula has size at most $S:=(c_T+1)\ell^2e^{\ell/e}$, and all operations are polynomial of a fixed degree $d$ in that size. So the time is at most $\ell\cdot S^d$, which is at most $2^{(d+1)\ell}$ for $\ell\ge\ell_0(T)$, since $e^{d/e}<2^d$ and the remaining factor is polynomial in $\ell$; smaller inputs go in a table. Completeness and soundness are as in \cref{thm:time:ntime}: citations name a template of $T$, so no membership test is needed, and a valid tree is a $\CK$-derivation from $\bigcup\inst(T)$ (each $\forall$E is an A4 instance plus MP).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Hard assigners make plain $\Lone$ pay, logarithmically]\status{proved, given the hierarchy theorem (known)}\src{model Cor 6.13}\label{cor:time:log}
Let $s(m)\ge m$ be time-constructible with $s(m)^2-(d+1)s(m+1)\to\infty$ (every polynomial; $2^m$). There is a decidable $X$ such that for every finite $\DT$ theory $T$ with $T\cup\Gamma_{f_X}$ consistent, for infinitely many $m$ some $w\in X$ of length $m$ has $\nu_{\min}(\varphi_w)>s(m)$; under the hypotheses of \cref{prop:time:codelength}, $-\ln P_T(\varphi_w)\ge\zeta\,s(m)-\ln(C/Z_T)$ on those data.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:time:log}]
Apply the hierarchy theorem with $t_1:=2^{(d+1)s}$ and $t_2:=2^{s^2}$; the condition $t_1(m+1)=o(t_2(m))$ is $s(m)^2-(d+1)s(m+1)\to\infty$. This gives a decidable $X\in\NTIME(2^{s^2})\setminus\NTIME(2^{(d+1)s})$. If, for some $T$ and all but finitely many $m$, every $w\in X$ of length $m$ had $\nu_{\min}(\varphi_w)\le s(m)$, \cref{thm:time:log} with $\ell=s$ and a table (for the exceptional lengths and for $\ell<\ell_0(T)$) would put $X$ in $\NTIME(2^{(d+1)s})$. Since $d$ does not depend on $T$, one $X$ serves every $T$. The likelihood bound is \cref{prop:time:codelength} with $\nu_{\min}(\varphi_w)>s(m)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:time:sigma}]
(a) Summing over valid trees $\pi$ concluding $\varphi$,
\[
\mu^\sigma_T(\varphi)=\sum_{\pi}\Pr(\pi)e^{-\kappa|\pi|}\le e^{-\kappa\ell^\sigma_{\min}(\varphi)}\sum_{\pi}\Pr(\pi)=e^{-\kappa\ell^\sigma_{\min}(\varphi)}\mu_T(\varphi)\le e^{-\kappa\ell^\sigma_{\min}(\varphi)}Z_T;
\]
divide by $Z^\sigma_T$.
(b) List the conclusions of all nodes of the tree in post-order (total size $\ell$). For each $\forall$E node $v$ with premise $u$, insert the A4 instance $\mathrm{concl}(u)\to\mathrm{concl}(v)$ before $v$, of size $|\mathrm{concl}(u)|+|\mathrm{concl}(v)|+1$; the substituted term has no bound variables, so it is free for $x$. Then $\mathrm{concl}(v)$ follows by MP; Gen nodes are $\CK$'s Gen, and citations are axioms of $\CK$ or members of $\bigcup\inst(T)$. Each node is the premise of at most one node and there are at most $\ell$ nodes, so the insertions total at most $3\ell$ and the derivation has size at most $4\ell$. If $\ell^\sigma_{\min}(\varphi_w)\le s(m)/4$, there would be a $\CK$-derivation of size $\le s(m)$; by \cref{cor:time:hard}(a) this fails for some $w\in X$ of length $m$, for infinitely many $m$. On those data (a) gives $-\ln P^\sigma_T(\varphi_w)\ge\kappa\,\ell^\sigma_{\min}(\varphi_w)-\ln(Z_T/Z^\sigma_T)>\kappa s(m)/4-\ln(Z_T/Z^\sigma_T)$.
(c) An explicit $\CK$-derivation written with at least one bit per symbol has bit length at least its symbol size, which exceeds $s(m)$ on the same data. So $g(\ell_T(\varphi_w))=2^{-\kappa\ell_T(\varphi_w)}<2^{-\kappa s(m)}$, and after normalisation $-\log_2P^g_T(\varphi_w)=\kappa\ell_T(\varphi_w)+\log_2Z_T>\kappa s(m)+\log_2Z_T$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Implicit in the notes.} Part (c) needs the explicit derivations of \cref{rem:model:graded} to be derivations in $\CK$ (or derivations of a calculus that translates into $\CK$ with linear overhead, which changes the constant) and the code to spend at least one bit per written symbol; the notes say ``bits, which is at least their symbol count''. We state both assumptions.

\paragraph{The route to \cref{conj:time:poly}, and what is missing.} Represent conclusions with sharing (formula DAGs, grammar-compressed terms), so that $\forall$E need not copy $t$ into every occurrence; for the doubling family above a DAG of size $O(k)$ suffices. That every tree with $\nu$ grammar nodes has such representations of size polynomial in $\nu$ is part of what has to be shown. MP needs equality of two compressed formulas; equality of compressed strings and of grammar-compressed trees is decidable in polynomial time (recalled by the track, not checked: Plandowski 1994; Busatto, Lohrey and Maneth 2008; Schmidt-Schau{\ss} 2005). The missing step is Gen and de Bruijn indices under sharing: abstracting a parameter turns it into an index whose value depends on the binder depth at each occurrence, so a shared subterm can need different indices in different contexts. With that step, the proof of \cref{thm:time:log} would give $\NTIME(\ell^c)$. Model \S10 lists this as problem 12, together with the derivation-penalised equivalence (problem 13), a single-sorted repair of H\"anni's schema for consistent but unsound assigners (problem 14), and for which $f$ the set $A^C_f$, or a set with its consequences and polynomial membership, is a finite union of $\DT$ templates (problem 15). \paragraph{Machines.} Hypotheses are programs of the universal prefix machine $U$: words of a prefix-free code, read to their end before the input, so $\mathrm{time}_p(x)\ge|p|$; for a fixed wrapper $W$, $W^\frown q$ is again a program that finds the end of $q$. \emph{Assumption (S)} (efficient self-simulation; standard for multitape machines, not checked for a particular $U$): there are constants $\alpha,a\ge1$ such that, for the fixed wrappers used below, reading $q$, copying it and computing $|q|$ takes at most $\alpha(|q|+1)$ steps; parsing a sentence, or comparing or copying strings of total length $L$, at most $\alpha(L+1)^2$ steps; and $W^\frown q$ can run the \emph{dovetailing schedule} on $q$ (at stage $s$, start $q$ on the $s$-th sentence and advance every unfinished run by one simulated step) while keeping a \emph{work counter} $T$ (the simulated steps, plus the lengths of the sentences started, plus $|q|$), within $\alpha(T+1)^a$ steps up to work $T$, not counting output, writing a string of length $L$ costing at most $\alpha L$ steps. The counter starts at $|q|$ and grows with every simulated step, so distinct detections happen at distinct counter values. Put $a':=\max(a,2)$ and $a^*:=\max(a,3)$. \emph{Clocked strings.} $U_0$ is a fixed plain universal machine. For a time-constructible $\tau$ and a string $f$, $f^\tau(\varphi):=U_0(f,\varphi)$ if $U_0$ halts within $\tau(|\varphi|)$ steps with output acc or rej, and $f^\tau$ abstains otherwise. A string has weight $w(f):=2^{-|E(f)|}$, where $E(f)$ is the Elias-$\delta$ code of $|f|+1$ followed by $f$; $\sum_fw(f)=1$, and the tails of this sum are computable. \emph{Overheads} (standard polynomial-overhead simulations; assumed, like (S)): $q$ is a fixed nondecreasing polynomial with nonnegative coefficients such that, on literal sequences (\cref{def:time:short}, $|\varphi_w|=|w|+2$), (i) a string labelling every $\varphi_w$ correctly within $T(|\varphi_w|)$ steps of $U_0$ (or a $U$-program within $T$ steps of $U$) yields a multitape machine deciding $X$ in time $q(C_fT(m+2)+m)$, $m=|w|$, $C_f$ depending on the string; (ii) a multitape machine deciding $X$ within $T(|w|)$ steps yields a string labelling each $\varphi_w$ correctly within $q(T(|w|)+|\varphi_w|)$ steps of $U_0$; (iii) $U_0$ runs a $U$-program $p$ for $T$ steps within $q(C_pT)$ steps. Since $q(Cx)\le C^{\deg q}q(x)$ for $C\ge1$, a growing factor $m$ absorbs every constant from some length on. $\DTIME$ and $\NTIME$ refer to multitape machines.

\begin{definition}[Penalised inductors]\src{time-followup Defs 1.1, 1.2}\label{def:time:penalties}
Let $t$ be time-constructible, nondecreasing, superadditive, with $t(m)\ge m$. Compatibility is as in \cref{def:time:inducers}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item $\AI^{\mathrm{chk}}_t$: deciders $p$ with $\mathrm{time}_p(\chi)=O(t(|\chi|))$, weight $2^{-|p|}$. $\AI^{\mathrm{Kt}}_t$: all deciders, weight $2^{-|p|}/c_p$, $c_p:=\sup_\chi\mathrm{time}_p(\chi)/t(|\chi|)$ (weight $0$ if $c_p=\infty$).
\item $\AI^{\mathrm{gen}}_t$: enumerators $g$, programs without input writing $a_1,a_2,\dots$, with $A_g:=\{a_i\}$; $e_i$ is the step at which $a_i$ is completely written, $e_0:=0$. Weight $2^{-|g|}$ if $e_i-e_{i-1}=O(t(|a_i|))$ (the \emph{delay form}; polynomial delay, \citealp{johnson1988generating}), $0$ otherwise. Variants: the \emph{strict form} $e_i=O(t(|a_i|))$ and the \emph{cumulative form} $e_i=O(t(|a_1|+\dots+|a_i|))$. Strict implies delay, and delay implies cumulative by superadditivity.
\item $\FIcons^\tau$: clocked strings $f$ with $B\cup\Gamma_{f^\tau}$ consistent, weight $w(f)$, compatible with $D$ if $f^\tau$ labels every datum correctly; $\FIall^\tau$ drops consistency. $\FIcons^{\mathrm{poly}}$ mixes over pairs $(f,k)$, $f$ clocked at $(|\varphi|+2)^k$, with weight $w(f)2^{-2\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil-1}$.
\item A \emph{predictor} maps a history $D$ and a sentence $\varphi$ to $(q_0,q_1)$ with $q_b\ge0$, $q_0+q_1\le1$; its loss is $\ell_P(D_n):=\sum_{j\le n}-\log_2q_{b_j}(D_{j-1},\varphi_j)$, which for a mixture in the semimeasure convention equals $\ell(D_n)$, by telescoping. It is \emph{computable} if a total computable function maps $(D,\varphi,k)$ to rationals within $2^{-k}$ of $q_0,q_1$. H\"anni's $S$ is read on labelled sentences: its components see the labelled history and the next sentence. Only this is used: $S$ is a computable predictor, and it is the Bayes mixture of its components as counted (each predicting $\frac12$ before its start and when over budget), one of which, ``label $1$ with probability $1$'', runs within every budget, has prior weight $\pi_1>0$ and is tracked from a finite step $\ell_1$ on.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded loss on an infinite sequence]\status{proved}\src{time-followup Lemma 1.6}\label{lem:time:bounded}
Let $W(D)=\sum_hv(h)[h\text{ compatible with }D]$ with $\sum_hv(h)<\infty$ and compatibility inherited by prefixes, and let $D_n$ be the prefixes of an infinite labelled sequence. Then $\sup_n\ell(D_n)<\infty$ iff a single $h$ with $v(h)>0$ is compatible with every $D_n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $h$ is, $W(D_n)\ge v(h)$ and $\ell(D_n)\le\log_2(W(\emptyset)/v(h))$. If none is, each indicator is eventually $0$, so $W(D_n)\to0$ by dominated convergence and $\ell(D_n)\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Every inductor of this subsection is such a mixture. The \emph{fit class} of an inductor is the set of $X$ with $\sup_n\ell(D^X_n)<\infty$ (literal sequences: \cref{def:time:short}).

\begin{lemma}[Padded Craig sets]\status{proved, under (S); computed}\src{time-followup Lemma 2.1; scripts: c2\_padding, referee\_penalty r2\_enumeration}\label{lem:time:padded}
Let $q$ be a program and $S_q$ the set of sentences on which it halts with output ``yes''. The enumerator $e_q:=W_{\mathrm{enum}}{}^\frown q$ runs the dovetailing schedule on $q$ and, when it first detects that $q$ says yes on $\chi$, at work counter $T$, writes $\chi^{\wedge(T+1)}$; $\mathrm{Pad}(q)$ is the set written. Then:
(a) $\Cn(B\cup\mathrm{Pad}(q))=\Cn(B\cup S_q)$;
(b) $\mathrm{Pad}(q)$ has a decider $d_q:=W_{\mathrm{dec}}{}^\frown q$ with $\mathrm{time}_{d_q}(\chi)\le\alpha(|q|+1)+3\alpha(|\chi|+1)^{a'}$;
(c) $e_q$ writes each member once; the member $a_i$, written at counter $T_i$, has $|a_i|\ge T_i+1$, and $e_i-e_{i-1}\le2\alpha|a_i|^a$, $e_i\le5\alpha|a_i|^{a^*}$ and $e_i\le2\alpha(|a_1|+\dots+|a_i|)^a$;
(d) $|d_q|,|e_q|\le|q|+c$, and $q\mapsto d_q$, $q\mapsto e_q$ are injective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) $\chi^{\wedge m}$ is equivalent to $\chi$; each $\chi\in S_q$ is detected once, so it has exactly one padded copy, and every member is a copy of a member of $S_q$.
(b) $d_q$ reads and copies $q$ and computes $|q|$, in $\alpha(|q|+1)$ steps. If $\chi=\psi^{\wedge m}$ with $m\ge2$, then $\psi$ is the left conjunct of $\chi$, and $m$ is fixed by $|\chi|$, since $|\psi^{\wedge m}|=m|\psi|+3(m-1)$ in primitive syntax; the only other candidate is $m=1$, $\psi=\chi$. Both are found within $\alpha(|\chi|+1)^2$ steps. A candidate with $m-1<|q|$ is rejected, as the counter starts at $|q|$. Otherwise $d_q$ runs the schedule without writing until the counter exceeds $m-1$, and accepts iff $q$ is detected saying yes on $\psi$ exactly at counter $m-1$; as $m\le|\chi|$, this costs at most $\alpha(|\chi|+1)^a$ steps by (S). The runs are bounded by the counter, so $d_q$ halts on every input, also when $q$ is partial.
(c) Detections are of distinct sentences, so each member is written once, and $|a_i|=|\chi_i^{\wedge(T_i+1)}|\ge T_i+1$. Between the completions of $a_{i-1}$ and $a_i$ the schedule costs at most $\alpha(T_i+1)^a$ and writing $a_i$ at most $\alpha|a_i|$: the delay bound. Up to $a_i$ at most $T_i+1$ members were written, at distinct counters $T_j\le T_i$, each of length at most $(T_j+1)(T_j+3)$ since $|\chi_j|\le T_j$ (the length of $\chi_j$ is part of the counter); so the total written length is at most $4(T_i+1)^3\le4|a_i|^3$, and $e_i\le\alpha|a_i|^a+4\alpha|a_i|^3$. Finally $e_i\le\alpha(T_i+1)^a+\alpha\sum_{j\le i}|a_j|$.
(d) $d_q$ and $e_q$ are fixed wrappers followed by $q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:time:onesided}]
(a) \emph{Lower bounds.} For an $\FIcons$ hypothesis $f$ let $q_f$ say yes on $\varphi$ if $f(\varphi)=\mathrm{acc}$ and on $\neg\varphi$ if $f(\varphi)=\mathrm{rej}$; then $S_{q_f}=\Gamma_f$ and $|q_f|\le|f|+c$. By \cref{lem:time:padded}(a), $\Cn(B\cup\mathrm{Pad}(q_f))=\Cn(B\cup\Gamma_f)$, which is consistent and decides each datum as $f$ does, so $d_{q_f}$ and $e_{q_f}$ are compatible whenever $f$ is. By (b) and (c) they meet the membership bound and every form of the generation bound for $t(m)\ge m^{a^*}$, and by (d) the maps are injective and add a constant to the length. \emph{Upper bounds.} $\AI^{\mathrm{chk}}_t$ has a subset of $\AI$'s hypotheses with the same weights; apply \cref{thm:time:equiv}. An enumerator $g$ goes to $f_g:=$ ``search for proofs of $\varphi$ and of $\neg\varphi$ from $B\cup A_g$'' ($A_g$ is r.e.), with $|f_g|\le|g|+c$; if $g$ is compatible, $f_g$ labels each datum correctly and $\Gamma_{f_g}\subseteq\Cn(B\cup A_g)$ is consistent with $B$.
(b) Let $X$ be $\AI$, $\AI^{\mathrm{chk}}_t$ or $\AI^{\mathrm{gen}}_t$. By (a) and \cref{thm:time:equiv}, $W_X(D)\ge2^{-c}W_{\mathrm{FI}}(D)$, and $W_X(\emptyset)\le1$ by Kraft. For a clocked string $h$ let $g_h$ be a fixed wrapper that runs $h^\tau$ and diverges when it abstains, followed by $E(h)$. It computes the same partial map as $h^\tau$, so it is an $\FIcons$ hypothesis, compatible with $D$ iff $h$ is; $|g_h|=|E(h)|+c_1$, and $h\mapsto g_h$ is injective. (Equal sets $\Gamma$ would not suffice: an assigner rejecting $\psi$ and one accepting $\neg\psi$ and abstaining on $\psi$ have the same $\Gamma$ but differ on the datum $(\psi,0)$.) So $W_{\mathrm{FI}}(D)\ge2^{-c_1}W_{\FIcons^\tau}(D)$ and
$\ell_X(D)\le-\log_2W_{\FIcons^\tau}(D)+c+c_1=\ell_{\FIcons^\tau}(D)-\log_2W_{\FIcons^\tau}(\emptyset)+c+c_1$, where $W_{\FIcons^\tau}(\emptyset)$ is at least the weight $2^{-c_\perp}$ of the always-abstaining string ($B$ consistent). Put $c_\tau:=c+c_1+c_\perp$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A Kt penalty costs a logarithm]\status{proved, under (S); computed}\src{time-followup Prop 2.3; scripts: referee\_penalty r1\_kt\_prior}\label{prop:time:kt}
Let $t(m)\ge m^{a^*}$ be time-constructible, $m_0$ the least length of a sentence, and $W_{\mathrm{FI}^1}(D):=\sum_f2^{-|f|}/(|f|+1)$ over the $\FIcons$ hypotheses compatible with $D$. Then $W_{\AI^{\mathrm{Kt}}_t}(\emptyset)\le t(m_0)$, and there is a constant $c$ with $2^{-c}W_{\mathrm{FI}^1}(D)\le W_{\AI^{\mathrm{Kt}}_t}(D)\le2^ct(m_0)W_{\mathrm{FI}^1}(D)$ for every $D$; so the two losses differ by at most $2c+2\log_2t(m_0)$, and an assigner $f$ pays $\log_2(|f|+1)+O(1)$ bits beyond its unpenalised weight.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{time}_p(\chi)\ge|p|$, so $c_p\ge|p|/t(m_0)$ and $p$ has weight at most $t(m_0)2^{-|p|}/|p|$; by Kraft the total is at most $t(m_0)$. \emph{Lower bound.} For an $\FIcons$ hypothesis $f$ let $d:=d_{q_f}$. By \cref{lem:time:padded}(b), as $(|\chi|+1)^{a'}\le2^{a'}|\chi|^{a^*}\le2^{a'}t(|\chi|)$ and $t\ge1$, $c_d\le\alpha(|q_f|+1)+3\alpha2^{a'}\le\beta(|f|+1)$ with $\beta$ depending only on $U$ and the coding; so $d$ has weight at least $2^{-|f|-2c}/(\beta(|f|+1))$, is compatible when $f$ is, and $f\mapsto d$ is injective. \emph{Upper bound.} A compatible $p$ goes to the proof-search assigner $f_p$ of \cref{thm:time:equiv}, compatible, with $|p|\le|f_p|\le|p|+c$; $p$'s weight is at most $t(m_0)2^{-|p|}/|p|\le t(m_0)(c+2)2^c\,2^{-|f_p|}/(|f_p|+1)$, and $p\mapsto f_p$ is injective. Apply both bounds to $D$ and to $\emptyset$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[What $\Q$ proves about computations]\src{time-followup Lemma 1.4}\label{lem:time:qfacts}
Write $\num k$ for the binary numeral of $k$ ($\num0:=0$, $\num1:=S0$, $\num{2k}:=SS0\cdot\num k$, $\num{2k+1}:=SS0\cdot\num k+S0$ for $k\ge1$) and $S^k0$ for the unary one.
(a) \status{known: the MRDP theorem \citep{matiyasevich1970enumerable,davis1973hilbert}; not checked} There are polynomials $E,E'$ with natural-number coefficients such that program $e$ halts on input $x$ with output $y$ iff $\N\models\exists\bar c\,(E(e,x,y,\bar c)=E'(e,x,y,\bar c))$. Put $\Out(e,x,y):=\exists\bar c\,(E=E')$, a $\Sigma_1$ formula without $<$ and without bounded quantifiers.
(b) \status{proved} For every closed $L_A$-term $t$ of value $k$, $\Q\vdash t=S^k0$.
(c) \status{proved} For $a\ne b$, $\Q\vdash\neg S^a0=S^b0$; so $\Q$ proves every true and refutes every false closed equation, and proves every true sentence $\exists\bar y\,(s=s')$.
(d) \status{proved; computed: referee script \texttt{r2\_q\_models}} For every $k$, $\Q\vdash\forall y\,(\exists z\,(z+y=S^k0)\to y=0\vee y=S0\vee\dots\vee y=S^k0)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(b) Induction on $t$: $St'$ by the equality axioms; $\Q\vdash S^a0+S^b0=S^{a+b}0$ by $b$ uses of Q5 and one of Q4; $\Q\vdash S^a0\cdot S^b0=S^{ab}0$ by induction on $b$ with Q6, Q7 and the $+$ case. (c) For $a<b$, $a$ uses of Q2 turn $S^a0=S^b0$ into $0=S^{b-a}0$, against Q1. A closed equation reduces by (b) to one between unary numerals. For a true $\exists\bar y(s=s')$, instantiate $\bar y$ by the numerals of a witness. (d) Induction on $k$. For $k=0$: if $y=Sy'$ (Q3), then $z+Sy'=S(z+y')\ne0$ by Q5 and Q1, so $y=0$. From $k$ to $k+1$: if $y\ne0$, then $y=Sy'$ and $S(z+y')=S(S^k0)$, so $z+y'=S^k0$ by Q2, and the induction hypothesis places $y'$, hence $y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:time:diagonal}]\src{time-followup Thm 3.2, Cor 3.3, Prop 3.4; scripts: c1\_diagonal, c4\_revision Part A, referee\_logic r1\_quine\_diagonal}
Let $\DET:=\forall e\forall x\forall y\forall y'\forall\bar c\forall\bar c'\,(E(e,x,y,\bar c)=E'(e,x,y,\bar c)\wedge E(e,x,y',\bar c')=E'(e,x,y',\bar c')\to y=y')$, a true $\Pi_1$ sentence, and $H_0:=\Q\cup\{\DET\}$.
\emph{Construction.} Let $G(e',n)$ be the program that, for $j=1,\dots,n$, forms $\sigma_j:=\Out(\num{e'},\num j,S0)$, asks $P$ for approximations $(a_0,a_1)$ within $2^{-j-3}$ of its pair on the history $((\sigma_1,\beta_1),\dots,(\sigma_{j-1},\beta_{j-1}))$ and the sentence $\sigma_j$, and sets $\beta_j:=0$ if $a_0\le a_1$, $\beta_j:=1$ otherwise; it outputs $\beta_n$. $G$ is total, as $P$ is. By Kleene's recursion theorem \citep[\S11.2; section not checked]{rogers1967theory} there is $e$ with $e(n)=G(e,n)$ for all $n$. The values $\beta_j$ computed inside $G(e,n)$ do not depend on $n$, so $e(j)=\beta_j\in\{0,1\}$ and $\sigma_j=s_j$; by \cref{lem:time:qfacts}(a), $\N\models s_n$ iff $e(n)=1$, so the label $b_n$ is $\beta_n$, and $P$ is queried exactly on the true histories $D_{j-1}$.
\emph{$H_0$ decides each $s_n$.} If $b_n=1$, $s_n$ is a true existential equation, provable in $\Q$ (\cref{lem:time:qfacts}(c)). If $b_n=0$, $\Out(\num e,\num n,0)$ is one, so $\Q$ proves it, and with $s_n$ and $\DET$ it gives $S0=0$, against Q1; so $H_0\vdash\neg s_n$. ($H_0$ is not complete; only the truth of $\DET$ is used below.)
\emph{The loss of $P$.} With $q_b:=q_b(D_{j-1},s_j)$ and $\varepsilon:=2^{-j-3}$, the tie rule gives $q_{b_j}\le a_{b_j}+\varepsilon\le a_{1-b_j}+\varepsilon\le q_{1-b_j}+2\varepsilon$; with $q_0+q_1\le1$ this gives $q_{b_j}\le\frac12+2^{-j-3}$, so $-\log_2q_{b_j}\ge1-\log_2(1+2^{-j-2})$, and $\sum_j\log_2(1+2^{-j-2})\le\sum_j2^{-j-2}/\ln2=1/(4\ln2)<0.361$. (With exact outputs the loss is at least $n$; the bound is attained up to rounding, script \texttt{c4\_revision}, Part A.)
\emph{Axiom induction.} $H_0$ is true in $\N$ and $B$ in an expansion of $\N$, so $B\cup H_0$ is consistent, and $H_0$ is compatible with every $D_n$. It is finite, so it has a decider running in linear time and an enumerator writing finitely many axioms; let $\omega>0$ be the least of their weights in $\AI$, $\AI^{\mathrm{chk}}_t$ and $\AI^{\mathrm{gen}}_t$. Then $W(D_n)\ge\omega$, $W(\emptyset)\le1$, and $\ell(D_n)\le\log_2(1/\omega)$, whatever $P$ is.
\emph{$S$, $\FIall^\tau$ and $\FIcons^\tau$.} $S$ is a computable predictor (\cref{def:time:penalties}). For $\FIall^\tau$ put $P(D):=(W_{\FIall^\tau}(D)+2^{-|D|})/2$ and $q_b(D,\varphi):=P(D+(\varphi,b))/P(D)$. Then $q_0+q_1\le1$, as $\FIall^\tau$ is a semimeasure and the uniform part halves at each step; $P$ is computable, as clocked compatibility is decidable and the weights have computable tails, and $P(D)\ge2^{-|D|-1}>0$; and $W_{\FIall^\tau}(\emptyset)=1$, so $P(\emptyset)=1$ and $\ell_P(D_n)=-\log_2P(D_n)$. On $P$'s sequence $W_{\FIcons^\tau}(D_n)\le W_{\FIall^\tau}(D_n)\le2P(D_n)$, so $\ell_{\FIall^\tau}(D_n)\ge n-1.37$ and $\ell_{\FIcons^\tau}(D_n)\ge\ell_P(D_n)-1+\log_2W_{\FIcons^\tau}(\emptyset)\ge n-1.37-c_\perp$, with $c_\perp:=-\log_2$ of the weight of the always-abstaining string.
\emph{The converse.} On the fair-coin literals of \cref{prop:time:fiall}, all labelled true, $S$ gives the label sequence at least the probability its constant component gives it, times $\pi_1$: at least $\pi_12^{-\ell_1}$. So $\ell_S\le\log_2(1/\pi_1)+\ell_1$, and $\FIall^\tau$ has loss at most $-\log_2w(\text{always accept})$ once $\tau$ covers the constant time of ``always accept''. By \cref{prop:time:fiall}, $\AI$ (and $\FIcons$) lose at least $n$ bits in expectation.
\end{proof}

\noindent ``Axiom induction becomes strictly stronger than every time-bounded function inductor'', in the first version of the track's answer, is refuted by the converse part: $S$ and $\FIall^\tau$ are time-bounded and incomparable with $\AI$. The statement proved is strict superiority over time-penalised \emph{consistent} function induction, the fair comparison of \cref{sec:time:inducers}.

\begin{proposition}[The evaluator; a time-hierarchy effect]\status{(a), (b) proved; (c) proof sketch, resting on H\"anni's construction of $S$ and his theorem for it}\src{time-followup Prop 4.1}\label{prop:time:evaluator}
(a) Let $u$ be the assigner that, on an input $\Out(\num e,\num n,S0)$, runs $e$ on $n$ and outputs acc if the result is $1$ and rej if it is anything else (looping if $e$ loops), and abstains on all other inputs. Then $\Gamma_u\subseteq\Th(\N)$, so $u$ is consistent with every $B$ true in an expansion of $\N$, and $|u|$ is a constant.
(b) On every sequence of \cref{thm:time:diagonal}, $u$ labels each datum correctly, so unpenalised $\FIcons$ (and $\FIall$) has $\ell(D_n)\le|u|$ there. The running time of $u$ on $s_n$ is at least that of $e$ on $n$, which includes the time $P$ spends on its first $n$ predictions.
(c) Let $S_p$ be H\"anni's predictor with per-step time $p(n)\log n$ and state reuse. The labels that defeat $S_p$ are produced by a predictor with per-step time $O(p(n)\log n)$ and state reuse that predicts its own labels with certainty; so $S_{p'}$, for a slightly larger $p'$ (e.g.\ $np(n)$), has bounded loss on the sequence that defeats $S_p$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) $\Out(\num e,\num n,S0)$ is true iff $e$ outputs $1$ on $n$, so $u$'s decisions are true. (b) In \cref{thm:time:diagonal} $e$ is total with values in $\{0,1\}$; then $\ell(D_n)=-\log_2W(D_n)+\log_2W(\emptyset)\le|u|$. (c) Not written out: H\"anni's version of his theorem that gives the compared predictors the same state reuse, and the machine-model caveat he names.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Disguised assigners keep their weight]\status{proved, under (S)}\src{time-followup Prop 5.1(a)}\label{prop:time:disguised}
Let $X$ be $\AI^{\mathrm{chk}}_t$ or $\AI^{\mathrm{gen}}_t$ (any form), $t(m)\ge m^{a^*}$. For a class $\Theta$ of deductively closed theories let $W(\Theta,D)$ be the weight of the compatible hypotheses $p$ with $\Cn(B\cup A_p)\in\Theta$. There is a constant $c$ with $2^{-c}W_{\AI}(\Theta,D)\le W_X(\Theta,D)\le2^cW_{\AI}(\Theta,D)$ for all $\Theta$ and $D$. So $W_X(\Theta,D)=0$ iff $W_{\AI}(\Theta,D)=0$; otherwise the posterior shares $W(\Theta,D)/W(D)$ under $X$ and $\AI$ differ by a factor at most $2^{2c}$, and so do the posterior odds of any two classes of positive weight, ``genuine'' and ``disguised'' alike.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Lower bound.} An $\AI$ hypothesis $p$ semi-decides $A_p$ via $q_p:=$ ``yes iff $p$ accepts''; \cref{lem:time:padded} gives $d_{q_p}$ and $e_{q_p}$, of length at most $|p|+c$, meeting the penalty, with $\Cn(B\cup\mathrm{Pad}(q_p))=\Cn(B\cup A_p)$, and compatibility depends only on the theory. \emph{Upper bound.} For $\AI^{\mathrm{chk}}_t$ the hypotheses are $\AI$'s with the same weights. For $\AI^{\mathrm{gen}}_t$ map $g$ to $d_{q_g}$, $q_g:=$ ``yes iff $g$ writes the input'', which preserves the theory and adds at most $c$ to the length. Divide, using $W(D)=W(\text{all theories},D)$.
\end{proof}

\noindent So under these penalties a slow consistent assigner $f$ keeps prior weight at least $2^{-|f|-c}$, as a padded Craig set, and its posterior share stays within a constant factor of its unpenalised one.

\begin{proposition}[H\"anni's schema with a binary witness bound]\status{proved, given the MRDP theorem (known); computed}\src{time-followup Prop 2.5; scripts: referee\_logic r2\_q\_models}\label{prop:time:bounded}
One sort, $L\supseteq L_A$, $B\supseteq\Q$ decidable, true or not. With $\Out$ from \cref{lem:time:qfacts}(a) write $\Out(\num{\gn f},x,\num{\gn{\mathrm{acc}}})=\exists\bar y\,(P_f(x,\bar y)=P'_f(x,\bar y))$, $\bar y=(y_1,\dots,y_r)$, and put $\mathrm{Acc}^{\le k}_f(x):=\exists\bar y\,(y_1\le\num k\wedge\dots\wedge y_r\le\num k\wedge P_f(x,\bar y)=P'_f(x,\bar y))$, with $y\le s$ written $\exists z\,(z+y=s)$; likewise $\mathrm{Rej}^{\le k}_f$. Let $A^{\mathrm{bd}}_f:=\{\mathrm{Acc}^{\le k}_f(\num{\gn\varphi})\to\varphi,\ \mathrm{Rej}^{\le k}_f(\num{\gn\varphi})\to\neg\varphi:\varphi\in\Sent_L,\ k\in\N\}$. Then $\Cn(B\cup A^{\mathrm{bd}}_f)=\Cn(B\cup\Gamma_f)$, for sound and unsound assigners alike, and membership in $A^{\mathrm{bd}}_f$ is decidable in time polynomial in $|\chi|$ without running $f$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\varphi$ and $k$. If $f$ accepts $\varphi$ and the acceptance equation has a solution in $\{0,\dots,k\}^r$, $\Q$ proves the antecedent (instantiate $\bar y$, and each $z_i$ by the numeral of $k-y_i$; every conjunct is a true closed equation, \cref{lem:time:qfacts}(c)), so the axiom is $\Q$-equivalent to $\varphi\in\Gamma_f$. Otherwise $\Q$ refutes the antecedent: by \cref{lem:time:qfacts}(b), (d) each $y_i$ is one of $0,S0,\dots,S^k0$, and each of the $(k+1)^r$ cases makes $P_f=P'_f$ a false closed equation, which $\Q$ refutes; so the axiom is a theorem of $\Q$. Rejections are the same, and every member of $\Gamma_f$ has its axiom (take $k$ large). Membership: parse $\chi$ against the fixed formula with two numeral slots and compare one slot with $\num{\gn\varphi}$.
\end{proof}

\noindent In the counterexample of \cref{prop:time:single} every antecedent $\mathrm{Acc}^{\le k}_f(\num{\gn{\Con(\PA)}})$ has no solution, so $\PA\cup A^{\mathrm{bd}}_f$ is equivalent to $\PA+\neg\Con(\PA)$, which is consistent: the numeral bound keeps out the nonstandard witnesses. This is a repair in the form of H\"anni's schema, for every consistent assigner, in one sort; in substance it is Craig's idea, the bound $k$ playing the role of the padding.

\paragraph{Deduction charged: syntax.} Formulas are as in \cref{def:model:calculus}: closure-normal form with de Bruijn indices, parameters $p_0,p_1,\dots$ read under universal closure, connectives $\neg,\to$, quantifier $\forall$, a finite signature with equality. A \emph{line} has no dangling index; a sentence has no parameter either. $|\chi|$ counts nodes (\cref{app:time}); for the formula nodes $v$ of $\chi$, $F(\chi):=\sum_v|\chi_v|$, $\chi_v$ the subtree at $v$. $\mathrm{Gen}_p(\chi):=\forall\,\mathrm{abs}_p(\chi)$, where $\mathrm{abs}_p$ replaces each occurrence of $p$ at binder depth $d$ by the index $d$ (a vacuous $\forall$ if $p$ does not occur). $\CK$ has A1--A3, A4 $\forall B\to B[t]$ ($t$ index-free), A5 $\forall(B\to C)\to(B\to\forall C)$ ($B$ closed), reflexivity $\forall(\#0=\#0)$ and substitutivity $p=q\to(B\to B')$, and the rules MP and Gen. A derivation from $Ax$ is a sequence of lines, each justified as an axiom (logical, or in $Ax$), by MP from two earlier lines, or by Gen on a parameter from an earlier line. $\mathrm{size}(\pi)$ is the sum of the line sizes; $I(\pi)$ is the set of distinct formulas of axiom lines; the \emph{material} is $M(\pi):=\sum_{\alpha\in I(\pi)}|\alpha|$, and $M_{\mathrm{nl}}(\pi)$ the same over the nonlogical ones. A line $\lambda$ \emph{sits at} a formula node $v$ of $\alpha$ \emph{with $k$ abstractions} if the $k$ nodes above $v$ are quantifier nodes, the topmost being $u$, and $\alpha_u=\mathrm{Gen}_{q_k}(\cdots\mathrm{Gen}_{q_1}(\lambda)\cdots)$ for some parameters $q_i$; with $k=0$, $\lambda=\alpha_v$. A derivation is \emph{normal} if its lines are distinct and each line but the last is a premise of a later line.

\begin{definition}[Readings of ``short axioms''; certificate function induction]\src{short-derivations Defs 1.2, 1.3, 1.6; time-followup Def 1.3}\label{def:time:short}
Let $L$ be finite and $B$ decidable, budgets nondecreasing. A hypothesis is a program $p$ deciding $A_p$ in time $O(t(|\chi|))$ such that $Ax_p:=B\cup A_p$ is closed under permutations of the parameters, condition (F1). It is compatible with $D$ if $Ax_p$ is consistent and each $\varphi_i^{b_i}$ ($\varphi^1:=\varphi$, $\varphi^0:=\neg\varphi$) has a $\CK$-derivation $\pi_i$ from $Ax_p$ in which, for $\AI^{\mathrm{a1}}[t,h]$, every axiom line has size at most $h(|\varphi_i|)$; for $\AI^{\mathrm{a2}}[t,h]$, every line justified as a member of $Ax_p$ does; for $\AI^{\mathrm b}[t,g]$, $M(\pi_i)\le g(|\varphi_i|)$; for $\AI^{\mathrm{b2}}[t,g]$, $M_{\mathrm{nl}}(\pi_i)\le g(|\varphi_i|)$; for $\AI[t,d]$, $\mathrm{size}(\pi_i)\le d(|\varphi_i|)$. The weight is $2^{-|p|}$.
$\FIcert[t',d']$: a hypothesis is a program $V$ with outputs in $\{\mathrm{acc},\mathrm{rej},\perp\}$ and $\mathrm{time}_V(\varphi,c)=O(t'(|\varphi|+|c|))$ on all inputs; $f_V(\varphi)$ is acc (rej) if some $c$ with $|c|\le d'(|\varphi|)$ has $V(\varphi,c)=\mathrm{acc}$ (rej), and undefined otherwise; $B\cup\Gamma_{f_V}$ must be consistent; $V$ is compatible if $f_V$ labels every datum correctly; the weight is $2^{-|V|}$.
\emph{Polynomial budgets.} $\AI^{\mathrm b}[\mathrm{poly},\mathrm{poly}]$ mixes over triples $(p,i,j)$, with $A_{p,i}:=\{\chi:U_0(p,\chi)=\text{yes within }(|\chi|+2)^i\text{ steps}\}$ satisfying (F1) with $B$, derivations of material at most $(|\varphi_i|+2)^j$, and weight $w(p)2^{-2\lceil\log_2(i+1)\rceil-2\lceil\log_2(j+1)\rceil-2}$; $\AI^{\mathrm{a1}}[\mathrm{poly},\mathrm{poly}]$ likewise with every axiom line of size at most $(|\varphi_i|+2)^j$; $\FIcons^{\mathrm{poly}}$ is as in \cref{def:time:penalties}. Constant factors in running times are absorbed by raising the exponent, as $|\chi|+2\ge2$.
\emph{Literal sequences.} $L$ contains a constant $e$, unary function symbols $s_0,s_1$ and a unary relation symbol $R$; $t_w:=s_{w_1}(\cdots s_{w_k}(e)\cdots)$ and $\varphi_w:=R(t_w)$, so $|\varphi_w|=|w|+2$; $B=\emptyset$; $D^X$ lists the data $(\varphi_w,[w\in X])$ over all words $w$ in length-lexicographic order; $\Gamma_{f_X}$ is consistent (the term algebra with $R:=\{t_w:w\in X\}$). Results whose proofs compute $\varphi_w$ from $w$ hold for every injective encoding computable and invertible in polynomial time, the paper's $R(\num w)$ included; the template constructions read $w$ off $t_w$.
\end{definition}

\noindent Every axiom set constructed below is a set of sentences, so it satisfies (F1); so do template instance sets. A finite injective renaming extends to a permutation.

\begin{lemma}[Subformula lemma]\status{proved; computed}\src{short-derivations Lemmas 1.1, 2.1, 2.2, Cor 2.3, Prop 2.4; scripts: c1\_subformula, referee\_logic r1\_subformula\_independent, r3\_sharp\_constant}\label{lem:time:subformula}
(a) Every derivation $\pi$ from $Ax$ containing a line $\varphi$ becomes, by deleting lines, a normal derivation from $Ax$ with last line $\varphi$; so $I(\pi)$ and the size do not grow.
(b) Let $\pi$ be normal with last line $\varphi$ and $J:=I(\pi)\cup\{\varphi\}$. There is an injective map $o$ from the lines of $\pi$ to the formula nodes of the members of $J$ (nodes of different members counted separately) such that every axiom or MP line sits at $o(\lambda)$ with $0$ abstractions, and every Gen line $\gamma$ sits at $o(\gamma)$ with $k\ge0$ abstractions, $q_1,\dots,q_k$ being the parameters abstracted along a chain of Gen steps that starts at $\gamma$. Hence every line has size at most $\max_{\alpha\in J}|\alpha|$, and $\mathrm{size}(\pi)\le\sum_{\alpha\in J}F(\alpha)\le(M(\pi)+|\varphi|)(M(\pi)+|\varphi|+1)/2$.
(c) For the least material $M_{\min}(\varphi)$ and least size $\ell_{\min}(\varphi)$ of derivations of $\varphi$ from $Ax$: $M_{\min}(\varphi)\le\ell_{\min}(\varphi)\le(M_{\min}(\varphi)+|\varphi|)(M_{\min}(\varphi)+|\varphi|+1)/2$; more precisely, every derivation $\pi$ of $\varphi$ can be replaced by one with instances among $I(\pi)$ and size at most $\sum_{\alpha\in I(\pi)\cup\{\varphi\}}F(\alpha)$.
(d) The constant $\tfrac12$ cannot be lowered: for sentences $r\ne b$, the root of $b$ not $\forall$, $\beta:=\forall^kr\to b$ and $Ax:=\{r,\beta\}$, the derivation $r$, $\forall r,\dots,\forall^kr$ (vacuous Gen), $\beta$, $b$ is normal, and its size and $(M+|b|)(M+|b|+1)/2$ are both $k^2/2+O(k)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Delete every line whose formula occurred earlier, redirecting citations to the first occurrence; cut after $\varphi$ and keep only its ancestors, in order. Justifications stay valid and nothing is added.
(b) \emph{Axiom and MP lines.} Let $o(\alpha)$ be the root of $\alpha\in I(\pi)$, and for an MP line $B$ with major premise $A\to B$ let $o(B)$ be the right child of $o(A\to B)$. The major premise has root $\to$, so it is not a Gen line (root $\forall$); by induction on the line number $o(A\to B)$ is defined and carries $A\to B$, so the subtree at $o(\lambda)$ is $\lambda$.
\emph{Gen lines.} For a Gen line $\gamma$ choose a chain $\gamma=\gamma_0,\dots,\gamma_k=:e$ in which $\gamma_{i+1}$ is justified by Gen on $\gamma_i$, abstracting $q_{i+1}$, and $e$ is the minor premise of an MP line or the last line. It exists and ends: by normality each $\gamma_i$ other than the last line is a later premise, not a major one (root $\forall$ against $\to$), so a minor or a Gen premise, and line numbers increase. If $e$ is the minor premise $A$ of an MP line with major premise $A\to B$, let $o_e$ be the left child of $o(A\to B)$; if $e=\varphi$, let $o_e$ be the root of $\varphi\in J$ (then $\varphi$ is a Gen line, so $\varphi\notin I(\pi)$). As $e=\mathrm{Gen}_{q_k}(\cdots\mathrm{Gen}_{q_1}(\gamma)\cdots)$, one descends from $o_e$ through $k$ quantifier nodes; $o(\gamma)$ is the node reached, and $\gamma$ sits there with $k$ abstractions.
\emph{Injectivity.} If $\lambda$ sits at $v$ and the subtree at $v$ has no dangling index, $\lambda$ is that subtree: an abstracted parameter occurring in $\lambda$ would leave an index pointing above $v$. Let $\lambda\ne\lambda'$ with $o(\lambda)=o(\lambda')=v$. If both are axiom or MP lines, both equal the subtree at $v$, against distinctness. If $\lambda$ is one and $\lambda'$ a Gen line, the subtree at $v$ is $\lambda$, which has no dangling index, so $\lambda'=\lambda$. If both are Gen lines, with chains of lengths $k\le k'$, the node $k$ levels above $v$ is $o_e$, with subtree $e=\gamma_k$, and $\gamma'_k$ sits there, so $\gamma'_k=\gamma_k$; as each line has one justification, $\gamma'_{k-1}=\gamma_{k-1}$, and so on down to $\gamma'=\gamma$: a contradiction.
\emph{Bounds.} $|\lambda|$ is the size of the subtree at $o(\lambda)$, so injectivity gives $\mathrm{size}(\pi)\le\sum_{\alpha\in J}F(\alpha)$ and the bound on each line. Each node of $\alpha$ lies in the subtree of each of its formula-node ancestors, so $F(\alpha)\le\sum_u(\mathrm{depth}(u)+1)$; in breadth-first order the $i$-th node has depth at most $i-1$, so $F(\alpha)\le|\alpha|(|\alpha|+1)/2$. As $x\mapsto x(x+1)/2$ is superadditive and $\sum_{\alpha\in J}|\alpha|\le M(\pi)+|\varphi|$, the last bound follows.
(c) $M(\pi)\le\mathrm{size}(\pi)$, as every member of $I(\pi)$ is a line; conversely normalise by (a) and apply (b).
(d) The lines are distinct and each is used by the next. $\mathrm{size}=(k+1)|r|+k(k+1)/2+|\beta|+|b|$ with $|\beta|=|r|+k+1+|b|$, and $M=|r|+|\beta|$; also $J=\{r,\beta,b\}$ and $F(\beta)=|\beta|+\sum_{j=1}^k(|r|+j)+F(r)+F(b)$, so $\sum_JF-\mathrm{size}$ is a constant.
\end{proof}

\noindent Normality is needed: unused lines, such as vacuous Gen chains, and repeated lines can be added to any derivation at will. Material counts the logical instances too; the case where they are free is short-derivations Prop 2.6, not reproduced here.

\begin{lemma}[Renaming; canonical closure]\status{proved; computed}\src{short-derivations Lemma 4.1; scripts: c3\_short\_axioms}\label{lem:time:renaming}
(a) For an injective renaming $\rho$ of parameters and a derivation $\pi$, $\rho(\pi)$, applied to every line and to the parameter of every Gen step (vacuous ones included), is a derivation with the same sizes whose nonlogical axiom lines are the $\rho$-images of those of $\pi$; under (F1) it is a derivation from $Ax$.
(b) Let $Ax$ satisfy (F1), let $D_H$ be the set of formulas with a derivation from $Ax$ all of whose lines have size at most $H$, and let $\mathrm{can}(\chi)$ rename the parameters of $\chi$ to $p_0,p_1,\dots$ in order of first occurrence. Then $\mathrm{can}(D_H)$ is the least set $S$ of canonical formulas of size at most $H$ that contains $\mathrm{can}(\alpha)$ for every axiom instance $\alpha$ of size at most $H$ (logical or in $Ax$), contains $\mathrm{can}(B)$ whenever it contains some $A\to B$ and $\mathrm{can}(A)$, and contains $\mathrm{can}(\mathrm{Gen}_p(X))$ whenever it contains $X$, $p$ occurs in $X$ or is one fixed parameter not in $X$, and $|\mathrm{Gen}_p(X)|\le H$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) $\rho(A\to B)=\rho A\to\rho B$; $\rho(\mathrm{Gen}_p\chi)=\mathrm{Gen}_{\rho p}(\rho\chi)$, as $\rho$ is injective; the logical schemas are closed under injective renaming (A4: $\rho(\forall B\to B[t])=\forall\rho B\to(\rho B)[\rho t]$; A5's side condition concerns indices; reflexivity has no parameter; in substitutivity $\rho B'$ arises from $\rho B$ by replacing occurrences of $\rho p$ by $\rho q$).
(b) By (a), $D_H$ is closed under injective renamings, so $\chi\in D_H$ iff $\mathrm{can}(\chi)\in D_H$. $S\subseteq\mathrm{can}(D_H)$ by induction on the construction of $S$ (an axiom is one line; for MP concatenate the derivations of $A\to B$ and $A$ and add $B$, which is smaller than $A\to B$; Gen adds one line). Conversely, by induction on a derivation with lines of size at most $H$: for an MP line $B$, $\mathrm{can}(A\to B)=\sigma A\to\sigma B$ for the canonicalising $\sigma$ and $\mathrm{can}(\sigma A)=\mathrm{can}(A)\in S$, so $\mathrm{can}(B)\in S$; for a Gen line, $\mathrm{can}(\lambda)=\sigma\lambda$ and $\mathrm{Gen}_{\sigma p}(\sigma\lambda)=\sigma\mathrm{Gen}_p(\lambda)$ has the canonical form of $\mathrm{Gen}_p(\lambda)$, and all vacuous Gens give the same formula.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The derivation code.} $\mathrm{Code}$ writes each line as a 2-bit tag (axiom, MP, Gen), then the Elias-$\gamma$ codes \citep{elias1975universal} of its premise line numbers plus one and, for Gen, of its parameter number plus one, then its formula in preorder with $b_s:=\lceil\log_2(\sigma_L+6)\rceil$ bits per token, an index or parameter being an escape token followed by the $\gamma$ code of its number plus one; a 2-bit end tag closes the code. $|\chi|_{\mathrm{bit}}$ is the length of a formula's code, so $|\chi|\le|\chi|_{\mathrm{bit}}$ and $|\neg\chi|_{\mathrm{bit}}=|\chi|_{\mathrm{bit}}+b_s$.

\begin{proposition}[Horn templates simulate a verifier]\status{proved; computed}\src{short-derivations Prop 6.1, Rem 6.4(3); scripts: c5\_templates, referee\_complexity rc2\_templates\_left}\label{prop:time:horn}
Let $M$ be a deterministic three-tape machine (input, certificate, work; tape alphabet $\Gamma\cup\{\text{blank}\}$, $\{0,1\}\subseteq\Gamma$) that computes $V(w,c)\in\{\mathrm{acc},\mathrm{rej},\perp\}$, halts on every input, outputs $\perp$ unless $w,c\in\{0,1\}^*$, and has $f_V$ consistent; let $X_{\mathrm{acc}}$ ($X_{\mathrm{rej}}$) be the set of words with an accepting (rejecting) binary certificate. Assume the normal form: no transition writes a blank, and none moves a head right from a cell that is blank after the step (at no cost: add a symbol that $M$ treats as blank and write it instead). Let $L\supseteq\{e,\mathrm{pr},R,C\}\cup\{s_a:a\in\Gamma\}$ with $\mathrm{pr}$ a binary function symbol and $C$ a binary relation symbol, and $B=\emptyset$. A tape with its head is coded by two terms over the $s_a$ ending in $e$: the cells left of the head, nearest first, and the cells from the head on; a configuration in state $q$ is $\mathrm{conf}(q,\dots):=\mathrm{pr}(t_q,\mathrm{pr}(l_0,\mathrm{pr}(r_0,\dots)))$, $t_q$ a word term for $q$'s index. $T_M$ consists of first-order-pattern templates, all of whose metavariables are $0$-ary term metavariables: Start, $C(x,\mathrm{conf}(q_0,(e,x),(e,z),(e,e)))$; one template $C(x,\text{pattern})\to C(x,\text{result})$ per transition (state, three read symbols and, for each head that moves left, its left neighbour or the left end); Accept, $C(x,\mathrm{pr}(t_{q_{\mathrm{acc}}},y))\to R(x)$; and Reject, $C(x,\mathrm{pr}(t_{q_{\mathrm{rej}}},y))\to\neg R(x)$. Then:
(i) $T_M$ has $O(|Q_M|)$ templates, each of size $O(\log|Q_M|)$, for fixed $\Gamma$;
(ii) $T_M\cup\Gamma_{f_V}$ is consistent; for words, $T_M\vdash R(t_w)$ iff $w\in X_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and $T_M\vdash\neg R(t_w)$ iff $w\in X_{\mathrm{rej}}$;

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(iii) Cite the Start instance with $x:=t_w$, $z:=t_c$; $\tau$ times, cite the transition instance at the current configuration and apply MP; cite Accept or Reject and apply MP. Configuration terms have size $O(\tau+|w|+|c|+\log|Q_M|)$.
(ii) Take the free term algebra $\mathcal H$ with equality the identity, and let $C^{\mathcal H}$ be the closure of the Start instances under the transition templates. Distinct templates have disjoint patterns, so the facts derived from one Start instance form a single run. For $x=t_w$, $z=t_c$ it is $M$'s run on $(w,c)$, by the normal form. If $x$ or $z$ has a node other than an $s_a$ or $e$, the run follows $M$ on the longest word prefixes $(\mathrm{wp}(x),\mathrm{wp}(z))$ and gets stuck when a head reaches that node, where $M$ would read a blank; if it halts first, $M$ makes the same run on the prefixes, since the node is never read. \emph{Model 1}: $R:=\{t_w:w\in X_{\mathrm{acc}}\}\cup\{\text{non-word }x:\text{some run from }x\text{ accepts}\}$. Accept holds by construction. Reject holds on words as $X_{\mathrm{acc}}\cap X_{\mathrm{rej}}=\emptyset$, and on a non-word $x$ because an accepting and a rejecting run from $x$ would give $V(\mathrm{wp}(x),\cdot)=\mathrm{acc}$ and $=\mathrm{rej}$ on binary certificates, so $\mathrm{wp}(x)$ would be a binary word (as $V$ outputs $\perp$ otherwise), against the consistency of $f_V$. $\Gamma_{f_V}$ holds. This gives consistency and $T_M\vdash R(t_w)\Rightarrow w\in X_{\mathrm{acc}}$. \emph{Model 2}: $R$ on words is the complement of $X_{\mathrm{rej}}$, the same as Model 1 on non-words; it gives $T_M\vdash\neg R(t_w)\Rightarrow w\in X_{\mathrm{rej}}$. The converses are (iii). Instances with parameters are read under closure; in $\mathcal H$ a closure holds iff all its closed instances do, and these are instances again.
(i) Count. The last claim: checking a computation is decidable, so $V$ halts on every input, and $f_V=f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:time:separations}(c)]\src{short-derivations Prop 4.12; scripts: referee\_logic r4\_a2\_finite\_theory}
Let $X$ be decidable and $M$ a machine in the normal form of \cref{prop:time:horn} computing $V(w,c):=\mathrm{acc}$ if $w\in X$ and $\mathrm{rej}$ if $w\notin X$, for binary $w,c$, and $\perp$ otherwise. Let $T^\forall_M$ be the set of universal closures of the templates of $T_M$: a finite set of sentences, of largest size $c_M$ and total size $\Sigma_M$. Model~1 of \cref{prop:time:horn}(ii) satisfies $T^\forall_M\cup\Gamma_{f_X}$, as its elements are closed terms, so a universal closure holds iff all its closed instances do. In the derivation of \cref{prop:time:horn}(iii) with $c$ empty, replace each cited instance by its closure in $T^\forall_M$ followed by A4 instances and MP, which are logical; the nonlogical instances used are members of $T^\forall_M$. For an unbounded nondecreasing $h$ with $h(m)\ge m+1$, the decider of $T^\forall_M\cup\{\varphi_w^{[w\in X]}:h(|\varphi_w|)<c_M\}$, a finite set of sentences, is (a2)-compatible with all of $D^X$, the finitely many short data being cited as themselves; for (b2) the same holds with $\Sigma_M$ in place of $c_M$. So every decidable $X$ is in the fit class, for every $t\ge$ linear.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\Cref{cor:time:hard} is tight on literal data]\status{proved, given the transcript method of \citet{book1970time} (known; not checked)}\src{short-derivations Cor 6.2(iii), (iv)}\label{cor:time:tight}
On literal sequences: (i) if $X\in\NTIME(\tau)$, $\tau(m)\ge m$ time-constructible, then a finite first-order-pattern theory $T$ with $T\cup\Gamma_{f_X}$ consistent derives every $\varphi_w$, $w\in X$, within size $c_X(\tau(|w|)+|w|)^2$; (ii) so for the $X$ of \cref{cor:time:hard}(a) (with word terms), which lies in $\NTIME(s^{c_1+1})$, a finite template theory consistent with $\Gamma_{f_X}$ derives each $\varphi_w$, $w\in X$, within size $c(s(m)^{c_1+1}+m)^2$, while every finite $\DT$ theory consistent with $\Gamma_{f_X}$ needs size above $s(m)$ infinitely often.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(i) A verifier reads a guessed transcript of a run of a nondeterministic machine for $X$ (the transitions taken, with the symbols read and written) and checks it tape by tape on its work tape, in time $O(\tau+|w|)$, with a binary certificate of length $O(\tau)$ \citep{book1970time}. It only accepts, so $X_{\mathrm{rej}}=\emptyset$ and Model~1 of \cref{prop:time:horn} sets $R$ to $X$ on words, so the negative literals of $\Gamma_{f_X}$ hold as well. Apply \cref{prop:time:horn}(iii). (ii) The proof of \cref{cor:time:hard}(a), guess and check, works for word terms as for $R(\num w)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Soft charges]\status{proved; computed}\src{short-derivations Def 5.1, Thm 5.2(a), (b), Prop 5.3; scripts: c4\_cert\_embedding, referee\_complexity rc1\_rate\_one}\label{prop:time:soft}
For a hypothesis $p$ let $\ell^{\mathrm{bit}}_p(\psi)$ be the least code length of a derivation of $\psi$ from $Ax_p$ ($\infty$ if none). \emph{Soft axiom induction} $\AI^\sigma_\kappa[t]$ has $W(D):=\sum_p2^{-|p|}\prod_i2^{-\kappa\ell^{\mathrm{bit}}_p(\varphi_i^{b_i})}$ over deciders with time $O(t)$ and $Ax_p$ consistent (H\"anni's graded score with $g(\ell)=2^{-\kappa\ell}$, both polarities, not normalised; (F1) is not required). \emph{Soft certificate function induction} ${\FIcert^\sigma}_{\kappa,\mu}[t']$ has $W(D):=\sum_V2^{-|V|}\prod_i2^{-\kappa\lambda_V(\varphi_i^{b_i})-\mu|\varphi_i|_{\mathrm{bit}}}$ over verifiers with time $O(t')$ and $B\cup\Gamma_{f_V}$ consistent, $f_V$ using certificates of any length, where $\lambda_V(\varphi^1)$ ($\lambda_V(\varphi^0)$) is the least length of an accepting (rejecting) certificate. Both are semimeasures, and $W(\emptyset)$, the weight of the consistent hypotheses, depends on neither rate. Let $e_1:=31b_s+24$, $r:=31b_s+26$, $c'$ ($c''$) a constant plus $\log_2$ of the inverse empty-data weight of soft axiom (certificate) induction. For every $D$:
(a) $\ell_{{\FIcert^\sigma}_{\kappa,\kappa}[t^+]}(D)\le\ell_{\AI^\sigma_\kappa[t]}(D)-\kappa\sum_i(2+b_s[b_i=0])+c'$;
(b) $\ell_{\AI^\sigma_\kappa[t'^\#]}(D)\le\ell_{{\FIcert^\sigma}_{2b_s\kappa,2\kappa}[t']}(D)+\kappa e_1|D|+c''$, and $\ell_{\AI^\sigma_\kappa[t'^\#]}(D)\le\ell_{{\FIcert^\sigma}_{r\kappa,r\kappa}[t']}(D)+c''$;
(c) on the all-negative literal sequence, $\ell_{\AI^\sigma_\kappa}(D^\emptyset_n)-\ell_{{\FIcert^\sigma}_{\kappa_c,\kappa_s}}(D^\emptyset_n)=(\kappa-\kappa_s)\sum_{i\le n}|\varphi_{w_i}|_{\mathrm{bit}}+\kappa(b_s+4)n+O(1)$; so no pair of rates $(\kappa_c,\kappa_s)$ gives two-sided constant regret with $\AI^\sigma_\kappa$, and the factor $r$ in (b) cannot be $1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Semimeasures.} For a consistent hypothesis at most one of $\varphi,\neg\varphi$ has finite cost, and every factor is at most $1$.
(a) Let $V_p(\varphi,c)$ be acc if $c$, followed by $\mathrm{Code}(\varphi)$ and the end tag, decodes to a derivation of $\varphi$ from $Ax_p$, rej if this holds for $\neg\varphi$, and $\perp$ otherwise. The code of a shortest derivation of $\varphi^b$ without its last formula and end tag is a certificate, so $\lambda_{V_p}(\varphi^b)\le\ell^{\mathrm{bit}}_p(\varphi^b)-|\varphi^b|_{\mathrm{bit}}-2$, and $V_p$'s factor $2^{-\kappa\lambda-\kappa|\varphi|_{\mathrm{bit}}}$ is at least $2^{-\kappa\ell^{\mathrm{bit}}_p(\varphi^b)+\kappa(2+b_s[b=0])}$. The certificate contains the codes of all parameter numbers, so no renaming hypothesis is needed. $V_p$ checks in time $O(t^+)$, $\Gamma_{f_{V_p}}\subseteq\Cn(Ax_p)$ is consistent with $B$, $|V_p|\le|p|+c$, and $p\mapsto V_p$ is injective; sum over $p$ and use $W_{\FIcert^\sigma}(\emptyset)\le1$.
(b) With $\theta_c$ as in the proof of \cref{prop:time:budgets}(c) and no bound on $|c|$, $A_V$ is decided in time $O(t'^\#)$ and $\Cn(B\cup A_V)=\Cn(B\cup\Gamma_{f_V})$. The derivation $\theta_c$, $\theta_c\to\varphi^b$, $\varphi^b$ has code length $2b_s|c|+2|\varphi^b|_{\mathrm{bit}}+29b_s+24$ ($\theta_c$ has $|c|+8$ ordinary nodes and six index nodes of $1+b_s$ bits; four 2-bit tags; the $\gamma$ codes of the two premises, $1+3$ bits), which is at most $2b_s|c|+2|\varphi|_{\mathrm{bit}}+e_1$ and, as $|\varphi|_{\mathrm{bit}}\ge1$, at most $r(|c|+|\varphi|_{\mathrm{bit}})$. Sum over $V$ and use $W_{\AI^\sigma}(\emptyset)\le1$.
(c) A code lists at least one line, whose 2-bit tag and formula precede the 2-bit end tag, so $\ell^{\mathrm{bit}}_p(\psi)\ge|\psi|_{\mathrm{bit}}+4$ and $\ell_{\AI^\sigma_\kappa}(D)\ge\kappa\sum_i(|\varphi_i^{b_i}|_{\mathrm{bit}}+4)$. On $D^\emptyset$ the decider $p_\emptyset$ of $\{\neg\varphi_w\}$ derives each datum in one line, of code length $|\varphi_w|_{\mathrm{bit}}+b_s+4$, so $\ell_{\AI^\sigma_\kappa}(D^\emptyset_n)$ is $\kappa\sum_{i\le n}(|\varphi_{w_i}|_{\mathrm{bit}}+b_s+4)$ up to an additive term in $[0,|p_\emptyset|]$. Every $\FIcert^\sigma$ factor is at most $2^{-\kappa_s|\varphi_i|_{\mathrm{bit}}}$, which $V_\emptyset(\varphi_w,\varepsilon):=\mathrm{rej}$ attains with $\lambda=0$; so $\ell_{\FIcert^\sigma}(D^\emptyset_n)$ is $\kappa_s\sum_{i\le n}|\varphi_{w_i}|_{\mathrm{bit}}$ up to a term in $[0,|V_\emptyset|]$. If $\kappa_s\le\kappa$ the difference tends to $+\infty$; if $\kappa_s>\kappa$ it tends to $-\infty$, since $\sum_{i\le n}|\varphi_{w_i}|_{\mathrm{bit}}\ge\sum_{i\le n}|w_i|\ge(n/2)(\log_2n-3)$ in length-lexicographic order.
\end{proof}

\noindent So at a common rate the certificate side gains at least $2\kappa$ bits per datum on every sequence. No soft fit classes or soft separations from deterministic function induction are claimed: every soft loss diverges on every infinite sequence, by the bound in (c).

\begin{proposition}[The parameter channel]\status{proved; computed}\src{short-derivations Prop 7.1; scripts: referee\_logic r2\_parameter\_channel}\label{prop:time:channel}
Let $V$ be a verifier with time $t_V$ and a time-constructible certificate bound $L_V$, and let $\mathrm{cert}(N)$ be the binary expansion of $N+1$ without its leading $1$. Let $A^{\mathrm{par}}_V$ consist of $\neg\varphi\to\neg(p_N=p_N)$ for $V(\varphi,\mathrm{cert}(N))=\mathrm{acc}$ and of $\neg\neg\varphi\to\neg(p_N=p_N)$ for $V(\varphi,\mathrm{cert}(N))=\mathrm{rej}$, over sentences $\varphi$ with $|\mathrm{cert}(N)|\le L_V(|\varphi|)$. Then $\Cn(B\cup A^{\mathrm{par}}_V)=\Cn(B\cup\Gamma_{f_V})$; $A^{\mathrm{par}}_V$ violates (F1); with time measured in symbols its membership is decidable in time $O(|\chi|^2+L_V(|\chi|)+t_V(|\chi|+L_V(|\chi|)))$; and every $\varphi^b$ with $f_V(\varphi)=b$ has a 9-line derivation from it of size $9|\varphi^b|+54$, material $5|\varphi^b|+40$ and largest instance $3|\varphi^b|+13$, whatever the certificate. So for $X\in\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$ this set fits $D^X$ under (a1) and (b) at linear budgets, with polynomial membership, and \cref{prop:time:aone}(a) without (F1) would put $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$ inside $\bigcup_c\DTIME(2^{cm\log m})$, an inclusion that is open.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The closure of a member is $\forall x(\neg\varphi\to\neg x=x)$, equivalent to $\varphi$ (for rejections to $\neg\varphi$), and every member of $\Gamma_{f_V}$ has a member. Renaming $p_N$ to $p_0$ gives a member only if $V$ decides $\varphi$ with the empty certificate, so (F1) fails once some $\varphi$ needs a non-empty one. Membership: parse $\chi$, reject if $\varphi$ contains a parameter, read at most $L_V(|\varphi|)+2$ bits of $N+1$, and run $V$; a sentence has only indices bounded by its depth. Derivation, with $\psi:=\varphi^b$ and $E:=(p_N=p_N)$: $\forall(\#0=\#0)$ (reflexivity); $\forall(\#0=\#0)\to E$ (A4); $E$; $E\to(\neg\psi\to E)$ (A1); $\neg\psi\to E$; $\neg\psi\to\neg E$ (the member); $(\neg\psi\to\neg E)\to((\neg\psi\to E)\to\psi)$ (A3); $(\neg\psi\to E)\to\psi$; $\psi$, of sizes $4$, $8$, $3$, $|\psi|+9$, $|\psi|+5$, $|\psi|+6$, $3|\psi|+13$, $2|\psi|+6$ and $|\psi|$. For $X\in\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$ take $V$ combining verifiers for $X$ and its complement; $L_V$ and $t_V$ are polynomial, and with $|\varphi^b|\le|\varphi|+1$ the budgets $h(m)=3m+16$ and $g(m)=5m+45$ suffice. \Cref{prop:time:aone}(a), if it held for this set, would give the inclusion stated.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Truncating parameter indices]\status{proved; computed}\src{short-derivations Lemma 7.2, Thm 7.4(a), (b); scripts: c6\_parameters, Part A}\label{lem:time:truncate}
Write formulas as strings of cells, an index or parameter number being a marker cell, its binary expansion plus one bit per cell, and an end cell. Let a machine reading its input sequentially decide membership in $Ax$ within $T(|\chi|)$ steps, $T$ nondecreasing, and let $\pi$ be a derivation from $Ax$ whose nonlogical axiom lines have size at most $H$. Put $K:=T(H)$, let $q_0,\dots,q_{r-1}$ be the parameters of $\pi$ (Gen parameters included) whose numbers $N$ have more than $K$ bits in the expansion of $N+1$, and $w:=\lceil\log_2(r+1)\rceil$. Let $\rho$ fix the other parameters and map $q_j$ to the parameter whose number plus one has as expansion the first $K$ bits of $q_j$'s, then $1$, then $j$ in $w$ bits. Then $\rho(\pi)$ is a derivation from $Ax$ with the same line sizes, its parameter numbers have at most $K+1+w$ bits, and $r\le\mathrm{size}(\pi)$. Consequently \cref{thm:time:ntime} holds as stated for axiom sets that violate (F1), with membership time measured in symbols, and so does the inclusion of the fit class of reading (b) in $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\rho$ is injective: the images have exactly $K+1+w$ bits and distinct serials, while the fixed parameters have at most $K$. So logical axioms, MP and Gen stay valid (\cref{lem:time:renaming}(a) uses only injectivity). A nonlogical axiom line $\chi$ and $\rho\chi$ agree on their first $K+1$ cells: before the first parameter with more than $K$ bits the cells are equal, that parameter's bits start at some cell $s\ge2$, after its marker, and its first $K$ bits are kept. The machine halts within $T(|\chi|)\le K$ steps on both inputs, as $|\rho\chi|=|\chi|\le H$, and reads only cells $1,\dots,K+1$, so $\rho\chi\in Ax$. Every such parameter occurs in a line or names a Gen step, so $r\le\mathrm{size}(\pi)$. For \cref{thm:time:ntime}: if $\varphi_w$ has a derivation of size at most $\ell$, it has one whose parameter numbers have $O(\ell^e)$ bits ($K=O(\ell^e)$, $w\le\log_2(\ell+1)+1$), so of bit length $O(\ell^{e+1})$; the machine of \cref{thm:time:ntime} guesses and checks such derivations. For reading (b), \cref{lem:time:subformula}(c) turns material at most $g$ into size at most $G$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Without (F1), the finer statements (the clock $2^{O(h\log h)}$ of \cref{prop:time:aone}(a) and the certificate length of \cref{prop:time:budgets}(b)) are not claimed; membership time measured in bits rather than symbols is treated in short-derivations Prop 7.1(e) and Thm 7.4.
