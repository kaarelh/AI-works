\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}

The positive answers of \cref{sec:intro:answers} need data drawn from a law of the model; the theorems humans state are not such data. We interpret the three refinements, state the good version, and go through H\"anni's note, related work and open problems.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The three refinements}\label{sec:disc:proposal}

\paragraph{Templates.} Templates, which H\"anni proposed against his collapse, block the acceptance part of his schema and the reflection schema (\cref{prop:time:notemplate,prop:pa:refl}), not the collapse itself: over $\PA$ one ground reflection sentence reproduces every $\Sigma_n$-sound assigner on $\Sigma_n$ sentences (\cref{prop:time:collapse}). Inside the class membership is matching, so the citation likelihood, its Dirichlet sum and the experiments' two-step chain can be computed exactly over a pool (\cref{prop:exp:exact}); the general derivation likelihood is only a computable real whose positivity is undecidable (\cref{prop:model:compute}). Unexpectedly, templates bring in the instance schema $\varphi(z)$, as simple as $\forall x\varphi$ and implying exactly the instances, which $\forall x\varphi$ never overtakes on closed instances in the comparisons analysed (\cref{thm:univ:B}); and usage, not the logical boundary of a schema, decides whether it is split (\cref{rem:ident:mdl}).

\paragraph{A derivation-length prior.} As a generative derivation grammar with a data-independent normaliser it supplies the size principle that H\"anni's scores lack (\cref{prop:model:whichsize}). Its code length counts grammar choices, which can be exponentially fewer than the written symbols (\cref{prop:time:lonesize}); to price computation, length should be measured in written symbols ($\Lsig$, or the graded score divided by its normaliser; \cref{rem:model:graded}). Read as ``statements given without proof in our data set should be fairly easily derived from axioms'', it makes the data theorems and identifies the working set of primitives of whoever produced them, frequently used lemmas included, not a canonical axiomatisation (\cref{prop:pa:memo}, \cref{prop:univ:quant}(c)).

\paragraph{A time penalty.} The penalty the question names, on the time to generate axioms or check axiomhood, leaves the direction from function to axiom induction intact: padded Craig sets have polynomial membership and polynomial-delay enumeration (\cref{prop:time:cheap,thm:time:onesided}). It leaves proof search free, so, as H\"anni objected, penalising consistent function induction but not deduction makes axiom induction strictly stronger, through free deduction rather than its axioms (\cref{sec:time:free}). Derivation size in written symbols prices the collapse (\cref{rem:time:summary}); a bound on each derivation's axiom material, logical instances included, one reading of his proposal of short axioms, makes axiom induction certificate function induction (\cref{thm:time:sandwich}). For plain $\Lone$ only a logarithmic charge is proved, and a polynomial one is conjectured (\cref{conj:time:poly}).

\subsection{A good version, and what it cannot promise}\label{sec:disc:good}

Collecting the pieces, a good version of the inducer has:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Hypotheses and prior}: finite sets of $\DT$ templates, a proper prefix-code prior and Dirichlet weights (\cref{def:model:prior,lem:model:kraft}); inconsistent theories removed by refutation at growing depth (\cref{prop:pa:refute}) or reported as a fourth value $\Inc$ (\cref{def:sound:tri}).
\item \emph{Likelihood}: a derivation grammar with a data-independent normaliser and a uniformly subcritical instantiation grammar (\cref{def:model:grammar,def:model:lone}), in written symbols ($\Lsig$) if it is to price computation; a model of which theorems get reported, by a known filter ($\Lsel$) or a prior over filters (\cref{thm:univ:omega}(e); for $\forall x\varphi$, \cref{sec:univ:intuition}); and a noise component or corruption channel, so that one false datum does not refute the true theory (\cref{prop:univ:robust}, \cref{rem:ident:nearmiss}).
\item \emph{Output}: the triple $(\Bel,\Dis,\Indep)$, a belief function with its plausibility (\cref{sec:sound:tri}); and a verifier that accepts $s$ when $\Belc(s)\ge1-\delta$ (\cref{rem:sound:belc}), with a threshold that shrinks with $n$ when the weights are learned and the likelihood is linear in them (\cref{thm:sound:shrink}); otherwise with the averaged guarantee (\cref{thm:sound:avg}).
\end{itemize}

\emph{What it can promise}, when the data are drawn from a law of the model: concentration on the generator class and convergence of predictions (\cref{thm:ident:doob,thm:ident:predded}); with plain $\Lone$ (parameters admissible) identification of the theorem set, with $\Lzero$ of the instance union, and with a selection model only of the reported part $\Th(T)\cap S$, deductive questions beyond $S$ keeping their prior share (\cref{cor:ident:deductive,thm:univ:omega}); no bound on the number of templates and a way around Gold's theorem (\cref{thm:ident:limit,prop:ident:gold}); soundness of the verifier with probability $1-\delta/\pi(C^*_d)$ for fixed weights, on average over learned weights, and with a shrinking threshold at each weight vector for likelihoods linear in the weights; for a derivation-grammar likelihood with learned weights only the averaged version is proved (\cref{thm:sound:fixed,thm:sound:avg,thm:sound:shrink}).

\emph{What it cannot promise.} It cannot single out the actual axioms: inside the generator class the posterior is the prior (\cref{cor:ident:prior}). It cannot certify $\forall x\varphi$ from closed instances (\cref{thm:univ:omega}). With learned weights it removes a false spare sentence $\sigma$, and with it, when $T^*$ refutes $\sigma$, the inconsistent theory $T^*\cup\{\sigma\}$, only polynomially (\cref{prop:ident:spare,rem:ident:sparetotal}): the likelihood charges a theory for the mass it spends off the data, not for being inconsistent (\cref{prop:pa:incons}), so certifying consistency needs refutation at growing depth (\cref{prop:pa:refute}). Above all, it promises nothing when the data are not drawn from a law of the model, the case the question cares about: in finite classes the posterior then goes to the best-fitting generator, weaker or unsound by turns (\cref{thm:ident:kl}, \cref{tab:ident:misspec}), in the full class even this can fail (\cref{rem:ident:fullclass}), and in an example a verifier on it accepts a falsehood with probability~$1$ (\cref{prop:sound:misspec}). The main lever is to model how the data were produced (which theorems are reported, how often each axiom form is used, which mistakes occur), a choice positive data cannot check.

\subsection{H\"anni's note, point by point}\label{sec:disc:hanni}

His two variants are scores without the size principle; ``does not contradict'' alone learns nothing beyond compatibility (\cref{prop:model:whichsize}(b), \cref{prop:pa:nc}). His graded score, normalised, is a two-part code (\cref{rem:model:graded}), which makes good his reading of the scores as a model of how the statements get generated. The trichotomy is best kept as a triple (\cref{sec:sound:tri}). His equivalence holds with the constant he expects, via Craig's trick, in the semimeasure convention he also considers; his consistency argument needs two sorts or a sound assigner (\cref{thm:time:equiv,prop:time:single}); his ``easy'' separation is \cref{prop:time:fiall}. Generating ``only a very small subset of observations'' is what a likelihood with a data-independent normaliser does. Allowing for ``making some mistakes'' is harmless for the general results if the mistakes are a noise component or corruption channel of the well-specified generator (\cref{prop:univ:robust,rem:ident:nearmiss}); modelled as axioms, as in E4(C1), they are accepted as theorems (\cref{rem:sound:e4}). Grading near misses is a corruption channel; his time-bounded note plausibly has an analogue for derivation-bounded inducers (\cref{conj:time:polytime}), and his predictor $S$ is incomparable with axiom induction (\cref{thm:time:diagonal}).

\subsection{Relation to the literature}\label{sec:disc:lit}

We cite and do not re-prove (unchecked references: \cref{app:ver:refs}). \Cref{thm:univ:confirm} is the countable-class analogue of the confirmation of universal hypotheses by the universal prior \citep{hutter2007universal}, though what is confirmed is ``all future data are instances'', not derivability; confirmation is not stepwise monotone \citep{leike2015solomonoff}, and for truth Gaifman's condition applies (\cref{prop:univ:gaifman}; \citealp{gaifman1964concerning}). \Cref{thm:ident:doob} is Doob's argument \citep{doob1949application}, extended to larger models by \citet{schwartz1965bayes} (see \citealp{ghosal2017fundamentals}); misspecified posteriors concentrate on KL-minimisers under conditions \citep{berk1966limiting,kleijn2006misspecification} not checked here. Gold's theorem limits learning from text \citep{gold1967language,angluin1980inductive}, not from stochastic positive data \citep{horning1969study,angluin1988identifying}. The size principle is that of \citet{tenenbaum2001generalization}, the code-length reading is MDL \citep{rissanen1978modeling} with the KT estimator \citep{krichevsky1981performance}, spare slots are overfitted mixture components \citep{rousseau2011asymptotic}, and $\Bel$, $\Pl$ are a belief function and its plausibility \citep{shafer1976mathematical}. The collapse results use Craig's trick \citep{craig1953axiomatizability}, the nondeterministic time hierarchy \citep{cook1972hierarchy,seiferas1978separating,zak1983turing} and \citet{pudlak1998lengths}; the diagonal separation is a log-loss form of an argument of \citet{legg2006elegant}, and certificate function induction rests on \citet{cook1979relative}; Kreisel's conjecture is related (\cref{rem:univ:kreisel}). AS is the non-Bayesian counterpart of this paper, close to inductive logic programming \citep{muggleton1991inductive}, and IL supplies its Ville argument \citep{ville1939etude}.

\subsection{Open problems}\label{sec:disc:open}

Main open problems: identification for every weight vector, and rigorous spare-slot rates, also for a spare in the span of $T^*$'s components but outside their hull (\cref{thm:ident:limit}(c), \cref{prop:ident:spare}); whether equal laws or instance sets imply equivalence for single $\DT$ templates (\cref{prop:ident:splits}(c)); unsound mass in the full class (\cref{rem:ident:fullclass}); completeness under the shrinking threshold, and soundness at a fixed weight vector for derivation-grammar likelihoods with learned weights; reversal through $\wedge$-detours, the split--memoriser races over $\Q$, and the limit of \cref{prop:univ:open}(d) for countable classes with learned-weight provers; \cref{conj:time:poly}; whether templates block the collapse for merely consistent assigners, and the rejection part of H\"anni's schema; templates for non-literal data, soft certificate correspondences with additive overhead, and the case $\cplx P\ne\cplx{NP}$, $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}=\cplx P$ (\cref{sec:time:free}); for $\PA$, the posterior over all template unions, non-read-once induction templates (\cref{conj:pa:broad}) and unrefuted inconsistent theories; search beyond a pool; $\PA$ under $\Lone$ in code. Further items and sketches: \cref{app:ver:sketches}.

\paragraph{Closing.} This was a check of an idea, not the design of a prover. A template prior with a generative derivation likelihood is a coherent inducer: it confirms predictions and, on data drawn from the model, finds a system with the right theorems and a verifier that adaptive provers rarely fool. It does not turn human-stated theorems into their axioms, because mathematicians do not select what they write down the way the model generates data.
