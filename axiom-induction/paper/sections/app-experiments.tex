\subsection{Grammar, prior code and pools}\label{app:exp:impl}

\emph{$\Qg$.} A term node is $0$, $S$, $+$, $\cdot$ with weights $.45$, $.35$, $.10$, $.10$, a variable with total weight $.3$ split uniformly over the variables in scope (holes and bound variables alike), or $p$ with weight $.15$ where the guard allows it. A formula node is $=$, $<$, $\neg$, $\wedge$, $\vee$, $\to$, $\leftrightarrow$, $\forall$, $\exists$ with weights $.35$, $.20$, $.10$, $.10$, $.05$, $.10$, $.02$, $.05$, $.03$. Weights are renormalised over the options available. The mean number of same-sort children is $0.75$ for closed terms, at most $0.75$ for other terms and $0.72$ for formulas, so bodies are finite almost surely; the referee's exact size distributions agree.

\emph{Prior code.} $\mathrm{bits}(\tau)$ is $-\log_2$ of a subcritical stochastic grammar over templates (formula and term nodes, metavariable occurrences, arities and guards); a theory of $m$ distinct components costs $|\gamma(m)|+\sum_i\mathrm{bits}(\tau_i)-\log_2m!$ with $\gamma$ the Elias code. The referee's independent implementation gives the same lengths to four decimals; Kraft's inequality holds (\cref{rem:model:priors}).

\emph{Pool construction.} The pool at $n$ is built by four rules. (1) Hand theories, listed per experiment in \cref{app:exp:results}. (2) For every build point $b\le n$: $\Min$ of random subsets of $D_b$ (AS Thm~3.3), skeleton clusters of $D_b$ (\DTRC{} without refutation, stopped at $k$ clusters; AS \S7.1) and, in PA runs, \DTRC{} with the PA refuter on $D_b$ and the observed-root split \expth{frag-observed@}$b$ ($\Q$ plus the fragments of the motive roots seen in $D_b$); a theory built at $b$ stays in the pool for all $n\ge b$. Build points: E1 $b\in\{1,2,\dots,64\}$ (powers of 2); E3(a) $\{8,32,64\}$; E2, E3(b), E7 $\{8,16,32,64\}$; E4 and E6 have none. (3) In PA runs (E2, E3(b), E7), for every pool theory $T$ with at least two components, $\Trim(T,D_n)$: the components $i$ of $T$ with $a_i(d)>0$ for some datum $d$ of $D_n$, i.e.\ those that could have been the first citation of a datum seen (under \Lzero: those that match one). (4) $\Mem(D_n)$. The first version built the data-derived theories once, from the first 16, 40 or 64 data, and used them at every $n$, and had no trimmed theories (\cref{rem:exp:refuted}).

\emph{Pools.} Theories are deduplicated at each $n$ by their components (canonical template plus guards); if $\Mem(D_n)$ equals a pool theory, that theory keeps its mass and $\Mem$ becomes an alias without mass (the first version counted it twice, which affected E6 at $n=1,2$ only). Under \Lzero{} $\Mem(D_n)$ is the best memoriser covering $D_n$: a further ground sentence costs prior bits and the factor of \cref{prop:ident:spare}(a) and cannot raise the likelihood. In E2 a theory of $\Q$ axioms and induction-type templates is tagged equivalent to $T^*$ iff it contains Q1, Q2, Q4--Q7 and a full-induction template ($\TInd$, \expth{IndSwap} or a connective fragment); other tags come from a budgeted refuter, and an unrefuted theory is ``unknown''. The false probes are $0=S0$, $\forall x\,(x+0=0)$, induction with a wrong base, $\forall x\,(Sx=x)$ and $\forall x\forall y\,(y=x)$; held-out induction instances are drawn outside the training stream.

\emph{\Lselc.} The class probabilities $c_i$ are exact for components $\forall^a\chi$ with $\chi$ quantifier-free, term metavariables only, every leading variable used and no MP components (a finite Markov chain on the number of leading $\forall$ and the presence of $p$); other components are left out of \Lselc{} pools. Monte Carlo agrees on the eight E6 forms (40000 chains each; largest $|z|=2.17$).

\subsection{Proof of \texorpdfstring{\cref{prop:exp:exact}}{the exactness proposition}}\label{app:exp:exact}

\emph{Proof idea.} (a) is the matching theorem, AS Thm~2.5. (b) expands the product and takes Dirichlet moments (\cref{lem:model:dirsum}); the upper bound uses concavity of the log-likelihood on the simplex. (c) Every step chooses among finitely many outcomes of total probability $1$, and no run fails. (d) Each predecessor set is in bijection with the matchers of a derived $\DT$ template, which are unique. The details follow.

\emph{(a)} Bodies from $\Qg$ contain no parameter other than $p$, so $\tau\theta$ is already in closure-normal form and the event ``the datum is $s$'' is the event $\tau\theta=s$. By AS Thm~2.5 at most one $\theta$ satisfies $\tau\theta=s$, so $P(\tau\theta=s)=\sum_{\theta:\tau\theta=s}\prod_M\Qg(\theta(M))$ has at most one term, $\prod_M\Qg(\theta_s(M))$. A body containing $p$ has probability $0$ under a closed guard. \qed

\emph{(b)} Expanding, $\prod_j\sum_iw_ia_{ij}=\sum_z\prod_iw_i^{n_i(z)}\prod_ja_{z_jj}$, and the Dirichlet moments $\Ex\prod_iw_i^{n_i}=\frac{\Gamma(K\alpha)}{\Gamma(K\alpha+n)}\prod_i\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+n_i)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}$ give the formula (\cref{lem:model:dirsum}).
\emph{The programme.} The summand depends on $z$ only through $\prod_ja_{z_jj}$ and the counts. A datum covered by one component contributes a fixed factor and one count. For the others, with $S_j:=\{i:a_{ij}>0\}$, the sum over their assignments is the polynomial $\prod_j\sum_{i\in S_j}a_{ij}x_i$ evaluated by the linear functional $x^c\mapsto\prod_i\Gamma(\alpha+c_i+c^0_i)/\Gamma(\alpha)$ ($c^0$ the fixed counts), times $\Gamma(K\alpha)/\Gamma(K\alpha+n)$. A dense array (at most three overlapping components) or variable elimination over count vectors computes the coefficients exactly. In the latter a component's factor is applied, and its count dropped, once no later group contains it; this is valid because the functional is a product over components.
\emph{The bounds.} All terms are nonnegative, so any single term is a lower bound. For the upper bound, $\Ex_wL(w)\le\max_wL(w)$ with $L(w)=\prod_j\sum_iw_ia_{ij}$, and $f:=\ln L$ is concave on the simplex. For an interior $w$ with $L(w)>0$ and any $w^\circ$ in the simplex, $f(w^\circ)\le f(w)+\nabla f(w)\cdot(w^\circ-w)\le f(w)+\max_i\partial_if(w)-n$, since $\nabla f(w)\cdot w^\circ\le\max_i\partial_if(w)$ and $w\cdot\nabla f(w)=\sum_j\sum_iw_ia_{ij}/\sum_kw_ka_{kj}=n$. \qed

\emph{(c)} At most $J$ rules are applied. Each step chooses among finitely many outcomes (stop, or a rule that applies) with probabilities summing to $1$; each rule's input (a term from $\Qelim$, bodies from $\Qg$, a uniform choice among the applicable MP components) has a probability distribution ($\Qg$ is proper). A rule is applied only when it applies, so no run fails; the runs have total probability $1$, and the datum is a function of the run. \qed

\emph{(d)} Let $G_k(s)_i$ be the probability that the chain reaches step $k$ at $s$ after citing component $i$, divided by $w_i$; so $G_0(s)_i$ is the \Lzero{} coefficient of (a). For $1\le k\le J$ the chain leaves $s'$ only if a rule applies to it, then takes rule $r$ with probability $(1-c_{\mathrm{stop}})c_r/\sum_{r'\text{ applies to }s'}c_{r'}$ and input $\xi$ with probability $P(\xi)$. Hence
\[
G_k(s)=\sum_{(s',r,\xi):\,r(s',\xi)=s}G_{k-1}(s')\,(1-c_{\mathrm{stop}})\,\frac{c_r}{\sum_{r'\text{ applies to }s'}c_{r'}}\,P(\xi),\qquad a_i(s)=\sum_{k\le J}G_k(s)_i\,\mathrm{stop}_k(s),
\]
with $\mathrm{stop}_k(s)=1$ if $k=J$ or no rule applies to $s$, and $c_{\mathrm{stop}}$ otherwise. The recursion is exact iff every predecessor $s'$ with $G_{k-1}(s')>0$ is enumerated. The following give complete predecessor sets.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(d\arabic*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{$\forall$E guided by templates.} Let $U=\forall x\,B$ be a component whose derived template $D:=B[u/x]$ ($u$ a fresh $0$-ary term metavariable) is in $\DT$. Then $x$ is no argument of a metavariable occurrence (else an argument would contain $u$), so $x$ occurs only at rigid positions, bodies never contain $x$, and $(B\theta)[t/x]=D(\theta\cup\{u\mapsto t\})$. So the pairs $(\theta,t)$ with $\forall\mathrm E(U\theta,t)=s$ correspond to the matchers of $D$ against $s$, of which there is at most one (AS Thm~2.5). If $x$ does not occur in $B$, the predecessor is $\forall x\,s$, with total weight $\sum_t\Qelim(t)=1$.
\item \emph{$\forall$E from $P$ or $\forall x\,P(x)$ at step $0$.} For a parameter-free $s$ the predecessors are $\forall x\,\psi_S$, with $\psi_S$ the sentence $s$ with a nonempty set $S$ of occurrences of one closed term $t$ replaced by $x$. Since the PCFG probability is a product over nodes and every node of $\psi_S$ lies in a context with a variable, $\Qg(\forall x\,\psi_S)=\Qg(\forall x\,s)\prod_{o\in S}r_o$ with $r_o:=(p_{\mathrm{var}}/\nu_o)/\Qg_{\mathrm v}(t)$; here $p_{\mathrm{var}}$ is the normalised variable weight, $\nu_o$ is $1$ plus the binder depth at $o$, and $\Qg_{\mathrm v}(t)$ is the probability of $t$ in a context with a variable. So $\sum_{S\ne\emptyset}\Qg(\forall x\,\psi_S)=\Qg(\forall x\,s)\big(\prod_o(1+r_o)-1\big)$; for $\forall x\,P(x)$ the same holds up to the factor of the root $\forall$. The code uses this closed form only in theories without MP components, where these parameter-free predecessors admit only $\forall$E. If $s$ contains $p$ and the guard is closed, $t$ contains $p$ and $S$ is all occurrences of $t$.
\item \emph{Gen, then $\forall$E} ($J=2$). A step-1 Gen output is $\mathrm{gen}(s_0)=\forall x\,s_0[x/p]$ for a cited $s_0=T\theta_0$ containing $p$, and $\forall\mathrm E(\mathrm{gen}(s_0),t)=s_0[t/p]$. Let $D$ be the component with its rigid occurrences of $p$ replaced by a fresh $0$-ary metavariable $u$, and suppose $D\in\DT$ (no rigid $p$ inside an argument; the track's notes leave this hypothesis implicit). Then $s_0[t/p]=D\theta$ with $\theta(M)=\theta_0(M)[t/p]$ and $\theta(u)=t$, so $\theta$ is the unique matcher of $D$ against $s$. Closed-guard bodies satisfy $\theta_0(M)=\theta(M)$; open-guard bodies $\theta_0(M)$ arise from $\theta(M)$ by replacing some occurrences of $t$ by $p$; without a rigid $p$, $t$ occurs in some open-guard body. The code enumerates exactly these candidates and keeps those with $\forall\mathrm E(\mathrm{gen}(s_0),t)=s$.
\item \emph{Gen and MP.} $\mathrm{gen}^{-1}(\forall x\,\chi)=\chi[p/x]$ if $\chi$ uses $x$ and $s$ has no parameter. For an MP component $A\to B$, $B\in\DT$ contains $A$'s metavariables, so the matcher $\theta$ of $B$ against $s$ is unique and the predecessor is $A\theta$.
\item \emph{Pruning.} A candidate is skipped only if its ($\forall$-spine, parameter flag) is outside the abstract set of step $k-1$, computed forward from the components; by induction on $k$ every step-$k$ sentence lies in that set. \qed
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The code admits into \Lone{} pools only theories whose $\forall$-rooted components have derived templates in $\DT$ (twice derived at $J=2$) and whose formula-metavariable components are $P$ or $\forall x\,P(x)$ at $J=1$ (none at $J=2$). A term with more than 12 occurrences in a datum stops the enumeration and raises a counter; it was $0$ in every run. The support check does not test the hypothesis of (d3). It holds for every component without a rigid $p$, which includes every hand theory of E1 and E6; for data-derived theories at $J=2$ (E1 \expgen{allq2}) exactness rests on the computed checks: the guided recursion equals a brute-force recursion over all subsets of all occurrences to $10^{-9}$ on samples from 10 theories at $J=1,2$ (same class, so not independent), and the referee's own recursion agrees on 904 triples at $J=1,2$ to $2.1\cdot10^{-14}$.

\subsection{Proof of \texorpdfstring{\cref{prop:exp:comm}}{the commutativity proposition}}\label{app:exp:comm}

\emph{(a)} $A_{xy}$ and $A_{yx}$ differ by the order of two universal quantifiers. $M_x^{\mathrm{open}}$ cites $\forall x\,(x+p=p+x)$ (body $z:=p$), which under the closure reading is $\forall w\forall x\,(x+w=w+x)$, i.e.\ $A_{yx}$; and each of its instances $\forall x\,(x+b=b+x)$, $b$ a term with at most the parameter $p$, follows from $A_{xy}$ by $\forall$E (and Gen on $p$ under the closure reading). Likewise $M_y^{\mathrm{open}}$ cites $\forall y\,(p+y=y+p)$, i.e.\ $A_{xy}$. Part (a) corrects a remark of the experiments track's referee that open guards cannot reach $\forall x\forall y$ under the single-parameter convention: one bound variable and the parameter already give two variables.

\emph{(b)} Take the domain $\N\sqcup\{\alpha,\beta\}$, standard on $\N$, with $<$ arbitrary, $S\alpha=\alpha$, $S\beta=\beta$, and for $a\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$, $n\in\N$: $a+n=n+a=a$; $a\cdot n=n\cdot a=a$ if $n\ne0$ and $0$ if $n=0$; $\alpha+\alpha=\alpha\cdot\alpha=\alpha$, $\beta+\beta=\beta\cdot\beta=\beta$, $\alpha+\beta=\alpha\cdot\beta=\alpha$, $\beta+\alpha=\beta\cdot\alpha=\beta$. Each $\N\cup\{a\}$ is closed under $S,+,\cdot$ and $+$ is commutative on it. Closed terms denote naturals, so the closed instances of $S_{ab}$ hold. An instance $\forall x\,(x+t=t+x)$ of $M_x$, $t$ closed of value $n$, holds for $x\in\N$ by arithmetic and for $x=a$ since $a+n=a=n+a$; likewise for $M_y$. An instance $\forall w\,(f(w)+g(w)=g(w)+f(w))$ of $S_{ab}^{\mathrm{open}}$ (one parameter) holds because for $w\in\N$ both values lie in $\N$ and for $w=a$ both lie in $\N\cup\{a\}$. But $\alpha+\beta=\alpha\ne\beta=\beta+\alpha$, so $A_{xy}$ fails and, by soundness, is not derivable. With two parameters, $p_1+p_2=p_2+p_1$ would be an instance of $S_{ab}^{\mathrm{open}}$; the convention excludes it. \qed

\noindent Computed (\texttt{checks/check\_models.out}): the 285 closed terms with at most 7 nodes denote naturals; the instances of $S_{ab}$, $M_x$, $M_y$ hold for $x\in\{0,\dots,14,\alpha,\beta\}$, and those of $S_{ab}^{\mathrm{open}}$ for 154 terms with at most 5 nodes and $w\in\{0,\dots,3,\alpha,\beta\}$; $A_{xy}$ and the instance $\forall x\,(x+p=p+x)$ of $M_x^{\mathrm{open}}$ fail at $(\alpha,\beta)$.

\subsection{The experiments in detail}\label{app:exp:results}

This subsection gives each experiment's setup, its tables and the findings not stated where \cref{tab:exp:summary} points. Pool names are explained in the caption of \cref{tab:exp:summary}.

\paragraph{E1: $\forall x\varphi$ from data about $\varphi$.}
For $\varphi\in\{x+0=x,\ 0+x=x,\ \neg Sx=0\}$, seeds 0--4 and $n=1,2,4,\dots,256$, five generators are used: \expgen{sch} (\Lzero{} citations of $\sigma_\varphi=\varphi(z)$, closed terms); \expgen{all} ($\Cch1$ from $\Hall$, closed $\forall$E terms, so about half of the data are $\forall x\varphi$ itself); \expgen{allq} (the same with open terms, e.g.\ $\varphi(Sp)$); \expgen{open} ($\Cch1$ from $\Hopen$, whose Gen steps emit sentences such as $\forall y\,\varphi(Sy)$); and \expgen{allq2} ($\Cch2$ from $\Hall$: $\forall$E with an open term, then Gen). The pool ($15$--$28$ theories at $n=256$) holds $\Hall$, $\Hsch$, $\Hopen$, $\Hboth$, $\Hall\cup\Hopen$, the over-specific $\{\varphi(0),\varphi(Sz)\}$, two root splits, three spare-slot theories, over-general generalisations of $\varphi(z)$ and $F_0$, causal theories and $\Mem(D_n)$. The likelihoods are \Lzero, \Lone{} with the generator's own chain, and \Lselc{} on \expgen{sch} data. Every generator has one component and is an atom of the prior in the fixed hand pool, so Doob's argument in the $(T,w)$ form covers the hand theories; the causal theories and $\Mem(D_n)$ are covered by no consistency theorem (\cref{rem:ident:x5}). Results in \cref{tab:exp:e1}.

\noindent\emph{(1) The factor of \cref{thm:univ:odds}, exactly.} In $\Cch J$ with closed $\forall$E terms, $\Hall$ emits $\forall x\varphi$ with probability $c_{\mathrm{stop}}$ and otherwise $\varphi(t)$ with probability $c_{\mathrm{ch}}\Qg(t)$, after which no rule applies; $\Hsch$ emits $\varphi(t)$ with probability $\Qg(t)$. So on closed instances the $\log_2$ odds $\Hsch:\Hall$ gain $\log_2(1/c_{\mathrm{ch}})=1$ bit per datum under \Lone{} and stay at the prior odds under \Lselc{} ($c_\forall=c_{\mathrm{ch}}$, $c_{\mathrm{sch}}=1$); this is experiments Prop X6 (proved), the $\Cch J$ case of the unnormalised $\mu_T$ formula $c^n$ of \cref{thm:univ:odds} with $c=c_{\mathrm{ch}}$. Computed: $n+0.090$ bits ($x+0=x$, $0+x=x$) and $n+1.440$ ($\neg Sx=0$) at every $n$ and seed, within $1.1\cdot10^{-11}$ bits of $n$ plus the prior difference from the referee's code; the \Lselc{} tie holds to $10^{-13}$.
\emph{(2) The $\omega$-gap.} Under \Lselc{} $P(T\vdash\forall x\varphi\mid D_n)$ tends to the prior share, in this code, of the provers among the theories tied with $\Hsch$ (\cref{thm:univ:omega}(b), \cref{tab:univ:share}), while held-out closed instances get mass $\ge0.999$ from $n=8$ in every run except \expgen{open} data under \Lzero{} (\cref{thm:univ:confirm}).
\emph{(3) The size principle.} At $n=8$ on \expgen{sch} data, $\Mem(D_n)$ has mass $\le2.5\cdot10^{-12}$ and the over-general theories $\le7\cdot10^{-5}$, both falling exponentially (\cref{thm:univ:size}). This is one memoriser; the memoriser class can decay more slowly (\cref{prop:univ:memo}). Under \Lone{} $\Mem(D_n)$ is not provably the best memoriser, since a ground $\forall$-sentence can raise its likelihood (the referee found $\Mem(D_n)\cup\{\forall x\,(x+0=x)\}$ 11--16 bits worse, the margin shrinking).
\emph{(4) Quantified data.} From a prover of $\forall x\varphi$, \Lone{} finds the generator by $n=16$ and $P(T\vdash\forall x\varphi\mid D_n)\to1$ (\cref{prop:univ:open}(a), \cref{prop:univ:quant}(a)). Under \Lzero{} derived sentences can only be axioms, so theories that list them win, and with Gen outputs of unbounded variety \Lzero{} ends at $\Mem(D_n)$.

\paragraph{E8: splits under a derivation likelihood.}
Let $\varphi(t):=(t+0=t)$, $\psi(t):=(0+t=t)$, whole $:=\{\varphi(z),\psi(z),\varphi(z)\to\psi(z)\}$, and split $:=$ whole with $\varphi(z)$ replaced by $\varphi(0),\varphi(Sz),\varphi(z+z'),\varphi(z\cdot z')$ ($K=4$ root productions). Under $\Cch1$ a citation of $\varphi(t)$ stops or applies MP with the cited conditional, giving $\psi(t)$, which can also be cited: the component behind a $\psi$-datum is latent. Data come from $\Cch1$ on whole at fixed weights $(0.5,0.25,0.25)$, with bodies from $\Qg$ (\expgen{wellspec}) or from another PCFG (\expgen{skew}: roots $0,S,+,\cdot$ with $.5,.1,.2,.2$); seeds 0--9; $n\le4096$. \Cref{tab:exp:e8} gives Bayes factors without the prior, which adds $76.4$ bits in favour of whole; the findings are in \cref{rem:ident:e8}. At matched weights ($w_\varphi p_f$) the two theories give every datum the same \Lone{} probability, to $1.1\cdot10^{-13}$ (experiments Prop X13, \cref{rem:ident:splitsL1}). With well-specified usage the split loses $1.60$ bits per doubling of $n$ under \Lone{} and $1.76$ under \Lzero{} on $n=1024\ldots4096$ (on $256\ldots4096$: \cref{rem:ident:e8}). With misspecified usage its mean gain per datum at $n=4096$ is $0.1995$ bits under \Lone{} and $0.0731$ under \Lzero; the gain per datum between $n=1024$ and $4096$ is in \cref{rem:ident:e8}.

\begin{remark}[Claims of the first version]\status{refuted}\expbrk\src{experiments \S16.4; referee M1--M4}\label{rem:exp:refuted}
Each claim is kept with its counterexample.
(1) E2: ``in 3 of 5 seeds an unsound lump has mass $\ge0.997$ at some $n\le32$ and a $\delta=0.05$ verifier accepts a false sentence''. True of a pool that used unseen data and lacked $\SeenQ(D_n)$; with causal pools, 2 of seeds 0--4 and 5 of 25. Counterexample to its representativeness: seed 2 at $n=32$, where the old \expth{skel4} (built from 40 data) had mass $1.0$ and the causal \expth{skel4@32} has $0.85$.
(2) E2: ``MAP at $n=8$: \DTRC($n=16$) three times'': built from data 9--16, unseen at $n=8$.
(3) E6: ``the five closed-guard forms are logically equivalent'': \cref{prop:exp:comm}(b).
(4) ``Soundness at a constant threshold for any well-specified data'': fails for multi-component $T^*$ at fixed weights (\cref{ex:sound:constant}).
(5) PA: ``robust at the level of theorems, not of axioms'': E3(b) atomic motives (\cref{prop:pa:atomic}).
(6) E1: ``$P(T\vdash\varphi(t^*)\mid D_n)=1.000$ from $n=8$ in every configuration'': \expgen{open} data under \Lzero{} give $0$ at $n=4,8$.
(7) E2: ``no false probe is accepted'': the mean over seeds hid seeds 1, 2 and 4.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Bounds and counters}\label{app:exp:pools}

\emph{Exact \Lone{} pools.} E1's support check left out 65 (\expgen{open}) and 43 (\expgen{allq2}) data-derived theories for $\varphi=\neg Sx=0$, summed over seeds, mostly skeleton clusters, and nothing else.
\emph{Dirichlet bounds} replaced the exact sum, with the largest posterior mass the theory could have had: in E1 for some skeleton theories and $\Mem(D_n)$ under \Lone, at most $8\cdot10^{-14}$; in E2 for \expth{skel6@32} and \expth{skel6@64} in 14 cases over 25 seeds and both pools, $0$ to double precision; in E3 and E4(B, C), where the largest possible mass is recorded per run, $0$ in every run; in E6, checked in seed 0 only, for $\Mem(D_n)$ in two cases, $0$.
\emph{Fallbacks.} The likelihood and derivability-oracle fallback counters were $0$ in every run of E1, E3(a) and E4; the referee re-ran the 75 E1 jobs with the oracle instrumented (5896 queries, no fallback).

\subsection{Reproduction and checks}\label{app:exp:repro}

All commands run from \texttt{code/} (Python 3.11, numpy 2.4, scipy 1.17). Results are in \texttt{results/*.md} and \texttt{*.json}, the first version's in \texttt{results/v1/}. AS's \texttt{dtrc} code is read, not modified. \texttt{python3 -m pytest -q tests} runs the 21 unit tests (18\,s); \texttt{experiments/e}$k$\texttt{\_*.py} runs E$k$; \texttt{sh run\_all.sh} runs everything (about 50 minutes on 4 shared cores; E3 10 min, E4 32 min, the rest at most 3 min each).
