\section{How the results were established}\label{app:ver}

\subsection{Process}\label{app:ver:process}

\paragraph{Roles.} A research brief (\texttt{research/00-brief.md}) quoted the question and set out hypotheses H1--H7 to be confirmed, refined or refuted; \cref{tab:intro:verdicts} gives their verdicts. Four research tracks then worked in parallel: \emph{model} (the general theory), \emph{universal} (the case $\forall x\varphi$), \emph{pa} ($\PA$ and $\ZF$) and \emph{experiments} (the package \texttt{code/bai} and the experiments E1--E8). Each track wrote notes (\texttt{notes.md}) with seeded check scripts. An adversarial referee for each track read the notes, re-derived the proofs, wrote independent code (\texttt{referee\_code/}) and filed a report (\texttt{referee.md}). The track revised its notes into \texttt{notes-final.md}, which ends with a verification log mapping every referee issue to its resolution. The referees found no fatal error. They raised, by track: model, 5 major and 16 minor issues and 6 questions; universal, 3 major and 11 minor issues and 7 questions; pa, 5 major and 11 minor issues and 5 questions the track had missed; experiments, 5 major and 14 minor issues. A planning editor then wrote an outline, a notation file and a ledger of every claim with its status and source, and reconciled 43 cross-track conflicts (\cref{app:ver:conflicts}). Section writers wrote each section from the final notes only, re-reading the proofs as they went and recording what they found (\cref{tab:ver:writing}). A framing editor wrote the introduction, the discussion and this appendix. Five AI reviewers then read the complete draft: three checked the mathematics and the numbers of the sections against the research notes and their outputs, one the consistency of the whole paper, one its readability. They reported 10 fatal and 66 major issues; the fatal ones were statements that claimed more than their proofs or sources support (among them parts of \cref{thm:univ:B,prop:pa:must,prop:pa:wellspec} and \cref{def:model:scores}). A lead editor checked every fatal and major issue against the research notes and decided the fixes, and four section editors applied them (\cref{tab:ver:writing}, second part; the reviews, decisions and edit logs are in \texttt{research/paper-review/}). Two follow-up tracks on the time penalty came later (\cref{app:ver:followups}).

\paragraph{Track against referee.} In five places the final notes disagree with the referee, with reasons, and the paper follows the final notes. (1) Pa, m11: AS Thm~5.16 holds in closure-normal form with parameters; the real gap is that it concerns a single covering template (\cref{prop:pa:isigma}). (2) Pa, M4: the law G1 does not force a formula metavariable; its 157 skeletons are covered by a term-only theory inside $\ISigma2$, and the posterior keeps $\PA$ through the Occam balance (\cref{ex:pa:skel}). (3) Experiments, M2: open guards do reach $\forall x\forall y$ under the single-parameter convention (\cref{prop:exp:comm}(a)). (4) Universal, M2: the referee's ``$\to0$ over $\Q$'' holds only with bounded split depth (\cref{prop:univ:sentences}). (5) Model, m13: the tightness of the soundness constant holds exactly, not only as a supremum (\cref{rem:sound:tight}).

\subsection{The follow-ups on the time penalty}\label{app:ver:followups}

\paragraph{The correction.} After the first version of this paper, our summary to H\"anni said: ``The time penalty doesn't block your collapse argument. The collapse into function induction (with Craig's trick) survives a penalty on checking or generating axioms.'' He objected (verbatim): ``if we penalize time on the function induction side and in the axiom generator but crucially NOT in the proof search from axioms, then intuitively i disagree with this: i think the time penalty should help? like the function induction guy that runs the proof search from axioms will be extremely slow; there's nothing comparably slow on the axiom induction side''. He is right: the sentence conflated the two directions of \cref{thm:time:equiv}. Penalties on checking or generating axioms leave the direction from function to axiom induction intact (\cref{thm:time:onesided}(a)); the direction from axiom to function induction runs proof search, which they leave free, and once function induction is penalised it fails (\cref{thm:time:onesided}(b), \cref{thm:time:diagonal}). The paper's summary remark said ``do not block the collapse'' without naming the direction, and the abstract, the short answer, A5, the discussion and the README repeated it; all are corrected. H\"anni then proposed (verbatim): ``hmm interesting point. but perhaps we can make AI actually interestingly time-limited by requiring that given statements have derivations from short axioms?''

\paragraph{The tracks and their checking.} Two follow-up tracks took up the objection and the proposal: \texttt{research/tracks/time-followup/} and \texttt{research/tracks/short-derivations/} (the second builds on the final version of the first). Each wrote notes with seeded check scripts, was refereed once, by two adversarial referees who wrote independent code, and was revised into \texttt{notes-final.md}, which ends with a verification log mapping every referee issue to its resolution. Neither was refereed again after its revision. No referee found a fatal issue. \emph{time-followup}: the logic referee raised 2 major and 18 minor issues, the penalty referee 5 major and 20 minor. \emph{short-derivations}: the logic referee raised 1 major and 12 minor issues, the complexity referee 3 major (one shared with the logic referee) and 14 minor. Each track reran its own and its referees' scripts from scratch copies, with byte-identical outputs, except one script changed on purpose (short-derivations \texttt{c2}, a corrected trial-division count that the paper does not use). The writer of \cref{sec:time:free} and \cref{app:time:free} worked from the final notes only. As elsewhere, nothing was checked by a human or in a proof assistant. \Cref{tab:ver:followups} lists what the paper takes from the follow-ups.

\paragraph{Open items found while writing and reviewing.}\label{app:ver:open}
These are not resolved in the paper. (1) Model Lemma 1.2(b) has only a proof sketch; the missing step is named in \cref{app:model:a4}. (2) The direction of \cref{thm:univ:B} is not proved in $\Cand$. (3) In \cref{prop:univ:eqfrag} the full-sum comparison at $q=\frac12$ is open; in \cref{prop:univ:open}(d) the limit $0$ is not proved for countable classes with learned-weight provers. (4) \Cref{prop:ident:spare}(c) needs a finite $\chi^2$-type sum, and a spare in the span of $T^*$'s components but outside their convex hull is not covered; \cref{rem:sound:vacuous} is proved for $\Lzero$ and likelihoods affine in $w$ only; the size of the generator class under near-miss channels is open (\cref{rem:ident:nearmiss}). (5) In \cref{ex:sound:constant} a $\chi^2$ limit uniform over the window $1\ll n\ll1/\varepsilon$ is not written out; completeness under the shrinking threshold, and the strictness of \cref{prop:sound:cautious}(d) for $\Hk k(\DT)$, are open. (6) In \cref{rem:time:upper} the $\Q$-proof size bound is recalled from \citet{pudlak1998lengths} and the size of the Tarski biconditional's proof is not bounded; for \cref{cor:time:hard} with a single $X$, the time-constructibility and gap conditions are not checked; the route to \cref{conj:time:poly} lacks Gen with de Bruijn indices under sharing, and a polynomial bound on the size of shared representations; \cref{prop:time:notemplate} is not proved for the rejection part of the schema, nor for templates whose instances mix the two parts; whether templates block the collapse for merely consistent assigners is open (\cref{prop:time:collapse}); the $\ISigma n$ comparison under a derivation-time penalty proposed by pa's referee was not quantified. (7) Pa: the stronger form of Rem 4.9 (\cref{rem:pa:shift}); \cref{prop:pa:incons} under the normalised $\Lone$; \cref{prop:pa:wellspec} and \cref{prop:pa:isigma}(a) with Dirichlet weights; whether the mass of not-yet-refuted inconsistent theories deriving a false sentence tends to $0$ (\cref{rem:pa:pointwise}). (8) Experiments: exactness of $\Lone$ for data-derived theories at $J=2$ rests on computed checks (brute force at $J=1,2$ and the referee's recursion on 904 triples), not on \cref{prop:exp:exact}(d), whose hypothesis the code does not check. (9) Further open problems of the research notes: whether the two-part maximum can change a deductive limit (\cref{prop:model:max}); whether $\Lone$ separates equivalent theories for all grammar parameters (\cref{prop:ident:sep}); partial splits that only $\Lone$ keeps alive (\cref{rem:ident:mdl}); soundness bounds in terms of the distance from the data law to the pool, and robust posteriors; the strictness in \cref{prop:sound:cautious}(d); an unknown selection learned jointly with the templates (\cref{thm:univ:omega}(e)); formulas with several variables; a single-sorted repair of H\"anni's schema short of Craig's idea (\cref{prop:time:bounded} gives one in the form of his schema, with Craig's padding as a numeral bound); the $\ISigma n$ comparison under a derivation-time penalty; \cref{conj:time:polytime}; theorem data under plain $\Lone$ or the full sum; Collection from Replacement without Foundation; a learned instantiation grammar and cheap remedies for early unsound lumps (\cref{rem:exp:limits}); the generator class under natural corruption channels (\cref{rem:ident:nearmiss}). (10) From the follow-ups on the time penalty (\cref{sec:time:free}; time-followup \S7, short-derivations \S9): the soft certificate correspondence with an additive overhead per datum, and soft separations from deterministic function induction (\cref{prop:time:soft}); at polynomial budgets, the case $\cplx P\ne\cplx{NP}$ with $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}=\cplx P$, and uniform domination in either direction (\cref{cor:time:separations}); the gap between $\DTIME(2^{h})$ and $\DTIME(2^{O(h\log h)})$ for reading (a1); history-using time-bounded predictors such as $S$ against reading (b) at polynomial budgets; whether a single hypothesis proves all diagonal sentences of \cref{thm:time:diagonal} within budgets polynomial in their length; finite template theories on non-literal data, an additive description length, and conservativity of the Horn theories of \cref{prop:time:horn}; whether the regret of the Kt-penalised inducer against $\FIcons^\tau$ is bounded; a computable consistency filter in place of ``$B\cup A_p$ consistent''. The open problem of the discussion of the first version, whether a symbol-size penalty makes axiom induction equal to function induction over assigners with short certificates, is answered for hard bounds by \cref{prop:time:budgets}; for soft charges only a correspondence up to a constant factor in the rate holds (\cref{prop:time:soft}).

\subsection{Reproduction}\label{app:ver:repro}

Every computed number comes from a seeded script whose output is saved next to it.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Track checks.} \texttt{research/tracks/<track>/checks/} holds each track's scripts and their \texttt{.out} files (model c1--c16; universal c1--c10; pa \texttt{test\_nd}, c1--c10; experiments' check scripts), and \texttt{referee\_code/} the referees' independent scripts and outputs. Universal re-ran all ten of its scripts and the referee's seven, and pa all twelve of its scripts, in a scratch directory, with byte-identical outputs; pa's \texttt{c10\_quotes.py} checks 87 quoted numbers of its notes against the outputs.
\item \emph{Code.} From \texttt{code/}: \texttt{python3 -m pytest -q tests} (21 unit tests, including a comparison with the referee's independent implementation); \texttt{sh run\_all.sh} (tests and E1--E8, about 50 minutes on four shared cores); seeds and per-experiment commands in \cref{app:exp:repro}. Results are in \texttt{code/results/*.md} and \texttt{*.json}, the first version's in \texttt{code/results/v1/}.
\item \emph{Paper.} From \texttt{paper/}: \texttt{./build.sh} merges the bibliographies and runs pdf\LaTeX{} and Bib\TeX; \texttt{./test-section.sh <section> <appendix>} builds one section alone.
\end{itemize}
