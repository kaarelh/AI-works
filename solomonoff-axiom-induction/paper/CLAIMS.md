# Claims ledger: the paper on \SAI

*Planning editor, 2026-10-16. Binding for every writer of `paper/`. Every numbered result of the paper (theorem, proposition, lemma, corollary, definition, example, remark with mathematical content, conjecture, question, assumption, numbered table or figure carrying results) is a row here, and every row is an item of the paper. Read with `OUTLINE.md` (what goes where, pages, credit footnotes) and `NOTATION.md` (symbols, macros).*

## 0. How to use this ledger

* **Label** is the LaTeX label (binding; never rename). **Source** is the item in the thread's FINAL record `research/tracks/<thread>/notes-final.md`, by the record's own item number (the records keep their development numbering); put exactly this in `\src{<thread> <item>}`. "syn" = `research/notes-final.md`; "fc" = `research/final-check.md`.
* **Statement (one line)** is a pointer, not a substitute. **Print the record's statement**, with every hypothesis, quantifier, constant and convention, in this paper's notation (NOTATION.md §11 lists the renames). Never strengthen it. Where the record and the synthesis differ, the record wins, as corrected in `final-check.md` §4 (listed in §11 below and marked **fc** in the rows).
* **Status** is the record's status and **how it must be rendered** (codes below). Put the record's status in `\status{...}` (it prints nothing) and render it in prose.
* **Place**: `main` (statement in the section, proof idea in 2–6 sentences, full proof in the section's appendix), `main+proof` (short proof in the main text), `app` (statement and proof in the appendix only; the main text may mention it in one sentence with `\cref`), `prose` (no environment: a paragraph that carries the content, keeping the label on the paragraph with `\paragraph{...}\label{...}` if listed).
* **How to state** gives the binding wording constraints (synthesis §1 "How to state it", §2.8, §5.5, the records' own warnings).
* **Deps**: labels the statement or proof relies on (cite them with `\cref`).

**Results of the earlier report.** Items that the records cite as "AI-paper", "TF" (time follow-up) or "SD" (short derivations) are all in the published earlier report \citet{claude2026axiominduction}: TF and SD are in its §6 and App. E (`../axiom-induction/paper/sections/time.tex`, `app-time.tex`, whose `\src{time-followup …}` / `\src{short-derivations …}` notes name the record item). Cite them by the printed number, read from `../axiom-induction/paper/main.aux` (e.g. `grep -n 'prop:time:aone' ../axiom-induction/paper/main.aux`). Never print "TF", "SD" or "AI-paper".

**Global "how to state" rules (binding for every row).**
1. **Equivalences with SOI are version by version** (syn H1): "\SAIabbr $\asymp$ coin-free consistent \SOIabbr" for the coin-free version; "one-tape \SAIabbr $\asymp$ one-tape consistent \SOIabbr" for $\MA$; Kolmogorov with Kolmogorov. **Never** the unqualified "\SAIabbr $\equiv$ \SOIabbr". They control semimeasure losses, not the meaning of "undefined".
2. **Every loss lower bound names its convention** (NOTATION §3.4).
3. **Shape separations** name the inducer ($\Klst$ or $\KtA$, never $K_A^{\mathcal R}$), the class (over the base variables, or with intermediate concepts) and the round budget. The simplex family is a reasoner gap only "over the base variables" or "for one round". The textbook theorem "is not a theorem about textbooks".
4. **Charged deduction**: any sentence "once deduction is charged the separation disappears" names the reading ((a1), (a2), (b), (b2)).
5. **The lottery with true data is a possibility result with adversarial (diagonal) data.** Say nothing realist about induced theories.
6. **Novelty**: never claim random axiom systems drawn by a universal machine as new (the modified Demski prior samples them); what is new is the single-draw proof-requiring law and its results. For `thm:shape:systems` write "we did not find it stated; it is probably known to specialists", and do not present it as a new lower-bound technique (`bib/bib-report.md` §4.1: the SAT 2026 paper is Beame and Sun's, on CDCL with parity equations, not on encodings). The bibliography report's §3–§4 "how to state it" lines are binding wherever a row below cites them.
7. **The Kepler example** (syn §5.5): every arcminute figure is against plan94, not the sky; minimax values are upper bounds witnessed by elements; the pool is hand-picked and heliocentric; "the syntax decides" only in the noise-free common-tolerance regime; Part C reproduces the abandonment of circles, not Kepler's oval-to-ellipse step; K3's worth depends on Kepler's kinematic parametrisation.
8. **Arguments versus theorems** (syn §2.8, binding): the rows marked As are arguments; the paper never presents them as theorems, and never presents a theorem in a stated model as a theorem about science.
