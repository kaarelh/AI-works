\section{Theoretical terms: Craig, Ramsey and the theoretician's dilemma}\label{sec:terms}

Theories introduce vocabulary that their data do not contain: field, entropy, gene. Hempel's theoretician's dilemma \citep{hempel1958theoretician} makes this a puzzle. If theoretical terms serve their purpose of systematising the observable facts, they are dispensable: by Craig's theorem every recursively enumerable theory has a recursive axiomatisation in the observational vocabulary \citep{craig1953axiomatizability}, and by Ramsey's method they can be quantified away \citep{ramsey1931theories}. If they do not serve it, they are pointless. \SAI gives the question a precise form. Data are labelled sentences of an observational language and hypotheses are axiom systems that may use further vocabulary, so one can ask in which hypothesis classes theoretical terms change the posterior, and what it is that pays: vocabulary, relations or entities. This is the third step of the ladder of \cref{sec:phil:map}. \Cref{thm:terms:dilemma} gives the answer; the rest of the section explains its parts and states the main ones precisely, and \cref{app:terms} has the proofs and the remaining statements.

\subsection{Setting and the dilemma theorem}\label{sec:terms:setting}

\begin{definition}[Observational restriction]\status{definition}\src{terms Def 1.1}\label{def:terms:obs}
Let $\mathcal L=\LO\cup\LT$ be a countable language with equality and computable syntax (finite where derivations are charged), with \emph{observational} part $\LO$; $B$ may contain theoretical symbols. The \emph{observational content} of $A\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ is $T_O(A):=\Cn(A)\cap\Sent_{\LO}$, and $D$ is \emph{observational} if $\dom D\subseteq\Sent_{\LO}$. Let $G_U^O:=\{(w,\chi)\in G_U:\chi\in\Sent_{\LO}\}$, $\MA^{\LO}:=\nu_{G_U^O}$, $\WA^{\LO}(D):=\sum_e\mm(e)\ind[B\cup(\re e\cap\Sent_{\LO})\text{ consistent and}\vdash\psi_D]$, and let $\KA^{\LO}(D)$ be the least $K(e)$ over the same $e$. For a Borel set $\Theta$ of observational contents (subsets of $\Sent_{\LO}$, product topology), $\mathcal C_\Theta:=\{T:T\cap\Sent_{\LO}\in\Theta\}$, and $\UA(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)$, $\MA^{\LO}(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)$, $\WA(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)$, $\WA^{\LO}(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)$ are the class-restricted laws and weights (\cref{def:setting:MA}). Observational restrictions of the penalised versions of \cref{thm:soi:penalty} are their sub-sums over hypotheses whose axioms are $\LO$-sentences on every tape.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Literal data and charged deduction]\status{definition}\src{terms Def 1.2}\label{def:terms:literal}
\emph{Literal data} are those of \cref{def:setting:fresh}, in the fresh-atom setting, with $\LO=\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,R\}$. \emph{Charged deduction} is that of \cref{sec:soi:charged}: a hypothesis decides an axiom set $A_p$ within time $t$, satisfies \cref{ass:soi:F1}, is consistent, and derives each datum in the calculus $\mathsf K$ with every axiom instance, logical ones included, of size at most $h$ (reading (a1)), every nonlogical instance of size at most $h$ ((a2)), material at most $g$ ((b)), or nonlogical material at most $g$ ((b2)); the mixtures are $W_A^{a1}[t,h]$, $W_A^{a2}[t,h]$, $W_A^{b}[t,g]$, $W_A^{b2}[t,g]$. At \emph{polynomial budgets} the degrees of membership time and budget are charged in the prior (Definition E.18 of \citet{claude2026axiominduction}); then $X$ is in the fit class iff one hypothesis is compatible with every $D^X_n$ (Lemma E.11 there).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The named objects]\status{definition}\src{terms Def 1.3}\label{def:terms:named}
$\ThT$ is the first-order theory of the named structure $(\mathfrak T;\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1)$. A theory in a language containing $\LO$ is \emph{about the named objects} if it has a model whose $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-reduct is $\mathfrak T$, and \emph{compatible with the named structure} if it is consistent with $\ThT$.
\end{definition}

The second notion allows objects other than the named ones, but they obey every first-order law of the named objects (for instance, $\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1$ are injective with disjoint ranges that miss $\mathtt e$, and $\mathtt s_ux\ne x$ for every nonempty word $u$). ``Named object'' is our formal stand-in for ``observable thing''.

\begin{theorem}[The theoretician's dilemma in \SAI]\status{proved, with the conditions of the parts listed in the proof}\src{terms Thm 8.1}\label{thm:terms:dilemma}
Data are observational sentences; in (ii)--(iv), literal data under the readings of \cref{def:terms:literal} at polynomial budgets.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{In principle.} With deduction free, restricting hypotheses to the observational vocabulary changes the prior weight of every Borel class of observational contents, the predictive probability of every observational sentence and the cumulative loss by at most a constant, in the one-tape, coin-free and Kolmogorov versions, and also when the time to check or to generate axioms is penalised (under \cref{ass:soi:S,ass:soi:Stape}). Tape by tape, the observational projection of a random theory has exactly the same law on observational data.
\item \emph{Craig's construction in practice.} When derivations are charged, Craig's axiomatisation of a theory whose observational consequences have derivations of size $\mathrm{ds}$ needs derivations of size at least $2^{\mathrm{ds}}$. Every axiomatisation by padded observational literals with polynomial-time membership fits exactly the polynomial-time decidable regularities, under every reading. Axiom sets with theoretical symbols fit $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$ (total material), $\cplx{EXPTIME}$ (every instance short) and every decidable regularity (only the theory's own axioms short); so Craig's construction loses unconditionally under the last two readings and, under the first, iff $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}\ne\cplx P$.
\item \emph{Eliminating the vocabulary in practice.} Theoretical vocabulary can be eliminated at polynomial cost: per hypothesis, with $O(1)$ bits and a polynomial change of budget, under total material, by storing proofs instead of run times; and at the level of fit classes under (a1), (a2), (b2), by storing the eliminated relations in objects that no observational term names. Observational hypotheses, and already finite observational theories, fit what all hypotheses fit.
\item \emph{What is indispensable.} A finite theory in the observational vocabulary with any unary theoretical predicates and constants, compatible with the first-order theory of the named objects, fits exactly the regular regularities, under every reading and also with free deduction. One binary theoretical relation over the named objects restores every decidable regularity ((a2), (b2)), $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$ ((b)) and $\cplx{EXPTIME}$ ((a1)). So does quantifying over unnamed objects; but a finite theory that does so without theoretical vocabulary, and fits a non-regular regularity, must prove some $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-sentence false in the named structure. For the theory built in \cref{thm:terms:binary} that sentence is $\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$, true of no named object.
\item \emph{Free deduction, finite theories.} With deduction free, finite theories gain without bound from a theoretical function (infinity), from theoretical sorts (the theorems of $\PA$) and from a binary relation over the named objects; and r.e.\ axiom induction is finite-theory induction with theoretical sorts up to $O(1)$, coin-free.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) is \cref{thm:terms:horn}. (ii) is \cref{lem:terms:unary,thm:terms:padded,cor:terms:table}, given \cref{cor:soi:fit}, the time hierarchy theorem and $\cplx{APSPACE}=\cplx{EXPTIME}$ \citep{chandra1981alternation}. (iii) is \cref{prop:terms:eliminate,prop:terms:a1fit,thm:terms:binary}. (iv) is \cref{thm:terms:regular,thm:terms:everyregular,thm:terms:binary,prop:terms:ontology}, given the Ehrenfeucht--Fraïssé theorem, the one-tape simulation of multitape machines and, for (a1), $\cplx{APSPACE}=\cplx{EXPTIME}$. (v) is \cref{thm:terms:infinity,thm:terms:PA,cor:terms:freededuction,thm:terms:KCV}; the clause on $\PA$ is given Ryll-Nardzewski's theorem and $\Con(\PA)$, the clause on sorts the $\Sigma_1$-completeness of $\Q$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{In principle}\label{sec:terms:horn}

\Cref{cor:basic:vocabulary} showed that new vocabulary changes old-vocabulary weights by a constant factor. Part (i) needs more: classes of contents, a theoretical background, the penalised versions, and the identity tape by tape.

\begin{theorem}[Theoretical vocabulary costs $O(1)$ bits, class by class]\status{(a), (b), (d) proved; computed (a finite analogue of (d)); (c) proved, under (S) and (S$_{\rm tape}$), given Lemma E.12 and Theorem 6.14 of the earlier report, thm:soi:penalty and prop:soi:classes}\src{terms Thm 2.1}\label{thm:terms:horn}
There is $c$, depending on $V$, the coding, $B$ and $\LO\subseteq\mathcal L$, such that for every finite observational $D$ and every Borel set $\Theta$ of observational contents:
(a) $2^{-c}\UA(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)\le\MA^{\LO}(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)\le2^c\UA(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)$;
(b) the same holds for $\WA(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)$ and $\WA^{\LO}(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)$, and $\lvert\KA^{\LO}(D)-\KA(D)\rvert\le c$;
(c) for each time-constructible $t(m)\ge m^{a^*}$ there is $c_t$ such that, class by class, the observational restrictions of $\WAchk t$ and $\WAgen t$ (all forms) agree with $\WA$ within $2^{\pm c_t}$ under \cref{ass:soi:S}, and those of $\MAchk t$ and $\MAgen t$ agree with $\MA$ within $2^{\pm c_t}$ under \cref{ass:soi:Stape};
(d) for every theory machine $G$ in $\mathcal L$, $G^O:=\{(w,\varphi):\varphi\in\Sent_{\LO},\ B\cup A^G_w\vdash\varphi\}$ is a theory machine emitting only $\LO$-sentences, and on every tape $\omega$: $A^{G^O}_\omega=T_O(A^G_\omega)$; $B\cup A^{G^O}_\omega$ is consistent iff $B\cup A^G_\omega$ is; and $B\cup A^{G^O}_\omega\vdash\varphi^b$ iff $B\cup A^G_\omega\vdash\varphi^b$, for all observational $\varphi$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$. Hence, for every set $\Theta$, the events defining $\nu_{G^O}(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)$ and $\nu_G(\mathcal C_\Theta;D)$ coincide tape by tape, and the two laws are equal.

\noindent Consequently, in each version, posterior odds between two classes of observational contents and predictive probabilities of observational sentences agree within $2^{\pm2c}$, and cumulative semimeasure losses on observational reveal sequences within $2c$.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof idea} (\cref{app:terms:horn}). (d) is compactness. (a) applies (d) to $G_U$ and the mixture identity of \cref{thm:basic:dominance} both ways; (b) maps $e$ to indices of $T_O(\re e)$ and of $\re e\cap\Sent_{\LO}$; (c) pads the observational content as in \cref{thm:soi:penalty}, the only place where Craig's padding enters. $G^O$ is the pull-back machine of \cref{thm:basic:translation} for the inclusion $\Sent_{\LO}\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$. Projecting matters: on a propositional analogue, dropping the theoretical axioms instead changes the law of 37 of 40 random theory machines (a computation; code in the accompanying repository).

\begin{remark}[Inductive systematisation in the mixture]\status{proved (the comparison); the reading is an assessment}\src{terms Rem 2.2}\label{rem:terms:hempel}
By \cref{thm:terms:horn}, the posterior predictive probability of every observational sentence, given observational data, is the same up to $2^{\pm2c}$ whether or not hypotheses may use theoretical vocabulary. So in the universal mixture, with deduction free, theoretical terms are not needed for inductive inference among observational sentences. This is a statement about the mixture, not about a single theory (\cref{rem:phil:hempel}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{In practice: Craig's construction and better eliminations}\label{sec:terms:craig}

Craig's construction makes an r.e.\ theory decidable by padding each consequence with the time at which it was found: the literal $\varphi$ found after $k$ steps becomes the axiom $\varphi^{\wedge(k+1)}$, a conjunction of $k+1$ copies. Under charged deduction this is useless for a precise reason. Canonical proof search tests all $2^{\mathrm{ds}}-1$ strings shorter than the shortest derivation of $\varphi^b$, of size $\mathrm{ds}$; and a set of sentences each equivalent to a literal proves a literal datum only through a member equivalent to it. So every derivation from the Craig set contains a line of size at least $2^{\mathrm{ds}}\lvert\varphi^b\rvert$ (\cref{lem:terms:unary}). For a seven-sentence Horn theory with two theoretical predicates for the parity of the number of 1s, in a small proof system for ground literals and at a word of length 24, a computation from closed forms gives a Craig axiom of more than $10^{204.6}$ symbols, against 795 for an axiom that carries a certificate (\cref{ex:terms:craig}). Call $A\subseteq\Sent_{\LO}$ \emph{run-padded} if each member is a power $\psi^{\wedge m}$ ($m\ge1$) of a literal $\psi\in\{\varphi_w,\neg\varphi_w\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Run-padded axiomatisations fit exactly $\cplx P$]\status{proved; (b) is Lemma E.22 of the earlier report in this form}\src{terms Thm 3.3}\label{thm:terms:padded}
On literal data: (a) if $A$ is run-padded, with membership decidable in polynomial time, and is compatible with every $D^X_n$ under reading (a1), (a2), (b) or (b2) with a polynomial budget $h$, or with derivations of size at most $h$, then $X\in\cplx P$; (b) every $X\in\cplx P$ is fitted under all these readings, at polynomial budgets with polynomial-time membership, by a run-padded set.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof idea} (\cref{app:terms:craig}). The member that a derivation of $\varphi_w^b$ cites and that is equivalent to $\varphi_w^b$ has at most $h(\lvert w\rvert+2)$ symbols, hence at most that many factors; so $w\in X$ iff one of polynomially many powers of $\varphi_w$ lies in $A$. A run-padded axiom carries $O(\log)$ bits, its length, while the axiom $\theta_\gamma\to\varphi^b$ of \cref{thm:soi:sandwich} carries the bits of a certificate $\gamma$. So the natural strengthening to all sets whose members are each equivalent to a literal fails: certificate sets are such sets and fit $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$ under (b).

We did not find a complexity-theoretic form of the classical reply in the literature. \Cref{thm:terms:padded} does not contradict the standard fact that every r.e.\ theory has an axiomatisation decidable in polynomial time, by a variant of Craig's construction \citep{krajicek2025proof}: it concerns what such sets fit when derivations are charged. Against all hypotheses (\cref{cor:soi:fit}), run-padded sets lose unconditionally under (a1), (a2), (b2), and under (b) iff $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}\ne\cplx P$ (\cref{cor:terms:table}).

The vocabulary itself can be eliminated at polynomial cost, by storing what it carries elsewhere. Under reading (b), replacing derivations by certificate axioms $\theta_\gamma\to\varphi^b$, which are observational because $\theta_\gamma$ uses only logical symbols, eliminates it hypothesis by hypothesis at $O(1)$ bits and a polynomial change of budget (\cref{prop:terms:eliminate}): what survives is the proofs. Under the other readings the content goes into objects that no observational term names. In the structure $\mathfrak U$ of words and formal pairs $\mathrm{pr}(a,b)$, with $\mathtt s_0\,\mathrm{pr}(a,b):=a$ and $\mathtt s_1\,\mathrm{pr}(a,b):=\mathrm{pr}(b,a)$, the observational formula $\theta_2(a,b):=\forall y\,(\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1y=y\to(\mathtt s_0y=a\to(\mathtt s_0\mathtt s_1y=b\to R(y))))$ says that $R$ holds of $\mathrm{pr}(a,b)$ (\cref{lem:terms:pairing}). Replacing each atom $\mathsf C(a,b)$ of a binary theoretical relation by $\theta_2(a,b)$, with quantifiers relativised to $\mathrm{Std}(y):=\neg\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1y=y$, translates hypotheses into observational ones (\cref{prop:terms:a1fit}), and finite theories into finite theories. For finite theories one binary relation suffices (\cref{thm:terms:binary}): for every \emph{verifier} $\mathcal V$, a halting machine that accepts or rejects pairs (word, certificate) consistently, there are a finite theory in $\LO\cup\{\mathsf C\}$ about the named objects and a finite set of $\LO$-sentences that prove exactly the literals that $\mathcal V$ certifies, with derivations whose length and line size are linear in the time and space of a one-tape simulation of $\mathcal V$ plus the input length. So finite theories of either kind fit every decidable set under (a2) and (b2), $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$ under (b) and, for alternating machines, $\cplx{EXPTIME}$ under (a1): the fit classes of all hypotheses. The construction compiles $\mathcal V$ into a two-stack program on pairs of words and writes the theory backwards, $\mathsf C(r,l)$ saying that the configuration $(r,l)$ leads to a halting configuration of the right kind (\cref{def:terms:twostack,lem:terms:twostack}). With deduction free the existence part is classical: as stated in \citet{pakhomov2019question}, every r.e.\ theory without finite models has a finitely axiomatised conservative extension in its language plus one binary relation symbol \citep{kleene1952finite}, even one into which every model of the theory expands \citep{craigvaught1958finite}, while additional unary predicates do not suffice in general. To our knowledge the construction with derivation bounds, hence the fit classes under charged deduction, and the observational translation are new; the comparison with unary vocabulary below is in the spirit of Craig and Vaught's example, in a different setting, and we have not established that it is new.

\subsection{Vocabulary or ontology}\label{sec:terms:ontology}

Without objects outside the named structure, unary theoretical vocabulary does not help a finite theory. For $X\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ and a word $v$, the \emph{residual} $X_v:=\{u:uv\in X\}$ is what the subtree below $t_v$ sees, and $X$ is regular iff it has finitely many residuals.

\begin{theorem}[Unary theoretical vocabulary sees only regular regularities]\status{proved, given the Ehrenfeucht--Fraisse theorem}\src{terms Thm 5.1}\label{thm:terms:regular}
Let $T$ be a finite set of sentences in $\LO$ plus unary predicate symbols $P_1,\dots,P_k$ and constants $\mathtt c_1,\dots,\mathtt c_j$ ($k,j\ge0$), compatible with the named structure. If $T\vdash\varphi_w^{\ind[w\in X]}$ for every word $w$, then $X$ is regular. Quantitatively, $X$ has at most $\mathrm{ncl}_q+\lvert\mathrm{Suf}_{\rm c}\rvert$ residuals, where $q$ is the quantifier rank of the relational translation of $\bigwedge T$, $\mathrm{ncl}_q$ the number of classes of rank-$q$ elementary equivalence in the relational signature of \cref{app:terms:ontology}, and $\mathrm{Suf}_{\rm c}$ the set of suffixes of the words $w_i$ with $\mathtt c_i=t_{w_i}$ in a fixed model of $T\cup\ThT$, together with the empty word.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof idea} (\cref{app:terms:ontology}). By $\ThT$ a single edge joins the subtree below $t_v$ to the rest of a model, and unary symbols are labels; so by the composition lemma for Ehrenfeucht--Fraïssé games (\cref{lem:terms:EF}) the labels of one subtree can be replaced by those of another of the same rank-$q$ type without changing the truth of $T$. As $T$ proves the literals, the two residuals coincide.

Conversely, every regular $X$ has a finite observational theory, true in $(\mathfrak T,X)$, with derivations of $O(\lvert w\rvert)$ lines of size $O(\lvert w\rvert)$ (\cref{thm:terms:everyregular}). So, under every reading, finite theories in $\LO$ plus unary predicates and constants, compatible with the named structure, fit exactly the regular sets, while with one binary relation over the named objects, or in $\LO$ alone with quantification over objects outside the named structure, they fit what all finite theories fit (\cref{cor:terms:ontology}); with deduction free the contrast holds for complexities too (\cref{cor:terms:freededuction}). The second way has a price. By \cref{thm:terms:regular} and compactness, a finite theory without theoretical vocabulary that fits a non-regular regularity must prove some $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-sentence false in the named structure; for the observational theory built above that sentence is $\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$, true of no named object (\cref{prop:terms:ontology}). The theory with the binary relation, by contrast, is conservative over the theory of the named objects plus the data (\cref{prop:terms:conservativity}). Ramsey sentences eliminate theoretical predicates by quantifying over them in second order; the pairing gadget does so in first order by quantifying over objects, and then the observational theory must say that there are objects unlike every observable one. With only unary observables \cref{thm:terms:regular} survives, but one binary observable relation breaks it already for the words with as many 0s as 1s (\cref{prop:terms:binaryobs}).

\subsection{Finite theories with free deduction}\label{sec:terms:finite}

With deduction free, r.e.\ hypotheses gain nothing from theoretical vocabulary; finite theories, the single sentences of $\Kfin$ and $\Wfin$ (\cref{def:setting:versions}), can gain without bound. On the data ``there are at least $n$ things'', the least complexity of a consistent sentence in pure equality plus unary predicates that proves the data is $\Kpad(n)\pm c$, which tends to infinity, though more slowly than every computable unbounded function, while with one unary function symbol the sentence saying that it is injective and not surjective gives $O(1)$ (\cref{thm:terms:infinity}); the same phenomenon separates $\Kfin$ from $\KA$ with named objects (\cref{rem:terms:named}), and in literal symbols the costs are at least $n$ in pure equality, $\Theta(\log n)$ with unary predicates and $O(1)$ with the function (\cref{prop:terms:bits}). Here the classical eliminations can be compared directly (\cref{prop:terms:ramsey}): the Ramsey sentence keeps the theory finite but leaves first-order logic; the observational axiomatisation $\{\exists x_1\cdots\exists x_m\bigwedge_{i<j}x_i\ne x_j:m\ge1\}$ stays first-order but is infinite; and no finite first-order theory without a function symbol has the same consequences. On the theorems of $\PA$ as data, finite $\Lar$-theories diverge, by Ryll-Nardzewski's theorem that no consistent extension of $\PA$ in $\Lar$ is finitely axiomatisable \citep{rylnardzewski1952role}, while a finite theory with theoretical sorts and exactly the arithmetical consequences of $\PA$ has bounded complexity (\cref{thm:terms:PA}); $\mathrm{ACA}_0$ is a classical instance (see \citealp{simpson2009subsystems}). Sorts suffice for every r.e.\ theory: for finite $\mathcal L$, r.e.\ axiom induction in $\mathcal L$ is, up to a constant and coin-free, finite-theory induction in a three-sorted extension $\mathcal L^+$ with numbers, assignments and a satisfaction relation: $\Kfin^{\mathcal L^+}=\KA^{\mathcal L}\pm c$ and $2^{-c}\WA^{\mathcal L}\le\Wfin^{\mathcal L^+}\le2^c\WA^{\mathcal L}$ (\cref{thm:terms:KCV}). This is an effective, many-sorted form of the theorem of \citet{kleene1952finite} and \citet{craigvaught1958finite}, of which only the Bayesian consequence is new: the r.e.\ prior already is a prior over finite theories with theoretical vocabulary and entities, which is why adding vocabulary costs nothing there. The equivalence fails for one tape: on the lottery stream of \cref{thm:logic:lottery}, random r.e.\ theories have bounded loss and random finite theories with any theoretical apparatus do not (\cref{rem:terms:onetape}).

A concept can also pay by factoring inferences.\footnote{The idea of introducing a concept that factors a complete bipartite pattern of inferences first appeared in H\"anni's working notes \citep{hanni2026notes}.} If each of $n_A$ unary predicates implies each of $n_B$ others, a theory of atom-to-atom implications needs $n_An_B$ members, and with a new intermediate predicate exactly $\min(n_An_B,n_A+n_B)$; so the new predicate pays in this restricted format iff $(n_A-1)(n_B-1)>1$, and only by a constant factor in full first-order logic (\cref{prop:terms:factoring}). The natural claim that it always saves $n_An_B-(n_A+n_B)$ members fails for small patterns. This is the elementary instance, with the exact gain, of the compression argument for predicate invention in inductive logic programming (cf.\ \citealp{muggleton1988machine}).

\subsection{What this does and does not show}\label{sec:terms:reading}

The classical reply to Hempel, that Craigian axiomatisations are useless in practice, becomes a theorem here, but only about Craig's construction and every run-padded axiomatisation, which are fast labellers in disguise (\cref{thm:terms:dilemma}(ii)); it is not a theorem about theoretical vocabulary, which can be eliminated at polynomial cost ((iii)). What bounded deduction and finiteness make indispensable is theoretical \emph{relations} (polyadic) or theoretical \emph{entities} (objects outside the named structure), either one ((iv)); theoretical \emph{properties} of observable things, unary predicates, never help a finite theory in this setting. In the idealised setting nothing theoretical is indispensable ((i)), because the hypothesis class already contains the apparatus ((v)). So where theoretical terms pay is a property of the hypothesis class. The results concern one poor observational language and literal data, and say nothing about whether science should use theoretical terms; they locate, inside these inducers, what can and cannot be traded away.

\paragraph{Hempel's three replies.}\label{rem:phil:hempel}\status{assessment, resting on thm:terms:horn and thm:terms:dilemma}\src{philosophy \S1.6; synthesis \S2.4} \emph{Impracticality}: Craig's replacement axiomatisation is unmanageable. This is a theorem, but only about Craig's construction and run-padded axiomatisations under charged derivations; vocabulary as such is eliminable at polynomial cost (\cref{thm:terms:dilemma}(ii), (iii)). \emph{Inductive systematisation}: Hempel argued that theoretical terms may be needed for inductive, not deductive, systematisation \citep{hempel1958theoretician}; the debate is surveyed by \citet{niiniluoto1972inductive}. \SAI is an inductive systematisation, so the reply can be tested in it, and in the universal mixture with free deduction it fails: theoretical terms add no inductive power among observational sentences beyond a constant factor (\cref{rem:terms:hempel}). This is a statement about the mixture; Hempel's own notion is relative to a single theory, and our statement is not a reconstruction of it. The reply survives, in corrected form, where something theoretical pays, for finite theories and under charged derivations, and there what pays is relations or entities, not vocabulary. In monadic inductive logic, by contrast, theoretical concepts can change inductive probabilities \citep{niiniluoto1973theoretical}. \emph{Aims beyond prediction}: \citet{putnam1965craig} argued that the instrumentalist use of Craig's theorem rests on the view that science aims only at prediction. The question-answering view of \cref{sec:phil} is a partial version of this reply: theories are scored on the questions they answer, which may concern unobservables only if the base language contains them.
