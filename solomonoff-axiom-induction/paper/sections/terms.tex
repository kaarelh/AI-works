\section{Theoretical terms: Craig, Ramsey and the theoretician's dilemma}\label{sec:terms}

Theories introduce vocabulary that their data do not contain: field, entropy, gene. Hempel's theoretician's dilemma \citep{hempel1958theoretician} runs: if theoretical terms serve to systematise the observable facts, they are dispensable, by Craig's theorem \citep{craig1953axiomatizability} or by Ramsey's method \citep{ramsey1931theories}; if not, they are pointless. In \SAI hypotheses may use vocabulary that the data lack, so one can ask in which hypothesis classes theoretical terms change the posterior, and what pays: vocabulary, relations or entities (the third step of the ladder of \cref{sec:phil:map}). \Cref{thm:terms:dilemma} answers; \cref{app:terms} has the proofs.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Setting and the dilemma theorem}\label{sec:terms:setting}

\begin{definition}[Observational restriction]\status{definition}\src{terms Def 1.1}\label{def:terms:obs}
Let $\mathcal L=\LO\cup\LT$ be a countable language with equality and computable syntax (finite where derivations are charged), with \emph{observational} part $\LO$; $B$ may contain theoretical symbols. The \emph{observational content} of $A\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ is $T_O(A):=\Cn(A)\cap\Sent_{\LO}$, and $D$ is \emph{observational} if $\dom D\subseteq\Sent_{\LO}$. Let $G_U^O:=\{(w,\chi)\in G_U:\chi\in\Sent_{\LO}\}$, $\MA^{\LO}:=\nu_{G_U^O}$, $\WA^{\LO}(D):=\sum_e\mm(e)\ind[B\cup(\re e\cap\Sent_{\LO})\text{ consistent and}\vdash\psi_D]$, and let $\KA^{\LO}(D)$ be the least $K(e)$ over the same $e$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Literal data and charged deduction]\status{definition}\src{terms Def 1.2}\label{def:terms:literal}
\emph{Literal data} are those of \cref{def:setting:fresh}, in the fresh-atom setting, with $\LO=\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,R\}$. \emph{Charged deduction} is that of \cref{sec:soi:charged}: a hypothesis decides an axiom set $A_p$ within time $t$, satisfies \cref{ass:soi:F1}, is consistent, and derives each datum in the calculus $\mathsf K$ with every axiom instance, logical ones included, of size at most $h$ (reading (a1)), every nonlogical instance of size at most $h$ ((a2)), material at most $g$ ((b)), or nonlogical material at most $g$ ((b2)); the mixtures are $W_A^{a1}[t,h]$, $W_A^{a2}[t,h]$, $W_A^{b}[t,g]$, $W_A^{b2}[t,g]$. At \emph{polynomial budgets} the degrees of membership time and budget are charged in the prior, as in Definition E.18 of our earlier report \citep{claude2026axiominduction}; then $X$ is in the fit class iff one hypothesis is compatible with every $D^X_n$ (Lemma E.11 there).
\end{definition}

A theory in a language containing $\LO$ is \emph{about the named objects} if it has a model whose $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-reduct is the named structure $\mathfrak T$, and \emph{compatible with the named structure} if it is consistent with the first-order theory $\ThT$ of $(\mathfrak T;\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1)$ (\cref{def:terms:named}); ``named object'' is our formal stand-in for ``observable thing''.

\begin{theorem}[The theoretician's dilemma in \SAI]\status{proved, with the conditions of the parts listed in the proof}\src{terms Thm 8.1}\label{thm:terms:dilemma}
Data are observational sentences; in (ii)--(iv), literal data under the readings of \cref{def:terms:literal} at polynomial budgets. In the fit-class statements of (iii), and in (iv) and (v), $B=\emptyset$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{In principle.} With deduction free, restricting hypotheses to the observational vocabulary changes the prior weight of every Borel class of observational contents, the predictive probability of every observational sentence and the cumulative loss by at most a constant, in the one-tape, coin-free and Kolmogorov versions, and also when the time to check or to generate axioms is penalised (under \cref{ass:soi:S,ass:soi:Stape}). Tape by tape, the observational projection of a random theory has exactly the same law on observational data.
\item \emph{Craig's construction in practice.} When derivations are charged, Craig's axiomatisation of a theory whose observational consequences have derivations of size $\mathrm{ds}$ needs derivations of size at least $2^{\mathrm{ds}}$. Every axiomatisation by padded observational literals with polynomial-time membership fits exactly the polynomial-time decidable regularities, under every reading. Axiom sets with theoretical symbols fit $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$ (total material), $\cplx{EXPTIME}$ (every instance short) and every decidable regularity (only the theory's own axioms short); so Craig's construction loses unconditionally under the last two readings and, under the first, iff $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}\ne\cplx P$.
\item \emph{Eliminating the vocabulary in practice.} Theoretical vocabulary can be eliminated at polynomial cost: per hypothesis, with $O(1)$ bits and a polynomial change of budget, under total material, by storing proofs instead of run times; and at the level of fit classes under (a1), (a2), (b2), by storing the eliminated relations in objects that no observational term names. Observational hypotheses, and already finite observational theories, fit what all hypotheses fit.
\item \emph{What is indispensable.} A finite theory in the observational vocabulary with any unary theoretical predicates and constants, compatible with the first-order theory of the named objects, fits exactly the regular regularities, under every reading and also with free deduction. One binary theoretical relation over the named objects restores every decidable regularity ((a2), (b2)), $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$ ((b)) and $\cplx{EXPTIME}$ ((a1)). So does quantifying over unnamed objects; but a finite theory that does so without theoretical vocabulary, and fits a non-regular regularity, must prove some $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-sentence false in the named structure. For the theory built in \cref{thm:terms:binary} that sentence is $\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$, true of no named object.
\item \emph{Free deduction, finite theories.} With deduction free, finite theories gain without bound from a theoretical function (infinity), from theoretical sorts (the theorems of $\PA$) and from a binary relation over the named objects; and r.e.\ axiom induction is finite-theory induction with theoretical sorts up to $O(1)$, coin-free.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) is \cref{thm:terms:horn}. (ii) is \cref{lem:terms:unary,thm:terms:padded,cor:terms:table}, given \cref{cor:soi:fit}, the time hierarchy theorem and $\cplx{APSPACE}=\cplx{EXPTIME}$ \citep{chandra1981alternation}. (iii) is \cref{prop:terms:eliminate,prop:terms:a1fit,thm:terms:binary}, under \cref{ass:soi:F1}, given the one-tape simulation and $\cplx{APSPACE}=\cplx{EXPTIME}$. (iv) is \cref{thm:terms:regular,thm:terms:everyregular,thm:terms:binary,prop:terms:ontology}, given the Ehrenfeucht--Fraïssé theorem, the one-tape simulation and, for (a1), $\cplx{APSPACE}=\cplx{EXPTIME}$. (v) is \cref{thm:terms:infinity,thm:terms:PA,cor:terms:freededuction,thm:terms:KCV}; the clause on $\PA$ is given Ryll-Nardzewski's theorem and $\Con(\PA)$, the clause on sorts the $\Sigma_1$-completeness of $\Q$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{In principle}\label{sec:terms:horn}

Part (i) is \cref{thm:terms:horn}, which states it for each version named in (i) and each Borel class of observational contents, and proves the identity of laws tape by tape.

\begin{remark}[Inductive systematisation in the mixture]\status{proved (the comparison); the reading is an assessment}\src{terms Rem 2.2}\label{rem:terms:hempel}
By \cref{thm:terms:horn}, the posterior predictive probability of every observational sentence, given observational data, is the same up to a factor $2^{\pm2c}$, $c$ the constant of that theorem, whether or not hypotheses may use theoretical vocabulary. So in the universal mixture, with deduction free, theoretical terms are not needed for inductive inference among observational sentences. This is a statement about the mixture, not about a single theory (\cref{rem:phil:hempel}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{In practice: Craig's construction and better eliminations}\label{sec:terms:craig}

Craig's construction pads each consequence with the time at which it was found: the literal $\varphi$ found after $k$ steps becomes the axiom $\varphi^{\wedge(k+1)}$, a conjunction of $k+1$ copies. When the time is that of canonical proof search, every derivation from the Craig set has a line of size at least $2^{\mathrm{ds}}\lvert\varphi^b\rvert$, $\mathrm{ds}$ the size of a shortest derivation of $\varphi^b$ from the original theory (\cref{lem:terms:unary}). For a seven-sentence Horn theory, in a small proof system for ground literals and at a word of length 24, a computation from closed forms gives a Craig axiom of more than $10^{204.6}$ symbols, against 795 for an axiom that carries a certificate (\cref{ex:terms:craig}). At polynomial budgets, every \emph{run-padded} axiomatisation (each member a power $\psi^{\wedge m}$ of a literal $\psi\in\{\varphi_w,\neg\varphi_w\}$) with polynomial-time membership fits exactly the polynomial-time regularities, under every reading (\cref{thm:terms:padded}); so the classical reply holds of Craig's construction, and we did not find this complexity-theoretic form of it in the literature.

Vocabulary itself can be eliminated at polynomial cost. Under reading (b), the certificate axioms $\theta_\gamma\to\varphi^b$ of \cref{thm:soi:sandwich}, observational because $\theta_\gamma$ uses only logical symbols, replace derivations hypothesis by hypothesis at $O(1)$ bits and a polynomial change of budget (\cref{prop:terms:eliminate}). Under the other readings the content goes into objects that no observational term names: in a structure of words and formal pairs, the pairs being the elements $z$ with $\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$, an observational formula $\theta_2(a,b)$, with a fresh bound variable $y_\theta$, says that $R$ holds of the pair of $a$ and $b$, and it can replace the atoms of a binary theoretical relation (\cref{lem:terms:pairing,prop:terms:a1fit}). With empty background, for every \emph{verifier} $\mathcal V$, a halting machine that accepts or rejects pairs (word, certificate) consistently, there are a finite theory in $\LO\cup\{\mathsf C\}$, $\mathsf C$ binary, about the named objects and a finite set of $\LO$-sentences that prove exactly the literals that $\mathcal V$ certifies, by derivations whose length and line size are linear in the time and space of a one-tape simulation of $\mathcal V$ plus the lengths of the input and the certificate; with a variant for alternating machines, finite theories of either kind fit what all hypotheses fit, under every reading (\cref{thm:terms:binary}). With deduction free the existence part is classical: as stated in \citet{pakhomov2019question}, every r.e.\ theory without finite models has a finitely axiomatised conservative extension with one more binary relation symbol \citep{kleene1952finite}, even one into which every model expands \citep{craigvaught1958finite}. To our knowledge the derivation bounds, hence the fit classes under charged deduction, and the observational translation are new.

\subsection{Vocabulary or ontology}\label{sec:terms:ontology}

Without objects outside the named structure unary theoretical vocabulary does not help: with empty background, a finite theory in $\LO$ plus unary predicates and constants, compatible with the named structure, that proves the literals of $X$ forces $X$ to be regular (\cref{thm:terms:regular}, given the Ehrenfeucht--Fraïssé theorem), and every regular $X$ has such a theory (\cref{thm:terms:everyregular}); one binary relation over the named objects, or quantification over unnamed objects in $\LO$ alone, lets finite theories fit what all finite theories fit, and with deduction free the contrast holds for complexities too (\cref{cor:terms:ontology,cor:terms:freededuction}). The second way has a price: a finite theory without theoretical vocabulary that fits a non-regular regularity proves some $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-sentence false in the named structure, for the observational theory built above $\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$ (\cref{prop:terms:ontology}), while the theory with the binary relation is conservative over the theory of the named objects plus the data (\cref{prop:terms:conservativity}). One binary observable relation breaks \cref{thm:terms:regular} (\cref{prop:terms:binaryobs}).

\subsection{Finite theories with free deduction}\label{sec:terms:finite}

With deduction free and $B=\emptyset$, finite theories, the single sentences of $\Kfin$ and $\Wfin$ (\cref{def:setting:versions}), can gain from theoretical vocabulary without bound. On the data ``there are at least $n$ things'', the least complexity of a consistent sentence in pure equality plus unary predicates that proves the data is $\Kpad(n)\pm c$, which tends to infinity, though more slowly than every computable unbounded nondecreasing function, while with a unary function symbol $f$ the sentence saying that $f$ is injective and not surjective gives $O(1)$ (\cref{thm:terms:infinity}). Its Ramsey sentence is finite but second-order, the observational axiomatisation of its $\{=\}$-consequences is first-order but infinite, and no finite first-order theory in pure equality, or in pure equality with unary predicates added, has the same consequences (\cref{prop:terms:ramsey}). On the theorems of $\PA$ as data, finite $\Lar$-theories diverge, given Ryll-Nardzewski's theorem \citep{rylnardzewski1952role}, while theoretical sorts give bounded complexity (\cref{thm:terms:PA}); and for finite $\mathcal L$, r.e.\ axiom induction is, up to a constant and coin-free, finite-theory induction in a three-sorted extension with numbers, assignments and satisfaction (\cref{thm:terms:KCV}), an effective, many-sorted form of the theorem of \citet{kleene1952finite} and \citet{craigvaught1958finite} of which only the Bayesian consequence is new. The equivalence is not one-tape: on the lottery stream of \cref{thm:logic:lottery}, random r.e.\ theories have bounded loss and random finite theories with any theoretical apparatus do not (\cref{rem:terms:onetape}).

A concept can also pay by factoring inferences.\footnote{The idea of introducing a concept that factors a complete bipartite pattern of inferences first appeared in H\"anni's working notes \citep{hanni2026notes}.} If each of $n_A$ unary predicates implies each of $n_B$ others, a theory of atom-to-atom implications needs $n_An_B$ members, with a new intermediate predicate exactly $\min(n_An_B,n_A+n_B)$; so the predicate pays in this format iff $(n_A-1)(n_B-1)>1$, and in full first-order logic only by a constant factor (\cref{prop:terms:factoring}). This is the elementary instance, with the exact gain, of the compression argument for predicate invention (cf.\ \citealp{muggleton1988machine}).

\subsection{What this does and does not show}\label{sec:terms:reading}

What bounded deduction and finiteness make indispensable is theoretical \emph{relations} or theoretical \emph{entities} (for the theory we build, objects outside the named structure), not vocabulary ((iii), (iv)); and in the idealised setting nothing theoretical is indispensable, because the r.e.\ prior already is a prior over finite theories with theoretical vocabulary and entities ((i), (v)). The results concern one poor observational language and literal data.

\paragraph{Hempel's replies.}\label{rem:phil:hempel}\status{assessment, resting on thm:terms:horn and thm:terms:dilemma}\src{philosophy \S1.6; synthesis \S2.4} \emph{Impracticality}: Craig's replacement axiomatisation is unmanageable. This is a theorem, but only about Craig's construction and run-padded axiomatisations under charged derivations; vocabulary as such is eliminable at polynomial cost (\cref{thm:terms:dilemma}(ii), (iii)). \emph{Inductive systematisation}: Hempel argued that theoretical terms may be needed for inductive, not deductive, systematisation \citep{hempel1958theoretician}; the debate is surveyed by \citet{niiniluoto1972inductive}. \SAI is an inductive systematisation, and in its universal mixture with free deduction the reply fails: theoretical terms add no inductive power among observational sentences beyond a constant factor (\cref{rem:terms:hempel}). This is a statement about the mixture; Hempel's own notion is relative to a single theory, and our statement is not a reconstruction of it. The reply survives, in corrected form, where something theoretical pays: for finite theories and under charged derivations, and there by relations or entities, not by vocabulary.
