\section{Logic: random theories, \texorpdfstring{$\Pi_1$}{Pi1} truth and G\"odel phenomena}\label{sec:logic}

Up to constant factors, every version of \SAIabbr is the corresponding version of consistent \SOIabbr (\cref{sec:soi}). This section studies what is specific to data that are sentences of a logic. Random theories can succeed where every r.e.\ theory fails (\cref{sec:logic:lottery,sec:logic:arith}); on $\Pi_1$ data the mixture is, up to a constant factor, one random true theory (\cref{sec:logic:pi1}); Gödel's theorems leave fixed marks on the posterior (\cref{sec:logic:goedel}); given all its instances, a universal sentence gets belief $1$ only when the background and the instances prove it (\cref{sec:logic:omega}); and the loss on true arithmetic grows at least polynomially (\cref{sec:logic:thn}). Proofs are in \cref{app:logic}.

\emph{Conventions.} $\Lar=\{0,S,+,\cdot,<\}$, with numerals $\num n$; $\Delta_0$, $\Sigma_1$, $\Pi_1$ are syntactic classes, and $\Pi_1^*$ consists of the finite conjunctions of $\Pi_1$ sentences and negations of $\Sigma_1$ sentences; ``$\Q$-equivalent'' means equivalent provably in $\Q$. ``$B$ is true'' means that $B$ has a model whose $\Lar$-reduct is $\mathbb N$, and ``$B\supseteq\Q$'' means $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$ (likewise for $\PA$ and $B\cup T$). A theory is \emph{$\Sigma_1$-sound} if it proves no false $\Sigma_1$ sentence; $\mathcal U$ is the class of $\Sigma_1$-unsound deductively closed theories, $\overline{\mathcal U}$ its complement, and $P_D(\mathcal C):=\UA(\mathcal C;D)/\MA(D)$. Standard facts (G2: Gödel's second theorem for r.e.\ extensions of $\PA$; Uniq: uniqueness of computation codes; $\Sigma_1$-completeness of $\Q$; the diagonal lemma) are listed in \cref{app:logic:conv} (see \citealp{smorynski1977incompleteness,boolos2007computability}). Losses are in the semimeasure convention unless stated otherwise.

\subsection{The lottery}\label{sec:logic:lottery}

The one-tape loss on a stream is bounded iff $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$ (\cref{prop:basic:limitpost}). For \SOIabbr on strings, bounded one-tape loss implies that some partial computable labeller extends the stream (\cref{prop:logic:arity}(b)). Does bounded one-tape loss of \SAIabbr imply that some consistent r.e.\ theory proves every datum? It does not; the obstruction is a consistency constraint of unbounded arity.

Let $\mathcal L$ contain $0$, $S$ and a binary relation symbol $\Win$, and let $B$ be decidable and consistent, not mention $\Win$, and have a model in which the numerals denote pairwise distinct elements (for example $B=\emptyset$, $\Q$ or $\PA$). With $N_k:=2^{k+1}-1$, the \emph{lottery sentences}
\[
\Lot_k:=\exists x\,\Win(\num k,x)\ \wedge\ \forall x\,\big(\Win(\num k,x)\to x=\num0\vee\dots\vee x=\num{N_k}\big)
\]
say that lottery $k$ has a winner among its tickets $0,\dots,N_k$. The \emph{random lottery theory} $\Glot$ reads the tape in consecutive blocks of $k+1$ bits, reads block $k$ as a number $J_k\in\{0,\dots,N_k\}$, and emits $\Lot_k$, $\Win(\num k,\num{J_k})$, and $\neg\Win(\num k,\num j)$ for $j\le N_k$, $j\ne J_k$. Its theory is consistent with $B$ on every tape.

\begin{theorem}[The lottery]\status{proved; computed}\src{logic Thm 2.2}\label{thm:logic:lottery}
There is an infinite stream $D_\infty$ of labelled sentences, computable from $\emptyset'$, such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item for every $n$, $\MA(D_n)\ge2^{-K(\Glot)-c}\prod_{k\ge0}(1-2^{-(k+1)})$; so $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$, the cumulative loss is at most $K(\Glot)-\log\prod_{k\ge0}(1-2^{-(k+1)})+c$, and the posterior converges in total variation to $P_\infty$;
\item no consistent r.e.\ theory proves (together with $B$) every member of $D_\infty^\pm$;
\item $\WA(D_n)\to0$ and $\KA(D_n)\to\infty$, so the coin-free loss is unbounded; and $\Kfin^{\mathcal L'}(D_n)\to\infty$ for every language $\mathcal L'\supseteq\mathcal L$ with computable syntax;
\item the posterior probability that the tape's program is a deterministic machine (one that emits only under the empty prefix) tends to $0$, and no tape in $E_\infty$ has a theory with r.e.\ deductive closure: the limit posterior lives on random theories whose closure is not r.e.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The product equals $0.288788\ldots$ (a computation; code in the accompanying repository). The stream is a diagonal: if $B\cup\re e$ is consistent and proves $\Lot_e$, it cannot refute every ticket of lottery $e$, and the stream reports as a loser the least ticket $j^*_e$ that it does not refute. So every consistent r.e.\ theory fails at its own lottery, while a random lottery theory dies at lottery $k$ only if its winner is the reported loser, with probability $2^{-(k+1)}$. Such a stream is neither r.e.\ nor, for the lottery classes, co-r.e., and the phenomenon needs proof-requiring compatibility (\cref{rem:logic:lotteryremarks}). The random lottery theories share the law $\{\Lot_k\}_k$ and differ only in random contingent facts, the winners: a forced form of the split between laws and random initial conditions (\cref{sec:laws}).

\paragraph{Where it cannot happen.} The lottery's minimal inconsistent sets $\{\Lot_k\}\cup\{\neg\Win(\num k,\num j):j\le N_k\}$ have unbounded size, and at every threshold the majority-vote theory refutes every ticket of the late lotteries, as in Kyburg's lottery paradox \citep{kyburg1961probability}. Bounded arity removes the obstruction.

\begin{proposition}[Majority vote]\status{proved; computed}\src{logic Prop 2.3}\label{prop:logic:arity}
(a) Let $Z\subseteq\Sent$ be decidable and $r\ge1$, and suppose that every $B$-inconsistent finite subset of $Z$ contains a $B$-inconsistent subset with at most $r$ elements. If $D_\infty^\pm\subseteq Z$ and $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$, then some consistent r.e.\ $T\subseteq Z$ contains $D_\infty^\pm$; hence $\inf_n\WA(D_n)>0$, and the coin-free loss is bounded too.
(b) For the one-tape law $\MF$ of \SOIabbr with inputs $X$ and labels $Y$: if $D_\infty:X\rightharpoonup Y$ is infinite and $\lim_n\MF(D_n)>0$, some partial computable $f$ extends $D_\infty$, so the coin-free version $W$ also has bounded loss on $D_\infty$.
\end{proposition}

The proof takes the majority vote at threshold $1-1/(r+1)$ above a Lebesgue density point of $E_\infty$ (\cref{lem:logic:density}), as in \citet{deleeuw1956computability}; the threshold $1/(r+1)$ is exact for the argument (\cref{app:logic:lottery}).

\begin{corollary}[Bounded loss and r.e.\ theories]\status{proved}\src{logic Cor 2.5}\label{cor:logic:Q1}
$\Unif(E_\infty)>0$ does not in general imply that a consistent r.e.\ theory proves every datum of $D_\infty$ (\cref{thm:logic:lottery}; inside true arithmetic, \cref{thm:logic:arith}). It does when the inconsistency arity of the data's sentence class is bounded (\cref{prop:logic:arity}), for streams of $\Pi_1$ type over a true background $B\supseteq\Q$ (\cref{cor:logic:onetrue}), and for co-r.e.\ streams over block-decidable sentence classes, which include the lottery classes (\cref{prop:logic:core}); and if a positive-measure set of true random theories proves the stream, a true r.e.\ theory does (\cref{prop:logic:true}).
\end{corollary}

We do not claim that these are the only cases; the last holds because truth, unlike consistency, is a constraint of arity one. Since consistent \SOIabbr is \SAIabbr version by version (\cref{thm:soi:coinfree,thm:soi:onetape}), the lottery separates the versions of consistent \SOIabbr: on its stream one-tape consistent \SOIabbr has bounded loss and coin-free consistent \SOIabbr unbounded loss, while plain coin-free \SOIabbr on sentences has bounded loss, through an inconsistent labeller (\cref{cor:logic:versions}).

On the lottery stream the coin-free loss diverges more slowly than any computable unbounded nondecreasing function, and $\KA(D_n)\le\lceil n/2\rceil+O(\log n)$ (\cref{cor:logic:rate}, through the proof horizon of \cref{prop:logic:horizon}).

\begin{question}\status{open}\src{logic E-Q2, \S2.6}\label{q:logic:computablefast}
Is there a stream with bounded one-tape loss on which $-\log\WA(D_n)\ge f(n)$ for some computable unbounded nondecreasing $f$? And does bounded one-tape loss on a co-r.e.\ stream imply, in general, that a consistent r.e.\ theory proves it?
\end{question}

\paragraph{Relatives.} The survival condition $\sum_k2^{-(k+1)}<\infty$ is the convergence condition of bounded diagonally non-computable functions (cf.\ \citealp{bienvenu2016deep}; \cref{app:logic:lottery}), and randomised learners can succeed where deterministic ones cannot \citep{pitt1989probabilistic,ambainis1999probabilistic}; here the random objects are the hypotheses. Random axiom systems drawn by a universal machine are not new: the modified Demski prior samples them \citep{garrabrant2015modification,garrabrant2016inductive}, and it too gives the lottery stream positive probability. To our knowledge, the comparison of the one-tape law with its own coin-free and Kolmogorov versions, the identification of the succeeding hypotheses as random theories r.e.\ in the tape, the arity boundary and the arithmetic versions are new.

\subsection{Lotteries in arithmetic}\label{sec:logic:arith}

Inside arithmetic, self-referential formulas behave like independent atoms with respect to consistency with a true background, with prescribed truth values in $\mathbb N$ (\cref{lem:logic:flexible}, a variant of the flexible formulas of \citealp{kripke1962flexible}). With them the lottery needs no fresh symbol, and survives even with true data. Let $\langle k,j\rangle$ be a $\Delta_0$ pairing with $k,j\le\langle k,j\rangle$.

\begin{theorem}[Lotteries in arithmetic]\status{proved, given \cref{lem:logic:flexible}; (b) computed}\src{logic Thm 2.9}\label{thm:logic:arith}
Let $\mathcal L=\Lar$, and let $B$ be decidable and true with $B\supseteq\Q$. For a formula $\Upsilon(x)$ put $\Tick_{k,j}:=\Upsilon(\num{\langle k,j\rangle})$ and $\Lot^{\rm ar}_k:=\bigvee_{j\le N_k}\Tick_{k,j}$.
(a) There are a formula $\Upsilon_0$ and a stream $((\Lot^{\rm ar}_k,1),(\Tick_{k,j^*_k},0))_{k\ge0}$ satisfying (a)--(d) of \cref{thm:logic:lottery} in which every $\Tick_{k,j}$ is $\Q$-equivalent to a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence; so the data are $\Q$-equivalent to false $\Sigma_1$ sentences labelled true and to true $\Pi_1^*$ sentences.
(b) There are a formula $\Upsilon_1$ and a stream of the same form satisfying (a)--(d) of \cref{thm:logic:lottery} in which every datum is true in $\mathbb N$ and $\Q$-equivalent to a Boolean combination of $\Sigma_1$ sentences.
\end{theorem}

In (b), $\Upsilon_1$ is chosen by self-reference so that the true winner of lottery $k$ is the least ticket that $\re k$ refutes ($0$ if none); the diagonal reports a different, unrefuted ticket as the loser, and that report is true.

\begin{corollary}[True data, false theories]\status{proved; computed}\src{logic Cor 2.10}\label{cor:logic:false}
On the stream of \cref{thm:logic:arith}(b):
(i) every datum is true, $\LA(D_n)$ is bounded, and $\KA(D_n)-\LA(D_n)\to\infty$; so no coding theorem of the form $\KA\le\LA+f(\LA)$ holds for true data beyond $\Sigma_1\cup\Pi_1$;
(ii) $P_\infty\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)\}=0$: the limit posterior is concentrated on false theories, although every datum is true;
(iii) the random lottery theory is $\Sigma_1$-unsound on every tape on which, for some $k$, its winner $J_k$ differs from the true winner of lottery $k$; conditionally on its survival this has probability $1$.
\end{corollary}

Part (ii) holds because a positive-measure set of true theories would give a true r.e.\ theory proving the stream (\cref{prop:logic:true}). Fed this stream of true sentences, the one-tape inducer predicts with bounded loss and comes to believe, with probability one, false theories: a random theory that guesses winners is right where the data check it and wrong where they do not. The data are adversarial (a $\Delta_2$ diagonal), so this is a possibility result about inferring truth from predictive success in an idealised setting, not evidence about actual science; it is one reason we draw no realist conclusion about the theories an inducer favours. Whether also $P_\infty(\overline{\mathcal U})=0$ is open (\cref{q:logic:soundtrue}).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\Pi_1$}{Pi1} data: derandomisation, a coding theorem, Chaitin's theorem}\label{sec:logic:pi1}

On $\Pi_1$ data positive probability of provability certifies truth, and the mixture collapses, up to a constant factor, to one random true theory. Throughout, $\mathcal L\supseteq\Lar$ and $B$ is decidable and true with $B\supseteq\Q$. A data set $D$ is \emph{of $\Pi_1$ type} if each literal in $D^\pm$ is $B$-equivalent to a $\Pi_1^*$ sentence or to a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence; then $B\vdash\psi_D\leftrightarrow\pi_D$ for some $\pi_D\in\Pi_1^*$, and $\MA(D)=\Bel_0(\pi_D)$ (\cref{app:logic:pi1}).

\begin{theorem}[The threshold machine]\status{proved; computed}\src{logic Thm 3.2}\label{thm:logic:threshold}
Let $\Gthr$ be the theory machine that, after reading $s$ tape bits $u$, emits every $\pi\in\Pi_1^*$ for which some lower approximation of $\Unif(O_\pi)$ exceeds $0.u+2^{-s}$ ($0.u$ the dyadic rational with digits $u$). For a tape $r$ that is not a dyadic rational, read as a real, $A^{\Gthr}_r=\{\pi\in\Pi_1^*:\Unif(O_\pi)>r\}$; this theory is inconsistent if $r<\Inc_0$ and true if $r>\Inc_0$. For every data set $D$ of $\Pi_1$ type, $\MA(D)\le\nu_{\Gthr}(D)\le2^{K(\Gthr)+c}\MA(D)$.
\end{theorem}

So on $\Pi_1$ data the universal mixture is, up to a constant factor, one random true theory: every $\Pi_1^*$ sentence whose prior probability of being proved exceeds a uniformly random level. The tape helps only by choosing the level, and that costs only what the probability of the datum already charges. Fixing $k+2$ bits of the level just above $\Inc_0$ gives a true r.e.\ theory $T_k$ of complexity at most $k+K(k)+c$ that proves every $\Pi_1^*$ sentence of prior probability of provability at least $2^{-k}$ (\cref{thm:logic:thresholdtheories}).

\begin{corollary}[A coding theorem for $\Pi_1$ data]\status{proved}\src{logic Cor 3.3}\label{cor:logic:coding}
For every $D$ of $\Pi_1$ type, $\LA(D)\le-\log\WA(D)+c\le\KA(D)+c\le\LA(D)+K(\lceil\LA(D)\rceil)+c'$.
\end{corollary}

Likewise, if every finite part of a stream is of $\Pi_1$ type and $\sup_n\LA(D_n)\le k$, the single true theory $B\cup T_k$ proves every datum (\cref{cor:logic:onetrue}). For literals no coding theorem holds (\cref{thm:basic:nocoding}). For $\WA$ the bound improves to $-\log\WA\le\LA+\Kpad(\lceil\LA\rceil+1)+c$ (\cref{rem:logic:Wbound}); whether $\KA\le\LA+O(1)$ is open (\cref{q:logic:codingO1}). Now code strings by length-lexicographic rank, so that the strings of length at most $m$ are the codes at most $\mathrm{rk}_m:=2^{m+1}-2$, and let $\mathrm{Comp}(p,t,y)$ be Kleene's $\Delta_0$ predicate ``$t$ codes the halting computation of $V$ on $p$, with output $y$''. The $\Pi_1$ sentence $\Kgt_m(x):=\forall t\,\forall p\,(p\le\num{\mathrm{rk}_m}\to\neg\mathrm{Comp}(p,t,\num x))$ is true iff $K(x)>m$.

\begin{corollary}[Chaitin's theorem for random theories]\status{proved}\src{logic Cor 3.6}\label{cor:logic:chaitin}
For all $m$ and $x$, in the semimeasure convention:
(a) if $K(x)\le m$, then $\LA(\{(\Kgt_m(x),1)\})=\infty$; if $K(x)>m$, then $\LA(\{(\Kgt_m(x),1)\})\ge m-K(m)-K(\lceil\LA\rceil)-c\ge m-2\log m-O(\log\log m)$;
(b) if $K(x)>m$, then $\LA(\{(\Kgt_m(x),1)\})\le\KA(\{(\Kgt_m(x),1)\})+c\le m+K(m)+c'$, and one true theory, $B$ plus a single $\Pi_1$ sentence of complexity at most $m+K(m)+c$, proves $\Kgt_m(x)$ for every such $x$ at once.
The same bounds, with other constants, hold in the three-valued convention, in which the cost of the label $1$ on this single datum is $-\log\Bel_\emptyset(\Kgt_m(x))=\LA(\{(\Kgt_m(x),1)\})+\log(1-\Inc_0)$.
\end{corollary}

The lower bound combines \cref{cor:logic:coding} with Chaitin's bound \citep{chaitin1974information}. One $m$-bit $\Pi_1$ law explains every true complexity lower bound at level $m$. Yet the datum carries at most one bit of label information: unconstrained \SOIabbr predicts it at cost $O(1)$ by always answering ``true'', and the lower bound of \cref{prop:basic:lower} is $O(1)$ here. In the normalised convention the $m$ bits disappear:

\begin{proposition}[The normalised convention: deciding, not predicting]\status{proved, given (Uniq); computed}\src{logic Prop 3.11}\label{prop:logic:normalised}
Let $B$ be decidable and true with $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B)$. There is $c$ such that for all $m$, $d$ and $j\ge0$, all strings $x\in\{0,1\}^d$ except at most $2^{2m+K(m)+j+c}+2^{m+1}$ of them satisfy $K(x)>m$ and $\Dis_\emptyset(\Kgt_m(x))\le2^{-j}\Bel_\emptyset(\Kgt_m(x))$; so the loss of $(\Kgt_m(x),1)$ in the normalised convention is at most $\log(1+2^{-j})\le1.45\cdot2^{-j}$.
\end{proposition}

The $m$ bits are the price of deciding the sentence, not of predicting its truth value: every $\Pi_1$ affirmation must be backed by a true theory, while a consistent extension of $\PA$ refutes $\Kgt_m(x)$ for fewer than $2^{m+1}$ strings.

The boundary of the coding theorem is exact. With $\mathcal L=\Lar$ and $B$ decidable and true with $B\supseteq\Q$, one false $\Sigma_1$ datum breaks it (\cref{prop:logic:falsesigma}), and so do two true data one level up: for every $d\ge2$ there are true data $D_d$, two sentences each $\Q$-equivalent to a Boolean combination of $\Sigma_1$ sentences, with $\KA(D_d)>\log d-2$ and $\LA(D_d)\le2+c$ (\cref{prop:logic:boundary}); on a stream, the gap is bounded against unbounded (\cref{cor:logic:false}). The threshold device is close to the averaging of \citet{deleeuw1956computability} and to soundness arguments for random true axioms \citep{bienvenu2014axiomatic,shen2011random}, which show among other things that all true statements ``$C(x)\ge n-c$'' together prove every true $\Pi_1$ sentence. To our knowledge, the threshold just above the unknown $\Inc_0$, the single random threshold theory and the coding theorem for an inductive inference rule are new; we have not found them stated, but cannot exclude that they are known.

\subsection{G\"odel phenomena}\label{sec:logic:goedel}

\begin{proposition}[The arithmetical shape of the trichotomy]\status{proved; computed}\src{logic Prop 4.1}\label{prop:logic:shape}
Let $B$ be decidable and consistent with $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$, and let $D$ be consistent.
(a) If $\sigma$ is a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence, then $\Bel_D(\sigma)=1$.
(b) If $\pi\in\Pi_1^*$ is false, then $(\Bel_D,\Dis_D,\Indep_D)(\pi)=(0,1,0)$; equivalently, $\Bel_D(\pi)>0$ or $\Indep_D(\pi)>0$ implies that $\pi$ is true.
(c) If $B\cup D^\pm$ is true, $\pi\in\Pi_1^*$ is true and $B\cup D^\pm\nvdash\pi$, then $\Bel_D(\pi)$, $\Dis_D(\pi)$, $\Indep_D(\pi)$ are all positive.
(d) If $B\cup D^\pm$ is true, $\sigma$ is a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence and $B\cup D^\pm\nvdash\neg\sigma$, then all three values of $\sigma$ are positive.
\end{proposition}

The inducer errs one-sidedly: on $\Sigma_1$ sentences it may believe a falsehood but never disbelieves a truth; on $\Pi_1$ sentences (consistency statements, Gödel sentences, $\Kgt_m(x)$) the reverse. So ``independent'' on a $\Pi_1$ question certifies truth, the inducer's form of ``the Gödel sentence is true because it is unprovable''; the normalised convention discards exactly this share.

\begin{theorem}[Soundness is never learned, coin-free]\status{proved, given (G2)}\src{logic Thm 4.2}\label{thm:logic:soundness}
Let $B$ be decidable and consistent with $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B)$. There is $c$ such that $\WA(\mathcal U;D)\ge2^{-c}\WA(\overline{\mathcal U};D)$ for every consistent $D$. Hence the coin-free posterior probability of $\Sigma_1$-soundness, and a fortiori of truth, is at most $1/(1+2^{-c})$ for every $D$, in particular for every finite part of true arithmetic; and in the Kolmogorov version the shortest compatible $\Sigma_1$-unsound theory is at most $c$ bits longer than the shortest compatible $\Sigma_1$-sound one.
\end{theorem}

Every consistent $\re e$ has a companion $\re e\cup\{\neg\Con(B\cup\re e)\}$, consistent by (G2), unsound, and only $O(1)$ bits longer. One-tape it gives $P_D(\mathcal U)\ge2^{\LA(D)-\KA(D)-c}$ (\cref{prop:logic:unsound}): confidence in soundness needs a large coding gap. On $\Pi_1$ data the bound is uniform:

\begin{theorem}[Soundness is never learned from $\Pi_1$ data]\status{proved, given (G2); computed}\src{logic Thm 4.4}\label{thm:logic:soundnessPi1}
Let $B$ be decidable and true with $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B)$. There is $c$ such that for every $D$ of $\Pi_1$ type with $\MA(D)>0$, $\UA(\mathcal U;D)\ge2^{-c}\MA(D)$; so $P_D(\overline{\mathcal U})\le1-2^{-c}$.
\end{theorem}

The companion of $\Gthr$ adds, for each prefix $w$ of the tape, the negated consistency statement of what $\Gthr$ emits on extensions of $w$. The answer to the next question is yes on $\Pi_1$ data, with a bounded coding gap, and on the $\Win$-lottery stream (\cref{app:logic:goedel}).

\begin{question}\status{open}\src{logic D-Q1, \S4.4}\label{q:logic:soundness}
With $B$ true and $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B)$, is $P_D(\overline{\mathcal U})$ bounded away from $1$ uniformly over all data $D$?
\end{question}

Given data presenting an r.e.\ theory $T$ with $B\cup T$ true and $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B\cup T)$, the consistency statement $\Con(B\cup T)$ keeps positive limit shares on all three answers, its belief and independent shares certify its truth, and which of ``true'' and ``false'' is favoured is a choice of universal machine (\cref{prop:logic:con}). The truth hypothesis is required: for $B=\PA+\neg\Con(\PA)$ and $T=\emptyset$, $B$ refutes $\Con(B)$, and the limit of $\Dis$ is $1$. Checking instances of a decidable property, the classic case of induction in mathematics,\footnote{The question of how checking instances can confirm a universal mathematical statement first appeared in H\"anni's working notes \citep{hanni2026notes}.} confirms nothing when deduction is free:

\begin{proposition}[Old evidence]\status{proved}\src{logic Prop 4.6}\label{prop:logic:oldevidence}
If $B\vdash\varphi^b$ for every $(\varphi,b)$ in $D$, then $\MA(D)=\MA(\emptyset)$ and the posterior equals the prior; likewise for $\WA$ and $\KA$. In particular, with $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$ and a $\Delta_0$ formula $P(x)$, the data $\{(P(\num n),1):n\le N\}$ (true, since otherwise they are inconsistent with $B$) leave $\Bel(\forall x\,P(x))$ at its prior value, for every $N$.
\end{proposition}

The checks are entailed by $\Q$ and carry no information, the problem of old evidence and logical omniscience (cf.\ \citealp{hacking1967slightly,garber1983old}); instances of a fresh predicate do confirm, up to the $\omega$-gap. Induction in mathematics needs a reasoner for whom computing $P(\num n)$ is costly, such as a logical inductor \citep{garrabrant2016logical} or a derivation-charged inducer (\cref{app:logic:goedel}).

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\omega$}{omega}-gap}\label{sec:logic:omega}

\begin{proposition}[Anatomy of the $\omega$-gap]\status{proved}\src{logic Prop 5.1}\label{prop:logic:omega}
Let $\mathcal L$ have a nonempty set $\mathrm{CT}$ of closed terms, $B$ be decidable and consistent, $\varphi(x)$ have one free variable, and $I:=B\cup\{\varphi(t):t\in\mathrm{CT}\}$ be consistent. Let the stream list $(\varphi(t),1)$ for all $t\in\mathrm{CT}$, in any order. Then $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$, the posterior converges in total variation to the prior restricted to the consistent extensions of $I$, and the limit trichotomy of $\forall x\,\varphi$ is
(i) $(1,0,0)$ if $I\vdash\forall x\,\varphi$;
(ii) $(0,1,0)$ if $I\vdash\neg\forall x\,\varphi$ ($I$ is $\omega$-inconsistent at $\varphi$);
(iii) otherwise in $(0,1)^3$, with $\lim\Dis=P_\infty(\text{the theory is $\omega$-inconsistent at }\varphi)$ and $\lim\Indep=P_\infty(\text{the theory proves every instance and decides }\forall x\,\varphi\text{ neither way})$.
\end{proposition}

So the Bayesian $\omega$-gap is the $\omega$-incompleteness of the background plus the instances, weighted by prior shares. In particular $\Bel$ satisfies the Gaifman condition \citep{gaifman1964concerning}, a desideratum of \citet{hutter2013probabilities}, exactly when $I\vdash\forall x\,\varphi$; otherwise the gap $\lim_n\Bel_0(\bigwedge_{i\le n}\varphi(t_i))-\Bel_0(\forall x\,\varphi)$ is the mass of the consistent random theories that prove every instance but not $\forall x\,\varphi$, at least $2^{-K(I)-c}$ (\cref{prop:logic:gaifman}). This is the provability analogue of Proposition B.10 of \citet{claude2026axiominduction}, where for probabilities on structures the gap is the mass of structures with non-standard elements in which every closed instance holds. The Gaifman condition is a desideratum for credences about truth; $\Bel$ is a probability of provability, and its failure is not a defect. The universal prior does not close the gap, even for true $\Pi_1$ sentences with trivially checkable instances:

\begin{theorem}[A uniform Gödelian $\omega$-gap]\status{proved, given (G2)}\src{logic Thm 5.3}\label{thm:logic:goedelomega}
Let $B$ be decidable and true with $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B)$. For every $k$ there is a $\Delta_0$ formula $\zeta_k(x)$ such that $\forall x\,\zeta_k$ is true, every instance $\zeta_k(\num n)$ is a true $\Delta_0$ sentence (so $\Q$-provable, and instance data are free by \cref{prop:logic:oldevidence}), and $2^{-k-K(k)-c}\le\Bel_0(\forall x\,\zeta_k)<2^{-k}$. Hence after any instance data the posterior belief is $\Bel_0(\forall x\,\zeta_k)/(1-\Inc_0)<2^{-k+c'}$, and $\Dis+\Indep>1-2^{-k+c'}$.
\end{theorem}

$\forall x\,\zeta_k$ is the consistency statement of $B\cup T_k$: were its belief at least $2^{-k}$, $T_k$ would contain it and $B\cup T_k$ would prove its own consistency, against (G2).

\begin{remark}[Where the gap closes]\status{(a) proved; (b) proved, given Dedekind's categoricity theorem; (c) known}\src{logic \S5.4}\label{rem:logic:closures}
(a) Under the $\omega$-rule, with $B\supseteq\Q$, every random theory that is consistent under that rule has $\Lar$-content $\Th(\mathbb N)$: the inducer is omniscient about arithmetic a priori. (b) Under full second-order consequence the loss on any presentation of $\Th(\mathbb N)$ is at most $K(\PA^2)+c$: the unlearnability of $\Th(\mathbb N)$ (\cref{prop:basic:noncomplete}) rests on effective consequence. (c) The generative variant closes the gap only with the universal sentence among the data: by Theorem 3.9(c1) of \citet{claude2026axiominduction}, with a faithful likelihood and $\forall x\,\varphi$ in the data space, the posterior probability of ``proves $\forall x\,\varphi$'' tends to $\ind[T^*\vdash_{\mathsf C}\forall x\,\varphi]$, whether the generator $T^*$ proves it, not to its truth value; for filtered instance data the gap stays open (its part (d1)).
\end{remark}

A bound on the number of lines of derivations does not close it either: under a full-support prior the limit belief is then $1$ iff a Kreisel-type property holds, and that fails already for a fresh predicate and nine lines (\cref{prop:logic:lines,ex:logic:lines}).

\subsection{True arithmetic}\label{sec:logic:thn}

Let $\mathcal L=\Lar$, $B\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)$ decidable, $(\varphi_i)_{i\ge1}$ a computable enumeration of all sentences, $D_n$ the first $n$ of them labelled by truth, and $b_{1:n}$ the labels. The loss is unbounded and at most $n+K(n)+c$ (\cref{prop:basic:noncomplete}). It grows at least polynomially, because with binary numerals the sentences ``the $k$-th digit of $\Omega_V$ is $1$'' occur early.

\begin{theorem}[A polynomial lower bound]\status{proved, given that $\Omega_V$ is Martin-L\"of random; the exponent computed}\src{logic Thm 6.1}\label{thm:logic:thn}
Suppose the enumeration lists sentences by length, and within a length in a fixed order, and that (1) at most $C\kappa^L$ sentences have length at most $L$, for every $L$, and (2) there are terms $\num k^{\rm b}$ with value $k$ and length at most $c_1\log k+c_2$. Put $\xi:=1/(c_1\log\kappa)$ and $m(n):=\lfloor(n/C')^\xi\rfloor$, where $C'$ depends only on the syntax and on a fixed formula for the binary digits of $\Omega_V$. Then for all $n$, in the semimeasure convention: (a) $K(b_{1:n}\mid n)\ge m(n)-K(n)-c$; (b) $\LA(D_n)\ge C'^{-\xi}n^\xi-K(n)-c$; (c) $\LA(D_n)\ge(2C')^{-\xi}n^\xi-2\log\log n-c$ for $n\ge4$.
\end{theorem}

For a stated Polish-notation syntax, $\xi\ge0.0823$: a computation with exact rational arithmetic (code in the accompanying repository) certifies the bound on formula counts that this needs (\cref{app:logic:thn}). The digit sentences among the first $n$ carry about $m(n)$ incompressible bits \citep{chaitin1975theory}, and a true theory decides correctly, so:

\begin{corollary}[Quantitative incompleteness]\status{proved}\src{logic Cor 6.2}\label{cor:logic:undecided}
Let $T$ be r.e.\ with $B\cup T\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)$, and let $u_n(T)$ be the number of $i\le n$ such that $B\cup T$ proves neither $\varphi_i$ nor $\neg\varphi_i$. Then $u_n(T)\ge C'^{-\xi}n^\xi/(2\log n)$ for all large $n$.
\end{corollary}

Conversely, $\LA(D_n)=o(n)$ if some true r.e.\ theory decides a set of sentences of asymptotic density one in the enumeration (\cref{prop:logic:generic}), a question of generic decidability that depends on the syntax.

\begin{question}\status{open}\src{logic Q3, \S6.2}\label{q:logic:thn}
In such an enumeration of the sentences of $\Lar$ labelled by truth, is $\LA(D_n)=o(n)$?
\end{question}
