\section{Basic theorems}\label{sec:basic}

This section develops the theory of $\MA$; proofs not given in place are in \cref{app:basic}.

\subsection{Structure and updating}\label{sec:basic:structure}

Recall from the companion paper \citep{claudeSFI2026fundamentals} that a map $\nu$ from data sets to $[0,1]$ is a \emph{weak semimeasure} if $\nu(\emptyset)\le1$ and the masses of the one-datum extensions of $D$ at an input sum to at most $\nu(D)$ (its Definition 3.2), and that the \emph{containment functional} of a random partial function $F$ is $D\mapsto\Pr[F\supseteq D]$ (its Definition 3.4).

\begin{proposition}[Structure]\status{proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Prop 2.1}\label{prop:basic:structure}
Let $G$ be a theory machine. (a) If $\nu_G(D)>0$, then $D$ is consistent; $\MA(D)>0$ iff $D$ is consistent, and $\MAthree(D)>0$ iff moreover $B\cup D^\pm$ decides no $\iota$-labelled sentence. (b) For two-valued $D$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$, $\nu_G(D+(\varphi,1))+\nu_G(D+(\varphi,0))+\nu^3_G(D+(\varphi,\iota))=\nu_G(D)$; so $\nu_G$ is a weak semimeasure, and for $\MA$ the defect at $\varphi$ divided by $\MA(D)$ is $\Indep_D(\varphi)$. (c) $\nu_G(D)$ depends on $D$ only through $\Cn(D^\pm)$; if $B\cup D^\pm\vdash\varphi^b$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$, then $\nu_G(D+(\varphi,b))=\nu_G(D)$ and $\nu_G(D+(\varphi,1-b))=0$: entailed data cost nothing and contradicting data are fatal. (d) For every consistent reveal sequence, in any order and with repeats, $\sum_{t\le n}-\log\MA(y_t\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)=\LA(D_n)-\LA(\emptyset)$, which depends only on $\Cn(D_n^\pm)$. (e) $0<\MA(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0<1$. (f) For three-valued $D$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$, $\sum_{y\in Y_3}\nu^3_G(D+(\varphi,y))=\nu^3_G(D)$, and $\nu^3_G(\emptyset)=\nu_G(\emptyset)$; so the conditionals of $\MAthree$ are probability distributions on $Y_3$. (g) $\nu^3_G(D)=\Unif\{\omega\in\mathrm{Cons}^G:\tri^G_\omega\supseteq D\}$, also for two-valued $D$ and $\nu_G$. So, when $\nu_G(\emptyset)>0$, $\nu^3_G/\nu_G(\emptyset)$ is the containment functional of the random total function $\tri^G_\omega$ under $\Unif(\cdot\mid\mathrm{Cons}^G)$, and $\nu_G/\nu_G(\emptyset)$ is that of the random partial function obtained by reading $\iota$ as ``undefined''.
\end{proposition}

By (g), $\MA$ is a (sub-probability) containment functional, which is strictly stronger than being a weak semimeasure (Example 3.5 of the companion paper; \cref{prop:basic:graded}(e)).

\begin{proposition}[Proof-requiring updating is geometric conditioning]\status{proved; computed; the names of the rules known}\src{fundamentals Prop 2.2}\label{prop:basic:geometric}
(a) If $\MA(D)>0$, then $\Bel_D(\chi)=\Bel_0(\psi_D\wedge\chi)/\Bel_0(\psi_D)=\Belhat_0(\psi_D\wedge\chi)/\Belhat_0(\psi_D)$ for every $\chi$. (b) $\Bel_0$ and every $\Bel_D$ are totally monotone on the Lindenbaum algebra of $B$: $\Bel_D(\bigvee_{i\le k}\chi_i)\ge\sum_{\emptyset\ne I\subseteq[k]}(-1)^{\lvert I\rvert+1}\Bel_D(\bigwedge_{i\in I}\chi_i)$ for all $\chi_1,\dots,\chi_k$. (c) There is a probability $P_D$ on the complete consistent extensions of $B$ (a Borel probability on the Stone space) with $\Bel_D(\chi)\le P_D(\chi)\le\Pl_D(\chi)$ for every $\chi$.
\end{proposition}

So $\Bel_D$ is a belief function \citep{shafer1976mathematical}, $\Bel_0$ is the probability of provability, as \citet{pearl1988probabilistic} puts it, and (a) is the \emph{geometric rule} of conditioning of $\Belhat_0$, usually attributed to \citet{suppes1977using}; (b) and (c) are the one-tape forms of Propositions 2.18 and 2.19 of our earlier report \citep{claude2026axiominduction}, stated there for countable classes of theories.

Consistency-requiring updating is Dempster's rule of conditioning \citep{dempster1967upper,shafer1976mathematical} (\cref{prop:basic:dempster}). With focal sets $F_\omega:=\mathrm{Mod}(B\cup A_\omega)$, proof-requiring updating keeps the $F_\omega$ contained in $\mathrm{Mod}(\psi_D)$, while Dempster's rule replaces each $F_\omega$ by $F_\omega\cap\mathrm{Mod}(\psi_D)$ and drops the empty ones. Under proof-requiring updating a datum must be explained by the hypothesis; under consistency-requiring updating it is added to it.

\subsection{Computability}\label{sec:basic:computability}

The function $D\mapsto\Unif(O_{\psi_D})$ is left-c.e.\ uniformly in $D$, and $\MA(D)=\Unif(O_{\psi_D})-\Inc_0$ with $\Inc_0$ left-c.e.\ (\cref{prop:basic:form}); but $\Unif(O_{\psi_D})$ is not a semimeasure: it gives an inconsistent theory credit for every datum. The correction that turns it into $\MA$ is one real, and that real is as complex as a left-c.e.\ real can be.

\begin{theorem}[The inconsistency probability is an $\Omega$-like real]\status{(a), (b) proved; (c) proved, given Chaitin's theorem that $\Omega_V$ is Martin-L\"of random and the Levin--Schnorr characterisation}\src{fundamentals Thm 3.2}\label{thm:basic:inc}
(a) $\Inc_0$ is not computable. Hence $\MA(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0$ is right-c.e.\ and not left-c.e., and $D\mapsto\MA(D)$ is not lower semicomputable. (b) For every left-c.e.\ $\alpha\in[0,1]$ there are a $V$-program $p_\alpha$ and a left-c.e.\ real $\gamma_\alpha$ with $\Inc_0=2^{-\lvert p_\alpha\rvert}\alpha+\gamma_\alpha$; consequently $\alpha\le_S\Inc_0$ for every left-c.e.\ $\alpha$, and $\Inc_0$ is Solovay complete. (c) $\Inc_0$ is Martin-L\"of random.
\end{theorem}

A machine that emits $\bot$ on the tapes whose binary real lies below $\alpha$ has inconsistency probability $\alpha$; splitting the tapes by their first program gives (b), and (a), (c) follow with the halting set and $\alpha=\Omega_V$ (\cref{lem:basic:demuth}). The theorem is a new application of the standard argument that $\Omega_V$ is Solovay complete. Machine laws of \SOIabbr are lsc and $\MF$ has $\emptyset'$-computable conditionals (Propositions 3.7 and A.10 of the companion paper); $\MA$ keeps the second property and loses the first by exactly one real: $\MA+\Inc_0$ is lsc (\cref{prop:basic:oracle}).

\subsection{Dominance and invariance}\label{sec:basic:dominance}

\begin{theorem}[Mixture identity and dominance]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Thm 4.1}\label{thm:basic:dominance}
For every two-valued $D$, $\MA(D)=\sum_{p\in\dom V}2^{-\lvert p\rvert}\nu_{G_{V(p)}}(D)+(1-\Omega_V)\ind[B\vdash\psi_D]=\sum_i\mm(i)\nu_{G_i}(D)+(1-\Omega_V)\ind[B\vdash\psi_D]$, and the same holds for $\MAthree$, the $\nu^3_{G_i}$, and the indicator that $B$ proves $\psi_D$ and decides no $\iota$-labelled sentence. Hence $\MA\ge2^{-K(\nu)}\nu$ for every theory-machine law $\nu$, and $\MAthree\ge2^{-K(G)}\nu^3_G$ for every theory machine $G$. In particular:
(i) $\MA(D)\ge2^{-K(e)-c}\ind[B\cup\re e\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D]$ for every index $e$; consequently, for every r.e.\ $T$ with $B\cup T$ consistent, $\UA[T]\ge2^{-c}\mmeq(T)\ge2^{-\Keq(T)-c}$ and $\UA(\Exts T)\ge2^{-c}\mm(\Exts T)$;
(ii) $\MA(D)\ge2^{-K(f)-c}\ind[f\supseteq D]$ for every partial computable $f:\Sent\rightharpoonup\{0,1\}$ with $B\cup\Gamma_f$ consistent, where $\Gamma_f:=\{\varphi^{f(\varphi)}:\varphi\in\dom f\}$;
(iii) $\MA(D)\ge2^{-K(v)-c}\sum_ev(e)\ind[B\cup\re e\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D]$ for every lsc semimeasure $v$ on indices; in particular $\MA\ge2^{-c}\WA$;
(iv) for an r.e.\ $T$ and a computable family $(\alpha_j)$, the law of $T$ plus the fair-coin facts $\alpha_j^{\omega_j}$ (the example of a theory plus coin facts in \cref{par:setting:examples}) is dominated with constant $2^{-K(T,(\alpha_j))-c}$.
\end{theorem}

The mixture and dominance arguments are the standard ones for universal mixtures (\citealp{zvonkin1970complexity}; Theorem 3.8 of the companion paper); what is new is the object. In particular $\LA(D)=\min_G[K(G)-\log\nu_G(D)]\pm c$: \SAIabbr is the best two-part code over theory machines, randomised theories included (\cref{prop:laws:twopart}). The universal machine matters only up to a constant factor (\cref{prop:basic:invariance}), and building a background $B'$ into $B$ gains at most $K(B')+c$ bits on any data, while the converse fails (\cref{prop:basic:background}). A change of language costs a constant too, if the translation preserves what is proved:

\begin{theorem}[Faithful translations]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Thm 4.4}\label{thm:basic:translation}
Let $(\mathcal L,B)$, $(\mathcal L',B')$ be two settings and $\tau:\Sent_{\mathcal L}\to\Sent_{\mathcal L'}$ computable with (T1) $B\cup\Gamma\vdash\varphi$ iff $B'\cup\tau[\Gamma]\vdash\tau(\varphi)$, for all $\Gamma\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ and $\varphi$; and (T2) $B'\vdash\tau(\neg\varphi)\leftrightarrow\neg\tau(\varphi)$ for all $\varphi$. For $D$ on $\mathcal L$ let $\MA'(\tau D):=\Unif\{\omega:B'\cup A'_\omega\text{ consistent and }\vdash\tau(\varphi)^{D(\varphi)}\text{ for all }\varphi\in\dom D\}$, with $A'$ the universal theory machine of $\mathcal L'$, and define $\WA'(\tau D)$, $\KA'(\tau D)$ likewise. Then $2^{-K(\tau)-c}\MA(D)\le\MA'(\tau D)\le2^{K(\tau)+c}\MA(D)$ for every $D$, the same holds for $\WA$, and $\lvert\KA'(\tau D)-\KA(D)\rvert\le K(\tau)+c$, with $c$ depending on both settings.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Adding vocabulary]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Cor 4.5}\label{cor:basic:vocabulary}
Let $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal L'$, keep $B$, and let $\tau$ be the inclusion. Then (T1) and (T2) hold, and for data in the old vocabulary $\MA^{\mathcal L'}(D)$ and $\MA^{\mathcal L}(D)$ agree up to a factor $2^{\pm c}$, $\WA$ likewise, $\KA^{\mathcal L'}(D)=\KA^{\mathcal L}(D)\pm c$, and predictive probabilities of $\mathcal L$-sentences agree up to $2^{\pm2c}$.
\end{corollary}

So in the unbounded universal setting new vocabulary buys at most a constant, since the pull-back of a theory to the old vocabulary is r.e.: this is the effective content of Craig's theorem \citep{craig1953axiomatizability} inside the prior. \Cref{thm:terms:horn} extends it class by class, and \cref{sec:terms} studies where vocabulary does pay.

\subsection{Loss bounds}\label{sec:basic:loss}

\begin{theorem}[Loss against a theory that proves the data]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Thm 5.1}\label{thm:basic:loss}
Let $(\varphi_t,b_t)_{t\le n}$ be any consistent reveal sequence, chosen in any way (in advance, adaptively, adversarially or non-computably; in any order; with repeats), and let $T$ be an r.e.\ theory with $B\cup T$ consistent and $B\cup T\vdash\varphi_t^{b_t}$ for all $t\le n$. Then in the semimeasure convention
\begin{align*}
\LA(D_n)-\LA(\emptyset)\ \le\ \LA(D_n)\ &\le\ -\log\UA(\Exts T)\\
&\le\ -\log\mm(\Exts T)+c\ \le\ -\log\mmeq(T)+c\ \le\ \Keq(T)+c,
\end{align*}
and also $\LA(D_n)\le-\log\WA(D_n)+c\le\KA(D_n)+c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every consistent extension of $T$ proves every datum, so $E_{D_n}$ contains every tape $\omega$ with $B\cup A_\omega$ consistent and $\Cn(A_\omega)\supseteq\Cn(T)$. Then use \cref{thm:basic:dominance}(i), $[T]\subseteq\Exts T$, $\mmeq(T)\ge2^{-\Keq(T)}$ and $\LA(\emptyset)\ge0$; the left side is the cumulative loss by \cref{prop:basic:structure}(d). The last chain is \cref{thm:basic:dominance}(iii) and $\WA\ge2^{-\KA}$.
\end{proof}

The bound favours the weakest theory that decides the data: a theory such as $\PA$ has small $\Keq$ although every axiomatisation of it is infinite. Compared with the bound $K(f)+c$ of Theorem 5.1(b) of the companion paper for data from a partial computable $f$, a program is replaced by a theory up to equivalence, which need decide only the sentences asked.

A law pays for its listed exceptions, positions included, and only one bit per exception when the exceptions are atoms it is silent about (\cref{prop:basic:exceptions}). The \emph{Bayes predictor}, which outputs the most probable of $1,0,\iota$, makes at most $\Keq(T)+c$ mistakes under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:basic:loss}; the \emph{Kolmogorov predictor}, which predicts with the first compatible program in a well-order of programs, errs or abstains at most $2^k$ times, $k:=\sup_n\KA(D_n)$, when the order refines length, and this hypothesis is needed (\cref{prop:basic:mistakes}). With three-valued data labelled by the random theory of a theory machine $G$ with $\nu_G(\emptyset)=1$, and questions chosen from the history and from randomness independent of the tape, the expected divergences between the predictions of $G$ and of $\MAthree$ sum to less than $K(G)$; and in any order the three-valued loss on data labelled by the trichotomy of an r.e.\ theory $T$ with $B\cup T$ consistent is at most $\Keq(T)+c$ (\cref{thm:basic:merging,cor:basic:threeloss}).

\subsection{No coding theorem}\label{sec:basic:nocoding}

\begin{proposition}[Coding lower bound and the chain]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Prop 6.1}\label{prop:basic:chain}
(a) If $S$ is a finite set of pairwise $B$-incompatible sentences and $\sigma\in S$, then $-\log\Bel_0(\sigma)\ge K(\sigma\mid S)-c$. (b) $\LA(D)\ge K(b_D\mid\dom D)-c$ for every $D$ (also \cref{prop:basic:lower}). (c) For every consistent $D$ and every finite set $S$ of pairwise $B$-incompatible sentences with $\psi_D\in S$, $2^{-\KA(D)}\le\WA(D)\le2^c\MA(D)\le2^{K(S)+c}2^{-\KA(D)}$. With $S$ the $2^{\lvert\dom D\rvert}$ label conjunctions on the two-valued part of $\dom D$, $K(S)\le K(\dom D)+c$.
\end{proposition}

The slack $K(S)$, in place of the $K(\dom D)$ of Theorem 4.4 of the companion paper, can be much smaller: for ``which of $m$ incompatible laws holds'' it is $K(m)+O(1)$ when $S$ is computable from $m$. It cannot be removed:

\begin{theorem}[No coding theorem for axiom systems]\status{proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Thm 6.2}\label{thm:basic:nocoding}
In the literal setting, for every $d\ge2$ there are distinct $x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}^d$ such that $D:=\{(R(t_{x_1}),0),(R(t_{x_2}),1)\}$ satisfies (i) $\KA(D)\ge\ell_d>\log d-2$, with $\ell_d:=\lceil\log(d/\log3)\rceil-1$, and $\WA(D)\le\Omtail{\ell_d}$; (ii) $-\log\WA(D)\le2+K(\lceil\log(d+1)\rceil)+c$; (iii) $-\log\MA(D)\le2+c$. Parts (ii) and (iii) hold for every data set of two literals on distinct words of length at most $d$. Hence $\KA(D)+\log\WA(D)\ge\log d-K(\lceil\log(d+1)\rceil)-c-4\to\infty$ and $\MA(D)/\WA(D)\ge2^{-c-2}/\Omtail{\ell_d}\to\infty$: no two of $2^{-\KA}$, $\WA$, $\MA$ agree up to a constant factor.
\end{theorem}

Short programs sort the words of length $d$ into fewer than $2^d$ classes by what they prove about each word's literal, while a coin per atom proves a given labelling of two atoms with probability $\frac14$. This is Theorem 4.9 of the companion paper for axiom systems; since $D$ is the single sentence $\psi_D$, no coding theorem holds for single sentences. For whole theories $\Keq$ and $-\log\mmeq$ agree up to a factor $3$ and logarithmic terms (\cref{prop:basic:wholetheory}).

\subsection{Why proof-requiring compatibility is primary}\label{sec:basic:primary}

The weaker requirements of \cref{def:setting:variants,def:setting:graded} fail as follows; let $H_\omega:=B\cup A_\omega\cup D^\pm$.

\begin{proposition}[The $0/1$ consistency-requiring weight does not discriminate]\status{proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Prop 7.1}\label{prop:basic:cr}
(a) For $\varphi\notin\dom D$, $\MAnc(D+(\varphi,1))+\MAnc(D+(\varphi,0))=\MAnc(D)+\Unif(N_\varphi)\ge\MAnc(D)$, where $N_\varphi$ is the set of tapes $\omega$ such that $H_\omega$ is consistent and decides neither $\varphi$ nor $\neg\varphi$; so $\MAnc$ is not a semimeasure; it is the plausibility $\Pl_0(\psi_D)$, the dual of a belief function. (b) $\MAnc(D)\ge\epsempty$ for every consistent $D$. The coin-free consistency-requiring weight is at least $2^{-\lvert p_\emptyset\rvert}$, and the Kolmogorov version $\min\{\lvert p\rvert:B\cup\re{V(p)}\cup D^\pm\text{ consistent}\}$ is at most $\lvert p_\emptyset\rvert$, on all consistent $D$. (c) The consistency-requiring posterior after $D$ is the prior restricted to the tapes whose theory is consistent with $D$: data eliminate hypotheses but never change the odds between survivors.
\end{proposition}

Part (c) is the one-tape form of Proposition 2.22(b) of the earlier report. The inductive work is left to adding the data to the hypothesis, and the proper predictor of this weight, Dempster's rule, pays for it: at least $\log\frac1{1-\epsempty}$ bits for every datum that the earlier data do not entail, and on suitable data of fresh atoms its cumulative loss depends on the reveal order, with a gap linear in the number of data (\cref{prop:basic:crorder}).

The graded weight $\MAhalf$ of \cref{def:setting:graded} is a weak semimeasure with $\MAhalf\ge\MA$, but it charges for entailed data, so it is the law of no theory machine, and its advantage over $\MA$ measures how the data are packaged, not induction (\cref{prop:basic:graded,prop:basic:gradedgap}). Its Möbius sum on the inputs $\varphi,\neg\varphi$ is $-\frac12$ times the mass of the consistent random theories that decide neither (negative when $B$ does not decide $\varphi$), against $+1$ times that mass for $\MA$, so $\MAhalf/\MAhalf(\emptyset)$ is not even a containment functional; and the consistency-requiring predictor is a mixture of the proof-requiring one and a component of weak theories plus the data (\cref{prop:basic:crmix}). The distinction exists only because the random theory is incomplete:

\begin{proposition}\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Prop 10.1(f)}\label{prop:basic:prcr}
If $B\cup T$ is complete and consistent, then $B\cup T\vdash\psi_D$ iff $B\cup T\cup D^\pm$ is consistent, for every $D$. Hence for any prior concentrated on complete theories, proof-requiring and consistency-requiring compatibility coincide, the trichotomy has no independent outcome, and conditioning on $D$ restricts to the complete theories that contain $D^\pm$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A complete consistent $B\cup T$ proves $\psi_D$ or $\neg\psi_D$, and is consistent with $\psi_D$ iff it does not prove $\neg\psi_D$.
\end{proof}

Priors of Demski's type complete their random theory, and so cannot see the difference between explaining a datum and tolerating it (\cref{prop:basic:demski}).

\subsection{Limits and identification}\label{sec:basic:limits}

On a consistent stream the posterior converges in total variation to the prior restricted to the consistent extensions of the data if these have positive prior mass, and the loss is then bounded; otherwise it diverges (\cref{prop:basic:limitpost}). Data presenting a consistent r.e.\ $T^*$ with $B\cup T^*$ complete concentrate the posterior on the class of $T^*$; if $B\cup T^*$ is incomplete, two-valued data leave each sentence independent of $B\cup T^*$ with limit belief and disbelief in $(0,1)$, and three-valued data identify the class of $T^*$ (\cref{prop:basic:limits}). As with Gold's text and informant (cf.\ \citealp{gold1967language}), no learner, computable or not, identifies from two-valued data every theory of a simple family of sets of fresh atoms (\cref{prop:basic:textinformant}). If $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$, the consistent random theory is almost surely incomplete (\cref{prop:basic:incomplete}).

\subsection{Order dependence and the Kolmogorov predictor}\label{sec:basic:order}

The normalised loss depends on the reveal order, by a number of bits linear in $n$ on some $2n$ data built from fresh atoms (\cref{thm:basic:order}); on logically independent data this is open (\cref{q:basic:indeporder}). For every $m$ and well-order of programs, the Kolmogorov predictor errs or abstains $2^m-1$ times on some reveal sequence with $\KA(D)\le m+K(m)+c$, where the Bayes predictor makes at most $\KA(D)+c$ mistakes (\cref{thm:basic:kolmogorov}). A \emph{random consistent theory} $\Theta$ is a Borel random variable with consistent, deductively closed values, and $\beta(\chi):=\Pr[\chi\in\Theta]$ its \emph{provability function}.

\begin{question}\status{open}\src{fundamentals Q6, Prop 11.4}\label{q:basic:domination}
Does $\MA$ dominate every lower semicomputable provability function, that is, is $\Bel_0\ge2^{-c_\beta}\beta$ whenever $\beta$ is lsc?
\end{question}

Domination holds with the slack $K(S)$ of \cref{prop:basic:chain}, and for the closure of a theory machine's output; on random sets of literals of a fresh predicate the question is equivalent to Question 3.14 of the companion paper for binary labels (\cref{prop:basic:domination}).
