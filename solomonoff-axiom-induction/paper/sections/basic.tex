\section{Basic theorems}\label{sec:basic}

This section develops the theory of $\MA$: its structure and updating rules, computability, dominance and invariance, loss bounds, the failure of a coding theorem, the reasons to prefer proof-requiring compatibility, and the limit of the posterior. Proofs not given in place are in \cref{app:basic}.

\subsection{Structure and updating}\label{sec:basic:structure}

Recall from the companion paper that a map $\nu$ from data sets to $[0,1]$ is a \emph{weak semimeasure} if $\nu(\emptyset)\le1$ and the masses of the one-datum extensions of $D$ at an input sum to at most $\nu(D)$ (its Definition 3.2), and that the \emph{containment functional} of a random partial function $F$ is $D\mapsto\Pr[F\supseteq D]$ (its Definition 3.4).

\begin{proposition}[Structure]\status{proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Prop 2.1}\label{prop:basic:structure}
Let $G$ be a theory machine. (a) If $\nu_G(D)>0$, then $D$ is consistent; $\MA(D)>0$ iff $D$ is consistent, and $\MAthree(D)>0$ iff moreover $B\cup D^\pm$ decides no $\iota$-labelled sentence. (b) For two-valued $D$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$, $\nu_G(D+(\varphi,1))+\nu_G(D+(\varphi,0))+\nu^3_G(D+(\varphi,\iota))=\nu_G(D)$; so $\nu_G$ is a weak semimeasure, and for $\MA$ the defect at $\varphi$ divided by $\MA(D)$ is $\Indep_D(\varphi)$. (c) $\nu_G(D)$ depends on $D$ only through $\Cn(D^\pm)$; if $B\cup D^\pm\vdash\varphi^b$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$, then $\nu_G(D+(\varphi,b))=\nu_G(D)$ and $\nu_G(D+(\varphi,1-b))=0$: entailed data cost nothing and contradicting data are fatal. (d) For every consistent reveal sequence, in any order and with repeats, $\sum_{t\le n}-\log\MA(y_t\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)=\LA(D_n)-\LA(\emptyset)$, which depends only on $\Cn(D_n^\pm)$. (e) $0<\MA(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0<1$. (f) For three-valued $D$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$, $\sum_{y\in Y_3}\nu^3_G(D+(\varphi,y))=\nu^3_G(D)$, and $\nu^3_G(\emptyset)=\nu_G(\emptyset)$; so the conditionals of $\MAthree$ are probability distributions on $Y_3$. (g) $\nu^3_G(D)=\Unif\{\omega\in\mathrm{Cons}^G:\tri^G_\omega\supseteq D\}$, also for two-valued $D$ and $\nu_G$. So, when $\nu_G(\emptyset)>0$, $\nu^3_G/\nu_G(\emptyset)$ is the containment functional of the random total function $\tri^G_\omega$ under $\Unif(\cdot\mid\mathrm{Cons}^G)$, and $\nu_G/\nu_G(\emptyset)$ is that of the random partial function obtained by reading $\iota$ as ``undefined''.
\end{proposition}

On $E^G_D$ the random theory is consistent, so at $\varphi$ it proves $\varphi$, refutes it, or decides neither, which are the three events of (b); (e) uses the tapes that begin with programs for the empty theory and for $\{\bot\}$ (\cref{app:basic:structure}). By (g), $\MA$ is a (sub-probability) containment functional, which is strictly stronger than being weak (the companion paper's Example 3.5, and \cref{prop:basic:graded}(e)).

\begin{proposition}[Proof-requiring updating is geometric conditioning]\status{proved; computed; the names of the rules known}\src{fundamentals Prop 2.2}\label{prop:basic:geometric}
(a) If $\MA(D)>0$, then $\Bel_D(\chi)=\Bel_0(\psi_D\wedge\chi)/\Bel_0(\psi_D)=\Belhat_0(\psi_D\wedge\chi)/\Belhat_0(\psi_D)$ for every $\chi$. (b) $\Bel_0$ and every $\Bel_D$ are totally monotone on the Lindenbaum algebra of $B$: $\Bel_D(\bigvee_{i\le k}\chi_i)\ge\sum_{\emptyset\ne I\subseteq[k]}(-1)^{\lvert I\rvert+1}\Bel_D(\bigwedge_{i\in I}\chi_i)$ for all $\chi_1,\dots,\chi_k$. (c) There is a probability $P_D$ on the complete consistent extensions of $B$ (a Borel probability on the Stone space) with $\Bel_D(\chi)\le P_D(\chi)\le\Pl_D(\chi)$ for every $\chi$.
\end{proposition}

So $\Bel_D$ is a belief function in the sense of \citet{shafer1976mathematical}, with random focal set $\mathrm{Mod}(B\cup A_\omega)$ and plausibility $\Pl_D$, and $\Bel_0$ is the probability of provability, as Pearl puts it \citep{pearl1988probabilistic}. Part (a), which holds because a theory proves $\psi_D$ and $\chi$ iff it proves $\psi_D\wedge\chi$, is the \emph{geometric rule} of conditioning of $\Belhat_0$, usually attributed to Suppes and Zanotti \citep{suppes1977using}: keep the focal sets inside the conditioning event and renormalise. Parts (b) and (c) are the one-tape forms of Propositions 2.18 and 2.19 of \citet{claude2026axiominduction}, stated there for countable classes of theories.

\begin{proposition}[Consistency-requiring updating is Dempster conditioning]\status{proved; computed; the name of the rule known}\src{fundamentals Prop 2.3}\label{prop:basic:dempster}
Let $\psi:=\psi_D$ and $\MAnc(D)>0$. Then $\MAnc(D)=\Pl_0(\psi)$ and
$\Belnc_D(\varphi)=\dfrac{\Bel_0(\varphi\vee\neg\psi)-\Bel_0(\neg\psi)}{\Bel_0(\top)-\Bel_0(\neg\psi)}=\dfrac{\Belhat_0(\varphi\vee\neg\psi)-\Belhat_0(\neg\psi)}{1-\Belhat_0(\neg\psi)}$.
\end{proposition}

The right-hand side is Dempster's rule of conditioning \citep{dempster1967upper,shafer1976mathematical}; the proof is the deduction theorem. With focal sets $F_\omega:=\mathrm{Mod}(B\cup A_\omega)$, proof-requiring updating keeps the $F_\omega$ contained in $\mathrm{Mod}(\psi)$, while Dempster's rule replaces each $F_\omega$ by $F_\omega\cap\mathrm{Mod}(\psi)$ and drops the empty ones. Under proof-requiring updating a datum must be explained by the hypothesis; under consistency-requiring updating it is added to it.

\subsection{Computability}\label{sec:basic:computability}

\begin{proposition}\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Prop 3.1}\label{prop:basic:form}
$\MA(D)=\Unif(O_{\psi_D})-\Inc_0$, where $\Unif(O_{\psi_D})$ is left-c.e.\ uniformly in $D$ and $\Inc_0=\Unif(O_\bot)$ is left-c.e.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:setting:measurable}(a), (c) for $G=G_U$.
\end{proof}

The lsc function $D\mapsto\Unif(O_{\psi_D})$ is not a semimeasure: it gives an inconsistent theory credit for every datum. The correction that turns it into $\MA$ is one real, and that real is as complex as a left-c.e.\ real can be.

\begin{theorem}[The inconsistency probability is an $\Omega$-like real]\status{(a), (b) proved; (c) proved, given Chaitin's theorem that $\Omega_V$ is Martin-L\"of random and the Levin--Schnorr characterisation}\src{fundamentals Thm 3.2}\label{thm:basic:inc}
(a) $\Inc_0$ is not computable. Hence $\MA(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0$ is right-c.e.\ and not left-c.e., and $D\mapsto\MA(D)$ is not lower semicomputable. (b) For every left-c.e.\ $\alpha\in[0,1]$ there are a $V$-program $p_\alpha$ and a left-c.e.\ real $\gamma_\alpha$ with $\Inc_0=2^{-\lvert p_\alpha\rvert}\alpha+\gamma_\alpha$; consequently $\alpha\le_S\Inc_0$ for every left-c.e.\ $\alpha$, and $\Inc_0$ is Solovay complete. (c) $\Inc_0$ is Martin-L\"of random.
\end{theorem}

A theory machine that emits $\bot$ on the tapes whose binary real lies below $\alpha$ has inconsistency probability $\alpha$; splitting tapes by their first program gives (b), the halting set gives (a), and $\alpha=\Omega_V$ \citep{chaitin1975theory} with Demuth's lemma (\cref{lem:basic:demuth}) gives (c). This is the standard argument that $\Omega$ is Solovay complete (for $\le_S$ see \citealp{solovay1975draft,downey2010algorithmic}), applied to the event that the random theory is inconsistent; probabilities of machine behaviours are studied in \citet{becher2006randomness} and \citet{barmpalias2016random}. By \citet{kucera2001randomness} and \citet{calude2001recursively}, $\Inc_0$ is an $\Omega$-number; we do not use this. Every machine law of \SOIabbr is lsc, and $\MF$ is lsc with $\emptyset'$-computable conditionals (Propositions 3.7 and A.10 of the companion paper). $\MA$ keeps the second property and loses the first by exactly one real: its values and conditionals are $\emptyset'$-computable and $\MA+\Inc_0$ is lsc (\cref{prop:basic:oracle}). Function machines are consistent by construction; the consistency of a theory machine's output is a $\Pi^0_1$ property of the tape.

\subsection{Dominance and invariance}\label{sec:basic:dominance}

\begin{theorem}[Mixture identity and dominance]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Thm 4.1}\label{thm:basic:dominance}
For every two-valued $D$, $\MA(D)=\sum_{p\in\dom V}2^{-\lvert p\rvert}\nu_{G_{V(p)}}(D)+(1-\Omega_V)\ind[B\vdash\psi_D]=\sum_i\mm(i)\nu_{G_i}(D)+(1-\Omega_V)\ind[B\vdash\psi_D]$, and the same holds for $\MAthree$, the $\nu^3_{G_i}$, and the indicator that $B$ proves $\psi_D$ and decides no $\iota$-labelled sentence. Hence $\MA\ge2^{-K(\nu)}\nu$ for every theory-machine law $\nu$, and $\MAthree\ge2^{-K(G)}\nu^3_G$ for every theory machine $G$. In particular:
(i) $\MA(D)\ge2^{-K(e)-c}\ind[B\cup\re e\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D]$ for every index $e$; consequently, for every r.e.\ $T$ with $B\cup T$ consistent, $\UA[T]\ge2^{-c}\mmeq(T)\ge2^{-\Keq(T)-c}$ and $\UA(\Exts T)\ge2^{-c}\mm(\Exts T)$;
(ii) $\MA(D)\ge2^{-K(f)-c}\ind[f\supseteq D]$ for every partial computable $f:\Sent\rightharpoonup\{0,1\}$ with $B\cup\Gamma_f$ consistent, where $\Gamma_f:=\{\varphi^{f(\varphi)}:\varphi\in\dom f\}$;
(iii) $\MA(D)\ge2^{-K(v)-c}\sum_ev(e)\ind[B\cup\re e\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D]$ for every lsc semimeasure $v$ on indices; in particular $\MA\ge2^{-c}\WA$;
(iv) for an r.e.\ $T$ and a computable family $(\alpha_j)$, the law of $T$ plus the fair-coin facts $\alpha_j^{\omega_j}$ (Example (iii) after \cref{def:setting:machine}) is dominated with constant $2^{-K(T,(\alpha_j))-c}$.
\end{theorem}

On the cylinder $[p]$ of a program $p\in\dom V$, $G_U$ emits what $G_{V(p)}$ emits on the rest of the tape, so $\Unif(E_D\cap[p])=2^{-\lvert p\rvert}\nu_{G_{V(p)}}(D)$. The mixture and dominance arguments are the standard ones for universal mixtures (\citealp{zvonkin1970complexity}; the companion paper's Theorem 3.8); what is new is the object. In particular $\LA(D)=\min_G[K(G)-\log\nu_G(D)]\pm c$: \SAIabbr is the best two-part code over theory machines, randomised theories included (\cref{prop:laws:twopart}). Neither the universal machine nor the background matters beyond a constant factor:

\begin{proposition}[Choice of the universal machine]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Prop 4.2}\label{prop:basic:invariance}
Call a theory machine $G'$ \emph{universal} if for every theory machine $G$ there is a string $p_G$ with $A^{G'}_{p_G\omega}=A^G_\omega$ for all $\omega$. If $G'$ is universal, then $2^{-c}\MA\le\nu_{G'}\le2^c\MA$, and the same for the three-valued laws, with $c$ depending only on $G_U$ and $G'$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[The background is a prior shift]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Prop 4.3}\label{prop:basic:background}
Let $B,B'$ be decidable and consistent with $B\subseteq\Cn_\emptyset(B')$ (plain logical consequence), and write $\MA^B$, $\MA^{B'}$ for \SAIabbr with these backgrounds. Then $\MA^B(D)\ge2^{-K(B')-c}\MA^{B'}(D)$ for every $D$, with $K(B')$ the complexity of an index of $B'$; likewise $\WA^B\ge2^{-K(B')-c}\WA^{B'}$ and $\KA^B\le\KA^{B'}+K(B')+c$. The converse fails: a $D$ consistent with $B$ and inconsistent with $B'$ has $\MA^{B'}(D)=0<\MA^B(D)$.
\end{proposition}

So building knowledge into the background gains at most $K(B')+c$ bits on any data. A change of language costs a constant too, if the translation preserves what is proved:

\begin{theorem}[Faithful translations]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Thm 4.4}\label{thm:basic:translation}
Let $(\mathcal L,B)$, $(\mathcal L',B')$ be two settings and $\tau:\Sent_{\mathcal L}\to\Sent_{\mathcal L'}$ computable with (T1) $B\cup\Gamma\vdash\varphi$ iff $B'\cup\tau[\Gamma]\vdash\tau(\varphi)$, for all $\Gamma\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ and $\varphi$; and (T2) $B'\vdash\tau(\neg\varphi)\leftrightarrow\neg\tau(\varphi)$ for all $\varphi$. For $D$ on $\mathcal L$ let $\MA'(\tau D):=\Unif\{\omega:B'\cup A'_\omega\text{ consistent and }\vdash\tau(\varphi)^{D(\varphi)}\text{ for all }\varphi\in\dom D\}$, with $A'$ the universal theory machine of $\mathcal L'$, and define $\WA'(\tau D)$, $\KA'(\tau D)$ likewise. Then $2^{-K(\tau)-c}\MA(D)\le\MA'(\tau D)\le2^{K(\tau)+c}\MA(D)$ for every $D$, the same holds for $\WA$, and $\lvert\KA'(\tau D)-\KA(D)\rvert\le K(\tau)+c$, with $c$ depending on both settings.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Adding vocabulary]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Cor 4.5}\label{cor:basic:vocabulary}
Let $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal L'$, keep $B$, and let $\tau$ be the inclusion. Then (T1) and (T2) hold, and for data in the old vocabulary $\MA^{\mathcal L'}(D)$ and $\MA^{\mathcal L}(D)$ agree up to a factor $2^{\pm c}$, $\WA$ likewise, $\KA^{\mathcal L'}(D)=\KA^{\mathcal L}(D)\pm c$, and predictive probabilities of $\mathcal L$-sentences agree up to $2^{\pm2c}$.
\end{corollary}

One direction of \cref{thm:basic:translation} emits $\tau[A_\omega]$, the other pulls a theory $A'$ back to $\{\varphi:B'\cup A'\vdash\tau(\varphi)\}$; (T1) for the inclusion holds because every $\mathcal L$-structure expands to an $\mathcal L'$-structure. So in the unbounded universal setting new vocabulary buys at most a constant: the pull-back of a theory, its consequences in the old vocabulary, is r.e., and the universal machine enumerates it. This is the effective content of Craig's theorem \citep{craig1953axiomatizability} inside the prior; \cref{thm:terms:horn} extends it class by class, and \cref{sec:terms} studies where vocabulary does pay.

\subsection{Loss bounds}\label{sec:basic:loss}

\begin{theorem}[Loss against a theory that proves the data]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Thm 5.1}\label{thm:basic:loss}
Let $(\varphi_t,b_t)_{t\le n}$ be any consistent reveal sequence, chosen in any way (in advance, adaptively, adversarially or non-computably; in any order; with repeats), and let $T$ be an r.e.\ theory with $B\cup T$ consistent and $B\cup T\vdash\varphi_t^{b_t}$ for all $t\le n$. Then in the semimeasure convention
\[
\LA(D_n)-\LA(\emptyset)\ \le\ \LA(D_n)\ \le\ -\log\UA(\Exts T)\ \le\ -\log\mm(\Exts T)+c\ \le\ -\log\mmeq(T)+c\ \le\ \Keq(T)+c,
\]
and also $\LA(D_n)\le-\log\WA(D_n)+c\le\KA(D_n)+c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every consistent extension of $T$ proves every datum, so $E_{D_n}\supseteq\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(A_\omega)\supseteq\Cn(T)\}$. Then use \cref{thm:basic:dominance}(i), $[T]\subseteq\Exts T$, $\mmeq(T)\ge2^{-\Keq(T)}$ and $\LA(\emptyset)\ge0$; the left side is the cumulative loss by \cref{prop:basic:structure}(d). The last chain is \cref{thm:basic:dominance}(iii) and $\WA\ge2^{-\KA}$.
\end{proof}

The complexity that appears is the algorithmic probability of the class of all consistent extensions of $T$, bounded by $\Keq(T)$, the complexity of $T$ up to deductive equivalence. So the bound favours the weakest theory that decides the data, and a theory such as $\PA$ has small $\Keq$ although every axiomatisation of it is infinite. Compared with the bound $K(f)+c$ of the companion paper (its Theorem 5.1(b)) for data from a partial computable $f$, a program is replaced by a theory up to equivalence, which need decide only the sentences asked.

\begin{proposition}[Laws plus exceptions]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Prop 5.2}\label{prop:basic:exceptions}
(a) $\LA(D_n)\le-\log\nu(D_n)+K(\nu)$ for every theory-machine law $\nu$. (b) For every r.e.\ $T$ and every $E\subseteq D_n$ with $B\cup T\cup E^\pm$ consistent and $B\cup T\cup E^\pm\vdash\psi_{D_n}$: $\LA(D_n)\le\Keq(T)+K(E)+c$. (c) Let $(\alpha_j)_{j\ge1}$ be a computable family of sentences and $T$ r.e.\ with $B\cup T\cup\{\alpha_j^{x_j}:j\ge1\}$ consistent for every $x\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}$. If every datum of $D_n$ is proved by $T$ or is one of the $\alpha_j$ with some label, and $m$ distinct $j$ occur, then $\LA(D_n)\le K(T,(\alpha_j))+m+c$.
\end{proposition}

In (b) a law pays for its listed exceptions, positions included; in (c) each exception costs one bit, without paying for which atoms were exceptional. \Cref{sec:laws} extends (c) from atoms a law is silent about to answers it only constrains.

\begin{proposition}[Mistake bounds]\status{(a) proved; (b), (c) proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Prop 5.3}\label{prop:basic:mistakes}
(a) Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:basic:loss}, the predictor that outputs the most probable of $1,0,\iota$ under $(\Bel,\Dis,\Indep)_{D_{t-1}}(\varphi_t)$ (ties broken arbitrarily; the stored label at a repeat) makes at most $-\log(\UA(\Exts T)/\MA(\emptyset))\le\Keq(T)+c$ mistakes, a mistake being any output other than $b_t$, $\iota$ included. (b) Fix a well-order of $\dom V$ that refines length. The \emph{Kolmogorov predictor} takes at step $t$ the first program $p_t$ with $B\cup\re{V(p_t)}$ consistent and proving $\psi_{D_{t-1}}$, predicts $1$ (resp.\ $0$) if $\re{V(p_t)}$ proves $\varphi_t$ (resp.\ $\neg\varphi_t$), and abstains otherwise. If $k:=\sup_n\KA(D_n)<\infty$ (for instance $k\le\Keq(T)+c$ as in \cref{thm:basic:loss}), it errs or abstains at most $2^k$ times. (c) The order hypothesis in (b) is needed: for every $m$ there are a well-order of $\dom V$ of type $\omega$ and a data set $D$ with $\KA(D)\le K(m)+c\le2\log m+c'$ on which the Kolmogorov predictor with that order errs or abstains $2^m-1$ times.
\end{proposition}

In (a) each mistake costs at least one bit (the halving argument; the companion paper's Corollary 5.4); in (b) a failing program is never chosen again, and Kraft's inequality allows at most $2^k$ programs of length at most $k$. The bound of (b) is essentially attained even for length-refining orders, where the Bayes predictor makes at most $\KA(D)+c$ mistakes (\cref{thm:basic:kolmogorov}): it is the selection rule that fails, not the prior. With three-valued data, the questions may be chosen by any rule:

\begin{theorem}[Merging for three-valued data]\status{proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Thm 5.4}\label{thm:basic:merging}
Let $G$ be a theory machine with $\nu_G(\emptyset)=1$, $\omega\sim\Unif$ and $A:=A^G_\omega$. Let $(u_t)$ be random variables independent of $\omega$, with any joint law, and let the questions be $\varphi_t:=s_t(h_{t-1},u_t)$ for arbitrary measurable rules $s_t$, where $h_{t-1}=(\varphi_1,y_1,\dots,\varphi_{t-1},y_{t-1})$ and $y_t\in Y_3$ is the trichotomy label of $\varphi_t$ under $A$. Let $D_t:=\{(\varphi_s,y_s):s\le t\}$ and $d_t:=\KL(\nu^3_G(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)\,\|\,\MAthree(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t))$ in bits ($0$ at a repeat). Then for every $n$, $\sum_{t\le n}\Ex d_t=\Ex\log\frac{\nu^3_G(D_n)}{\MAthree(D_n)}+\log\MA(\emptyset)\le K(G)+\log\MA(\emptyset)<K(G)$. Hence $d_t\to0$ almost surely, and $\sum_t\Ex\lVert\nu^3_G(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)-\MAthree(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)\rVert_1^2\le2\ln2\cdot K(G)$.
\end{theorem}

Given the history and the side randomness, the next label has law $\nu^3_G(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)$, so the log-ratio telescopes and dominance bounds it. With two-valued data the question rule would have to see $A$ for the two-valued conditionals to be the data law, and the expected bound can fail, as in the companion paper's remark after its Theorem 5.3; the pathwise bound \cref{prop:basic:exceptions}(a) still holds.

\begin{corollary}[Three-valued loss]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Cor 5.5}\label{cor:basic:threeloss}
If $D_n$ is three-valued and labelled by the trichotomy of an r.e.\ $T$ with $B\cup T$ consistent, then in any reveal order the loss in the three-valued convention is $-\log\MAthree(D_n)+\log\MA(\emptyset)\le-\log\UA[T]\le\Keq(T)+c$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Lower bound]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Prop 5.6}\label{prop:basic:lower}
$\LA(D)\ge K(b_D\mid\dom D)-c$ for every $D$, where $b_D$ is the vector of two-valued labels in canonical order. So in the semimeasure convention, on fair-coin labels of any fixed $n$ sentences, $\Ex\LA\ge n-c$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{No coding theorem}\label{sec:basic:nocoding}

\begin{proposition}[Coding lower bound and the chain]\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Prop 6.1}\label{prop:basic:chain}
(a) If $S$ is a finite set of pairwise $B$-incompatible sentences and $\sigma\in S$, then $-\log\Bel_0(\sigma)\ge K(\sigma\mid S)-c$. (b) $\LA(D)\ge K(b_D\mid\dom D)-c$ for every $D$. (c) For every consistent $D$ and every finite set $S$ of pairwise $B$-incompatible sentences with $\psi_D\in S$, $2^{-\KA(D)}\le\WA(D)\le2^c\MA(D)\le2^{K(S)+c}2^{-\KA(D)}$. With $S$ the $2^{\lvert\dom D\rvert}$ label conjunctions on the two-valued part of $\dom D$, $K(S)\le K(\dom D)+c$.
\end{proposition}

Since $\MA$ is not lsc, the coding theorem in (a) is applied to an auxiliary lsc semimeasure on $S$, the probability that a dovetailed proof search finds a given member of $S$ first. The slack $K(S)$, in place of the companion paper's $K(\dom D)$ (its Theorem 4.4), can be much smaller: for ``which of $m$ incompatible laws holds'' it is $K(m)+O(1)$ when $S$ is computable from $m$. It cannot be removed:

\begin{theorem}[No coding theorem for axiom systems]\status{proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Thm 6.2}\label{thm:basic:nocoding}
In the literal setting, for every $d\ge2$ there are distinct $x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}^d$ such that $D:=\{(R(t_{x_1}),0),(R(t_{x_2}),1)\}$ satisfies (i) $\KA(D)\ge\ell_d>\log d-2$, with $\ell_d:=\lceil\log(d/\log3)\rceil-1$, and $\WA(D)\le\Omtail{\ell_d}$; (ii) $-\log\WA(D)\le2+K(\lceil\log(d+1)\rceil)+c$; (iii) $-\log\MA(D)\le2+c$. Parts (ii) and (iii) hold for every data set of two literals on distinct words of length at most $d$. Hence $\KA(D)+\log\WA(D)\ge\log d-K(\lceil\log(d+1)\rceil)-c-4\to\infty$ and $\MA(D)/\WA(D)\ge2^{-c-2}/\Omtail{\ell_d}\to\infty$: no two of $2^{-\KA}$, $\WA$, $\MA$ agree up to a constant factor.
\end{theorem}

Fewer than $2^{\ell_d}$ programs are shorter than $\ell_d$, and they sort the words of length $d$ into fewer than $2^d$ classes by what they prove about each word's literal, so two words are never separated by a short program; a coin per atom proves a given labelling of two atoms with probability $\frac14$. This is the companion paper's Theorem 4.9 for axiom systems, proved directly with theory machines: deterministic axiom sets cannot separate atoms cheaply, while a coin can. Since $D$ is the single sentence $\psi_D$, no coding theorem holds for single sentences. On literal data the companion paper's two-point rate transfers (\cref{cor:soi:transfer}); for whole theories $\Keq$ and $-\log\mmeq$ agree up to a factor $3$ and logarithmic terms (\cref{prop:basic:wholetheory}).

\subsection{Why proof-requiring compatibility is primary}\label{sec:basic:primary}

Proof-requiring compatibility has the guarantees above. The weaker requirements of \cref{def:setting:variants} fail as follows; let $H_\omega:=B\cup A_\omega\cup D^\pm$.

\begin{proposition}[The $0/1$ consistency-requiring weight does not discriminate]\status{proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Prop 7.1}\label{prop:basic:cr}
(a) For $\varphi\notin\dom D$, $\MAnc(D+(\varphi,1))+\MAnc(D+(\varphi,0))=\MAnc(D)+\Unif\{\omega:H_\omega\text{ consistent and decides neither }\varphi\text{ nor }\neg\varphi\}\ge\MAnc(D)$: $\MAnc$ is not a semimeasure; it is the plausibility $\Pl_0(\psi_D)$, the dual of a belief function. (b) $\MAnc(D)\ge\epsempty$ for every consistent $D$. The coin-free consistency-requiring weight is at least $2^{-\lvert p_\emptyset\rvert}$, and the Kolmogorov version $\min\{\lvert p\rvert:B\cup\re{V(p)}\cup D^\pm\text{ consistent}\}$ is at most $\lvert p_\emptyset\rvert$, on all consistent $D$. (c) The consistency-requiring posterior after $D$ is the prior restricted to the tapes whose theory is consistent with $D$: data eliminate hypotheses but never change the odds between survivors.
\end{proposition}

Part (c) is the one-tape form of Proposition 2.22(b) of \citet{claude2026axiominduction}. The inductive work is left to adding the data to the hypothesis, and the proper predictor of this weight, Dempster's rule, pays for it:

\begin{proposition}[The consistency-requiring predictor]\status{(a), (b) proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Prop 7.2(a), (b)}\label{prop:basic:crorder}
(a) For every consistent $D$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$ with $B\cup D^\pm\nvdash\varphi^b$, $\Belnc_D(\varphi^b)\le1-\epsempty/\MAnc(D)\le1-\epsempty$. So on every consistent reveal sequence the cumulative loss $\sum_t-\log\Belnc_{D_{t-1}}(\varphi_t^{b_t})$ is at least $\log\frac1{1-\epsempty}$ times the number of steps whose datum is not entailed by the earlier data. (b) In the literal setting, let $a_i,s_{i,j}$ ($i,j\ge1$) be fresh atoms, computable in $(i,j)$, $e_{i,j}:=\neg s_{i,1}\wedge\dots\wedge\neg s_{i,j-1}\wedge s_{i,j}$, $b_{i,j}:=a_i\vee e_{i,j}$, and $c_R:=\lvert p_R\rvert$ for a $V$-program $p_R$ of an index of the deterministic machine emitting $\forall x\,R(x)$. (b1) For $D_j:=\{(a_1,1),(b_{1,j},1)\}$ and all but at most $1/(\Bel_0(a_1)\epsempty)$ indices $j$, the consistency-requiring probability of $D_j$ is strictly larger in the order $(a_1,b_{1,j})$ than in the order $(b_{1,j},a_1)$. (b2) For every $n$ there are $j_1,\dots,j_n\le\lceil2^{c_R+1}/\epsempty\rceil$ such that, for $D_n:=\{(a_i,1),(b_{i,j_i},1):i\le n\}$, the cumulative loss in the order $(b_{1,j_1},a_1,b_{2,j_2},a_2,\dots)$ exceeds that in the order $(a_1,b_{1,j_1},a_2,b_{2,j_2},\dots)$ by at least $n\log(1+2^{-c_R}\epsempty/2)$ bits.
\end{proposition}

On the stream $(\alpha_j,1)_{j\ge1}$ of fresh atoms no datum is entailed, so (a) gives a loss linear in $n$, while $\{\forall x\,R(x)\}$ decides the stream and the proof-requiring loss is $O(1)$ (\cref{thm:basic:loss}). In (b) revealing the stronger datum first is more probable; under normalised proof-requiring prediction the weaker datum first is (\cref{thm:basic:order}). The graded weight is no better a law of theories:

\begin{proposition}[Graded consistency-requiring weight]\status{proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Prop 7.3}\label{prop:basic:graded}
(a) For consistent $A$, $\nu^{1/2}_A$ is an order-independent weak semimeasure with $\nu^{1/2}_A(\emptyset)=1$; hence $\MAhalf$ is a weak semimeasure with $\MAhalf(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0$, and the conditionals of $\MAhalf/\MAhalf(\emptyset)$ form an order-independent predictor. (b) $\MAhalf\ge\MA$. (c) For fresh atoms $p,q$ and $D:=\{(p,1)\}$, $\MAhalf(D+(p\vee q,1))\le\MAhalf(D)-\epsempty/4$, although $B\cup D^\pm\vdash p\vee q$; so $\MAhalf$ is not the law of any theory machine. (d) $\MA(\{(p\wedge q,1)\})=\MA(\{(p,1),(q,1)\})$, but $\MAhalf(\{(p\wedge q,1)\})-\MAhalf(\{(p,1),(q,1)\})\ge\epsempty/4$. (e) $\MAhalf/\MAhalf(\emptyset)$ is not a containment functional, not even of a random partial function on $\Sent$ that ignores logic: for every $\varphi$, its Möbius sum on the inputs $\varphi,\neg\varphi$ (the companion paper's Proposition A.2) equals $-\frac12\Unif\{\omega\in\mathrm{Cons}:A_\omega\text{ decides neither }\varphi\text{ nor }\neg\varphi\}$, which is at most $-\frac12\epsempty<0$ when $B$ does not decide $\varphi$; for $\MA$ the same sum is $\Unif\{\omega\in\mathrm{Cons}:A_\omega\text{ decides neither}\}\ge0$.
\end{proposition}

So graded compatibility charges for entailed data and depends on how the data are packaged; its advantage over $\MA$ can come within $2\lvert D\rvert+O(\log)$ bits of $K(\dom D)+\lvert D\rvert$ (\cref{prop:basic:gradedgap}), which measures packaging, not induction. The consistency-requiring predictor is a mixture of the proof-requiring one and a component of weak theories plus the data (\cref{prop:basic:crmix}). Finally, the distinction exists only because the random theory is incomplete:

\begin{proposition}\status{proved}\src{fundamentals Prop 10.1(f)}\label{prop:basic:prcr}
If $B\cup T$ is complete and consistent, then $B\cup T\vdash\psi_D$ iff $B\cup T\cup D^\pm$ is consistent, for every $D$. Hence for any prior concentrated on complete theories, proof-requiring and consistency-requiring compatibility coincide, the trichotomy has no independent outcome, and conditioning on $D$ restricts to the complete theories that contain $D^\pm$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A complete consistent $B\cup T$ proves $\psi_D$ or $\neg\psi_D$, and is consistent with $\psi_D$ iff it does not prove $\neg\psi_D$.
\end{proof}

In short: under proof-requiring compatibility a theory must explain every datum; under consistency-requiring compatibility it need only tolerate it, and toleration is too weak to discriminate between theories. Priors of Demski's type complete their random theory, and so cannot see the difference (\cref{prop:basic:demski}).

\subsection{Limits and identification}\label{sec:basic:limits}

Let $D_\infty$ be a consistent stream, $E_\infty:=\bigcap_nE_{D_n}$ and $P_n:=\Unif(\cdot\mid E_{D_n})$. If $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$, the posterior converges in total variation to $P_\infty:=\Unif(\cdot\mid E_\infty)$, the prior restricted to the tapes whose theory is a consistent extension of the data, complete or not, and the loss is bounded; otherwise the loss diverges (\cref{prop:basic:limitpost}).

\begin{proposition}[Limits]\status{proved; computed}\src{fundamentals Props 8.2, 8.3}\label{prop:basic:limits}
Let $T^*$ be consistent and r.e. (a) If $B\cup T^*$ is complete (hence decidable) and $\Cn(D_\infty^\pm)=\Cn(T^*)$, then $P_n\{\omega:\Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn(T^*)\}\to1$, $\sup_\chi\lvert\Bel_{D_n}(\chi)-\ind[T^*\vdash\chi]\rvert\to0$, $\sup_\chi\Indep_{D_n}(\chi)\to0$, and the cumulative loss converges to $-\log(\UA[T^*]/\MA(\emptyset))\le\Keq(T^*)+c$; if $T^*$ is axiomatised by one sentence $\tau$ and $(\tau,1)\in D_n$, the first probability is $1$ from then on. (b) If $B\cup T^*$ is incomplete and $\Cn(D_\infty^\pm)=\Cn(T^*)$, then $P_\infty$ is the prior restricted to the consistent extensions of $T^*$, and for every $\psi$ independent of $B\cup T^*$: $\lim_n\Bel_{D_n}(\psi)=\UA(\Exts(T^*+\psi))/\UA(\Exts T^*)\in(0,1)$, $\lim_n\Dis_{D_n}(\psi)=\UA(\Exts(T^*+\neg\psi))/\UA(\Exts T^*)\in(0,1)$ and $\lim_n\Indep_{D_n}(\psi)\ge\UA[T^*]/\UA(\Exts T^*)>0$. The most probable limit class can be a strict extension: in the literal setting, with fresh atoms $\alpha_i$ on a computable sequence of distinct words, $U:=\{\alpha_i:i\in\mathbb N\}$ and $T_j:=U\setminus\{\alpha_j\}$, for all but finitely many $j$ the limit posterior on data presenting $T_j$ gives the class of $U$ more probability than the class of $T_j$; a most probable class exists and strictly extends $T_j$. (c) If every sentence appears with its $T^*$-trichotomy label, then $\Unif(\{\omega:\Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn(T^*)\}\mid E^3_{D_n})\to1$.
\end{proposition}

A complete consistent theory has no proper consistent extension, which gives (a); in (b) the classes of the $T_j$ are distinct, so their prior masses tend to $0$, while $U$ is simple. Two-valued data thus leave the prior on the extensions of an incomplete truth, and three-valued data identify it. This is the Bayesian shadow of Gold's distinction between learning from text and from an informant (cf.\ \citealp{gold1967language}): no learner, computable or not, identifies from two-valued data every theory of a simple family of finite and infinite sets of fresh atoms (\cref{prop:basic:textinformant}), so the failure is not an artefact of the prior. Above arithmetic the random theory is almost never complete: if $\mathcal L\supseteq\Lar$ and $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$, the consistent random theory of $\MA$ is almost surely incomplete, and random labellers almost surely fail consistent guessing (\cref{prop:basic:incomplete}, a new application of the fact that separating sets of effectively inseparable pairs have measure zero).

\subsection{Order dependence and the Kolmogorov predictor}\label{sec:basic:order}

The semimeasure-convention loss is order independent (\cref{prop:basic:structure}(d)); the normalised loss is not. Already for $D=\{(a,1),(a\vee c,1)\}$, with $B\cup\{a\}$ consistent and $B\cup\{\neg a\}$ consistent and deciding neither $c$ nor $\neg c$, the normalised probability of $D$ is strictly larger in the order $(a\vee c,a)$, because a theory proving $a$ always decides $a\vee c$ and one refuting $a$ need not; and two orders of the same $2n$ fresh-atom data can differ by at least $2^{-c_1-1}n$ bits (\cref{thm:basic:order}). The witnesses use entailment between data, so this does not answer the companion paper's question on logically independent data (\cref{q:basic:indeporder}). The single shortest compatible theory can also be a poor predictor where the mixture is good: on data built from fresh atoms $a_1,\dots,a_m$, the Kolmogorov predictor errs or abstains $2^m-1$ times for any well-order, while $\KA(D)\le m+K(m)+c$ and the Bayes predictor makes at most $\KA(D)+c$ mistakes (\cref{thm:basic:kolmogorov}). We close with the main open question about the object itself. Call a Borel random variable $\Theta$ with values in the consistent, deductively closed sets of sentences a \emph{random consistent theory}, and $\beta(\chi):=\Pr[\chi\in\Theta]$ its \emph{provability function}.

\begin{question}\status{open}\src{fundamentals Q6, Prop 11.4}\label{q:basic:domination}
Does $\MA$ dominate every lower semicomputable provability function, that is, is $\Bel_0\ge2^{-c_\beta}\beta$ whenever $\beta$ is lsc?
\end{question}

Domination holds with the slack $K(S)$ of \cref{prop:basic:chain}, and for the closure of a theory machine's output; on random sets of literals of a fresh predicate the question is equivalent to the companion paper's Question 3.14 for binary labels (\cref{prop:basic:domination}).
