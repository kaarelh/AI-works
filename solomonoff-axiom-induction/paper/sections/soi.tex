\section{Relation to \SOI}\label{sec:soi}

\SOI \citep{claudeSFI2026fundamentals} puts the universal prior on programs that label inputs and conditions on the labelled inputs seen. With sentences as inputs its hypotheses are partial labellers of sentences, and a labeller whose labels are consistent with $B$ is the program-shaped counterpart of a theory; so we compare \SAIabbr with \SOIabbr restricted to consistent labellers.\footnote{The comparison of \SAIabbr with consistent \SOIabbr, the example in which plain \SOIabbr beats \SAIabbr (\cref{rem:soi:encoding}), and the axiom schema of \cref{rem:soi:schema} first appeared in H\"anni's working notes \citep{hanni2026notes}.}
The two agree version by version, up to constant factors (\cref{sec:soi:equiv}); on positive literals of a fresh predicate \SAIabbr \emph{is} \SOIabbr on strings (\cref{sec:soi:literals}); logically structured data separate them (\cref{sec:soi:breaks}); and under time bounds the separations come from free deduction (\cref{sec:soi:time,sec:soi:charged,sec:soi:presburger}). \Cref{tab:soi} collects the relations; the proofs are in \cref{app:soi}. Losses are in the semimeasure convention unless stated, and $\asymp$ is equality within $2^{\pm c}$ on every finite data set.

\subsection{The objects and four maps}\label{sec:soi:objects}

\begin{definition}[The operator-induction side]\status{definition}\src{sfi \S1.1, Def 1.1}\label{def:soi:objects}
Let $\mathcal L$ and $B$ be as in \cref{def:setting:data}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item A \emph{function machine} is a c.e.\ set $F\subseteq\Sent\times\{0,1\}^*\times\{0,1\}$ such that $(\varphi,w,y),(\varphi,w',y')\in F$ with $w,w'$ comparable implies $y=y'$ \citep[Definition 3.6]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals}. On a tape $\omega$ it computes the partial labeller $f^F_\omega$: $f^F_\omega(\varphi):=y$ if $(\varphi,w,y)\in F$ for a prefix $w$ of $\omega$, and $f^F_\omega(\varphi)$ is undefined ($\bot$) otherwise; its law is $\nu_F(D):=\Unif\{\omega:f^F_\omega\supseteq D\}$. $F_U$ is the universal function machine (read $p\in\dom V$, then run $F_{V(p)}$ on the rest of the tape), $f_\omega:=f^{F_U}_\omega$, and $\MF:=\nu_{F_U}$.
\item For a partial $f:\Sent\rightharpoonup\{0,1\}$ let $\Gamma_f:=\{\varphi^{f(\varphi)}:\varphi\in\dom f\}$. The labeller $f$ is \emph{consistent} if $B\cup\Gamma_f$ is consistent, and \emph{closed} if moreover $f(\varphi)=b$ iff $B\cup\Gamma_f\vdash\varphi^b$, for all $\varphi$ and $b$.
\item \emph{One-tape consistent and closed \SOIabbr}: $\MFcons(D):=\Unif\{\omega:f_\omega\supseteq D,\ f_\omega\text{ consistent}\}$, and $\MFcl(D)$ likewise with ``closed and consistent''; $\nu^{\mathrm{cons}}_F$, $\nu^{\mathrm{cl}}_F$ are the same for one machine $F$.
\item \emph{Random deciders} are function machines $F$ with $f^F_\omega$ total for every $\omega$, read as theory machines with axiom set $A^{\mathrm{dec}}_F(\omega):=\{\chi:f^F_\omega(\chi)=1\}$; $\MAdec(D)$ is the sum of $2^{-\lvert p\rvert}\,\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A^{\mathrm{dec}}_{F_{V(p)}}(\omega)\text{ consistent and}\vdash\psi_D\}$ over the $p\in\dom V$ for which $F_{V(p)}$ is a random decider.
\item \emph{Coin-free and Kolmogorov}: $\Wcons(D)$ is the sum of $2^{-\lvert p\rvert}$ over the $p\in\dom V$ for which the partial computable labeller $\{V(p)\}$ is consistent and extends $D$, and $\KFcons(D)$ is the least such $\lvert p\rvert$; $\Wcl$, $\KFcl$ likewise with closed labellers; $\Wall$ drops the consistency condition (plain coin-free \SOIabbr on sentences). $W$ and $\KF$ are the companion paper's coin-free and Kolmogorov versions on strings.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Four maps.} The equivalences rest on four computable maps between hypotheses (\cref{lem:soi:maps}), each a constant-size prefix in front of a program, hence injective and weight-preserving up to a constant factor. \emph{Proof search} turns a theory machine $G$ into a function machine $F_G$ that labels $\varphi$ by searching for a derivation of $\varphi$ or $\neg\varphi$ from $B$ and the axioms emitted so far; on every tape on which the theory is consistent, its labeller is consistent and closed and decides every sentence as the theory does. \emph{Emission} turns a function machine $F$ into the theory machine $G_F$ whose axioms on $\omega$ are $\Gamma_{f^F_\omega}$. \emph{Craig's map with the tape} turns $F$ into a random decider whose axioms on $\omega$ are padded copies $\psi^{\wedge(k+1)}$ of the members $\psi$ of $\Gamma_{f^F_\omega}$, $k$ the number of steps $F$ takes to certify $\psi$. \emph{Closure} turns a consistent labeller $f$ into the closed labeller that searches for proofs from $B\cup\Gamma_f$.

\subsection{Equivalence, version by version}\label{sec:soi:equiv}

\begin{theorem}[Coin-free equivalence]\status{proved; Craig's construction known}\src{sfi Thm 2.1}\label{thm:soi:coinfree}
There is $c$, depending only on $V$, the coding of syntax and $B$, such that for every finite data set $D$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item $2^{-c}\WA(D)\le\Wcons(D)\le2^c\WA(D)$, \ $2^{-c}\WA(D)\le\WAdec(D)\le2^c\WA(D)$, \ and \ $2^{-c}\Wcons(D)\le\Wcl(D)\le\Wcons(D)$;
\item $\KA(D)$, $\KAdec(D)$, $\KFcons(D)$ and $\KFcl(D)$ agree up to $\pm c$, and all are finite iff $D$ is consistent;
\item for every $\varphi\notin\dom D$ and $o\in\{1,0,\bot\}$, $2^{-c}\WA(D;\varphi\mapsto o)\le\Wcl(D;\varphi\mapsto o)\le2^c\WA(D;\varphi\mapsto o)$, where $\WA(D;\varphi\mapsto o)$ is the weight of the enumerators compatible with $D$ whose theory proves $\varphi$ ($o=1$), proves $\neg\varphi$ ($o=0$) or decides neither ($o=\bot$), and $\Wcl(D;\varphi\mapsto o)$ is the weight of the closed labellers extending $D$ with $f(\varphi)=o$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence the semimeasure losses of $\WA$, $\WAdec$, $\Wcons$ and $\Wcl$ agree within $2c$ on every data set, in every order.
\end{theorem}

Proof search and emission give $\WA\asymp\Wcons$ with no Craig construction; Craig's map, needed only for deciders, halts on every input even when the labeller is partial, since each of its runs is bounded by the exponent of the candidate conjunction. For deciders, (a) is Theorem~6.3 of our earlier report \citep{claude2026axiominduction}. The construction is Craig's; the theorem shows that it works inside the universal prior, weight by weight, and apart from that report we have not found the weight equivalence stated in the literature. A different route, through the axiom schema $\mathrm{Acc}_f(\gn\varphi)\to\varphi$, $\mathrm{Rej}_f(\gn\varphi)\to\neg\varphi$, works with two sorts or with bounded witnesses but fails in one sort over $\PA$ (\cref{rem:soi:schema}); the theorem does not need it.

\begin{theorem}[One-tape equivalence]\status{proved; computed}\src{sfi Thm 3.1}\label{thm:soi:onetape}
There is $c$, depending only on $V$, the coding and $B$, such that for every finite $D$, the empty one included:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item $2^{-c}\MA(D)\le\MFcons(D)\le2^c\MA(D)$ and $2^{-c}\MA(D)\le\MAdec(D)\le2^c\MA(D)$;
\item tape by tape: for every theory machine $G$ and tape $\omega$, if $B\cup A^G_\omega$ is consistent and proves $\psi_D$, then $f^{F_G}_\omega$ is consistent and extends $D$; for every function machine $F$ and tape $\omega$, if $f^F_\omega$ is consistent and extends $D$, then $G_F$ and Craig's random decider of $F$ are compatible with $D$ on $\omega$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Part (b) is the four maps applied tape by tape, and summing over programs gives (a); the tapes that carry no program have theory $B$ and are matched by the deterministic labeller ``proof search from $B$''. For $\MA\asymp\MFcons$ no Craig construction is needed: a theory machine reads a tape, so it can emit $\Gamma_{f_\omega}$ itself.

\begin{corollary}[Closed labellers and the trichotomy]\status{proved; computed}\src{sfi Cor 3.2}\label{cor:soi:closed}
For every finite $D$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$, the three outcome weights $\MA(D+(\varphi,1))$, $\MA(D+(\varphi,0))$, $\MAthree(D+(\varphi,\iota))$ (the theory proves $\varphi$, proves $\neg\varphi$, decides neither) agree within $2^{\pm c}$ with the measures of the tapes on which $f_\omega$ is closed, consistent, extends $D$ and has $f_\omega(\varphi)=1$, $0$, $\bot$ respectively; and $2^{-c}\MA\le\MFcl\le\MFcons$.
\end{corollary}

So the trichotomy of \SAIabbr (true, false or independent of the sampled theory) is the three-way split of \SOIabbr over \emph{closed} labellers; over all consistent labellers it is not (\cref{prop:soi:closure}).

\paragraph{Penalties on the axiom side.} One may charge a hypothesis for the time it takes to check or to generate its axioms, with deduction still free. $\MAchk{t}$ is the one-tape mixture over random decider programs that answer every query $\chi$ within $O(t(\lvert\chi\rvert))$ steps on every tape, and $\MAgen{t}$ the mixture over random enumerator programs that write each axiom $a_i$ within $O(t(\lvert a_i\rvert))$ steps of the previous one on every tape (the delay form; the strict and cumulative forms are in \cref{def:soi:penalised}); $\WAchk{t}$ and $\WAgen{t}$ are their coin-free forms (Definition~E.10 of \citealp{claude2026axiominduction}). These results rest on an assumption about the universal machine $U$ whose programs are the hypotheses, taken from Appendix~E.6 of the earlier report; it is standard for multitape machines, and we do not prove it for a particular $U$.

\begin{assumption}[(S): efficient self-simulation]\status{assumption}\src{sfi \S7 (the earlier report's (S))}\label{ass:soi:S}
Penalised hypotheses are programs of a fixed universal prefix machine $U$, read to their end before the input. There are constants $\alpha,a\ge1$ such that the fixed wrappers used in the proofs read, copy and measure a program $q$ within $\alpha(\lvert q\rvert+1)$ steps; parse, compare or copy strings of total length $L$ within $\alpha(L+1)^2$ steps; and run the \emph{dovetailing schedule} of $q$ (at stage $s$, start $q$ on the $s$-th sentence and advance every unfinished run by one simulated step) while keeping a \emph{work counter} $T$ (the simulated steps, plus the lengths of the sentences started, plus $\lvert q\rvert$), within $\alpha(T+1)^a$ steps up to work $T$, writing a string of length $L$ at cost at most $\alpha L$. Put $a^*:=\max(a,3)$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[(S$_{\rm tape}$): self-simulation with a tape]\status{assumption}\src{sfi \S3.3, Assumption (S$_{\rm tape}$)}\label{ass:soi:Stape}
There are constants $\alpha,a\ge1$ with the properties of \cref{ass:soi:S} when $q$ also reads a one-way random tape: all runs of the dovetailing schedule read the same tape $\omega$ one-way from its start, the work counter also counts tape reads, and up to work $T$ the schedule costs at most $\alpha(T+1)^a$ steps and reads at most $T$ tape bits.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Axiom-side penalties change nothing]\status{(a) proved, under (S$_{\rm tape}$); (b) proved, under (S)}\src{sfi Thm 3.4 (a); Thm 7.1 (b)}\label{thm:soi:penalty}
Let $t(m)\ge m^{a^*}$ be time-constructible. There is $c$ such that, for every finite $D$ and in the strict, delay and cumulative forms:
(a) under \cref{ass:soi:Stape}, $2^{-c}\MFcons(D)\le\MAchk{t}(D)\le2^c\MA(D)$ and $2^{-c}\MFcons(D)\le\MAgen{t}(D)\le2^c\MA(D)$, the empty $D$ included; so $\MAchk{t}\asymp\MAgen{t}\asymp\MA\asymp\MFcons$;
(b) under \cref{ass:soi:S}, $2^{-c}\Wcons(D)\le\WAchk{t}(D)\le\WAdec(D)\le2^c\Wcons(D)$ and $2^{-c}\Wcons(D)\le\WAgen{t}(D)\le2^c\Wcons(D)$.
\end{theorem}

The lower bounds pad Craig's set by the work counter, one copy $\psi^{\wedge(T+1)}$ per detection at work $T$, tape by tape. The equivalence also holds class by class: the posterior odds of any two classes of deductively closed theories, genuine axiom systems and disguised labellers alike, move by at most $2^{2c}$ (\cref{prop:soi:classes}, under \cref{ass:soi:Stape}; coin-free, \cref{prop:soi:disguised}).

\subsection{Positive literals: \SAIabbr is \SOIabbr}\label{sec:soi:literals}

\begin{theorem}[Transfer principle]\status{proved; computed}\src{sfi Thm 5.1}\label{thm:soi:transfer}
Let $\mathcal I\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ be the companion paper's (infinite, decidable) input set and $x\mapsto t_x$ a computable injective map from $\mathcal I$ to closed $\mathcal L$-terms, with decidable range and computable inverse. Let $R$ be a unary relation symbol that does not occur in $B$, and suppose some model of $B$ interprets the terms $t_x$ as pairwise distinct elements. For $D$ with $\dom D\subseteq\{R(t_x):x\in\mathcal I\}$ put $D^{\rm str}:=\{(x,b):(R(t_x),b)\in D\}$. Then there is $c$ with
\[
\lvert\KA(D)-\KF(D^{\rm str})\rvert\le c,\qquad\WA(D)\asymp W(D^{\rm str}),\qquad\MA(D)\asymp\MF(D^{\rm str})\ \ (D\ne\emptyset),
\]
where $\KF$, $W$, $\MF$ are the companion paper's objects over $\mathcal I$ with labels $\{0,1\}$; the same holds for $\KAdec$, $\WAdec$, $\MAdec$, and for the three outcome weights of a literal query $R(t_y)$ (proves, refutes, decides neither; label $1$, $0$, undefined at $y$).
\end{theorem}

A labeller of strings, read on the literals, is consistent: in a model of $B$ with distinct $t_x$ the symbol $R$ can be interpreted as the set of the $t_x$ labelled $1$. Without distinct terms the transfer fails: if $B\vdash t_x=t_y$, the data $(R(t_x),1),(R(t_y),0)$ are inconsistent for \SAIabbr, while $D^{\rm str}$ is fine for \SOIabbr.

\begin{corollary}[What transfers]\status{proved, by \cref{thm:soi:transfer} and the cited results of the companion paper}\src{sfi Cor 5.2, \S5.5}\label{cor:soi:transfer}
In the setting of \cref{thm:soi:transfer}, every result of the companion paper about binary labels that holds up to additive constants in $K$, $-\log W$ and $-\log\MF$ holds for \SAIabbr on literal data, with the same quantities. In particular:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{no coding theorem for axiom systems}: for every $d\ge2$ there are distinct $x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}^d$ such that $D:=\{(R(t_{x_1}),0),(R(t_{x_2}),1)\}$ has $\KA(D)\ge\log d-2-c$, $-\log\WA(D)\le2+K(\lceil\log(d+1)\rceil)+c$ and $-\log\MA(D)\le2+c$; and over two-point literal data $\log d-c\le\max_D(\KA(D)-\LA(D))\le\log d+O(\log\log d)$ \citep[Theorem 4.9, Corollary 4.10]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals}; \cref{thm:basic:nocoding} proves the first three bounds directly;
\item \emph{random theories beat deterministic ones}: $\sup_D\MA(D)/\WA(D)=\infty$ over two-point literal data, and $\WA(D)\ge2^{-\lvert D\rvert-\Kpad(2^{d+1})-c}$ for every literal data set over words of length at most $d$ \citep[Propositions 4.5(a), 4.6]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals};
\item \emph{shared randomness is not a mixture of private coins}, read as a comparison of $\MA$ on literal data $D$ with the companion paper's product mixture $\Mprod$ on $D^{\rm str}$: $\MA/\Mprod$ is unbounded \citep[Proposition 4.7]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals}; no product law internal to \SAIabbr is defined here;
\item \emph{Solomonoff induction}: with unary numerals $t_i:=S^i0$ and $B\in\{\emptyset,\Q\}$, $\MA(\{(R(S^i0),x_{i+1}):i<n\})$ is within $2^{\pm c}$ of the universal semimeasure of $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ \citep[Proposition 3.24]{claudeSFI2026fundamentals}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

Cumulative statements in the normalised convention do not transfer (each normalised conditional transfers only within $2^{\pm2c}$), nor do computability statements ($\MA$ is not lower semicomputable, \cref{thm:basic:inc}), statements about private randomness other than (c), or statements about weak semimeasures. So the content of \SAIabbr beyond \SOIabbr lies in logically structured data, where sentences entail, exclude or leave open one another.

\begin{remark}[An encoding effect]\status{proved; computed}\src{sfi Rem 5.4}\label{rem:soi:encoding}
Let $\chi_i$ be $R(t_{w_i})$ or $\neg R(t_{w_i})$ by independent fair coins, for distinct words $w_i$, with data $(\chi_i,1)$, $i\le n$. Plain coin-free \SOIabbr on sentences, $\Wall$, has bounded loss, through the labeller ``always $1$'', while \SAIabbr and consistent \SOIabbr, coin-free or one-tape, lose at least $n$ bits in expectation (Proposition~E.1 of \citealp{claude2026axiominduction}). This is an encoding effect: the map $(\neg R(t_w),1)\mapsto(R(t_w),0)$ turns these data into fair-coin labels on positive literals and leaves $\MA$ and $\WA$ unchanged, since they depend on $D$ only through $\Cn(D^\pm)$ (\cref{prop:basic:structure}); there \SAIabbr is \SOIabbr on strings, which loses at least $n-c$ bits in expectation on fair coins. The labeller ``always $1$'' is inconsistent; consistency is what ties $\neg R(t_w)$ to $R(t_w)$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What breaks}\label{sec:soi:breaks}

\begin{proposition}[Logical closure]\status{proved; (a), (b), (d) computed}\src{sfi Prop 6.1}\label{prop:soi:closure}
Let $D$ be consistent and $\chi\notin\dom D$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item If $B\cup D^\pm\vdash\chi$, every hypothesis of \SAIabbr compatible with $D$ proves $\chi$: $\MA(1\mid D,\chi)=1$, $\MA(0\mid D,\chi)=0$ and $\Indep_D(\chi)=0$; the same for $\WA$.
\item If $B\cup D^\pm\vdash\chi$, then $\Wcons(0\mid D,\chi)=0$ and $\Wcons(1\mid D,\chi)\ge2^{-2c}$; the same for $\MFcons$.
\item For every $\chi\notin\dom D$, entailed or not, the consistent labellers extending $D$ that are undefined at $\chi$ have weight at least $2^{-K(\chi)-c}\,\Wcons(D)$, and the tapes on which $f_\omega$ is consistent, extends $D$ and is undefined at $\chi$ have measure at least $2^{-K(\chi)-c}\,\MFcons(D)$.
\item Over closed labellers, the three predictive probabilities agree with those of \SAIabbr within $2^{\pm2c}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

So in the semimeasure convention consistent \SOIabbr never becomes certain of a logical consequence: for a fixed simple $\chi$ its undefined mass stays above $2^{-K(\chi)-c}$ whatever the data (in the normalised convention its prediction on an entailed $\chi$ is $1$, by (b)). For (c), make a consistent labeller undefined at $\chi$, at the cost of a description of $\chi$. The equivalences control the losses, not the undefined mass, which in \SOIabbr mixes logical independence with laziness.

\paragraph{An observed ``independent'' label.} With three-valued data the label $\iota$ is observed. A partial labeller $f$ with values in $Y_3$ is \emph{consistent} if $B\cup\Gamma_f$ is consistent ($\Gamma_f$ formed from the labels $1$, $0$) and decides none of the sentences $f$ labels $\iota$. $\WAthree(D)$ is the weight of the enumerators whose theory is consistent, proves the $1/0$ data of $D$ and decides no $\iota$-labelled sentence; $\Wthreecons(D)$ is the weight of the consistent three-valued labellers extending $D$; $\MAthree$ is as in \cref{def:setting:MA}, and $\MFthreecons(D):=\Unif\{\omega:f_\omega\supseteq D,\ f_\omega\text{ consistent}\}$ for the universal function machine with labels in $Y_3$. $\IndB$ is the set of sentences that $B$ decides neither way.

\begin{proposition}[An observed independent label breaks the equivalence]\status{proved, given the productivity lemma (classical) and, one-tape, the Lebesgue density theorem; computed}\src{sfi Props 6.3, 6.5}\label{prop:soi:independent}
Let $\mathcal L\supseteq\Lar$, and let $B$ be decidable and consistent with $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item $2^{-c}\Wthreecons(D)\le\WAthree(D)$ for every three-valued $D$, and $\MFthreecons(D)\le2^c\MAthree(D)$ for every three-valued $D\ne\emptyset$.
\item For every $n$ there is $\sigma_n\in\IndB$ with $\Wthreecons(\{(\sigma_n,\iota)\})\le\Omtail{n+1}$ while $\WAthree(\{(\sigma_n,\iota)\})\ge2^{-c}$; and there is $\sigma'_n\in\IndB$ with $\MFthreecons(\{(\sigma'_n,\iota)\})\le2^{1-n}+\Omtail{n+1}$ while $\MAthree(\{(\sigma'_n,\iota)\})\ge2^{-c}$.
\item For every $\sigma\in\IndB$, $\WAthree(\{(\sigma,\iota)\})\le2^{K(\sigma)+c}\,\Wthreecons(\{(\sigma,\iota)\})$ and $\MFthreecons(\{(\sigma,\iota)\})\ge2^{-K(\sigma)-c}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Large gaps have no r.e.\ witnesses]\status{proved}\src{sfi Prop 6.4}\label{prop:soi:noRE}
In the setting of \cref{prop:soi:independent}: (a) for every r.e.\ $S\subseteq\IndB$ and every $\sigma\in S$, $\Wthreecons(\{(\sigma,\iota)\})\ge2^{-K(S)-c}$ and $\MFthreecons(\{(\sigma,\iota)\})\ge2^{-K(S)-c}$, with $K(S)$ the complexity of an index of $S$; so no computable sequence $\sigma_n\in\IndB$ has $\Wthreecons(\{(\sigma_n,\iota)\})\to0$. (b) If $n(\sigma)$ is the length of a shortest program of a consistent three-valued labeller that labels $\sigma$ with $\iota$, then $2^{-n(\sigma)}\le\Wthreecons(\{(\sigma,\iota)\})\le\Omtail{n(\sigma)}$.
\end{proposition}

The mechanism: that a theory decides neither $\sigma$ nor $\neg\sigma$ is a $\Pi_1$ property, which a theory has without committing to anything, while a labeller must output $\iota$ after finitely many steps. The sentences that the short consistent labellers label $\iota$ form an r.e.\ subset of $\IndB$, which is productive by a classical effective-inseparability argument (\cref{lem:soi:productive}; cf.\ \citealp{smullyan1961theory}). One-tape, thresholding the $\iota$-mass at a Lebesgue density point (\cref{lem:logic:density}), in the manner of the majority vote of \citet{deleeuw1956computability}, restores such an r.e.\ subset. An r.e.\ family of independent sentences, in contrast, is labelled $\iota$ by one consistent labeller (\cref{prop:soi:noRE}).

Consistency constraints of unbounded arity separate the versions of consistent \SOIabbr: in the setting of \cref{thm:logic:lottery}, on its lottery stream, one-tape consistent \SOIabbr has bounded loss while coin-free consistent \SOIabbr and $\WA$ do not, and plain $\Wall$ has bounded loss (\cref{cor:logic:versions}). Finally, none of the versions is lower semicomputable when consistency of finite data sets is not r.e.; $\WA$, $\Wcons$, $\MA$ and $\MFcons$ are computable relative to $\emptyset'$, and the closed and decider versions relative to $\emptyset''$ (\cref{prop:soi:computability}).

\subsection{Time bounds with free deduction}\label{sec:soi:time}

On the labeller side, a labeller is run for a bounded time. For a time-constructible $\tau$ and a string $f$, let $f^\tau(\varphi):=U_0(f,\varphi)$ if a fixed plain universal machine $U_0$ halts within $\tau(\lvert\varphi\rvert)$ steps with a label, and let $f^\tau$ abstain otherwise; $f$ has weight $w(f):=2^{-\lvert E(f)\rvert}$, with $E$ a prefix code (Elias-$\delta$ code of $\lvert f\rvert+1$, then $f$) whose weights sum to $1$ with computable tails. \emph{Clocked consistent} \SOIabbr is $W^{\mathrm{cons},\tau}(D):=\sum_fw(f)\ind[f^\tau\text{ consistent and}\supseteq D]$, and $W^{\mathrm{all},\tau}$ drops consistency; the one-tape forms $\MF^{\mathrm{cons},\tau}$, $\MF^{\mathrm{all},\tau}$ run $f$ with a tape-reading instruction (\cref{thm:soi:onetapetime}). Let $c_\bot:=-\log w(f_\bot)$ for the always-abstaining string $f_\bot$. A \emph{computable predictor} $P$ maps a data set $D$ and a sentence $\varphi\notin\dom D$ to a pair $(q_0,q_1)$ with $q_0+q_1\le1$, computable to any precision; its loss on a reveal sequence is $\ell_P(D_n):=\sum_{j\le n}-\log q_{b_j}(D_{j-1},\varphi_j)$.

\begin{corollary}[A one-sided equivalence]\status{proved; for the penalised versions under (S)}\src{sfi Cor 7.3$'$; Thm 7.8(a)}\label{cor:soi:onesided}
For every time-constructible $\tau$ there is $c_\tau$ such that, on every data set $D$, the losses of $\WA$ and $\WAdec$ exceed that of $W^{\mathrm{cons},\tau}$ by at most $c_\tau$, and the loss of $\MA$ exceeds that of $\MF^{\mathrm{cons},\tau}$ by at most $c_\tau$. Under \cref{ass:soi:S} the same holds for $\WAchk{t}$ and $\WAgen{t}$, $t(m)\ge m^{a^*}$ time-constructible.
\end{corollary}

A clocked consistent labeller is, unclocked, a consistent labeller computing the same partial map, so a domination lemma (\cref{thm:soi:domination}) bounds the regret by the cost of the clock wrapper and $c_\bot$. The reverse fails, on sentences that encode a diagonal argument. Let $\mathrm{Out}(e,x,y)$ be a $\Sigma_1$ formula without $<$ that holds in $\mathbb N$ iff program $e$ halts on input $x$ with output $y$ (by the MRDP theorem, \citealp{matiyasevich1970enumerable,davis1973hilbert}; Lemma~E.14 of \citealp{claude2026axiominduction}), and let $\mathrm{DET}$ be the true $\Pi_1$ sentence saying that no program has two outputs on one input.

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal separation]\status{proved, given Kleene's recursion theorem and the MRDP theorem}\src{sfi Thm 7.4}\label{thm:soi:diagonal}
Let $\mathcal L\supseteq\Lar$ and let $B$ be decidable and true in an expansion of $\mathbb N$. For every computable predictor $P$ there is a program $e$ such that, with $s_n:=\mathrm{Out}(\num e,\num n,S0)$ labelled by truth: $e$ is total with values in $\{0,1\}$; $\Q\cup\{\mathrm{DET}\}$ decides every $s_n$; $\ell_P(D_n)\ge n-\sum_j\log(1+2^{-j-2})\ge n-1/(4\ln2)\ge n-0.361$; and $\WA$, $\WAchk{t}$, $\WAgen{t}$ lose at most $\log(1/\omega_0)$, where $\omega_0>0$ is the least of the weights of a fixed decider and a fixed enumerator of $\Q\cup\{\mathrm{DET}\}$ in these mixtures, independent of $P$. Moreover $W^{\mathrm{all},\tau}$ loses at least $n-1.37$ and $W^{\mathrm{cons},\tau}$ at least $n-1.37-c_\bot$ on such sequences.
\end{theorem}

The program $e$ is a fixed point that runs $P$ on its own sentences and outputs the less probable label (Theorem~6.15 of \citealp{claude2026axiominduction}). The same holds one-tape: $\MF^{\mathrm{all},\tau}$ and $\MF^{\mathrm{cons},\tau}$ lose at least $n-1.37$ and $n-1.37-c_\bot$ on a computable sequence on which $\MA$ loses at most $\log(1/\omega_0)$ (\cref{thm:soi:onetapetime}).

Conversely, on the data of \cref{rem:soi:encoding} the time-bounded $W^{\mathrm{all},\tau}$ has bounded loss while every consistent inducer loses $n$ bits in expectation, so $W^{\mathrm{all},\tau}$ and \SAIabbr are incomparable (\cref{prop:soi:incomparable}): the natural strengthening, that \SAIabbr is strictly stronger than every time-bounded predictor, fails, and what holds is strict superiority over clocked \emph{consistent} \SOIabbr. Nor is this gain one of axioms over programs:

\begin{proposition}[The separation comes from free deduction]\status{proved}\src{sfi Prop 7.6 (a), (b)}\label{prop:soi:freededuction}
(a) Let $u$ be the labeller that, on a sentence $\mathrm{Out}(\num e,\num n,S0)$, runs $e$ on $n$ and outputs $1$ if the result is $1$ and $0$ if it is anything else (diverging if $e$ diverges), and is undefined on all other sentences. Then $\Gamma_u\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)$, so $u$ is consistent with every $B$ true in an expansion of $\mathbb N$, and $\lvert u\rvert$ is a constant.
(b) On every sequence of \cref{thm:soi:diagonal}, \emph{unpenalised} $\Wcons$ (and $\Wall$) has loss at most $\lvert u\rvert+c$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{What the time bounds show.}\label{rem:soi:timereading}\status{assessment}\src{sfi Rem 7.9} Penalising the axiom side, by the time to check or to generate axioms, does not change which theories win: the equivalence survives, also class by class (\cref{thm:soi:penalty}). Penalising the labeller side makes the relation one-sided (\cref{cor:soi:onesided,thm:soi:diagonal}), and the gap is the free deduction of \SAIabbr, which unpenalised consistent \SOIabbr also has, through one constant-size evaluator (\cref{prop:soi:freededuction}). So we read the time-bounded separations as showing not that axioms are better hypotheses than programs, but that free deduction is free computation. (Under a time bound the defeating sequence is itself computable within a slightly larger bound, a time-hierarchy effect; we give only a proof sketch, \cref{rem:soi:freededuction}.)

\subsection{Charged deduction}\label{sec:soi:charged}

The natural repair is to charge deduction. We use the setting of Definition~E.18 of \citet{claude2026axiominduction}: $\mathcal L$ is finite, $B$ is decidable in polynomial time, derivations are in the Hilbert calculus $\mathsf K$ of that report, with sizes counting symbols, and a hypothesis is a program $p$ that decides an axiom set $A_p$ in time $O(t)$. Each datum $\varphi$ must have a derivation of $\varphi^b$ from $B\cup A_p$ whose size is at most $s(\lvert\varphi\rvert)$ ($W_A[t,s]$); in which every axiom instance, logical ones included, has at most $h(\lvert\varphi\rvert)$ symbols (reading (a1), $W_A^{a1}[t,h]$), or every nonlogical instance does (reading (a2), $W_A^{a2}[t,h]$); or whose \emph{material}, the total size of the distinct instances used, is at most $g(\lvert\varphi\rvert)$ (reading (b), $W_A^{b}[t,g]$), or whose nonlogical material is (reading (b2), $W_A^{b2}[t,g]$). \emph{Certificate} \SOIabbr, $\Wcert[t',s']$, weighs the consistent labellers $f_{\mathcal V}$ defined by verifiers $\mathcal V$ running in time $O(t')$: $f_{\mathcal V}(\varphi)$ is $1$ (resp.\ $0$) if some certificate of length at most $s'(\lvert\varphi\rvert)$ makes $\mathcal V$ accept (resp.\ reject). All charged results assume the renaming condition of that report; without it the index of a single parameter, which costs one symbol, could carry a certificate of any length (\cref{rem:soi:F1}).

\begin{assumption}[(F1): renaming closure]\status{assumption}\src{sfi \S8 (the earlier report's (F1))}\label{ass:soi:F1}
For every hypothesis $p$ of a charged reading, $B\cup A_p$ is closed under permutations of the parameters.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Charged deduction is certificate \SOIabbr]\status{proved, under (F1); the correspondence of proof systems with NP known}\src{sfi Thm 8.1}\label{thm:soi:sandwich}
Under \cref{ass:soi:F1}, with time-constructible nondecreasing budgets:
(a) in a normal derivation of $\varphi$ every line sits at its own node of an axiom instance used or of $\varphi$, up to abstracting parameters; so its size is at most $(M+\lvert\varphi\rvert)(M+\lvert\varphi\rvert+1)/2$, with $M$ its material, and the factor $\frac12$ is attained;
(b) with $G(m):=(g+m+1)(g+m+2)/2$, $t^+(m):=m^{k_0}(t(m)+1)$ and $t'^\#(m):=m^{k_0}+t'(m)$, for constants $k_0$, $\beta$ and $c$: $W_A[t,g]\le W_A^b[t,g]\le W_A[t,G]$ hypothesis by hypothesis; $W_A^b[t,g]\le2^c\,\Wcert[t^+,\beta G\log(G+2)]$; and $\Wcert[t',s']\le2^c$ times each of $W_A^b[t'^\#,2s'+m+31]$, $W_A^{a1}[t'^\#,s'+m+16]$ and $W_A[t'^\#,2s'+2m+31]$.
\end{theorem}

This is Theorem~6.16 of the earlier report, with explicit constants; its classical core is the correspondence of propositional proof systems with $\cplx{NP}$ \citep{cook1979relative}. A derivation is a certificate; conversely, a certificate $\zeta$ becomes the axiom $\theta_\zeta\to\varphi^b$, where $\theta_\zeta$ is a tautology of size $\lvert\zeta\rvert+14$ that encodes $\zeta$. On literal sequences $D^X$ (\cref{def:setting:fresh}), at polynomial budgets whose degrees are charged in the prior and under \cref{ass:soi:F1}, this fixes the fit classes (Corollary~6.17 of the earlier report; \cref{cor:soi:fit}): reading (b) fits exactly $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$, so it beats polynomial-time consistent \SOIabbr on some sequence if $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}\ne\cplx P$; reading (a1) fits exactly $\cplx{EXPTIME}$, using $\cplx{APSPACE}=\cplx{EXPTIME}$ \citep{chandra1981alternation}; readings (a2) and (b2) fit every decidable set.

\paragraph{What is charged.}\label{rem:soi:whatcharged}\status{assessment}\src{sfi Rem 8.5} Every reading bounds the size of a derivation that \emph{exists}; none charges \emph{finding} it. Derivation-charged \SAIabbr is certificate \SOIabbr, a notion of the type $\cplx{NP}\cap\cplx{coNP}$, not deterministic time-bounded \SOIabbr. So charged deduction cannot express the point that a written theory is far from sufficient for a simple reasoner; a deterministic bounded reasoner can (\cref{sec:shape}, in particular \cref{thm:shape:knowhow}).

\subsection{A natural separation: Presburger arithmetic}\label{sec:soi:presburger}

The diagonal sequences of \cref{thm:soi:diagonal} are built against the predictor; a natural theory separates as well. Let $\mathcal L_{\rm Pr}:=\{0,1,+\}$ (with equality), $B=\emptyset$, and let $D^{\rm Pres}$ list all $\mathcal L_{\rm Pr}$-sentences in length-lexicographic order, labelled by truth in $(\mathbb N,0,1,+)$; $\mathrm{PrA}:=\Th(\mathbb N,0,1,+)$. The length of a sentence is its symbol count, and clocks and budgets are functions of it. Presburger's axioms (successor, addition and the induction schema) form a decidable, consistent and complete axiomatisation of $\mathrm{PrA}$ \citep{presburger1929vollstandigkeit}, with membership decidable in polynomial time. \citet{fischer1974super} show that some $c_{\rm FR}>0$ has the following property: every decision procedure for $\mathrm{PrA}$, deterministic or nondeterministic, takes more than $2^{2^{c_{\rm FR}n}}$ steps on some sentence of length $n$, for every large $n$. Under a proviso on their measure of length (\cref{rem:soi:lengths}) this gives a constant $c>0$ such that, for all large $m$, every such procedure takes more than $2^{2^{cm}}$ steps on some sentence with exactly $m$ symbols; $c$ denotes this constant below.

\begin{proposition}[Presburger arithmetic separates \SAIabbr from clocked \SOIabbr]\status{proved, given Presburger's theorem, the Fischer--Rabin bound and, for (c), the Lebesgue density theorem}\src{sfi Props 9.1, 9.2}\label{prop:soi:presburger}
(a) $\WA$, $\WAdec$ and $\MA$, and the penalised $\WAchk{t}$, $\WAgen{t}$ and $\MAchk{t}$ for a large enough polynomial $t$, have bounded loss on $D^{\rm Pres}$.
(b) If $\tau$ is time-constructible and $\tau(m)\le2^{2^{c'm}}$ for large $m$, with $c'<c$, the losses of $W^{\mathrm{cons},\tau}$ and $W^{\mathrm{all},\tau}$ on $D^{\rm Pres}_n$ tend to infinity.
(c) If $\tau(m)\le2^{c'm}$ for large $m$, with $c'<c$, the losses of $\MF^{\mathrm{cons},\tau}$ and $\MF^{\mathrm{all},\tau}$ on $D^{\rm Pres}_n$ tend to infinity.
\end{proposition}

The decider of Presburger's axioms is one deterministic hypothesis compatible with every prefix. Conversely, bounded loss of a clocked mixture yields one clocked labeller that labels every sentence correctly: a decision procedure for $\mathrm{PrA}$ below the Fischer--Rabin bound, deterministically in (b), and in (c) by a majority vote over the tape completions at a density point, which costs a factor $2^{\tau(m)}$. Under two further assumptions, a $\Sigma_2$-time lower bound for $\mathrm{PrA}$ and a time-bounded form of $\cplx{BPP}\subseteq\Sigma_2^p$, one-tape clocked \SOIabbr is also unbounded for doubly exponential clocks (\cref{prop:soi:presburgerCond}). With deduction charged, \SAIabbr meets the same bound:

\begin{proposition}[Charged deduction on Presburger data]\status{proved, given the Fischer--Rabin bound, Presburger's theorem, Lemma E.28, Proposition E.23(a) and the proof of Theorem 6.9 of the earlier report}\src{sfi Props 9.4, 9.4$'$}\label{prop:soi:presburgerCharged}
Let $p$ be a hypothesis of a charged reading over $\mathcal L_{\rm Pr}$ with $B=\emptyset$, deciding $A_p$ in polynomial time and satisfying \cref{ass:soi:F1}, and compatible with every prefix of $D^{\rm Pres}$. Let $c''<c$.
(a) Size reading, budget $s$: $s(m)>2^{2^{c''m}}$ for infinitely many $m$, and for all large $m$ if $s$ is time-constructible.
(b) Material reading (b), budget $g$: the same with $g$ in place of $s$.
(c) Reading (a1), with $h$ nondecreasing and time-constructible: $h(m)>2^{c''m}$ for all large $m$.
\end{proposition}

A mixture of such hypotheses has bounded loss on $D^{\rm Pres}$ only if one of them is compatible with every prefix. For (a) and (b), guessing a derivation within the budget and checking it is a nondeterministic decision procedure; for (c), closing the set of formulas within the instance budget under the rules is a deterministic one, in time $2^{O(h\log h)}$. Whether (a1) needs doubly exponential budgets, and what (a2) and (b2) need, is open (\cref{q:soi:presburger}).

\paragraph{Each charged reading matches one version of \SOIabbr.}\label{rem:soi:presburgerSummary}\status{assessment (summary of proved results; the (a1) correspondence is a reading for sentence data)}\src{sfi Rem 9.5} Once deduction is charged, the budget \SAIabbr needs on $D^{\rm Pres}$ is that of the matching version of \SOIabbr: under the size and material readings \SAIabbr is certificate \SOIabbr (\cref{thm:soi:sandwich}), and the nondeterministic Fischer--Rabin bound forces doubly exponential budgets on both; under (a1) \SAIabbr behaves like deterministic \SOIabbr with an exponential clock (Proposition~E.23 and Corollary~6.17(b) of the earlier report; the converse direction is proved there for literal data only, so for sentence data we offer this as a reading), and the deterministic bound forces exponential instance sizes, which the clock turns into doubly exponential time. So on a natural theory, as on the diagonal sequences, the separation from clocked \SOIabbr is the free deduction, for every reading in which the derivation is charged and the conclusion is proved; for (a2) and (b2) the question is open.

\paragraph{How to read the table.} The equivalence is version by version: coin-free with coin-free (through Craig's construction), one-tape with one-tape (without it), Kolmogorov with Kolmogorov. It controls semimeasure losses, not the meaning of ``undefined'': the undefined mass of \SOIabbr mixes logical independence with laziness, and once ``independent'' is an observed label the relation is one-sided. Penalising the axiom side changes nothing; penalising the labeller side makes the relation one-sided, and the gap is the free deduction of \SAIabbr. Charging deduction turns \SAIabbr into certificate \SOIabbr, a nondeterministic notion. The natural separation is Presburger arithmetic, and once deduction is charged its strength depends on the reading. On literals \SAIabbr and \SOIabbr coincide; they separate only through logical closure, observed independence, consistency constraints of unbounded arity, or deduction that is free. In the idealised setting every consistent labeller is an axiom system at a cost of $O(1)$ bits, and every theory a closed labeller, so the formalism cannot see the difference in shape between a theory and a program; where shape matters, it is relative to a reasoner and a vocabulary (\cref{sec:shape}).
