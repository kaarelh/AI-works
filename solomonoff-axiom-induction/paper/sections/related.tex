\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Universal and operator induction.}\status{known}\src{syn 5.7 item 1; syn 4; U6; U7; consistency 9}
Solomonoff induction predicts sequences with a universal prior over programs \citep{solomonoff1964formal,solomonoff1978complexity,li2008introduction}. Operator induction predicts the answer to a new question from an unordered set of question--answer pairs \citep{solomonoff1999two,solomonoff2008three}; the companion paper \citep{claudeSFI2026fundamentals} and the companion paper on steeper priors \citep{claudeSFI2026steeper} develop its one-tape universal law and its versions, and by \cref{sec:soi} \SAIabbr agrees, version by version and up to constants, with operator induction over consistent labellers of sentences, in which deduction ties the answers together. Whether a universal prior justifies a preference for simplicity is disputed \citep{sterkenburg2016solomonoff,sterkenburg2019putnam}. Our theorems do not depend on it; our use of \SAIabbr as an ideal of the evaluation of theories does, and we take the universal prior as a modelling choice whose dependence on the machine and the language is bounded by constants that, at the scale of our examples, are not small (\cref{prop:basic:invariance,thm:basic:translation,sec:kepler:pool}). The computable, finite-class precursors are our earlier report \citep{claude2026axiominduction}, on a Bayesian axiom inducer with template priors and derivation likelihoods and on coin-free axiom induction under time penalties, which \cref{sec:soi} extends, and a second report \citep{claude2026axiomschemas}, on learning axioms and schemas from their instances.

\paragraph{Probabilities on sentences.}\status{known; the comparisons proved}\src{syn 5.7 item 2; fundamentals 10, 12.2; logic 7; bib-report 3; PHIL-20}
Logical probabilities on sentences go back to \citet{carnap1950logical}, coherent probabilities on first-order sentences to \citet{gaifman1964concerning,scott1966assigning,gaifman1982probabilities}; \citet{hutter2013probabilities} extend given probabilities of some sentences to all sentences of a higher-order logic under desiderata that include the Gaifman condition; \citet{christiano2014nonomniscience} gives a tractable algorithm; and logical inductors \citep{garrabrant2016logical} refine probabilities of a given theory's sentences over time. These are probabilities of truth, and logical inductors are given the axioms; \SAIabbr is given labelled sentences and is uncertain about the axioms. $\Bel$ is a belief function \citep{dempster1967upper,shafer1976mathematical} and satisfies the Gaifman condition exactly when the instances prove the universal sentence (\cref{prop:logic:gaifman}). \citet{sawin2013computable} show that computable distributions that satisfy natural coherence conditions and come to believe the true $\Pi_1$ sentences must give some short true $\Pi_2$ sentences arbitrarily low limiting probability; \cref{thm:logic:threshold,cor:logic:false} show a parallel divide for the ideal \SAIabbr posterior, not a consequence of theirs. Demski's prior \citep{demski2012logical}, as usually described, draws random sentences and keeps each one consistent with those kept; the modification due to Fallenstein, Demski and Garrabrant \citep{garrabrant2015modification}, used in \citet{garrabrant2016inductive}, draws theories as outputs of a universal machine on random bits, which is the sampling stage of $\MA$. Those processes iterate draws until the theory is complete; $\MA$ uses a single draw, whose theory is incomplete with positive probability; for complete theories the two compatibility notions coincide (\cref{prop:basic:demski,prop:basic:prcr}).

\paragraph{Learning theories from data.}\status{known}\src{syn 5.7 items 4-6; scout L 3.3-3.5; PHIL-19}
Formal learning theory studies identification in the limit \citep{gold1967language,putnam1965trial}, also of first-order theories \citep{osherson1991universal,kelly1996logic,martin1998elements}; \cref{prop:basic:textinformant} is a Bayesian form of Gold's distinction between text and informant. Shapiro's model inference infers theories from facts by conjectures and refutations \citep{shapiro1981algorithm,shapiro1991inductive}; it outputs one conjecture at a time, without a prior. Inductive logic programming (cf.\ \citealp{muggleton1994inductive,cropper2022inductive}) asks a hypothesis to entail the positive examples and to be consistent with the negative ones, a mixture of the two compatibility notions of \cref{def:setting:variants}, invents predicates \citep{muggleton1988machine}, and justifies theories by compression \citep{muggleton1995inverse,conklin1994complexity}. In cognitive science, intuitive theories are modelled as logical laws from a probabilistic grammar, learned by Bayesian inference over a finite space \citep{kemp2008theory,kemp2010probabilistic,ullman2012theory}.

\paragraph{Random axioms and Gödel phenomena.}\status{known}\src{syn 5.7 items 7-8; logic 3.4, 7; bib-report 4.2}
What a probabilistic machine produces with positive probability can be produced deterministically (cf.\ \citealp{deleeuw1956computability}); the lottery (\cref{thm:logic:lottery}) is where this averaging fails for random theories, since majority votes over consistency constraints of unbounded arity can be inconsistent (cf.\ the lottery paradox, \citealp{kyburg1961probability}). Its closer relative is that randomness helps for mass problems: a class of positive measure, such as a class of bounded diagonally non-computable functions, can have no computable member (cf.\ \citealp{kucera1985measure,bienvenu2016deep}). That randomisation can succeed where determinism cannot, below a success-probability threshold, is familiar for learners \citep{pitt1989probabilistic,ambainis1999probabilistic}; here the random objects are the hypotheses themselves. \citet{bienvenu2014axiomatic} add randomly chosen true complexity axioms to $\PA$ and show that they do not help to prove new interesting theorems but can shorten proofs unless $\cplx{NP}=\cplx{PSPACE}$; the threshold theories of \cref{thm:logic:threshold} use a soundness threshold of a related kind, placed just above the unknown inconsistency probability of the universal random theory. \Cref{sec:logic} also meets Chaitin's theorem \citep{chaitin1974information,raatikainen1998interpreting}, flexible formulas (cf.\ \citealp{kripke1962flexible,woodin2011potential}), Kreisel's conjecture (cf.\ \citealp{hrubes2009kreisel}) and the problem of old evidence \citep{garber1983old}.

\paragraph{Craig, Ramsey, Hempel.}\status{known}\src{syn 5.7 item 9; terms 10; bib-report 4.3; PHIL-15}
Craig's theorem \citep{craig1953axiomatizability,craig1956replacement} is the background of Hempel's theoretician's dilemma \citep{hempel1958theoretician} and of its replies (Craigian axiomatisations are unmanageable; theoretical terms serve inductive systematisation, cf.\ \citealp{niiniluoto1972inductive}; science does not aim only at prediction, cf.\ \citealp{putnam1965craig}), which \cref{sec:terms} sets against our results. In monadic inductive logic theoretical concepts can change inductive probabilities (cf.\ \citealp{niiniluoto1973theoretical}); in the universal mixture they cannot, beyond a constant factor, that is, by more than a bounded number of bits over all data (\cref{thm:terms:horn}). Every r.e.\ theory has a polynomial-time axiomatisation by a variant of Craig's trick \citep{krajicek2025proof}, and every r.e.\ theory without finite models has a finitely axiomatised conservative extension with one more binary relation symbol (\citealp{kleene1952finite,craigvaught1958finite}, as reported by \citealp{pakhomov2019question}), of which \cref{thm:terms:KCV} is an effective many-sorted form. Ramsey sentences and Lewis's definitions \citep{ramsey1931theories,lewis1970define} appear in \cref{prop:terms:ramsey}.

\paragraph{Usable theories.}\status{known}\src{syn 5.7 item 11; shape 9; bib-report 4.1}
Knowledge compilation studies the size of theories a weak reasoner can use \citep{selman1996knowledge,darwiche2002knowledge,delval1994tractable,babka2013complexity}; the declarative/procedural question is old in AI \citep{mccarthy1959programs,winograd1975frame,kowalski1979algorithm}.

\paragraph{Laws, approximation, explanation, and what a theory is.}\status{known}\src{syn 5.7 items 12-15; laws 9.2; philosophy 7}
Lewis's best-system account \citep{lewis1983new,lewis1994humean} and its language-relative version \citep{cohen2009better} are the counterpart of \cref{sec:laws:best}, by analogy only. That a model includes its noise distribution and that measurement precision enters the code length are standard in MDL and MML \citep{rissanen1978modeling,wallace2005statistical,grunwald2007minimum}; algorithmic statistics studies the split into model and noise \citep{gacs2001algorithmic,vereshchagin2004kolmogorov}; Akaike-based accounts give a statistical form of the dependence of selection on precision \citep{forster1994how}. On truthlikeness, explanation and what a theory is, see \cref{sec:limits:approx,sec:phil}. Discovery systems such as BACON, finding Kepler's third law \citep{langley1987scientific}, symbolic regression \citep{schmidt2009distilling,udrescu2020ai} and the Apperception Engine \citep{evans2021making} search; \SAIabbr evaluates.

\paragraph{What is new.}\status{novelty: to our knowledge (search level)}\src{fundamentals 12.2; sfi; logic 3.4, 7; terms 10; shape 9; laws 9.2; bib-report 4; U7}
To our knowledge the following are new: the single-draw proof-requiring law $\MA$ and its basic theory (\cref{sec:basic}); the one-tape and Kolmogorov equivalences with consistent \SOIabbr and the transfer principle (\cref{thm:soi:onetape,thm:soi:coinfree,thm:soi:transfer}); the lottery, its comparison of versions and its arithmetic versions with true data (\cref{thm:logic:lottery,cor:logic:false}); the threshold theories on $\Pi_1$ data and the coding theorem for an inductive inference rule (\cref{thm:logic:threshold,cor:logic:coding}); the reasoner-relative separations inside a universal inducer (\cref{sec:shape}), where the bound for systems of laws (\cref{thm:shape:systems}) is a short combination of known results that we did not find stated and that is probably known to specialists; a complexity-theoretic form of the impracticality reply to Hempel, which we did not find in the literature, and the derivation-bounded constructions and fit classes of \cref{sec:terms}; and, for a proof-requiring inducer, the choice between honest laws, laws with exceptions and laws with error models (\cref{prop:laws:peaked,prop:laws:errormodel}). Not new are random axiom systems drawn by a universal machine, the mixture and dominance arguments, Craig's construction (the coin-free weight equivalence is that construction inside the universal prior, proved for deciders in the earlier report), the rules of conditioning for belief functions, and the reading of a law with its noise distribution as a two-part code.
