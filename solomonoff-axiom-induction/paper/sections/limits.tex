\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

The axiom view, and \SAIabbr as its formal model, leave out much of what scientific theories are and do. For each limitation we say whether it is partially addressable, and by what, or a genuine limit of a declarative view. The judgements are arguments, not theorems; sentences that rest on a theorem cite it.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Statistical confrontation with data.}\label{sec:limits:stat}\status{assessment}\src{philosophy 5.0 (L0); syn 2.7}In physics a theory meets data statistically: with models of the instruments and the noise it assigns likelihoods to data, often near-raw (\cref{rem:phil:practice}), and statistical theories answer with probabilities, tested against frequencies. The proof-requiring core of \SAIabbr has no native noise model: a datum is proved or not. Graded fit enters only through randomised theories in $\MA$, such as laws with calibrated error models (\cref{prop:laws:randomised,prop:laws:errormodel}); through the generative variant, whose updating we prove given a support condition and whose identification we only sketch (\cref{prop:basic:generative,rem:basic:generative}); or through sentences about probabilities, whose relation to finite frequencies is not deductive. It matters for our computations: on noise-free interval data the syntax of the law texts decides among the forms compatible with the Mars data, and with graded fit the data decide (\cref{tab:kepler:map,ex:kepler:graded}). So the proof-requiring core is the noise-free special case; its natural extension inside $\MA$ is a law with a calibrated error model; a native treatment is open.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Written theories and their readers.}\label{sec:limits:textbook}\status{assessment, resting on thm:shape:invisible, cor:shape:regimes, thm:shape:knowhow, prop:phil:extractor}\src{philosophy 5.1 (L1); syn 2.7, 2.8 row 2; DECISIONS U1}A written theory joined to a simple reasoner does not solve the problems the theory is used for. We think this is true, and see three gaps. \emph{The bridge gap:} a problem posed in ordinary terms (a rope over a pulley) becomes formal only through idealisations and bridge principles that the theory does not contain (cf.\ \citealp{cartwright1983how,morgan1999models}); \cref{prop:phil:extractor} prices the bridge from raw data to questions as the complexity $K(\eta)$ of an extractor, large in real cases. \emph{The computational gap:} for the formalised problem, theory and mathematics suffice logically, but finding the answer needs methods, lemmas and approximation schemes (\cref{sec:kepler:newton}). In the model of \cref{sec:shape} this gap is a theorem, visible only when what is written, or the time to write it, is charged: program length sees neither reasoner nor vocabulary (\cref{thm:shape:invisible}), while charged as written the size of a usable theory depends exponentially on the pair (reasoner, vocabulary) (\cref{cor:shape:regimes}); charged deduction, which bounds a derivation without charging its search, cannot express the gap (\cref{thm:shape:knowhow}, with derivation sizes sketched in \cref{rem:shape:knowhowsketch}). So insufficiency for a simple reasoner is a property of the pair (theory, reasoner) and of the vocabulary, in the model of \cref{sec:shape}, when what is written, or the time to write it, is charged. It is not a theorem about textbooks (\cref{rem:shape:reading}): a declarative account of a weak reasoner's competence must include the lemmas, which does not mean that people carry exponentially many of them. \emph{The tacit gap:} perceptual and manual skills \citep{polanyi1958personal,polanyi1966tacit} and exemplars, through which new problems are seen as like solved ones \citep{kuhn1970structure,kuhn1977second}, are not sentences; in \SAIabbr exemplars are only data. This is a genuine limit.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Approximate truth.}\label{sec:limits:approx}\status{assessment, resting on prop:laws:strength, prop:laws:errormodel, prop:laws:peaked, prop:laws:humility}\src{philosophy 5.2 (L2); laws 9.1; DECISIONS U3}``Kepler's laws are only approximately true'' is a limitation of proof-requiring compatibility with deterministic theories, which rewards the false exact law over its true weakening (\cref{prop:laws:strength}), not of the axiom view as \SAIabbr formalises it: its mixture contains randomised theories, and under its pricing an approximately true law is a law with a calibrated error model (\cref{prop:laws:errormodel}). The error model is carried by the randomness of the theory machine, not stated as an axiom; a treatment in which it is stated, by sentences about probabilities, belongs to the open problem of the first paragraph of this section. Whether a deterministic theory holds a law honestly or with exceptions depends on how exceptions and silence are priced, a modelling choice (\cref{prop:laws:peaked}); humility is partiality for deterministic theories and calibration for randomised ones (\cref{prop:laws:humility}). Open are a canonical metric for approximate predictions, which we think does not exist, since purposes set the tolerances, and a comparative notion of truthlikeness, known to be hard: on Popper's definition no false theory is closer to the truth than another (cf.\ \citealp{popper1963conjectures,miller1974popper,tichy1974popper}), and the similarity approach needs a metric (cf.\ \citealp{niiniluoto1987truthlikeness}). Our notion is local: a question family, a tolerance, a domain.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Domains, several models, and inconsistent theories in use.}\label{sec:limits:domains}\status{assessment, resting on prop:laws:sound, rem:laws:typical, prop:laws:domain, rem:laws:domainlearning}\src{philosophy 5.3 (L3); syn 2.7; phil review issue 13}Practice uses mutually inconsistent models of one system, each in its domain (the Earth as flat, as a sphere, as an oblate spheroid), while proof-requiring \SAIabbr gives a globally inconsistent theory weight $0$. A sound device exists: state each model in its honest form, ``$q$ lies within $\varepsilon$ of $f$ on the domain'', and reason classically. Over a true background that decides the comparisons, exact models that give one length the values $100$ and $99.9$ prove everything, while honest forms of true models are jointly consistent and give true conclusions with propagated tolerances (\cref{prop:laws:sound,rem:laws:typical}). But the device does not reproduce practice: physicists rarely state or propagate worst-case bounds on the error of an approximate model (measurement uncertainties are propagated routinely, and statistically); worst-case propagation becomes vacuous over long chains and for chaotic dynamics; and Lanford's theorem, the rigorous honest form of the Boltzmann equation as described in expositions \citep{lanford1975time}, holds only for a fraction of the mean free time in the low-density limit, while practice uses the equation far beyond that, and rightly. The device does not say how to choose a model, a domain or a tolerance; we take that to be part of what written theories leave to tacit understanding. Under \SAIabbr's pricing a domain usually appears as a tolerance with an error distribution that varies with the system, not as a sharp boundary (\cref{prop:laws:domain,rem:laws:domainlearning}). Knowing when a model applies is a recognizer, a genuine limit.

\paragraph{Concepts.}\label{sec:limits:concepts}\status{assessment}\src{philosophy 5.4 (L6, the table); syn 2.7; phil review issue 18}Theories are built from concepts. \Cref{tab:limits:concepts} lists features of human concepts that an account of concepts as pieces of code, combined to generate predictions, misses, and says whether the axiom view captures them.\footnote{The list follows, with changes, an unpublished draft essay of H\"anni's on concepts.} We accept them as features of human concepts, the first only comparatively (\cref{sec:phil:map}), with two further qualifications: inferential role is one major account of meaning (cf.\ \citealp{sellars1953inference}), disputed by referential accounts (cf.\ \citealp{kripke1980naming}) and supported for theoretical terms by the Ramsey--Lewis treatment \citep{lewis1970define}; and natural types we find plausible. One feature supports the axiom view: if a notion can have different recognizers, its identity is fixed otherwise, by inferential role or reference.

For analogy the universal prior gives a bound. With $K(T)$ the least $K(e)$ such that $\re e=T$, for every r.e.\ theory $T_1$ and computable map $\tau$ from sentences to sentences
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limits:analogy}
K\big(T_1\cup\tau[T_1]\big)\ \le\ K(T_1)+K(\tau)+c,
\end{equation}
\status{proved}\src{philosophy 5.4 (analogy row)}since an index of $T_1\cup\tau[T_1]$ is computable from indices of $T_1$ and $\tau$, and $K(x,y)\le K(x)+K(y)+c$. Structure mapping \citep{gentner1983structure} and models in science (cf.\ \citealp{hesse1963models}) are accounts of such partial interpretations; concepts that behave in patches (cf.\ \citealp{wilson2006wandering}) or stretch under counterexamples (cf.\ \citealp{lakatos1976proofs}), and interpretation as abduction \citep{hobbs1993interpretation}, are outside the view.

\paragraph{Other limitations.}\label{sec:limits:other}\status{assessment}\src{philosophy 5.4 (L4, L5, L7, L9-L12); syn 2.7; DECISIONS U6, U7; phil review issues 5, 14; consistency review item 17}\emph{Science predicts very particular things, in set-ups it builds.} \SAIabbr captures half: its data are selected sentences and its theories partial. Which questions are worth asking, and the making of phenomena by experiment (cf.\ \citealp{hacking1983representing}), are outside it; value-weighted questions would be a handle on the first. \emph{Concept formation, discovery and understanding.} Step 3 of the ladder (\cref{sec:phil:map}) is partially addressable (\cref{thm:terms:dilemma,cor:shape:regimes}); step 4, the revision of the base abstractions, is a genuine limit for now. \SAIabbr is an ideal of the evaluation of theories over a fixed space of hypotheses, not of discovery or understanding: in \cref{sec:kepler} the pool of forms is given, not found. \emph{Theory-laden and fallible data.} A base language may presuppose a theory (heliocentric longitudes presuppose Copernican geometry; \cref{def:kepler:language}; \cref{sec:phil:map}), which biases comparisons between theories that disagree about it. And \SAIabbr takes labels as correct: a deterministic theory must prove even a mislabelled datum, by an exception axiom; a noise component in the likelihood, as in our earlier report \citep{claude2026axiominduction}, is a partial remedy. \emph{Purposes and technology} (engines, circuits, lenses) are outside the view, a genuine limit. \emph{Science as a social structure,} in which theories are communicated, divided, checked piece by piece and extended, is a genuine limit of a model with one inducer, and may be where the question of \cref{q:phil:declarative} finds its answer. \emph{The mathematics is free, and large} (in \cref{sec:kepler}, ordered fields with trigonometry). Building a background $B'$ into $B$ gains at most $K(B')+c$ bits on any data (\cref{prop:basic:background}), a constant that does not grow with the data but is not small for the mathematics physics uses, and that matters when rival theories need different mathematics; we expect it to matter much more under charged deduction. More generally, all our verdicts are relative to the universal machine, up to constants that are not small at the scale of real theories (\cref{prop:basic:invariance}). And with free deduction, data that the background entails carry no information: if $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$, checking instances $P(\num0),\dots,P(\num N)$ of a $\Delta_0$ property leaves $\Bel(\forall x\,P(x))$ at its prior (\cref{prop:logic:oldevidence}), the problem of old evidence and logical omniscience (cf.\ \citealp{hacking1967slightly,garber1983old}). Induction in mathematics needs a reasoner for whom deduction is costly.

\paragraph{What the axiom view captures.}\status{assessment}\src{philosophy 5.5; syn 2.7, 2.8}
It captures the static, declarative and inferential side of theories and concepts: contents, inferential relations, the composition of contents, learning by weighing axioms (globally, not concept by concept), pointers, partiality, and approximate truth in honest or error-model form. It misses the procedural side (recognizers, methods, the search for derivations, action), the statistical side of the contact with data in its proof-requiring core, and the dynamic side (purposes, softness, clarification, meaning change, discovery, the social process). That is less than a theory of concepts asks for, but we do not offer one. We offer a model of the declarative core of scientific theories, in which the procedural side reappears formally as the dependence of a written theory's worth on its reasoner and its vocabulary (\cref{sec:shape}).
