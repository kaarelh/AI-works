## 4. Deviations from OUTLINE / CLAIMS, with reasons

1. **Three statements moved to App. D** (rule B.3, page budget): `prop:phil:conservation` (ledger: main), `prop:phil:predicting` (ledger: main (short) + app) and `prop:phil:flagpolesystem` (ledger: main (informal) + app). The ledger's "place" column should be updated.
2. **`prop:phil:flagpole`(b): the record's text-length clause corrected.** `philosophy` Prop 3.1(b) says that the two hub theories $T_H$ and $T'_S$ "have texts that differ by $|\Lambda_s|-|\Lambda_h|$, a constant independent of $N_{\rm p}$ and of the data". That holds for the **law texts** only. In a literal code the initial conditions $\mathrm{ht}(p_i)=a_i$ and $\mathrm{sh}(p_i,1)=a_i$ differ by $\Delta_{\rm sh}$ per pole, so the full texts differ by $(|\Lambda_s|-|\Lambda_h|)+N_{\rm p}\Delta_{\rm sh}$. This is the record's own Prop 3.2(c) at $k=0$, and the saved output of its computation `checks/c6_flagpole.out` (3) shows it: $T_H$ has 40, 112, 832 tokens and $T'_S$ has 61, 142, 952 tokens at $N_{\rm p}=1,10,100$; only $|\Lambda_s|-|\Lambda_h|=20$ is independent of $N_{\rm p}$. The paper states: the law texts differ by the constant; the initial conditions differ by $N_{\rm p}\Delta_{\rm sh}$; with a primitive for the reference shadow ($\Delta_{\rm sh}=0$) the record's sentence holds. The main-text statement keeps only the derivation counts and "the hub is a property of the formulation"; the precise form is in App. D. This is a weakening and correction, not a strengthening. **The planning editor may want to add this to the ledger's corrections (§11).**
3. **`prop:phil:extractor`: the constant depends on the question family.** The record writes $\MA(D)\ge2^{-K(\mu)-K(g)-c}\mu_g(D)$ with a machine constant $c$. The theory machine of the proof must write the sentences $\varphi_q$, so $K(G)\le K(\mu)+K(\eta)+c$ only with $c$ including an index of the computable family $(\varphi_q)$. Without that dependence the bound fails. Example: fix $n$ fresh atoms on simple words, a random $b\in\{0,1\}^n$, and the conjunction $\chi_b$ of the literals with signs $b$; take the constant family $\varphi_q:=\chi_b$, $\eta\equiv1$ and any trivial $\mu$, so $K(\mu)+K(\eta)=O(1)$ and $\mu_\eta(\{(\chi_b,1)\})=1$. A consistent theory proves at most one of the $2^n$ conjunctions $\chi_{b'}$, so $b'\mapsto\MA(\{(\chi_{b'},1)\})$ is a lower semicomputable semimeasure given $n$ and the words, and the coding theorem gives $\MA(\{(\chi_b,1)\})\le2^{-n+O(\log n)}$ for incompressible $b$, far below $2^{-c}$. The paper says "here $c$ depends also on the family $(\varphi_q)$" in the statement and explains it after the proof. This is a weakening of the constant, matching the proof.
4. **Coin-free posterior odds (`prop:phil:flagpole`(a)):** "on every data set compatible with them", since the odds of two incompatible programs are $0/0$.
