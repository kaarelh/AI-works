## 0. Summary

**What the paper is about.** A Bayes mixture over axiom systems. A universal machine reads a random tape and emits sentences; the prior probability of a set of labelled sentences is the probability that the emitted theory is consistent and proves every datum. Its predictions are a belief function with three outcomes (proves, refutes, independent). The paper develops this object, relates it exactly to Solomonoff operator induction, finds what is specific to *logic* in it, and uses it to make precise, and to test, claims about what scientific theories do and are.

**The results in one paragraph each** (ranked in §1).
1. *SAI is consistent SOI, in every version.* Coin-free, one-tape and Kolmogorov versions agree with consistent SOI up to constants; on positive literals of a fresh predicate SAI *is* SOI on strings, so P1's binary-label results that hold up to additive constants transfer (`sfi` Thms 2.1, 3.1, 5.1; the exceptions are listed in `sfi` §5.5). This is Craig's construction made Bayesian; it means the idealised formalism cannot see the difference between a theory and a program.
2. *What is specific to logic.* Random theories can succeed where every r.e. theory fails (the lottery, `logic` Thm 2.2), already in true arithmetic with true data, where the limit posterior then lives on false theories (`logic` Thm 2.9(b), Cor 2.10); the phenomenon is exactly the failure of the majority vote for consistency constraints of unbounded arity. On $\Pi_1$ data the mixture is, up to a constant factor, one random threshold theory that is true whenever it is consistent, which gives a coding theorem and Chaitin's theorem for random theories (`logic` Thms 3.1–3.2, Cors 3.3, 3.6).
3. *Theory shape is relative to a reasoner and a vocabulary.* Under program length neither is visible (`shape` Thm 1.11). Charged as written, a theory's size depends exponentially on the reader's algebra, its depth, and the intermediate concepts available, and, once intermediate concepts are available and the reasoner can chain through them, only by a constant factor on declarative against procedural form (without them, or with one round, by the factor $m$, the number of query directions) (`shape` Cor 3.9, Thm 4.3). This makes the author's "textbook point" a theorem in a stated model, and gives the precise answer the brief asked for to "in what sense is a theory better shaped like a logical theory than a program".
4. *The theoretician's dilemma inside an ideal inducer.* Theoretical vocabulary costs $O(1)$ bits with free deduction; Craig's construction is useless under charged deduction because it writes run times in unary, but vocabulary can be eliminated at polynomial cost by other means; what finite theories cannot do without is a polyadic theoretical relation or theoretical entities (`terms` Thm 8.1). In the idealised mixture Hempel's inductive-systematisation reply fails (`philosophy` §1.6).
5. *Approximate laws.* Proof-requiring compatibility with deterministic theories rewards false exact laws; SAI's mixture contains randomised theories and, on law-plus-i.i.d.-error data, prefers the law together with its error distribution; on real Kepler residuals, among the Kepler-based theories compared, a law with a fitted error histogram wins in every cell computed (`laws` Props 2.1, 1.9(a), 3.6a, §3.6). Lewis's "kind nature" is a kink of the hull (`laws` Prop 5.6).
6. *The fundamentals* are sound and partly surprising: $M_A$ is a containment functional with two defects, proof-requiring updating is geometric conditioning and consistency-requiring updating is Dempster conditioning, the inconsistency probability is a Solovay-complete random real (so $M_A$ is not lower semicomputable), the loss against any consistent r.e. theory deciding the data is at most $K_\equiv(T)+c$, and there is no coding theorem (`fundamentals` Prop 2.1, Props 2.2–2.3, Thm 3.2, Thm 5.1, Thm 6.2).

**The philosophy (§2).** Claim D (theories answer questions posed in a given language) holds in its comparative form; its formal content is the data type, logical coherence, partiality and the choice of target, not a new inference principle. Claim S (a theory is a logical theory, not a program) holds as a descriptive observation about "declarative" textbook presentations; the explanatory question is open and necessarily reasoner-relative, and the best formal answer available (`shape`) locates the large effects in the pair (reasoner, vocabulary), not in logical form. Claim E (theories explain propositions) holds in the unification sense with four known gaps. The ladder is agreed; SAI sits on step 2 with partial results on step 3. The limitations section leads with the absence of a native noise model (L0) and the textbook point.

**The relation to SOI (§3)** is a table of equivalences (coin-free, one-tape, Kolmogorov, penalised on the axiom side, positive literals) and separations (an observed "independent" label, logical closure, the lottery against coin-free consistent SOI, Presburger under clocks, the diagonal under labeller clocks, charged deduction as certificate SOI).

**The paper (§5).** One paper of about 42 pages of main text plus appendices, in 13 sections, with the Kepler/Mars example running through the philosophy part; title options and the full plan in §5.

**Possible errors in the author's view (§7).** None survived review. The two candidates raised by scouts were withdrawn by `philosophy` after its referee showed that each attacked a reading the author's text does not make; their content is kept as remarks. No new disagreement is raised here.

## 1. Headline results, ranked by importance and coolness

*Ranking criterion.* Importance for the paper's aims (what the author asked for; what bears on what a theory is and does) weighted with how surprising and clean the mathematics is. Each entry gives the statement as proved, with hypotheses, the pointer, the status, what is known, and how the paper must state it. Items 1–5 are the paper's centrepieces; 6–10 are main supporting results; 11–15 are notable smaller results.

### H1. SAI is consistent SOI, in every version; on positive literals SAI is SOI

* **Coin-free** (`sfi` Thm 2.1) [proved; the construction is Craig's, known]. $B$ decidable and consistent. There is $c$ (depending on $V$, the coding, $B$) such that for every finite $D$: $W_A\asymp W^{\rm cons}\asymp W_A^{\rm dec}$, and $2^{-c}W^{\rm cons}\le W^{\rm cl}\le W^{\rm cons}$; $K_A$, $K_A^{\rm dec}$, $K_F^{\rm cons}$, $K_F^{\rm cl}$ agree up to $\pm c$ and are finite iff $B\cup D^\pm$ is consistent; and for every query $\varphi\notin\dom D$ the three outcome weights (proves / refutes / decides neither) of $W_A$ agree within $2^{\pm c}$ with those of the closed labellers ($1/0/\bot$). The semimeasure losses of the four coin-free inducers agree within $2c$ on every $D$ in every order.
* **One tape** (`sfi` Thm 3.1, Cor 3.2) [proved; computed on finite stand-ins, `sfi/checks/c1_onetape_finite.out`]. For every finite $D$, the empty one included: $M_A\asymp M_F^{\rm cons}\asymp M_A^{\rm dec}$, tape by tape through three wrappers (proof search, emission, Craig with the tape); against closed one-tape labellers the three outcome weights agree within $2^{\pm c}$. No Craig trick is needed for $M_A\asymp M_F^{\rm cons}$.
* **Axiom-side time penalties** (`sfi` Thm 3.4, Prop 3.5; Thm 7.1) [proved under the self-simulation assumptions (S$_{\rm tape}$), (S)]. Membership-time or generation-time penalties ($t\ge m^{a^*}$, time-constructible) change nothing up to constants, one-tape and coin-free, also class by class (posterior odds of any two classes of theories move by at most $2^{2c}$).
* **Transfer principle** (`sfi` Thm 5.1, Cor 5.2) [proved]. If $R$ does not occur in $B$ and some model of $B$ interprets the terms $t_x$ as pairwise distinct ("fresh atoms", `fundamentals` Def 1.1′), then on data sets of literals $R(t_x)^b$: $K_A=K_F\pm c$, $W_A\asymp W$, $M_A\asymp M_F$ ($D\ne\emptyset$), with P1's objects on the string data, and the same for the three-way weights. Hence P1's binary-label results that hold up to additive constants hold for SAI on literal data (not its cumulative normalised-convention, computability, private-randomness or weak-semimeasure statements; `sfi` §5.5): no coding theorem with the two-point rate $\log d-O(1)\le\max(K_A-L_A)\le\log d+O(\log\log d)$ (P1 Cor 4.10), shared randomness is not a mixture of private coins (P1 Prop 4.7, read as a comparison of $M_A$ with P1's $M_{\rm prod}$; no SAI-internal product law is defined), and the content of SAI beyond SOI lies in logically structured data. Without distinct terms the transfer fails (`sfi` after Thm 5.1).
* **Novelty.** The construction is Craig (1953); the Bayesian weight equivalence was not found in the literature (scout L §5.2); the one-tape and Kolmogorov forms and the transfer principle are new here (scout L). The coin-free decider form is AI-paper Thm 6.3. Hänni's schema route to the equivalence needs two sorts or a sound assigner (AI-paper Props 6.4, 6.5; `sfi` Prop 2.2); the conclusion does not.
* **How to state it.** "SAI ≍ *coin-free consistent* SOI" for the coin-free version, and "one-tape SAI ≍ one-tape consistent SOI" for $M_A$; never the unqualified "SAI ≡ SOI", since plain SOI on sentences differs (H7, §3). The equivalence controls semimeasure losses, not the meaning of "undefined" (H7).
* **Why it matters.** It is the theorem behind "the idealised formalism cannot see shape" (§2, claim S): every consistent assigner is an axiom set at $O(1)$ bits.

### H2. The lottery: random theories that no r.e. theory can replace

* **The lottery** (`logic` Thm 2.2) [proved; computed: `logic/checks/c1` A1, A2; referee `rb1`]. There is a stream $D_\infty$ of labelled sentences, computable from $\emptyset'$, such that (a) $M_A(D_n)\ge2^{-K(G_{\rm lot})-c}\prod_{k\ge0}(1-2^{-(k+1)})$, the product being $0.288788\ldots$, so the one-tape cumulative loss is bounded and the posterior converges in total variation; (b) no consistent r.e. theory proves every datum (with $B$); (c) $W_A(D_n)\to0$, $K_A(D_n)\to\infty$, and $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L'}(D_n)\to\infty$ for every $\mathcal L'\supseteq\mathcal L$ with computable syntax; (d) the posterior probability of deterministic machines tends to 0, and $E_\infty$ contains no tape whose theory has an r.e. closure: the limit posterior lives on random theories that are not r.e. Setting: $\mathcal L$ contains $0$, $S$ and a binary $\mathrm{Win}$; $B$ decidable, consistent, without $\mathrm{Win}$, with a model in which the numerals are distinct.
* **Exactly where it cannot happen** (`logic` Props 2.3, 2.4, 2.7, Cor 3.5) [proved; computed `c2`, `c6` R6]. If every inconsistent subset of the data's sentence class has an inconsistent subset of size $\le r$, a majority vote at a Lebesgue density point gives a consistent r.e. theory proving the stream; the threshold $1/(r+1)$ is exact for the argument. Also excluded: $\Pi_1$ data over a true background $\supseteq\Q$; co-r.e. streams over block-decidable classes (which include the lottery classes); and streams that a positive-measure set of *true* random theories proves. So scout F's Q1 ("bounded one-tape loss implies an r.e. theory proves the stream") is false in general and true in these cases (`logic` Cor 2.5; that they are the only ones is not claimed).
* **In true arithmetic with true data** (`logic` Thm 2.9(b), Cor 2.10) [proved, given the flexible-formula Lemma 2.8; computed `rb3`]. With $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$, $B$ decidable, true, $\supseteq\Q$: a lottery stream exists whose data are all **true**, each $\Q$-equivalent to a Boolean combination of $\Sigma_1$ sentences; on it $L_A$ is bounded, $K_A-L_A\to\infty$ (so no coding theorem $K_A\le L_A+f(L_A)$ holds for true data beyond $\Sigma_1\cup\Pi_1$), and $P_\infty\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)\}=0$: **the limit posterior is concentrated on false theories although every datum is true**. The data are adversarial (diagonal), so this is a possibility result.
* **Separating versions of SOI** (`sfi` Cor 4.2) [proved, given `logic` Thm 2.2, Prop 2.3(b)]. On the lottery stream one-tape consistent SOI is bounded, coin-free consistent SOI and $W_A$ are unbounded, and plain coin-free SOI on sentences ($W^{\rm all}$) is bounded.
* **Rate** (`logic` Cor 2.6′) [proved]: on the lottery streams $-\log W_A(D_n)\le\Kpad(n)+c$, so the coin-free loss diverges more slowly than any computable unbounded nondecreasing function.
* **Known and new.** Ancestors: the majority vote of de Leeuw, Moore, Shannon and Shapiro (1956); Kyburg's lottery paradox (1961) (the majority-vote theory refutes every ticket and is inconsistent); the bounded diagonally non-computable functions and Bienvenu–Porter's dichotomy (the survival condition $\sum_k2^{-(k+1)}<\infty$ is the convergence condition) [known at snippet/secondary level]. The modified Demski prior also draws random axiom systems from a universal machine, so "random axiom systems" are not new; what is new is the comparison of the one-tape law with its own coin-free and Kolmogorov versions, the identification of the succeeding hypotheses as incomplete random theories r.e. in the tape, the arity boundary, and the arithmetic versions with true data (`logic` §7).
* **Why it matters.** A clean phenomenon of logical consistency with no analogue in plain SOI; it shows that the successful hypotheses of SAI can be *random theories* (a law plus a random completion of contingent facts), and it is the formal reason the paper should say nothing realist about induced theories (§2, liability 7).

### H3. Theory shape is relative to a reasoner and a vocabulary (the textbook point as a theorem)

*Model* (`shape` §1): laws are Boolean (linear laws over GF(2), parity, the implication chain); data are conditional questions $(\alpha,\ell)$ in every direction, compatible with $\Phi$ iff $\Phi\cup\alpha$ is consistent and the reasoner derives $\ell$ (encoding (E3); the two-valued encoding "$\alpha\to\ell$ is true" is degenerate, Prop 1.8); reasoners are $\mathrm{UP}_k$ ($k$ parallel rounds of unit propagation), $\mathrm{UP}$, $\mathrm{FC}_k$ (forward chaining on directed rules), $\mathrm{GE}$ (Gaussian elimination), $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$; inducers are program length $K_A^{\mathcal R}$, listing $K_{\rm lst}^{\mathcal R}$ (the theory charged as written) and time-charged $Kt_A^{\mathcal R}$; class $\mathcal C^X$ (base variables only) or $\mathcal C^{XY}$ (with auxiliary variables = intermediate concepts).
* **Program length sees neither the reasoner nor the vocabulary** (`shape` Thm 1.11, Thm 1.12(c)) [proved]. For every sound reasoner $\mathcal R'$ over explicit or linear theories and every conditional data set, $K_A^{\mathrm{UP}_1,X}\le K_A^{\mathcal R'}+c$; on the question data of an affine law $K_A^{\mathcal R}=K(\mathcal S)\pm c$ for every named reasoner, with or without intermediate concepts, because a fixed program writes out the prime-implicate library. This is Craig's collapse again, for reasoners.
* **Charged as written, the size is exact without intermediate concepts** (`shape` Props 2.5, 2.5a, Thm 2.6, Cor 2.8) [proved; computed]. For every affine law, the clauses common to all $\mathrm{UP}$-usable theories over the base variables are exactly those of the indecomposable circuits; when $d_*\ge3$ (least weight of a nonzero derived equation) they form the unique minimum. Simplex family: $m2^{(m-1)/2}$ clauses against $\log_2(m+1)$ equations for $\mathrm{GE}$.
* **Intermediate concepts help only a reasoner that can chain through them** (`shape` Prop 3.1a, Prop 3.8, Rem 3.2a) [proved; (c) given Håstad 1986]. One round: every $\mathrm{UP}_1$-usable theory with any auxiliary variables contains all prime implicates ($2^{m-1}$ clauses for one parity law). Fixed $k$ rounds: $2^{\Omega(m^{1/(2k-1)})}$ written length for one parity law. $2\lceil\log_2m\rceil-1$ rounds: $4m-6$ clauses suffice. Simplex family with unbounded propagation: at most $m(2m-4)$.
* **Systems of laws: exponential for any vocabulary and depth** (`shape` Thm 3.6) [proved, given Göös–Kamath–Robere–Sokolov 2019]. For the hidden-state law $\mathcal H_q$ (observed quantities are fixed linear functionals of an unobserved state), every $\mathrm{UP}$-usable explicit theory, with any auxiliary variables, clause widths and rounds, has written length $2^{\Omega(m^{\varepsilon/3})}$, while $\mathrm{GE}$ uses $m$ equations of weight $\le5$.
* **The regimes** (`shape` Cor 3.9) [proved as indicated; reading an assessment]: the exponential separations are between *pairs* (reasoner, vocabulary).
* **Directionality is real but cheap** (`shape` Prop 4.2, Thm 4.3) [proved]. For one parity law, a direction-complete rule base needs exactly $m$ times the clauses (the number of query directions), also for one round with any vocabulary; with intermediate concepts and unbounded rounds the factor is at most 9 in written length (3 for the chain encoding), and for every round budget at most the largest clause width of a smallest usable clausal theory; a program with internal variables answers all directions with $O(m)$ operations; under program length the factor vanishes.
* **Where know-how lives** (`shape` Prop 5.1, Thm 5.2) [proved, one derivation count a proof sketch]: in the reasoner, in written lemmas, or in an uncharged computation. The AI-paper's charged-deduction readings, extended to conditional data, charge the full lemma library no more than a time-bounded description of the law, and no compatible hypothesis is cheaper than a description of the law: they cannot express the textbook point.
* **Learning** (`shape` Thm 6.2) [proved; computed]: without intermediate concepts a $\mathrm{UP}$ inducer learns parity pattern by pattern (fully correct only after all $2^{m-1}$ patterns; Bayes loss at least $\mathrm{len}_m\approx m\log_2m$ bits per pattern on block-complete data), while $\mathrm{GE}$ or intermediate concepts pay $O(m\log m)$ bits in total.
* **Depth against size** (`shape` §7) [proved; values computed; $\Theta(m\,\mathrm{ia}_k(m))$ known]: for the implication chain the least $\mathrm{UP}_k$-usable theory over the base variables is a minimum $k$-hop TC-spanner of the path.
* **Known and new** (`shape` §9). Known or folklore: propagation-complete encodings and their cost (Babka et al.), Gwynne–Kullmann's superpolynomial bound for XOR systems, the monotone-circuit view (Bessiere et al.), TC-spanner sizes. New as far as the searches found: the exact forced clauses for every affine law and the unique minimum for $d_*\ge3$; the readings inside a universal inducer (Thms 1.11, 1.12), the directionality theorem and its limit, the regimes, the three homes of know-how, the learning theorem. **Thm 3.6's novelty is unconfirmed**: a SAT 2026 paper on propagation-complete XOR encodings surfaced and could not be read; it must be read before any novelty claim.
* **How to state it.** Every separation must name its inducer ($K_{\rm lst}$ or $Kt$, never $K_A^{\mathcal R}$), its class and its depth budget; the simplex family is a reasoner gap only "over the base variables" or "for one round". The recommended sentence (`shape` §10, adopted by `philosophy` §5.1): *"Whether a written theory suffices depends on the reader. Paired with a reasoner that can do algebra, a linear law's few equations suffice; paired with one that only applies rules, the theory must spell out exponentially many derived laws, unless it is written with new intermediate quantities and the reader can chain many steps through them. Even then, some systems of laws need exponentially long written theories for such a reader whatever quantities are introduced. The idealised inducer cannot see any of this, because it charges a theory by the length of a program that may compute the missing lemmas."* It is not a theorem about textbooks.

### H4. The theoretician's dilemma inside axiom induction

`terms` Thm 8.1 [proved, with the conditions listed under it], data observational, in (ii)–(iv) literal data in $\mathcal L_O=\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,R\}$ under SD's readings of charged deduction ((a1) every instance short, (a2) nonlogical instances short, (b) total material, (b2) nonlogical material) at polynomial budgets:
* **(i) In principle** (`terms` Thm 2.1) [proved; penalised forms under (S), (S$_{\rm tape}$)]. With free deduction, restricting hypotheses to the observational vocabulary changes the prior weight of every Borel class of observational contents, every observational predictive probability and the cumulative loss by at most a constant, one-tape, coin-free and Kolmogorov, also under axiom-side penalties. Tape by tape the observational projection of a random theory has *exactly* the same law on observational data.
* **(ii) Craig's construction in practice** (`terms` Lemma 3.1, Thm 3.3, Cor 3.5) [proved, given SD, the time hierarchy and APSPACE = EXPTIME]. Craig's padding writes a run time in unary: derivations of size $\mathrm{ds}$ become derivations of size $\ge2^{\mathrm{ds}}$. Every run-padded observational axiomatisation with polynomial-time membership fits exactly the polynomial-time regularities, under every reading. Axiom sets with theoretical symbols fit $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ under (b), $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ under (a1), every decidable set under (a2), (b2). So Craig loses unconditionally under (a1), (a2), (b2), and under (b) iff $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}\ne\mathsf P$.
* **(iii) Eliminating vocabulary in practice** (`terms` Prop 4.1, 4.3, Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7) [proved under (F1)]. Theoretical vocabulary is eliminable at polynomial cost by storing content in objects no observational term names: per hypothesis at $O(1)$ bits under (b); at the level of fit classes under (a1), (a2), (b2), where already *finite* observational theories fit what all hypotheses fit.
* **(iv) What is indispensable** (`terms` Thm 5.1, 5.3, 4.6, Prop 9.1) [proved, given Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé, the one-tape simulation and, for (a1), CKS]. A finite theory in the observational vocabulary with any unary theoretical predicates and constants, compatible with the first-order theory of the named objects, fits exactly the **regular** regularities, under every reading and with free deduction. One **binary** theoretical relation restores every decidable regularity ((a2), (b2)), $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ ((b)), $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ ((a1)). Quantifying over unnamed objects also does, but a finite theory that does so without theoretical vocabulary and fits a non-regular regularity must prove some $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-sentence false in the named structure (for the theory built there: $\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$, true of no named object): a forced ontological commitment.
* **(v) Free deduction, finite theories** (`terms` Thm 6.2, 6.4, 6.5) [proved; Thm 6.4(d) a proof sketch]. On "there are at least $n$ things", every consistent monadic finite theory has $K_{\rm fin}=\Kpad(n)\pm c\to\infty$, while one unary function symbol gives $O(1)$; on PA's theorems finite $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-theories diverge (given Ryll-Nardzewski) while theoretical sorts give $O(1)$; and r.e. axiom induction *is* finite-theory induction in a three-sorted extension up to $O(1)$, coin-free ($K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}=K_A^{\mathcal L}\pm c$, $W_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}\asymp W_A^{\mathcal L}$), but not one-tape (`terms` Rem 6.6, via the lottery). `fundamentals` Prop 1.8(d) is the named-objects variant of the infinity separation; the paper states it once.
* **Hempel's inductive-systematisation reply** (`philosophy` §1.6) [assessment, resting on `terms` Thm 2.1 and Rem 2.2, proved]: SAI is an inductive systematisation, and in the universal mixture the observational predictive probabilities are the same up to $2^{\pm2c}$ with or without theoretical vocabulary; so in the idealised Bayesian setting Hempel's reply fails. Hempel's own definition (relative to a single theory) was not checked against his text.
* **Known and new** (`terms` §10). With free deduction, the finite conservative extension with one binary relation is Kleene (1952) / Craig–Vaught (1958) [known, secondary level]; Thm 6.5 is an effective many-sorted form of it, only its Bayesian corollary new. New: the derivation-bounded constructions and fit classes (two-stack programs stated backwards), the regularity theorem for unary vocabulary (novelty plausible, not established), the forced ontological commitment, the complexity-theoretic form of "Craigian axiomatisations are useless in practice" (scout L found none earlier).
* **How to state it.** "The classical reply becomes a theorem about Craig's construction and run-padded axiomatisations, not about theoretical vocabulary." What pays is theoretical *relations* and *entities*, not vocabulary; in the idealised setting nothing theoretical is indispensable. The results concern one poor observational language (observation sequences) and literal data; one binary observable moves the boundary (`terms` Prop 9.3).

### H5. $\Pi_1$ data: derandomisation, a coding theorem, Chaitin's theorem for random theories

* **Threshold machine** (`logic` Thm 3.1, Thm 3.2) [proved; computed `c3`]. With $B$ true and $\supseteq\Q$: the machine $G_{\rm thr}$ that, on a tape read as a real $r$, emits every $\Pi_1^*$ sentence $\pi$ with $\Unif(O_\pi)>r$ produces an inconsistent theory if $r<\Inc_0$ and a **true** one if $r>\Inc_0$, and for every data set of $\Pi_1$ type $M_A(D)\le\nu_{G_{\rm thr}}(D)\le2^{K(G_{\rm thr})+c}M_A(D)$. On $\Pi_1$ data the universal mixture *is*, up to a constant factor, one random true theory: a uniformly random threshold on the prior probability of provability. Every $\Pi_1$ sentence proved with prior probability $2^{-k}$ is proved by one true theory of complexity $k+O(\log k)$.
* **Coding theorem for $\Pi_1$ data** (`logic` Cor 3.3) [proved]: $L_A\le-\log W_A+c\le K_A+c\le L_A+K(\lceil L_A\rceil)+c'$. **Bounded loss on a $\Pi_1$ stream is explained by one true theory** (Cor 3.5).
* **Chaitin's theorem for random theories** (`logic` Cor 3.6) [proved] — **in the semimeasure and three-valued conventions**: for $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x)$ ("$K(x)>m$", a $\Pi_1$ sentence), if $K(x)>m$ then $m-K(m)-K(\lceil L_A\rceil)-c\le L_A(\{(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x),1)\})\le m+K(m)+c'$, i.e. $m\pm O(\log m)$ bits; one true theory of complexity $\le m+K(m)+c$ proves all true $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x)$ at once.
* **But in the normalised convention** (`logic` Prop 3.11) [proved, given unique computation codes]: with $B$ true and $\supseteq\PA$, the same datum costs at most $\log(1+2^{-j})$ for all but $2^{2m+K(m)+j+c}+2^{m+1}$ strings $x$ of length $n$. The $m$ bits are *the price of deciding the sentence, not of predicting its truth value*.
* **The boundary is exact** (`logic` Props 3.8, 3.9; Cor 2.10) [proved, given the flexible-formula Lemma 2.8]: one false $\Sigma_1$ datum, or two **true** data that are Boolean combinations of $\Sigma_1$ sentences, already give the $\log d$ gap $K_A>\log d-2$ with $L_A\le2+c$.
* **Known and new** (`logic` §3.4, §7). The threshold device is Bienvenu–Romashchenko–Shen–Taveneaux–Vermeeren (2014) and de Leeuw et al.; Chaitin (1974) is the deterministic bound (only its upper-bound form is used, as Raatikainen 1998 requires). New: the threshold just above the unknown $\Inc_0$, the universal mixture, the single random threshold theory and the coding theorem for an inductive inference rule.

### H6. The object and its basic theory

* **Definition** (`fundamentals` Defs 1.2, 1.4). A theory machine is a c.e. set $G\subseteq\{0,1\}^*\times\Sent$; $\nu_G(D)$ is the probability that the emitted theory is consistent (with $B$) and proves $\psi_D$; $M_A:=\nu_{G_U}$ for the universal theory machine. No consistency condition is imposed on machines: consistency is an event.
* **Structure** (`fundamentals` Prop 2.1) [proved; computed]. $\nu_G(D+(\varphi,1))+\nu_G(D+(\varphi,0))+\nu_G^3(D+(\varphi,\iota))=\nu_G(D)$; $M_A$ is a (sub-probability) containment functional of the random trichotomy function; two defects: global $1-M_A(\emptyset)=\Inc_0>0$ and local $\Indep_D(\varphi)$; weights depend only on $\Cn(D^\pm)$, entailed data are free, contradicting data fatal; the cumulative semimeasure loss is order independent.
* **Updating rules** (`fundamentals` Props 2.2, 2.3) [proved; rule names known at secondary level]. Proof-requiring updating is the *geometric* conditioning of the belief function $\widehat\Bel_0$ (Suppes–Zanotti), with focal sets $\mathrm{Mod}(B\cup A_\omega)$; $\Bel_D$ is totally monotone, with a probability on completions between $\Bel_D$ and $\Pl_D$. Consistency-requiring updating is *Dempster's* rule. In one sentence: under PR a datum must be *explained* by the hypothesis; under CR it is *added* to it.
* **Computability** (`fundamentals` Thm 3.2) [proved; (c) given Chaitin and Levin–Schnorr]. $\Inc_0$ is not computable, is Solovay complete and Martin-Löf random; so $M_A(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0$ is right-c.e. and not left-c.e., and $M_A$ is **not lower semicomputable**, unlike $M_F$. Values are $\emptyset'$-computable; $M_A+\Inc_0$ is lsc (Prop 3.4). A new application of the standard Solovay-completeness argument.
* **Dominance and the loss bound** (`fundamentals` Thm 4.1, Thm 5.1) [proved]. $M_A\ge2^{-K(\nu)}\nu$ for every theory-machine law $\nu$. On any consistent reveal sequence (any order, adaptive or adversarial), against any r.e. $T$ with $B\cup T$ consistent that decides every datum: $L_A(D_n)-L_A(\emptyset)\le-\log\Unif_A(\Ext T)\le-\log\mm(\Ext T)+c\le K_\equiv(T)+c$. The complexity is that of $T$ *up to deductive equivalence*, the bound favours the weakest theory deciding the data, and $\PA$ has small $K_\equiv$ although infinitely axiomatised.
* **Invariance** (`fundamentals` Props 4.2, 4.3, Thm 4.4, Cor 4.5) [proved]: machine invariance; the background as a prior shift by $K(B')$; faithful translations change weights by $2^{\pm(K(\tau)+c)}$; new vocabulary changes old-vocabulary weights by $O(1)$.
* **No coding theorem** (`fundamentals` Thm 6.2) [proved; computed `c4`]: two literals of a fresh predicate on words of length $d$ already give $K_A\ge\log d-2$ against $-\log M_A\le2+c$; no two of $2^{-K_A}$, $W_A$, $M_A$ agree up to a constant.
* **Why proof-requiring is primary** (`fundamentals` §7.5) [proved items]: the 0/1 CR weight is bounded below by the empty theory's mass on all consistent data (it never reweights survivors), its Dempster predictor pays at least a constant per non-entailed datum, and its cumulative loss depends on the reveal order with a linear gap (Prop 7.2(b), new in the revision); graded CR charges entailed data and is not even a containment functional (Prop 7.3(e)). For complete theories PR and CR coincide (Prop 10.1(f)).
* **Prior work** (`fundamentals` §10). The *modified Demski prior* (Fallenstein, Demski, Garrabrant, forum posts c. 2015; used in *Inductive Coherence*, 2016) already samples theories as outputs of a universal machine on random bits [known at snippet level only; the definition text was never read]. What is distinctive of $M_A$: one draw instead of iterated draws to completion, so the theory stays incomplete with positive probability (almost surely above $\Q$, Prop 8.7); a belief function of provability with an independent outcome; proof-requiring conditioning; and the results above. **The paper must quote the modified prior's definition from a readable copy before relying on details.**

### H7. Where SAI and SOI separate, and where the time-bounded separations come from

* **An observed "independent" label** (`sfi` Props 6.3–6.5) [proved, given the classical productivity Lemma 6.3′ (Smullyan; Rosser) and, one-tape, the Lebesgue density theorem]. $\mathcal L\supseteq\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$, $B$ decidable and consistent with $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$. The equivalence becomes one-sided: $2^{-c}W^{3,\rm cons}\le W_A^3$, but for every $n$ some $B$-independent $\sigma_n$ has $W^{3,\rm cons}(\{(\sigma_n,\iota)\})\le\Omtail{n+1}\to0$ while $W_A^3(\{(\sigma_n,\iota)\})\ge2^{-c}$; the same one-tape ($M_F^{3,\rm cons}\le2^{1-n}+\Omtail{n+1}$). The witnesses form no r.e. family (Prop 6.4). Mechanism: "decides neither" is a $\Pi_1$ property a theory has without committing; a labeller must output $\iota$ after finitely many steps.
* **Logical closure** (`sfi` Prop 6.1(c)) [proved]: in the semimeasure convention consistent SOI never becomes certain of a consequence: its undefined mass at any unasked $\chi$ is $\ge2^{-K(\chi)-c}$ uniformly in $D$ (in the normalised convention its prediction on an entailed $\chi$ is 1, Prop 6.1(b)); SAI predicts entailed data with certainty.
* **A natural separation: Presburger arithmetic** (`sfi` Props 9.1–9.4′) [proved, given Fischer–Rabin at abstract level; one case conditional]. SAI (all versions, also axiom-side penalised) has bounded loss on the Presburger data; clocked coin-free consistent SOI is unbounded for clocks $\le2^{2^{c'm}}$, one-tape for $\le2^{c'm}$ (doubly exponential one-tape: conditional on two results verified only at secondary level). With charged deduction the budget SAI needs is the matching SOI version's: doubly exponential under derivation size or material (Prop 9.4), at least singly exponential under (a1) (Prop 9.4′; doubly exponential open), open under (a2), (b2). **Any sentence "once deduction is charged the separation disappears" must name the reading.**
* **Time bounds with free deduction** (`sfi` Thms 7.1, 7.3–7.4, Prop 7.6(a), (b), Rem 7.9) [proved; restated from AI-paper Thms 6.14, 6.15, TF]: axiom-side penalties keep the equivalence; labeller-side clocks make it one-sided (SAI's regret against clocked consistent SOI $\le c_\tau$; on a computable diagonal sequence every computable predictor loses $\ge n-0.361$ while SAI loses $O(1)$); and *unpenalised* consistent SOI matches SAI there through one constant-size evaluator ($|u|+c$). So **the time-bounded separations come from free deduction, not from axioms**: they do not show that axioms are better hypotheses than programs. "SAI is strictly stronger than every time-bounded function inductor" is refuted (TF; `sfi` Prop 7.5).
* **Charged deduction is certificate SOI** (`sfi` Thm 8.1, Cor 8.2) [proved under (F1); restated from AI-paper Thm 6.16, Cor 6.17, SD]: fit classes $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ (b), $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ (a1), every decidable set (a2), (b2); none charges *finding* a derivation (Rem 8.5), which is why it cannot express the textbook point (H3).
* **Computability** (`sfi` Prop 6.7) [proved]: none of the versions is lsc when consistency of finite sets is not r.e.; $W_A,W^{\rm cons},M_A,M_F^{\rm cons}$ are $\emptyset'$-computable, the closed and decider versions $\emptyset''$-computable (their $\emptyset'$-computability is not claimed).

### H8. Approximate laws: honest bands, exceptions, and laws with error models

* **Over deterministic theories PR rewards false exact laws** (`laws` Prop 2.1, Cor 2.2) [proved; scout P's Prop 2.3]: compatibility is monotone in logical strength, so data never move the odds towards a hedge; the false exact law beats its true tolerance-weakened version on every data set the latter fits.
* **Precision regimes** (`laws` Props 2.4, 2.5) [proved; computed]: under guaranteed tolerances the switch is sharp at $\varepsilon^*+\delta_0$; under dithered rounding an exact false law survives $n$ data with probability $(1-p_\Delta)^n$ and is refuted after about $\Delta/\Ex|e|$ data, however coarse the cells. (This corrects the brief's precision seed.)
* **SAI is the best two-part code over theory machines** (`laws` Prop 1.9(a)) [proved]: $L_A=\min_G[K(G)-\log\nu_G]\pm c$; $M_A$'s mixture contains randomised theories that complete a law at random inside what it allows (Prop 1.8). Neither $K_A$ nor literal-exception codes over finite theories match it within $O(1)$ (Prop 1.9(c)); over all theory machines a steeper penalty collapses onto the universal machine (Prop 1.9(d)), so best-system analyses use classes of laws with private literal facts.
* **Honest law against exact law with exceptions** (`laws` Props 3.4, 3.5) [proved]: under deterministic patches (code Q) the honest law has the lower rate iff the peakedness $\mathrm{pk}(e)<\gamma_{\rm ex}/(2\gamma_{\rm si})$; the optimum of the family is the $(1-\theta_{\rm ex})$-quantile tolerance with the rest as exceptions (known in form: $\nu$-SVR).
* **Under SAI's own pricing** (`laws` Lemma 3.6, Prop 3.6a) [proved]: the uniform band is the best completion inside the band only for uniform errors; on data from a law with i.i.d. errors SAI's asymptotic rate is that of the law *together with its error distribution*, and no theory machine does better (Prop 3.6a(c)); the two-point form "exact, except in a share $\rho$, then within $\varepsilon$" beats the uniform band iff $\Ex\ln(1+|e|/\varepsilon)<2\ln2-1$, which needs peakedness above 1.79 and holds whenever it exceeds 2.12.
* **Real Kepler residuals** (`laws` §3.6) [computed: `laws/checks/l4_kepler_pk`, `l10_revision`]: seven bodies, windows 12–2000 yr; per-fit peakedness 1.60–3.95 (minimax fit) and 1.89–7.08 (mean fit). Among the Kepler-based machines compared, a law with a fitted error histogram beats both the uniform honest band and the exact law with exceptions in all 429 (body, window, precision) cells, 0.4–26% below the band; the two-point form beats the band for 34 of 39 body–window pairs. **Headline: under SAI's pricing an approximately true law is a law with a calibrated error model**, not a silent partial theory and not an exact law with listed exceptions.
* **Almost-sure selection** (`laws` Thm 3.8, Cor 3.9) [proved]: under integrable costs, with Wald bounds on switch times and the phase sequence $0\to E\to P\to H\to N$.
* **Humility** (`laws` Prop 3.10): for deterministic theories it is the "independent" mass of the honest law (the trichotomy is its formal home); for SAI's randomised theories it is calibration of an error distribution.
* **Sound reasoning with approximate models** (`laws` Props 4.1–4.3, Rem 4.4) [proved; computed]: derivation horizons (exact for linear maps in one dimension); given a background that decides the comparisons (true for semialgebraic models over RCF), honest models are jointly consistent and sound, exact ones explode, and joint consistency is a pairwise check in one dimension (Helly); worst-case propagation is conservative ($7.96\varepsilon$ typical against $100\varepsilon$ worst after 100 steps).
* **Known** (`laws` §9.2): precision-dependent selection in AIC/MDL/MML form (Forster–Sober; Rissanen; Wallace; Grünwald); law plus error model is the standard MDL situation (Gibbs, Barron); what is specific is the reading for proof-requiring axiom inducers and the honest/exception dichotomy with its peakedness criteria.

### H9. Gödel phenomena and the ω-gap

* **Arithmetical shape of the trichotomy** (`logic` Prop 4.1) [proved; computed `c4`]: with $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$, true $\Sigma_1$ sentences are never independent, false $\Pi_1$ sentences get $(0,1,0)$, so any belief or independent mass on a $\Pi_1$ sentence certifies its truth — the formal content inside the inducer of "the Gödel sentence is true because unprovable".
* **Soundness is never learned** (`logic` Thm 4.2, Thm 4.4) [proved, given G2]: coin-free, with $B$ consistent $\supseteq\PA$, the posterior probability of $\Sigma_1$-soundness is at most $1/(1+2^{-c})$ for every $D$, true arithmetic included; one-tape on $\Pi_1$-type data, $P(\text{sound}\mid D)\le1-2^{-c}$ uniformly ($B$ true $\supseteq\PA$); in general one-tape confidence in soundness needs a large coding gap (Prop 4.3); the general one-tape case is open.
* **$\Con(B\cup T)$** (`logic` Prop 4.5) [proved, given G2; **hypothesis corrected**: $B\cup T$ true]: given data presenting $T$, its consistency statement keeps three positive limit shares, and which direction is favoured is a choice of universal machine. (The development round's statement without the truth hypothesis is refuted: $B=\PA+\neg\Con(\PA)$, $T=\emptyset$.)
* **Old evidence; induction in mathematics** (`logic` Prop 4.6) [proved]: $B$-entailed data leave the posterior unchanged, so checking instances $P(0),\dots,P(N)$ of a $\Delta_0$ property leaves $\Bel(\forall xP)$ at its prior; induction in mathematics needs a bounded deducer.
* **The ω-gap** (`logic` Props 5.1, 5.2, Thm 5.3) [proved]: given all closed instances, the limit trichotomy of $\forall x\varphi$ is $(1,0,0)$, $(0,1,0)$ or fully mixed according as $B$ plus the instances proves, refutes or leaves it open; $\Bel$ satisfies the Gaifman condition exactly when the instances deductively prove the universal (the provability analogue of AI-paper Prop B.10); uniformly, for every $k$ a true $\Pi_1$ sentence with $\Q$-provable instances has belief below $2^{-k}$ (Thm 5.3: $B$ true $\supseteq\PA$, given G2). Closures: the ω-rule and full second-order consequence; the generative variant only with the universal among the data (§5.4(c), corrected after the referees). Line budgets and a Kreisel-type property: Prop 5.4, with the non-degenerate Example 5.4(ii) as the one to show.

### H10. Best systems, explanatory power, correction

* **Lewis's "kind nature" is a kink** (`laws` Prop 5.6) [proved]: the best system is robust to the balance of simplicity and strength exactly at a kink of the data's hull (P2's kink lemmas); **the trivialisation is blocked** by the coding bound, up to the complexity of a language chosen after the data (Prop 5.7).
* **Laws vs initial conditions** (`laws` Props 5.1, 5.3, 5.5) [proved]: in a steeper MAP, facts used once never enter for $\lambda>1$; the model–randomness split separates reused facts from facts used once, *not* laws from initial conditions (well-observed elements sit with the law); laws are singled out by how their explanatory power scales with the number of systems. K3 is a law about initial conditions: with the law in place it pays iff $s|S|>L_3$ at exchange rate 1 (a comparison of law choices, not membership of the $\lambda$-MAP). Scout N2's criterion is refuted. In the question format, initial conditions are question antecedents (Prop 5.9).
* **Explanatory power** (`laws` Def 5.2, Prop 5.8) [proved]: EP is the per-bit log-likelihood ratio of a module; Myrvold's unification term is $I_h(D_1;D_2)-I_\flat(D_1;D_2)$, zero with stated parameters and the bits of shared unknown parameters otherwise. Copernicus against Ptolemy with stated coincidences: $C$ wins at every steepness iff $H<\sum_pa_p$ — unification without better prediction (`laws` §5.3; `philosophy` §3.4, with the two comparisons kept separate).
* **Domains and retention** (`laws` Props 6.1, 6.2) [proved; computed]: a domain restriction pays iff the law, even honest, is worse than literal data; under SAI's pricing never for a band below about 66° of longitude (a sufficient bound), so the Moon is kept with a tolerance and an error distribution; a superseded theory is retained for data coarser than a computable $\Delta^*$.
* **Explanation by approximate correction** (`laws` Prop 7.1) [proved; exact rational toy]: the successor proves the honest old law on every window with uniform tolerance $\varepsilon_{\min}(W)\le((1+\eta\|P\|)^W-1)|a|$, refutes the exact old law, and among uniform-tolerance forms the strongest it proves is the strongest both prove; the whole common content is the disjunction of the two theories (the earlier "exactly what the two have in common" is withdrawn).

### H12. Questions against raw data; explanation's asymmetry

* **Conservation-law world** (`philosophy` Prop 2.2) [proved; computed `c5`, `c9`]: the law saves between $\frac12\log_2\frac{\pi m}2+o(1)$ and one bit more per raw step (for $t\le Z/2$), a vanishing share $O(\log m/m)$ of the raw loss, but its absolute gain per step equals, by the chain rule, the raw no-law model's loss on the question about the conserved quantity; a raw Bayes mixture learns the law at one bit. So the difference between the tasks is the *target* (which losses count) and the extraction of answers, not what must be learned.
* **Question answering costs no more description than raw modelling plus an extractor** (`philosophy` Prop 2.4) [proved, given `fundamentals` Thm 4.1]: for a computable raw world $\mu$ and a partial computable extractor $g$ whose labellings are almost surely consistent with $B$, $M_A(D)\ge2^{-K(\mu)-K(g)-c}\mu_g(D)$ on every finite question data set; the extractor is the bridge from raw data to the question language, and its cost $K(g)$ is not small in real cases.
* **Flagpole** (`philosophy` Props 3.1, 3.2) [proved; computed `c6`]: deductively equivalent formulations keep their prior odds forever; under the universal prior their weights differ by $O(1)$ whatever the number $k$ of further laws; a listing code without definitions recovers Kitcher's answer with a gap growing in $k$; derivation charges penalise only the mixed theory; interventional questions separate causal theories with one datum. The asymmetry is recovered from simplicity only for a listing code, like the textbook point.

### H14. Limits, identification, and $\Th(\mathbb N)$

* **Limits** (`fundamentals` Props 8.1–8.4, 8.7) [proved]: complete decidable truths are identified up to equivalence; for incomplete truths two-valued data leave the prior restricted to the consistent extensions (the most probable limit class can be a strict extension), and three-valued data identify $[T^*]$ — the Bayesian shadow of Gold's text/informant distinction (Gold 1967, known in substance); random theories above $\Q$ are almost surely incomplete, and random labellers almost surely fail consistent guessing.
* **$\Th(\mathbb N)$** (`logic` Thm 6.1, Cor 6.2, Prop 6.3) [proved, given $\Omega_V$ random; exponent certified by an exact certificate, `c6` R4]: in a length-lexicographic enumeration, $L_A(D_n)\ge C'^{-\delta}n^\delta-K(n)-c$ with $\delta\ge0.0823$ for a stated syntax, and every true r.e. theory leaves $\gtrsim n^\delta/\log n$ of the first $n$ sentences undecided; whether $L_A=o(n)$ reduces to generic decidability and is open.

## 2. The philosophy: argument map, the Kepler example, limitations

*Source: `philosophy` §§1–6 (final record), which owns the assessments; the formal results are attached with their thread pointers and statuses. ### 2.1 Two problems

* **P1, what a theory does.** Candidates: predicting observations (the raw-data view behind Solomonoff induction); answering questions posed in some language, including why-questions (van Fraassen, ch. 5); explaining (Hempel–Oppenheim); solving problems (Laudan).
* **P2, what a theory is.** A program that prints or predicts observations; a set of sentences closed under consequence (the received view); a class of models (the semantic view: Suppes, van Fraassen); a core of models with intended applications (structuralism: Sneed; Balzer–Moulines–Sneed); a practice of exemplars and skills (Kuhn, Polanyi).

The axiom view answers P1 with "answering questions in a given language" and P2 with "a logical theory"; the raw-data view answers "predicting observations" and "a program".

### 2.3 The descriptive observation and the explanatory question (the stance agreed with the author)

* **Observation [assessment].** Textbook presentations of fundamental theories are *declarative*: laws, definitions, principles, models given by equations over mathematical structures; not procedures that print predictions. Two qualifications the paper must state: *declarative is not sentential* (physics texts write something closer to a class of models specified by equations; first-order axioms over "enough true analysis" are a reconstruction); and *theories in use have procedural parts* (methods attached to concepts, numerical schemes, perturbation series, simulation codes; in quantum chemistry a "level of theory" is a computational method; even deriving a Mars position from K12 is root-finding for Kepler's equation).
* **Question [open].** Why that shape is good, and whether an ideal inducer should produce it. Craig rules out an answer based only on what is representable (H1). Any answer must be relative to a reasoner with costs, or to a community of reasoners. The best formal answer available (H3) says: in its model, what matters is a reasoner that can do algebra or chain, plus intermediate concepts — *not* logical form as such. The paper should say this, and say that the explanation of declarative shape may lie in communication and piecemeal checking (L11), for which there is no formal setting yet.
* **A correction of the brief's own argument (S4), not the author's claim.** "A program computes one direction cheaply" is true of a particular program, not of the class: a program may contain a generic solver at $O(1)$ extra length, and a straight-line program answers all directions of parity with $O(m)$ operations (`shape` Thm 4.3(2), Thm 1.11).

### 2.4 Hempel's dilemma and its three replies, against the formal results

* *Impracticality* (Craig's replacement is unmanageable). A theorem, but only about Craig's construction and run-padded axiomatisations under charged derivations (H4(ii)); vocabulary as such is eliminable at polynomial cost (H4(iii)). This corrects the brief's seed.
* *Inductive systematisation* (Hempel; the debate surveyed by Niiniluoto 1972). In the universal mixture with free deduction, theoretical terms add no inductive power among observational sentences beyond a constant factor (H4(i)): the reply fails there. It survives in corrected form where something theoretical pays (finite theories; charged derivations), and there what pays is relations or entities, not vocabulary (H4(iv), (v)). Contrast: in Hintikka–Tuomela's monadic inductive logic theoretical concepts do change inductive probabilities [known, abstract level]. Hempel's own definition was not checked against his text; the statement is about the mixture, not a single theory.
* *Aims beyond prediction* (Putnam 1965). The question-answering view is a partial version of this reply: theories are scored on the questions they answer, which may be about unobservables only if the base language contains them.

### 2.5 What formally separates SAI from raw-data prediction (the honest form of claim P)

(`philosophy` §1.7; numbering as there.)

**Advantages for claims D and E.**
1. *Data type.* Labelled sentences of a chosen language; questions are sets of sentences. (Definition.)
2. *Logical coherence.* Every consequence of the data gets posterior probability 1; in the semimeasure convention consistent SOI never becomes certain of an unasked consequence (its undefined mass at $\chi$ stays $\ge2^{-K(\chi)-c}$, `sfi` Prop 6.1(c)); $\Bel$ is closed under conjunction up to additive error and monotone under entailment (`philosophy` Prop 2.5(d)). [proved]
3. *Partiality.* "Independent" is a native third value; the semimeasure defect is its mass; for a $k$-way question the unanswered mass and the answer masses sum to 1 (`fundamentals` Prop 2.1; `philosophy` Prop 2.1). [proved]
4. *An observed "independent" label separates theories from labellers* by an unbounded amount on one datum, coin-free and one-tape, above $\Q$ (H7). [proved]
5. *Laws are sentences with belief values*, and $\Bel_D(\forall x\varphi)$ differs from "all future data conform" (the ω-gap, H9). [proved]
10. *Question answering costs no more description than raw modelling plus an extractor, and can cost far less* (`philosophy` Props 2.4, 2.2). [proved]

**Neutral differences.**
6. *Piecemeal confirmation.* Credence in individual laws ($\Bel_D(\mathrm{K3})$) although only whole theories meet the data — the standard Bayesian treatment of Duhem–Quine (Dorling; Strevens's objection), inherited with its debate; the allocation of blame is fixed by the prior, hence by the syntax. [definitions; assessment; Dorling and Strevens unverified here]
8. *Truth from unprovability* (H9, `logic` Prop 4.1(b)): a curiosity of arithmetic data, not progress in what a theory is or does. [proved]
11. *Explanatory direction needs questions the language can pose* (flagpole, `philosophy` Prop 3.1(c)). [the toy proved; the generalisation an assessment]

**Liabilities.**
7. *Random theories, false theories.* On the lottery stream bounded loss is achieved only by random theories; in true arithmetic with true data the limit posterior lives on false theories (H2). So the paper should say nothing realist about induced theories. [proved; a possibility result with adversarial data]
9. *Language-relative simplicity, bounded.* Faithful translations change weights by $2^{\pm(K(\tau)+c)}$ (`fundamentals` Thm 4.4); within that bound the base language decides real choices (the Mars pool in the noise-free regime, H11). Best-system accounts face the same relativity (Lewis treats it as a problem and appeals to natural properties; Cohen–Callender accept it); the relation is an analogy, since best systems are a metaphysics of laws and SAI is inference from finite data. [proved; computed; assessment]

**What does not separate them:** the inference principle (a Bayes mixture over a universal class) and, in the idealised setting, the shape of hypotheses.

### 2.6 The worked example: Kepler's laws, the Mars pool, and what Newton adds

The example runs through the philosophy part of the paper (§5.5). Its formal content:
* **The language** (`philosophy` §4.1). Sorts bodies and reals; heliocentric data language $\mathcal L_{\rm obs}$ (longitude, latitude, distance), its direction-only part $\mathcal L_{\rm dir}$, and a geocentric language $\mathcal L_{\rm geo}$ (a modern idealisation of Tycho's: the geometric plan94 positions, without aberration or light time); background $B$ = real-closed-field axioms with $\sin,\cos,\pi$ and enough true analysis (a sound decidable fragment; the full theory with $\sin$ is undecidable); theoretical vocabulary: positions and the elements $a,e,i,\Omega,\varpi,M_0,P$. K12 (via Kepler's equation; with the bridge to geocentric directions); K3: $P(b)^2a(b')^3=P(b')^2a(b)^3$; elements as numerals or intervals (initial conditions); data are true interval sentences "the direction of $b$ at $t$ lies within $\delta$ of $v$"; questions are covers by such sentences and structural questions (planarity, periodic return, equal areas).
* **What the laws say, and in which language** (H11): K3 has no content in heliocentric directions, content in geocentric directions, and, given the other elements, is worth exactly the size element $\rho$: 11.3–14.7 bits at 8′–1′. Compression alone does not select K3 at Kepler's precision in any of three codes computed. K3 is a law about initial conditions, worth what it saves on the elements of the bodies it relates (`laws` Prop 5.5); this depends on Kepler's kinematic parametrisation; in Newton's theory K3 is a consequence of the one law that does the other work.
* **How wrong Kepler is** (H11; scout P's scripts): errors grow with the domain; K3's constant differs between planets at $10^{-3}$, which Newtonian mechanics explains.
* **The proof-requiring posterior over the historical Mars pool** (H11): compatibility refutes circle, eccentric, equant, vicarious hypothesis, ellipse in the historical order; in the noise-free regime the syntax decides among compatible forms; with graded fit the data decide within tens of observations except one longitude-only step; distances eliminate the circle-based vicarious hypothesis syntax-free. The reconstruction is heliocentric, against plan94, and the pool is hand-picked (discovery is not modelled).
* **What Newton adds** (`philosophy` §4.5) [assessment, with computed figures]: no fewer parameters, a longer law, and no measurable gain on planetary longitudes at Kepler-era precision over 20 years; the gain is unification across domains (Moon test, tides, comets, satellite systems, the mass-dependent K3 constant), corrections inside the old domain (Mercury's perihelion: Newtonian perturbations about 531″ of about 574″ per century, the rest relativity [known: Clemence 1947, secondary]; the great inequality), new questions in the old language (Neptune) and in a new one (masses are $T$-theoretical, ladder step 3). Newton's axioms do not entail Kepler's laws; with masses and initial conditions they entail corrected statements and honest tolerance forms (`laws` Prop 7.1 is the provable toy). A formal derivation of one such bound needs an existence theorem and a validated perturbation estimate: the computational gap of the textbook point in a concrete case. Most of this is the achievement of 150 years of Newtonian mechanics, not of Newton alone.
* **Approximate laws** (H8): over deterministic theories PR rewards the false exact law; inside $M_A$ an approximately true law is a law with a calibrated error model; under rounding every form in the pool is eventually refuted.

### 2.7 The limitations of the axiom view

(`philosophy` §5. Each is "partially addressable" with its formal handle, or "a genuine limit" of the declarative view.)
* **L0. Statistical confrontation of theory with data** (promoted to a headline limitation). In physics the default contact is statistical: theory plus instrument and noise models assign likelihoods, often to near-raw data; statistical mechanics, quantum mechanics and population genetics answer with probabilities. SAI's proof-requiring core has no native noise model; graded fit enters only through randomised theories (honest bands and calibrated error models inside $M_A$, `laws` Props 1.8, 3.6a; Gaussian samplers, `philosophy` §4.4 Part A′), the generative variant (`fundamentals` Prop 9.1, partly a proof sketch), or sentences about probabilities. *Partially addressable; the paper must say that the proof-requiring core is the noise-free special case.*
* **L1. Written theories are far from sufficient for a simple reasoner** (the textbook point; the author's central limitation; agreed). Three gaps: *(a) the bridge gap* (idealisations and bridge principles the theory does not contain; priced as the extractor's $K(g)$ in `philosophy` Prop 2.4; partially addressable); *(b) the computational gap* (methods, lemmas, approximation schemes; a theorem in `shape`'s model, H3, visible only when the written theory is charged; SAI's program-length prior cannot see it); *(c) the tacit gap* (perceptual and manual skills, exemplars; a genuine limit). The refined claim the paper should make: "insufficient for a simple reasoner" is a property of the pair (theory, reasoner) and of the vocabulary, when what is written is what is charged.
* **L2. Approximate truth.** Kepler's laws as stated are false; partially addressable and largely done (H8); no canonical metric (purpose-relative); global truthlikeness is known to be hard (Popper's definition fails; the similarity approach needs a metric); our notion is deliberately local (question, tolerance, domain).
* **L3. Domains of applicability, several models, inconsistent theories in use.** A sound formal device exists (honest tolerance forms, propagated; `laws` Prop 4.3), but it neither explains nor reproduces practice: worst-case propagation is vacuous over long chains and for chaotic dynamics, and the rigorous domain of the Boltzmann equation (Lanford's theorem, unverified here) is far narrower than the domain of correct practice. In SAI a domain is usually a tolerance with an error model varying with the system, not a sharp boundary (`laws` Prop 6.1). Knowing when a model applies is a recognizer: a genuine limit. Globally inconsistent theories get weight 0 (classical electrodynamics of point charges has been argued inconsistent; disputed).
* **The condensation essay's list** (`philosophy` §5.4). Captured: answering questions; inferential relations as constitutive (meaning postulates; Ramsey–Lewis); compositionality of contents; concepts built from pointers (reference-fixing axioms; Le Verrier's "Neptune"); one notion with different recognizers (which *supports* the axiom view: identity fixed by inferential role or reference). Partly: support for learning (a global, static mixture, not concept-local); softness and meaning change (sphere/spheroid via honest tolerances; metaphorical extension not modelled); analogy (partial interpretations; rewarded insofar as it compresses, $K(T_1\cup\tau[T_1])\le K(T_1)+K(\tau)+c$; finding analogies not modelled); new concepts from old (definitional extensions: $O(1)$ in the universal class, about $(k-1)\ell$ symbols in a literal code); methods attached to concepts (mathematical methods as lemmas for a bounded reasoner; Craig's construction writes run times in unary); natural types (sorts and arities). Not captured: recognizers; purposes; clarification; action-facing concepts; "concepts are inert alone" is captured *as a limitation* (H3).
* **L4–L12** (briefly): science predicts very particular things and builds the set-ups that make questions answerable (half captured; the making of phenomena a genuine limit); concept formation and language change (step 3 partly, step 4 a genuine limit); discovery is not selection (SAI is an ideal of evaluation; a genuine limit); theory-laden and fallible data (partially addressable; one mislabelled datum forces an exception axiom); purposes and technology (genuine limit); science as a social structure (genuine limit; possibly where the explanatory question is answered; open); the mathematics is free and huge (charging $B$ changes little in the universal setting, `fundamentals` Prop 4.3, and much under charged derivations; with free deduction, checking $B$-decided instances confirms nothing, so induction in mathematics needs a costly deducer, `logic` Prop 4.6).
* **Verdict** (`philosophy` §5.5) [assessment]: the axiom view captures the static, declarative, inferential side of theories (contents, inferential relations, composition of contents, global learning by weighing axioms, pointers, partiality, approximate truth in honest or error-model form). It misses the procedural side (recognizers, methods, the search for derivations, action), the statistical side in its proof-requiring core (L0), and the dynamic side (purposes, softness, clarification, meaning change, discovery, the social process). The paper offers a model of the declarative core of scientific theories, and the procedural side reappears formally as the reasoner- and vocabulary-relativity of H3.

### 3.5 How to read the table (for the paper)

* The equivalence is version by version: coin-free with coin-free (Craig), one-tape with one-tape (no Craig needed), Kolmogorov with Kolmogorov. The earlier slogan "axiom induction ≡ consistent function induction" must be qualified accordingly (`sfi` §0).
* It controls semimeasure losses, not the meaning of "undefined": SOI's $\bot$ mixes logical independence with laziness (S2), and with "independent" as an observed label the equivalence is one-sided (S3).
* Penalising the axiom side changes nothing (E7–E9); penalising the labeller side makes the relation one-sided, and the gap is the free deduction of SAI (S5–S7). Charging deduction turns SAI into certificate SOI (E10), a nondeterministic notion; none of the readings charges finding a derivation.
* The natural (non-diagonal) separation is Presburger arithmetic (S8–S10), and once deduction is charged its strength depends on the reading.
* The content of SAI beyond SOI lies in logically structured data (E6): on literals they coincide; separations need closure (S2), observed independence (S3), consistency constraints of unbounded arity (S11) or deduction that is free (S5–S9).

### 4.2 Slogans of the earlier report, and their status now

* "Axiom induction ≡ consistent function induction" (AI-paper §6, abstract): true **version by version** (coin-free, one-tape, Kolmogorov), for semimeasure losses, with "≡" meaning within $2^{\pm c}$; one-sided once "independent" is an observed label (H1, H7). The schema proof route needs two sorts or a sound assigner.
* "Time-penalising axiom checking or generation leaves the collapse intact; penalising consistent function induction makes axiom induction strictly stronger" (abstract): true, and now read through Prop E.15 / `sfi` Prop 7.6: the strict superiority is the free deduction of SAI, which unpenalised consistent SOI also has through a constant-size evaluator. It does not show that axioms are better hypotheses than programs.
* "Written derivation length prices the collapse" (abstract): true in the sense of the sandwich (certificate SOI); but no reading charges the *search* for derivations, so the charged-deduction results do not model the textbook point (`shape` Thm 5.2; `sfi` Rem 8.5).
* Claude-authored overclaims corrected in this round (not claims of the author's): "SAI is strictly stronger than every time-bounded function inductor" (refuted already in TF); "random axiom systems drawn by a universal machine are new" (withdrawn: the modified Demski prior; `fundamentals` §10, `logic` §7); the earlier report's sampling stage is likewise not new.

## 6. Open problems

*Collected from the threads' final records, with the partial results they rest on. The five marked ★ are the ones the discussion section should name.*

### 6.1 Fundamentals and versions
1. ★ **Does $M_A$ dominate every lower semicomputable provability function?** (`fundamentals` Q6, Prop 11.4.) Partial domination with slack $K(S)$ is proved; on random literal sets Q6 is equivalent to P1's Question 3.14 for binary labels. Open in general.
2. **Does the random tape help for whole theories?** (`fundamentals` Q2, Prop 11.5.) $\Unif_A[T]\asymp\mm_\equiv(T)$ and $K_\equiv\le-\log\mm_\equiv+O(1)$ are equivalent to the question whether randomness helps to enumerate a set (a coding theorem for enumeration complexity, constant 1 against Solovay's 3 and Vereshchagin's 2). For true $\Pi_1^*$-axiomatised theories the extension version holds up to $K(k)$ (`logic` Prop 3.7); the exact-class version is open there too.
3. **Normalised order dependence on logically independent data** (`fundamentals` Thm 11.1 remarks): on positive literals of a fresh predicate this is P1's Question A.8 in another guise; the transfer principle holds only up to constants, which do not control normalised conditionals.

5. Is $W_A$ lower semicomputable when consistency of finite sets is decidable? (`sfi` §15.) Is $M_F^{\rm cons}/M_F^C$ bounded in general (pathwise against machine-wise consistency)? (`sfi` Prop 6.8(d).)

## 7. "Possible error in the author's view" notes that survived review

**None survived.** No note is passed on to the author, and none should appear in the paper.

*What was raised and how it was resolved* (for the record; the brief asks that a disagreement be raised only when it has a decent probability of being right):
1. **"i don't think any nice 'formal criterion' is going to be right?"** (Hänni's working note on the domain of applicability of a scientific model.) Raised by scout N2 (§7 there): a sound formal criterion exists (state approximations with tolerances and propagate them; `laws` Prop 4.3). Kept by `philosophy`'s development record; **withdrawn** in its final record after its referee A (issue 7): the note's natural reading is explanatory and descriptive (what explains how practice avoids catastrophic reasoning is not a formal criterion), and on that reading the author is right — the sound device neither explains nor reproduces practice (worst-case propagation is vacuous over long chains; Lanford's rigorous domain for the Boltzmann equation is tiny compared with the domain of correct practice). *What the paper says:* the sound device exists and is worth stating; it blocks the catastrophe but does not say how to choose a model, a domain or a tolerance, and would forbid much correct practice (`philosophy` §5.3).
2. **"there is really nothing fundamentally right about these information-theoretic losses"** (the condensation essay). Raised by scout P (§1.8 there): grading a prediction for use is purpose-relative, but updating beliefs by likelihood is not arbitrary (the log score's uniqueness). **Withdrawn** in `philosophy`'s final record: the essay's context is grading predictions of bounded models for a purpose; the counter-claim that the log score is privileged is itself contested (accuracy-first epistemology argues for the Brier score; conditionalisation is justified for strictly proper epistemic utilities generally); and for bounded or misspecified models different proper losses select different best-in-class models, which is the essay's point. *What the paper says:* the purpose enters by fixing which propositions are data; that is all (`philosophy` §2.2 remark). `logic` Prop 3.11 is a formal example of convention dependence of a log-loss verdict.
