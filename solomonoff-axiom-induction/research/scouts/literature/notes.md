# Scout L (literature and novelty): the formal neighbours of axiom induction

Folder: `research/scouts/literature/`. Deliverables: this file and `refs.bib` (verified entries only, each with a comment saying how it was verified). Written 2026-10-15.

Notation follows the other scouts. **AI** is axiom induction in general. **M_A** is the one-tape proof-requiring law of scout F (`../fundamentals/notes.md` §2): a universal theory machine reads a random tape and enumerates an axiom set A_ω, and M_A(D) = Pr_ω[B ∪ A_ω is consistent and proves every labelled datum of D]. **W_AI** is the coin-free version of the earlier report (deciders p of axiom sets, weight 2^(−|p|)). **Bel_D(φ)** is the posterior probability that the random theory proves φ; the trichotomy is (Bel_D(φ), Bel_D(¬φ), the rest). **SOI/SFI** is Solomonoff operator (function) induction; older records say SFI. **The earlier report** is `../axiom-induction` (labels such as `thm:time:equiv` are its LaTeX labels; claims ledger `../axiom-induction/paper/CLAIMS.md`).

## 0. Summary

**What is closest.** No published work found defines a Solomonoff-style mixture over axiom systems whose data are sentences labelled true or false and whose compatibility relation is provability. Five lines of work come close, each from a different side:

1. **Demski (2012), "Logical prior probability".** A prior over first-order theories "similar in nature to the priors of algorithmic probability": random sentences are added one at a time if consistent with those already chosen. This is the nearest prior work on the side of priors. It differs from M_A in three ways:
   * its random theory is complete, and its law is a probability on sentences, with no "independent" outcome;
   * a theory's description cost is a product of sentence weights, not the length of a program enumerating the axioms, so a schema gets no short-description credit;
   * it conditions on sentences being true and has no proof-requiring compatibility. See §3.2 and Observation L2.
2. **Shapiro's Model Inference System (1981; 1991 chapter).** Hypotheses are finite sets of clauses that must derive the positive facts and not derive the negative ones, within a computable resource bound h. The system identifies such theories in the limit. This is the nearest learning-theoretic precedent of the proof-requiring inducer and of the earlier report's derivation-budget readings. It outputs one hypothesis at a time, with no prior and no probabilities (§3.3).
3. **Inductive logic programming with compression.** The normal ILP setting requires the hypothesis to entail the positive examples and not the negative ones. Muggleton's compression measures and Conklin and Witten's (1994) model complexity and proof complexity are MDL scores for logic programs. They are precedents of the generative, derivation-likelihood variant: encoding the data by their proofs (§3.4).
4. **Bayesian theory learning in cognitive science.**
   * Kemp, Goodman and Tenenbaum (2008); Kemp et al. (2010); Ullman, Goodman and Tenenbaum (2012).
   * Theories are sets of logical laws generated by a probabilistic grammar, in a hierarchical Bayesian model with stochastic search.

   This is the same design as the earlier report's template prior plus derivation likelihood, on finite hypothesis spaces. The earlier report does not cite it; the new paper must (§3.5).
5. **Probabilities on sentences:**
   * Gaifman (1964); Scott and Krauss (1966); Gaifman and Snir (1982);
   * Hutter, Lloyd, Ng and Uther (2013); Christiano (2014);
   * logical induction (Garrabrant et al. 2016), and Sawin and Demski's Π1/Π2 limitation.

   These assign probabilities to sentences, not to axiom systems. The sharpest formal difference is Observation L1 [proved]: M_A's belief function violates the Gaifman condition, by a margin of at least 2^(−|p|) for a short program p. This is exactly the earlier report's Bayesian ω-gap. Hutter et al. impose the Gaifman condition, and logical induction cannot satisfy it (§3.2, §4).

Other close neighbours:
* **Random axioms.** Bienvenu, Romashchenko, Shen, Taveneaux and Vermeeren (2014) add randomly chosen true axioms "C(x) > n" to PA. Such axioms prove no new interesting theorems in the probabilistic sense, but can shorten proofs unless NP = PSPACE. This bears on two seed ideas, randomised axiom systems and theories for bounded reasoners (§3.7).
* **Machine discovery of logical theories.** The Apperception Engine (Evans et al. 2021) builds symbolic causal theories, not next-datum predictors. Khardon and Roth's (1997) "learning to reason" acquires question-answering competence without an explicit theory, which bears on the textbook point.

## 2. The reference points

What every comparison below is measured against (the definitions are scout F's and the earlier report's):

* **Data.** Finite sets of sentences of a fixed first-order language L, each labelled true or false. They are unordered; the predictor is queried on any sentence.
* **Hypotheses.** Axiom systems given by programs:
  * W_AI uses deciders, with prior 2^(−|p|);
  * M_A uses enumerators reading a random tape.
* **Compatibility.**
  * *Proof-requiring*: B ∪ A is consistent, proves each true datum, and refutes each false one.
  * *Consistency-requiring*: B ∪ A ∪ D is consistent.
  * *Generative*: the earlier report's derivation-grammar likelihood L1 on finite template theories, under which data are sampled theorems.
* **Prediction.** The trichotomy: proved, refuted, or independent, with the independent mass kept (the semimeasure convention).
* **Relation to SFI.** Untimed, W_AI is coin-free SFI restricted to consistent labellers, up to a constant factor (Craig's trick; `thm:time:equiv`). M_A is one-tape SFI restricted to consistent sampled labellers (scouts F and S).

## 3. The formal neighbours, area by area

Each entry gives what the work does and the precise difference from the axiom inducers above. Bib keys are those of `refs.bib`.

### 3.1 Universal induction (Solomonoff, Levin, Hutter)

* **solomonoff1964formal** [X: scout P; B]. Prediction of observation sequences by a universal prior over programs. *Difference.*
  * Data form a sequence; AI's data are an unordered set of labelled sentences with logical relations among them.
  * Hypotheses are programs; AI's are axiom systems.

  Untimed, the second difference disappears up to O(1) by Craig's trick. What remains is the consistency restriction and the logical closure of the predictions (scout S, §2.6).
* **solomonoff1978complexity** [B]. The convergence theorem for computable measures. [S: Hutter's 2003 open-problem paper describes the result as convergence of the universal posterior to the true one with probability 1.] It is the template for the loss bounds of scout F (§3.5).
* **solomonoff1999two, solomonoff2008three** [X: SFI bib, read in full there; B for 2008 also here: Computer Journal 51(5):566–570, doi 10.1093/comjnl/bxm120]. *Operator induction*: predict the answer to a new question from question–answer pairs; unordered sets.
  * *Difference.* Answers are strings with a conditional probability density. There are no logical relations among questions.
  * AI is operator induction in which the questions are sentences, and in which deduction ties the answers together: φ and ¬φ, φ ∧ ψ, consequences.
* **zvonkin1970complexity** [B, A] and **levin1973universal** [B, A: Math-Net abstract].
  * Zvonkin–Levin is the survey of Kolmogorov complexity and the universal semimeasure.
  * Levin's 1973 paper introduces universal sequential search problems. The paper's text, hosted on Fortnow's site and quoted in the search results, names "searching for proofs of limited length" among them [A].
  * Relevance: the Levin-style time penalties (Kt) of the earlier report's §6.
* **hutter2005universal** [B] and **rathmanner2011philosophical** [B, A].
  * The treatise argues for Solomonoff induction as a solution to the problem of induction and discusses the black ravens paradox and confirmation [A].
  * Per the search summary [S], it remarks that hypotheses play the role of logical expressions: probability 0/1 corresponds to false/true, and intermediate values to statements true in some models and false in others.
  * *Difference.* Logic appears as a limiting case of probability on observation sequences. No induction over axiom systems is defined.
* **hutter2007universal** [X: earlier report; A]. The universal prior has no zero-prior problem: it confirms universal hypotheses.
  * *Relation.* The earlier report's `thm:univ:confirm` is the countable-class analogue, and it already cites this.
  * The difference is the ω-gap: what is confirmed is "all future data are instances", not "the axioms prove ∀xφ" (Observation L1).
* **hutter2010complete** [B, A]. A complete theory of everything = an objective theory (a program) plus a subjective observer process. Without the observer's location, predictive power tends to zero [A]. *Relevance.*
  * This is the program view of theories in its purest published form.
  * Its split into an objective theory and the observer's location is a version of the seed "laws versus initial conditions" (§5.1, seed 5).
* **lattimore2011universal** [X: SFI bib; B] and **bauwens2026solomonoff** [B, A: arXiv 2610.18632, submitted 13 Oct 2026].
  * The setting is Solomonoff induction on selected bits and online classification.
  * Bauwens and Kuznetsov show three things [A]:
    * the total error over a computable pattern's positions can diverge;
    * the sum of squared errors is bounded, exponentially in the pattern's program length;
    * for bits fixed by a computable classification function with a computable time bound, the bound is polynomial.
  * *Relevance.* This is the universal-induction setting closest to SOI's question–answer data, and a recent paper the SOI papers may also want to cite. *Difference from AI.* Positions carry no logical relations.
* **leike2015solomonoff** [X], **leike2015computability** [X: SFI bib]. Nicod's criterion fails for Solomonoff induction; the computability levels of Solomonoff induction.
* **sterkenburg2016solomonoff** [B, A], **sterkenburg2019putnam** [B, A], **sterkenburg2026solomonoff** [B, A: arXiv 2603.20274, a preliminary version].
  * Solomonoff's simplicity measure is "better perceived" as an assumption of effectiveness, and it does not justify Occam's razor (2016).
  * Putnam's diagonal argument defeats the Solomonoff–Levin attempt at a universal learning machine, with a generalised diagonalisation (2019, 2026).
  * *Relevance.* The earlier report's diagonal separation (`thm:time:diagonal`) is a member of this family. The philosophy section should cite Sterkenburg wherever it leans on Occam justifications.
* **li2008introduction** [X: SFI bib]. The standard textbook for the universal semimeasure, prefix complexity and the coding theorem. **chaitin1975theory** [X: SFI bib] is the prefix-complexity paper.
* **vitanyi2000minimum** [B, A]. Ideal MDL derived from Bayes with Kolmogorov complexity, and the "fundamental inequality" for when it applies. Relevant to the Kolmogorov version K_A, the shortest consistent axiomatisation proving the data.
* **schmidhuber2002speed**, **filan2016loss** [X: SFI bib]. Time-penalised universal priors. Relevant to the time-bounded variants.
* **An informal antecedent, not for the bibliography.** A LessWrong post by "cousin_it", "Using the universal prior for logical uncertainty", is marked *retracted* [S]. It proposed feeding the universal prior one bit per PA sentence, set when the sentence is proved or refuted. That is SFI on sentences. It was retracted because the claim that the prior picks up all computable regularities was unproved [S]. The same post reports that Demski's 2012 paper describes a "naive way to use the universal prior for logical uncertainty" and its problems [S].

### 3.2 Probabilities on sentences

* **gaifman1964concerning** [X: earlier report, scout P; B].
  * Probabilities on the sentences of a first-order language are measures on models.
  * The *Gaifman condition*: P(∀xφ) = inf_n P(φ(t_1) ∧ … ∧ φ(t_n)) over closed terms, cited as in the earlier report's universal track, Prop U15.
  * *Difference.* Gaifman's P is a coherent probability. AI's Bel is a probability of provability, a belief function in Shafer's sense (scout F §2.4). It keeps an independent share and violates the Gaifman condition by design (Observation L1).
* **scott1966assigning** [B, A: JSL review description]. A set-theoretic bridge between measures on Boolean algebras and probabilities of logical formulas. Same difference as Gaifman.
* **hintikka1966two** [B; S: Britannica's summary].
  * In Carnap's λ-continuum, strict universal generalisations in an infinite universe have prior probability 0, and no evidence raises it.
  * Hintikka puts positive prior probability on *constituents* instead.
  * *Relevance.* The zero-prior problem is the classical background of the ω-gap. AI avoids it on the theory side, since theories proving ∀xφ have positive weight, but reproduces it on the provability side: instances do not move the odds of provers against schemas.
* **gaifman1982probabilities** [X: scout P; B].
  * Probabilities over rich languages containing empirical predicates and arithmetic, and randomness relative to a language.
  * [R] Its convergence theorem: for almost every world, the posterior of each sentence in the evidence σ-algebra converges to its truth value. The content was not verified this session; the search could not retrieve the abstract.
  * [S] A 2026 arXiv paper (2606.00363, "At the Edge of Putnam's Program"; not read) calls the framework "the first major advance in developing an inductive logic satisfying Putnam's conditions".
  * *Difference.* The prior is an arbitrary coherent measure, not a simplicity prior over theories.
* **gaifman2004reasoning** [B, A]. Assigning probabilities to arithmetical statements under limited deductive capacity. Relevant to seed 4 (bounded reasoners).
* **hacking1967slightly** [B, A] and **garber1983old** [B; S].
  * Hacking: Bayesianism for agents who cannot compute logical consequences; the far-off digit of π.
  * Garber: solve old evidence by conditioning on "H entails E".
  * *Relevance.* Proof-requiring AI assumes logical omniscience about its own hypotheses (free deduction). The deduction-charged variants of the earlier report's §6 relax it.
* **demski2012logical** [B, A; S for the construction]. "A Bayesian prior over first-order theories is defined. It is shown that the prior can be approximated, and the relationship to previously studied priors is examined." (Springer abstract, quoted from the search result.) BibBase gives the aim as a prior "similar in nature to the priors of algorithmic probability".
  * **The construction** [S: Sawin–Demski's and Hahn's descriptions]: repeatedly draw a random sentence, weighted by length, and add it unless it contradicts, or (in one description) already follows from, the sentences chosen so far. P(φ) is the probability that φ ends up in the theory. Exact computation needs a consistency (halting) oracle. The prior is approximable in the limit.
  * **Relation to M_A.** This is the closest prior work. The differences, made precise in Observation L2:
    * *Description cost.* Demski charges each sentence separately, so a theory given by an infinite decidable schema gets no short-description credit. M_A charges the length of a program that enumerates the axioms, so a schema costs O(1) beyond its generator.
    * *Completeness.* Demski's random theory is complete with probability 1, and its law is a probability on sentences. M_A's random theory is r.e. and almost surely incomplete above Q (scout F, F27), and M_A keeps the independent outcome.
    * *Data.* Demski conditions on sentences; AI uses proof-requiring compatibility, under which false data must be refuted.
    * *Computability.* Demski's process needs an oracle to run. M_A's theory machine needs none to define, only to evaluate the consistency event (M_A(∅) = 1 − Inc_0 is right-c.e.; scout F, F3).
  * An uploaded summary claims the logical prior "is exponentially dominant over the Solomonoff distribution when applied to sequence prediction" [S, unreliable]. It is not to be cited without reading the paper.
* **sawin2013computable** [B: MIRI technical report, released December 2014, from a July 2013 workshop; A].
  * Computable probability distributions that converge to believing true Π1 sentences must assign arbitrarily low limiting probability to some short true Π2 sentences, given plausible coherence properties [A].
  * Garrabrant et al. paraphrase it [S]: non-dogmatism, Gaifman inductivity and weak coherence admit no computable approximation.
  * *Relevance.* This is the sharpest known limit for the seed "learning theories in the limit" on Th(ℕ)-type data. Any computable approximation of an AI posterior that learns Π1 truths inherits it.
* **christiano2014nonomniscience** [B, A: MIRI announcement]. A tractable algorithm for assigning probabilities to first-order sentences and updating them on observations. Logical consistency is relaxed for bounded reasoners, and "mathematical theories" are treated "as latent structure constraining physical observations".
  * *Relevance.* The latent-theory view is AI's.
  * *Difference.* The aim is tractability. Per the abstract, the object is a probability on sentences, not a mixture over axiom systems. The details were not verified.
* **hutter2013probabilities** [B, A, S].
  * Given probabilities of some sentences (a knowledge base), assign probabilities to all sentences of a higher-order logic.
  * The desiderata include consistency with the knowledge base, reduction to deductive logic at 0/1, the Gaifman condition, and the Cournot condition (satisfiable sentences get positive probability). Gaifman is inconsistent with strong Cournot and consistent with a weaker form.
  * Probabilities that are Gaifman and Cournot "allow learning in the limit". A minimum-relative-entropy probability relative to a Gaifman–Cournot prior exists [A, S].
  * *Difference.* The object is a probability on sentences extending given probabilities, with Gaifman imposed. AI's object is a mixture over axiom systems with a code-length prior and a belief-function trichotomy, and it violates Gaifman (Observation L1). Hutter et al. answer "how probable is φ?"; AI answers "how probable is it that the right axioms prove φ?".
* **garrabrant2016logical** [B, A; S for the theorem numbers] and **garrabrant2017formal** [B: TARK 2017, EPTCS 251:221–235].
  * A logical inductor is a computable sequence of belief states over the sentences of a theory Γ, defined so that no polynomial-time trader with finite risk earns unbounded profit.
  * Its limit P_∞ is coherent: a probability measure on the completions of Γ (Thm 4.1.2) [S]. It dominates the universal semimeasure in the limit (Thm 4.6.5), strictly (Thm 4.6.6) [S].
  * Probabilities of Γ-independent sentences stay bounded away from 0 and 1 [S]. It is not Gaifman-inductive (Desideratum 13), because of Sawin–Demski [S].
  * *Difference.* The two treat complementary uncertainties.
    * A logical inductor is *given* the axioms Γ, through a deductive process, and is uncertain about their consequences under bounded computation.
    * AI is given labelled sentences and is uncertain about the axioms, with deduction free.

    So Γ is the input of LI and the output of AI. The earlier report's deduction-charged readings sit between the two.
  * The domination of the universal semimeasure is the LI analogue of scout S's transfer principle: AI equals SFI on positive literals up to constants.
* **garrabrant2016inductive** [B, A]. *Inductive coherence*: a strengthening of coherence that constrains finite approximations, with an algorithm satisfying it.
* **carnap1950logical** [X: scout P]. The ancestor of all of the above: probabilities on the sentences of a formal language.

### 3.3 Formal learning theory of theories and inquiry

* **gold1965limiting** [B, A] and **putnam1965trial** [B; pages 49–57 per a Crossref-derived citation, 49–58 per a dealer listing].
  * Gold introduced *limiting recursive* functions and sets: decided by a procedure whose guesses are eventually correct and constant.
  * Putnam introduced *trial-and-error predicates*.
  * [R] By the Shoenfield limit lemma, these are the Δ2 sets.
  * *Relevance.*
    * Δ2 marks what can be learned with finitely many mind changes on arithmetic.
    * Th(ℕ) is not Δ2, so no limiting procedure decides it. AI on Th(ℕ) data has unbounded loss (scout F, F26) and keeps no consistent r.e. theory (`prop:pa:thn`).
    * M_A(∅) is right-c.e., hence Δ2.
* **gold1967language** [X]. Identification in the limit, from text against from informant. Scout F's limits (F25) are the AI analogue: two-valued data from an incomplete theory leave the prior on its consistent extensions, while three-valued data identify it.
* **shapiro1981algorithm** [B, A], **shapiro1991inductive** [B: DBLP, 199–254], **shapiro1983algorithmic** [X].
  * The *Model Inference Problem*: from facts about an unknown model, infer a theory. Machine learning and program synthesis from examples are instances.
  * The algorithm is incremental, "based on Popper's methodology of conjectures and refutations" (quoted from the IJCAI abstract). A Prolog implementation infers Horn theories.
  * The algorithm "can identify in the limit any model in a family of complexity classes", with the complexity bound given by a computable h on ground atoms [A, S].
  * *Relation.* This is the nearest learning-theoretic precedent of proof-requiring AI and of derivation-budget readings:
    * a hypothesis is a finite axiom set;
    * it must derive the positive facts;
    * it must not derive the negative facts within the resource bound.
  * *Differences.*
    * It identifies (outputs one conjecture), rather than mixing.
    * The order is that of enumeration and refinement, with no prior weights.
    * Negatives are handled by non-derivation (closed world), where proof-requiring AI demands refutation.
    * The language is Horn clauses.
* **osherson1986systems** [B]; **osherson1986identification** [B, A]; **osherson1991universal** [B, A]; **osherson1992universal** [B, A].
  * Learning in the limit of first-order sentences over models.
  * There is a computable "formal scientist", in a first-order framework, that is universal: it solves every problem any scientist can solve. No regular logic extending first-order logic and satisfying Löwenheim–Skolem admits such universal scientists (1991, abstract).
  * A "simple" universal method (1992).
  * *Relation.* Their universality is the non-probabilistic counterpart of M_A's dominance (scout F, F4): universality over solvable problems against domination of every theory-machine law. Their success criterion is convergence to the truth value of a target sentence in every model of a background theory, not loss bounds.
* **martin1998elements** [X: scout P; A]. Formal learning theory extended to a logical, model-theoretic setting with belief revision, explicitly as an alternative to Bayesian accounts [A].
* **kelly1996logic** [X], **kelly1989convergence** [B, A], **kelly1995computable** [B; S].
  * Kelly–Glymour define convergence to the truth for first-order theories (two criteria).
  * Kelly–Schulte show that a universal hypothesis can be refutable with certainty by an effective procedure even though no program derives its predictions. They use Hilbert and Bernays' implicit definability of arithmetical truth [S: the authors' opening pages].
  * *Relevance.* The theory's content in its intended models outruns what any program computes, and what the theory proves. A proof-requiring inducer credits a theory only with its theorems. This is the ω-gap again, seen from learning theory, and it bears on the "what a theory is" debate. A theory is more than its r.e. consequences only through its intended models, which AI's compatibility does not see.
* **lauth1993inductive** [B, A]. Learning functions for first-order languages; several types of convergence and identification in the limit.
* **mukouchi1995towards** [B; S]. *Refutable* inference: identify the target in the limit if it lies in the hypothesis space, otherwise refute the whole space in finite time. Rich spaces are refutably inferable from informant, very few from text [S].
* **angluin1980inductive** [X].

### 3.4 Inductive logic programming

* **plotkin1970note** [X]. Least general generalisation, the lineage of the axiom-schemas report's anti-unification.
* **muggleton1988machine** [B, A]. Inverting resolution to *invent* predicates (CIGOL), so the learner is not limited to the teacher's vocabulary. This is the standard early reference for predicate invention: theoretical terms in ILP.

* **muggleton2015meta** [B, A].

* **cropper2022inductive** [B]. The current survey ("ILP at 30").
* **sharma2025learning** [B, A]. Bayesian ILP learning minimum-message-length hypotheses from noisy data. It outperforms MDL-based ILP and learns from positive examples alone [A].

### 3.5 Bayesian theory learning in cognitive science

* **goodman2008rational** [B, A]. Bayesian inference over a concept language of logical rules generated by a grammar (rational rules).
* **kemp2008theory** [B, A: CogSci 2008]. Intuitive theories are represented in a compositional language of thought, and theory learning in such a language "tends to favour theories with short descriptions" [A].

* **tenenbaum2006theory** [B partly: July 2006 and TiCS confirmed, volume and pages from the query only; A]. Theory-based Bayesian models.
* **tenenbaum2001generalization** [X]. The size principle.
* **chater2003simplicity** [B, A]. Simplicity as a unifying principle in cognitive science.
* *Relation and difference.* The earlier report's model (finite template theories, a prefix-code prior, a branching-process grammar over bodies, a derivation-grammar likelihood) follows the same design as Ullman, Goodman and Tenenbaum, and that report does not cite them. The new paper should.

### 3.7 Chaitin, and the complexity of theories and of random axioms

* **chaitin1974information** [B; S].
  * A formal system cannot prove K(x) > n for n much larger than the system's own complexity.
  * **raatikainen1998interpreting** [B, A]: the constant in Chaitin's theorem depends on the coding of machines, not on the theory's complexity, so "the received view is false".
  * **vanlambalgen1989algorithmic** [B, A]: a critical discussion of the claim that algorithmic information theory sheds new light on Gödel's first incompleteness theorem.
  * **calude2005complexity** [arXiv math/0408144; A; journal version not confirmed]. With a suitable measure (δ-complexity), theorems cannot be much more complex than the theory, and the probability that an n-bit statement is provable tends to 0.
  * *Relevance.* Do not write "a theory of k bits can explain at most k bits" as if Chaitin proved it. Scout F's loss bound in K_≡(T) (§3.5 there) is a different statement, about predictive loss, and is unaffected.
* **bienvenu2014axiomatic** [B, A, S].
  * Axioms asserting that randomly chosen strings are incompressible are added to PA "probabilistically". This "does not help us prove new interesting theorems", but "randomly chosen axioms may help make proofs much shorter (unless NP=PSPACE)" [A].
  * Adding *all* true statements "C(x) > n" proves every true Π1 sentence. One such statement per n, chosen carefully, suffices, while statements for most strings of length n give a weak theory [S].
  * *Relevance.* This is the nearest literature to two seeds: randomised axiom systems (random axioms add no deductive power with positive probability) and bounded reasoners (axioms buy proof-length).
* **solovay1977random** [B; S]: a set's enumeration complexity is at most about 3 times −log of the probability that a random program enumerates it. **vereshchagin2007kolmogorov** [X: scout F; abstract-level there]: factor 2 for finite sets.
  * *Relevance.* The Kolmogorov-versus-semimeasure question for axiom sets. There is no additive coding theorem for r.e. sets, only a multiplicative one. This is scout F's candidate 10.
* **deleeuw1956computability** [B; S]. A machine with a random tape enumerates a non-r.e. set only with probability 0. So random axiom sets are pathwise r.e. relative to their tape, and only r.e. sets get positive mass in M_A.

## 4. Three formal comparisons

**Observation L1 (M_A violates the Gaifman condition; the ω-gap as a margin) [proved].**

*Setting.*
* Let L contain a constant 0, a unary function symbol S and a unary predicate R. Let B = ∅, or any decidable B with a model whose R and S behave as below.
* Write Bel_0(ψ) := Pr_ω[B ∪ A_ω is consistent and B ∪ A_ω ⊢ ψ], the prior probability of provability under M_A.
* Let A := {R(S^k 0) : k ∈ ℕ}, and let p_A be a self-delimiting program for the theory machine that ignores its tape and enumerates A.

*Claim.* lim_n Bel_0(R(0) ∧ R(S0) ∧ … ∧ R(S^n 0)) − Bel_0(∀x R(x)) ≥ 2^(−|p_A|). The limit exists because the sequence is non-increasing. The same holds for the posterior Bel_D after any data D that A is compatible with (for example, any finite set of instances R(S^k 0) labelled true), with margin 2^(−|p_A|)/M_A(D).

*Proof.*
1. Let E_n be the event that B ∪ A_ω is consistent and proves R(S^k 0) for all k ≤ n. These events are measurable (each is an intersection of a Σ1 event with the consistency event) and decrease in n. So Bel_0(∧_{k≤n} R(S^k 0)) = Pr(E_n) decreases to Pr(E), where E := ∩_n E_n.
2. Let F be the event that B ∪ A_ω is consistent and proves ∀x R(x). Then F ⊆ E.
3. On the cylinder of tapes beginning with p_A, the universal theory machine runs p_A, so A_ω = A.
   * A is consistent: ℕ with R everywhere true is a model.
   * A proves every R(S^k 0).
   * A does not prove ∀x R(x): take the structure ℕ ⊔ {∗}, with S(∗) = ∗ and R true exactly on ℕ.

   So this cylinder, of probability 2^(−|p_A|), lies in E ∖ F. Hence Pr(E) − Pr(F) ≥ 2^(−|p_A|).
4. For the posterior, conditioning on the event of compatibility with D divides by M_A(D), and the cylinder stays inside it. ∎

*Reading.*
* This is the earlier report's ω-gap (`thm:univ:omega`) in the universal one-tape setting, stated as a failure of the Gaifman condition for the belief function. It needs only one short instance-only axiom set in the support.
* Hutter et al. (2013) impose the Gaifman condition on probabilities of truth. Logical inductors cannot satisfy it (Sawin–Demski). M_A violates it by design, because it tracks provability, not truth.
* The earlier report's universal track already separates the two (Prop U15, `prop:univ:gaifman`): for credences about *truth*, the Gaifman condition is the probabilistic ω-rule.
* For the paper: say that AI's beliefs are about provability, and so are exempt from Gaifman-type desiderata. Truth-directed credences are a separate object.

**Observation L2 (Demski's process against theory machines) [proof sketch; conditional on the secondary descriptions of Demski's construction in §3.2].**

*Setting.* Let μ be Demski's sentence distribution, assumed to have full support on Sent_L. Draw sentences i.i.d. from μ. Keep a drawn sentence iff it is consistent with B and the sentences kept so far (and, in one description, not already implied by them). Let T_∞ be the deductive closure of the kept sentences.

*Claims.*
* (a) T_∞ is a complete consistent theory with probability 1.
* (b) For every φ independent of B, P_Demski(φ) + P_Demski(¬φ) = 1, whereas M_A gives the independent outcome for φ probability at least 2^(−|p_∅|) > 0.
* (c) The acceptance step decides a Π1 property (consistency), so the process is computable relative to 0′. A theory machine of M_A needs no oracle; only the consistency event that M_A conditions on is Π1.
* (d) A theory axiomatised by an infinite decidable schema S gets no credit for the shortness of S's decider. Demski's process reaches it only by drawing (consequences of) its instances one at a time, so the probability that T_∞ ⊇ S depends on the sentence weights, not on |decider of S|.

*Sketch.*
* (a) Fix φ. With probability 1, φ is drawn at some step t. If it is rejected, the kept set S_t ∪ {φ} is inconsistent, so S_t ⊢ ¬φ. If it is accepted, φ is kept, or is already implied in the variant. Either way T_∞ decides φ. Countably many φ.
* (b) This follows from (a) for Demski. For M_A, the program p_∅ enumerating the empty set has a cylinder of probability 2^(−|p_∅|) on which A_ω = ∅, so B ∪ A_ω is consistent and decides neither φ nor ¬φ.
* (c) This is by inspection.
* (d) This is by inspection of the weights. It is a qualitative statement; no quantitative comparison is claimed. ∎

*Reading.* Demski's prior and M_A are both "algorithmic-probability-like priors over first-order theories", and they differ in the two places that matter for AI:
* M_A gives schemas their code-length credit, because hypotheses are programs.
* M_A keeps the independent outcome, because random theories are r.e. and incomplete.

The paper should cite Demski (2012) as the closest prior construction and state these two differences.

**Observation L3 (logical induction and AI are complementary) [assessment].**
* A logical inductor takes the axioms as given and prices their consequences under bounded computation. AI takes the consequences (labelled sentences) as given and weighs the axioms, with free deduction.
* They meet at two points:
  * (i) LI's domination of the universal semimeasure (its Thm 4.6.5) mirrors AI's equivalence with SFI on positive literals (scout S, Thm 2.7);
  * (ii) the deduction-charged readings of the earlier report (`thm:time:sandwich`) are the part of AI where LI-type concerns (costly deduction) enter.
* A combined object, a market or mixture over unknown axiom systems with costed deduction, was not found in the literature. It is a candidate research question, not a result.
