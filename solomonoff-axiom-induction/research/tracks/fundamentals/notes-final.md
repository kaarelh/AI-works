## 1. Definitions

### 1.1 Language, background, data

**Definition 1.1 (setting).**
* $\mathcal L$ is a countable first-order language with equality and computable syntax; $\Sent:=\Sent_{\mathcal L}$; $\vdash$ is classical first-order derivability in a fixed sound and complete calculus (derivation size plays no role in this record).
* The **background** $B\subseteq\Sent$ is decidable and consistent; default $B=\emptyset$. (An r.e. background already makes proof search from $B$ effective; decidability is a convenience, available without loss by Craig's trick.)
* $\Cn(\Gamma):=\{\varphi:B\cup\Gamma\vdash\varphi\}$. $\Gamma$ is *consistent* if $B\cup\Gamma$ is. $\varphi^1:=\varphi$, $\varphi^0:=\neg\varphi$. $\Gamma$ *decides* $\varphi$ if $B\cup\Gamma$ proves $\varphi$ or $\neg\varphi$. "$\Gamma$ proves $\varphi$" always means $B\cup\Gamma\vdash\varphi$.
* A **data set** is a finite partial function $D:\Sent\rightharpoonup\{0,1\}$ (label 1 = true); a **three-valued** data set maps into $Y_3=\{1,0,\iota\}$ ($\iota$ = independent). $D^\pm:=\{\varphi^{D(\varphi)}:D(\varphi)\in\{0,1\}\}$; $\psi_D:=\bigwedge D^\pm$ in a fixed canonical order ($\top$ if empty). $D$ is *consistent* if $B\cup D^\pm$ is. $D+(\varphi,y)$ is defined for $\varphi\notin\dom D$.
* A **reveal sequence** $(\varphi_t,y_t)_{t\le n}$ is *consistent* if $D_n:=\{(\varphi_t,y_t):t\le n\}$ is a partial function; $D_t$ are its prefixes. An infinite stream $D_\infty$ has $D_\infty^\pm:=\bigcup_nD_n^\pm$.
* **Conditionals.** For $\nu$ from data sets to $[0,1]$: $\nu(y\mid D,\varphi):=\nu(D+(\varphi,y))/\nu(D)$ for $\varphi\notin\dom D$ ($0$ if $\nu(D)=0$), and $\nu(y\mid D,\varphi):=\ind[y=D(\varphi)]$ for $\varphi\in\dom D$ (P1's repeated-input convention, P1 Def 3.1).

*Remark.* This is P1's data format with inputs $X=\Sent$. The data $(\varphi,1)$ and $(\neg\varphi,0)$ are different (different sentences) with the same content; Prop 2.1(c) shows that weights depend only on content. Scout F allowed non-functional data sets; with partial functions, a sentence with two labels is excluded by format rather than by weight $0$, and nothing else changes.

**Definition 1.1′ (fresh atoms; NOTATION-draft §3.4).** In the *literal setting*, $\mathcal L$ contains a unary relation symbol $R$ that does not occur in $B$, and closed terms $t_w$ ($w\in\{0,1\}^*$, for instance the word terms of NOTATION-draft §3.4) such that some model of $B$ interprets the $t_w$ as pairwise distinct elements. **Fresh atoms** are sentences $R(t_w)$ for *pairwise distinct* words $w$. (So $R(t_w)$ and $\neg R(t_w)$ are never two fresh atoms.) Lemma 6.2a gives the two facts used about them: every set of literals with at most one literal per word is consistent with $B$, and a consistent such set proves exactly its own members among the literals. Several results below (Props 7.2, 7.3, 8.3(b), 8.4, 11.2, Thms 6.2, 11.1(b), 11.3) are stated for fresh atoms.

### 1.2 Theory machines

**Definition 1.2 (theory machine).** A **theory machine** is a c.e. set $G\subseteq\{0,1\}^*\times\Sent$ ("after reading $w$ from the tape, emit $\chi$"). For a finite $w$ put $A^G_w:=\{\chi:(v,\chi)\in G,\ v\sqsubseteq w\}$, and for a tape $\omega\in\{0,1\}^\infty$ put $A^G_\omega:=\bigcup_nA^G_{\omega_{1:n}}$, the axioms emitted on $\omega$. Let
$$O^G_\chi:=\{\omega:B\cup A^G_\omega\vdash\chi\},\qquad C^G:=\{0,1\}^\infty\setminus O^G_\bot,\qquad E^G_D:=O^G_{\psi_D}\cap C^G .$$
* The **law** of $G$ is $\nu_G(D):=\Unif(E^G_D)$: the probability that the random theory is consistent and proves every datum.
* The **three-valued law** is $\nu^3_G(D):=\Unif(E^{3,G}_D)$ with $E^{3,G}_D:=E^G_D\cap\bigcap_{D(\varphi)=\iota}\big(\{0,1\}^\infty\setminus(O^G_\varphi\cup O^G_{\neg\varphi})\big)$: in addition, the theory decides no $\iota$-labelled sentence.
* $(G_i)$ is a fixed effective enumeration of the c.e. subsets of $\{0,1\}^*\times\Sent$; $K(G):=\min\{K(i):G_i=G\}$ and, for a law $\nu$, $K(\nu):=\min\{K(i):\nu_{G_i}=\nu\}$.

No consistency condition is imposed on $G$: emitting more axioms is always allowed, and consistency is a property of the output, a random event. This is the difference from P1's function machines (P1 Def 3.6), whose triples must be consistent.

**Lemma 1.3 (measurability and the basic form) [proved].**
(a) $O^G_\chi=\bigcup\{[w]:B\cup A^G_w\vdash\chi\}$ is an effectively open class, uniformly in an index of $G$ and in $\chi$; hence $\Unif(O^G_\chi)$ is left-c.e., uniformly.
(b) $C^G$ is closed (a $\Pi^0_1$ class), and $O^G_\bot\subseteq O^G_\chi$ for every $\chi$.
(c) $\nu_G(D)=\Unif(O^G_{\psi_D})-\Unif(O^G_\bot)$, and $E^{3,G}_D$ is Borel.

*Proof.* (a) If $B\cup A^G_\omega\vdash\chi$, a derivation uses finitely many axioms $\chi_1,\dots,\chi_r\in A^G_\omega$, each emitted under some prefix of $\omega$. The prefixes of $\omega$ are linearly ordered, so the longest of them, $w$, has $\chi_1,\dots,\chi_r\in A^G_w$, and every $\omega'\sqsupseteq w$ has $B\cup A^G_{\omega'}\vdash\chi$. Conversely $[w]\subseteq O^G_\chi$ whenever $B\cup A^G_w\vdash\chi$. The set of pairs $(w,\chi)$ with $B\cup A^G_w\vdash\chi$ is c.e. (enumerate $G$ and all derivations; $B$ is decidable). (b) $C^G$ is the complement of an effectively open class. An inconsistent set proves everything. (c) $E^G_D=O^G_{\psi_D}\setminus O^G_\bot$ with $O^G_\bot\subseteq O^G_{\psi_D}$; $E^{3,G}_D$ is $E^G_D$ intersected with finitely many closed classes. ∎

**Examples.** (i) A deterministic r.e. theory $\mathsf W_e$: $G=\{(\epsilon,\chi):\chi\in\mathsf W_e\}$, with $\nu_G(D)=\ind[B\cup\mathsf W_e\text{ consistent and }\vdash\psi_D]$. (ii) Fair-coin facts on a family of sentences $(\alpha_j)_{j\ge1}$: $G=\{(w,\alpha_j^{w_j}):|w|=j\}$. (iii) A theory plus coin facts: the union of (i) and (ii). (iv) A finite mixture of finite theories $T_1,\dots,T_r$: $G=\{(w_j,\chi):\chi\in T_j\}$ for a prefix-free set $\{w_1,\dots,w_r\}$, giving $T_j$ weight $2^{-|w_j|}$. These weights need not sum to $1$: a tape with no prefix among the $w_j$ emits nothing, so its theory is the empty one (consistent, since $B$ is). The finite stand-ins of `checks/` are laws of this kind; since the revision, their priors put the remaining mass on the empty theory explicitly.

### 1.3 The universal theory machine and SAI

**Definition 1.4 (proof-requiring SAI).**
* $G_U:=\{(pw,\chi):p\in\dom V,\ (w,\chi)\in G_{V(p)}\}$: read a self-delimiting program, then run that machine on the rest of the tape. It is c.e. Write $A_\omega:=A^{G_U}_\omega$, $O_\chi:=O^{G_U}_\chi$, $C:=C^{G_U}$, $E_D:=E^{G_U}_D$, $E^3_D:=E^{3,G_U}_D$.
  * If $\omega=p\omega'$ with $p\in\dom V$, then $A_\omega=A^{G_{V(p)}}_{\omega'}$: a pair $(p'w,\chi)\in G_U$ with $p'w\sqsubseteq\omega$ has $p'\in\dom V$ comparable with $p$, so $p'=p$ by prefix-freeness, and $w\sqsubseteq\omega'$. If $\omega$ has no prefix in $\dom V$, then $A_\omega=\emptyset$.
* **SAI** (unqualified) is $M_A:=\nu_{G_U}$; $M_A^3:=\nu^3_{G_U}$; $L_A:=-\log M_A$; $\Inc_0:=\Unif(O_\bot)$, the probability that the random theory is inconsistent.
* The **posterior** after $D$ with $M_A(D)>0$ is the law of $A_\omega$ under $\Unif(\cdot\mid E_D)$ (three-valued: under $\Unif(\cdot\mid E^3_D)$).
* **Trichotomy.** $\Bel_D(\chi):=\Unif(E_D\cap O_\chi)/\Unif(E_D)$, the posterior probability that the random theory proves $\chi$; $\Dis_D(\chi):=\Bel_D(\neg\chi)$; $\Indep_D(\chi):=1-\Bel_D(\chi)-\Dis_D(\chi)$.
* **Prior probability of provability.** $\Bel_0(\chi):=M_A(\{(\chi,1)\})=\Unif(O_\chi)-\Inc_0$. It is *not normalised*: $\Bel_0(\top)=1-\Inc_0$. Its normalisation is $\widehat\Bel_0:=\Bel_0/(1-\Inc_0)$, and $\widehat\Bel_0=\Bel_\emptyset$ (the posterior after the empty data set, $E_\emptyset=C$). $\Pl_0(\chi):=\Bel_0(\top)-\Bel_0(\neg\chi)$.
* **Class probabilities.** For a theory $T$: $\Unif_A[T]:=\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn(T)\}$ and $\Unif_A(\Ext T):=\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(A_\omega)\supseteq\Cn(T)\}$.

By Prop 2.1: $M_A(D)=\Bel_0(\psi_D)$, $M_A(b\mid D,\varphi)=\Bel_D(\varphi^b)$ and $M_A^3(\iota\mid D,\varphi)=\Indep_D(\varphi)$ for $\varphi\notin\dom D$.

### 1.4 Conventions and the two defects

**Definition 1.5 (loss conventions).**
* *Semimeasure convention* (primary): the cumulative loss of a consistent reveal sequence is $\sum_{t\le n}-\log M_A(y_t\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)=L_A(D_n)-L_A(\emptyset)$ (Prop 2.1(d)).
* *Normalised convention*: each conditional is divided by $\Bel_D(\varphi)+\Dis_D(\varphi)$. Upper bounds proved in the semimeasure convention hold here too (each normalised conditional is at least the raw one); lower bounds do not transfer, and the loss depends on the order (Thm 11.1).
* *Three-valued convention*: labels in $Y_3$, scored by $M_A^3$, whose conditionals sum to $1$ (Prop 2.1(f)).
* The *consistency-requiring* predictor of §7 has its own loss, $\sum_t-\log\Bel^{\rm nc}_{D_{t-1}}(\varphi_t^{b_t})$ (Prop 7.2).

**Remark 1.6 (two defects).** $M_A$ has a *global* defect $1-M_A(\emptyset)=\Inc_0>0$ and a *local* defect $\Indep_D(\varphi)$ at each query. P1's $M_F$ has only the local one ($M_F(\emptyset)=1$), because function machines are consistent by definition. The correspondence with P1 is: "proves $\varphi$" is $\Sigma_1$ in the tape, like "halts with output 1"; "independent" is $\Pi_1$, like "diverges"; "inconsistent" corresponds to "halts with both outputs", which a function machine cannot do. Hänni's working notes considered keeping the semimeasure (rather than renormalising or splitting 50/50); the AI-paper (Exs 2.15, 2.16, Prop 2.17) shows that the two alternatives are incoherent, and this record follows the semimeasure convention.

### 1.5 Coin-free and Kolmogorov versions

**Definition 1.7.** For a data set $D$:
* $W_A(D):=\sum_{p\in\dom V}2^{-|p|}\ind[B\cup\mathsf W_{V(p)}\text{ consistent and }\vdash\psi_D]=\sum_e\mm(e)\,\ind[\cdots]$ (enumerators; coin-free);
* $K_A(D):=\min\{|p|:p\in\dom V,\ B\cup\mathsf W_{V(p)}\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D\}$ ($\infty$ if none; Kolmogorov version);
* $W_A^{\rm dec}$, $K_A^{\rm dec}$: the same over *deciders*, $p\in\dom V$ such that $V(p)$ is an index of a total $\{0,1\}$-valued function $d$, read as the axiom set $\{\chi:d(\chi)=1\}$ (AI-paper Def 6.2, there written $W_{\rm AI}$);
* $W_A^{{\rm gen},t}$: the same over enumerators of the delay form of TF Def 1.1 (P2), for $t$ as in Prop 1.8(b);
* $K_{\rm fin}(D):=\min\{K(\psi):\psi\in\Sent,\ B\cup\{\psi\}\text{ consistent},\ B\cup\{\psi\}\vdash\psi_D\}$ and $W_{\rm fin}(D):=\sum_\psi\mm(\psi)\ind[\cdots]$ (finite axiomatisations).

**Proposition 1.8 (versions agree; finite axiomatisations do not).** There is $c$ such that for every $D$:
(a) **[proved]** $2^{-c}W_A^{\rm dec}(D)\le W_A(D)\le2^{c}W_A^{\rm dec}(D)$ and $|K_A(D)-K_A^{\rm dec}(D)|\le c$;
(b) **[proved, given TF Lemma 2.1 and Thm 2.2(b), under TF's assumption (S)]** for $t$ time-constructible, nondecreasing and superadditive with $t(m)\ge m$ (TF Def 1.1) and $t(m)\ge m^{a^*}$ (TF Thm 2.2): $2^{-c}W_A(D)\le W_A^{{\rm gen},t}(D)\le2^cW_A(D)$, and likewise for the Kolmogorov versions;
(c) **[proved]** $K_A(D)\le K_{\rm fin}(D)+c\le K(\psi_D)+c$ if $D$ is consistent; if $D$ is inconsistent, $K_A(D)=K_{\rm fin}(D)=\infty$ (while $K(\psi_D)$ is finite);
(d) **[proved]** $K_{\rm fin}-K_A$ is unbounded: in the language $\{R\}\cup\{c_j:j\in\mathbb N\}$ ($R$ unary, $c_j$ constants), $B=\emptyset$, the data sets $D_n:=\{(R(c_j),1):j<n\}\cup\{(\exists x\neg R(x),1)\}$ have $K_A(D_n)\le c$ and $K_{\rm fin}(D_n)=\Kpad(n)\pm c$, where $\Kpad(n):=\min_{N\ge n}K(N)$ is nondecreasing and unbounded.

*Proof.* (a) *Deciders to enumerators.* By universality of $V$ there is $r_1$ with $V(r_1p)$ an index of the r.e. set $\{\chi:\{V(p)\}(\chi)=1\}$ whenever $p$ is a decider. The map $p\mapsto r_1p$ is injective, adds $|r_1|$ to the length and preserves the axiom set. *Enumerators to deciders (Craig's trick).* Write $\chi^{\wedge1}:=\chi$, $\chi^{\wedge(m+1)}:=\chi\wedge\chi^{\wedge m}$. For an index $e$ fix the step-by-step enumeration of $\mathsf W_e$ and let $A^C_e:=\{\chi^{\wedge(s+1)}:\chi\text{ is enumerated at step }s\}$. A sentence $\theta$ has at most two readings $\theta=\chi^{\wedge m}$: $(\chi,m)=(\theta,1)$, and, if $\theta$ is a conjunction, $\chi$ its left conjunct with $m$ the number of times $\chi$ can be stripped off (if $\theta=\chi^{\wedge m}$ with $m\ge2$, then $\chi$ is the left conjunct, and stripping stops exactly at $\chi$ because $\chi$ is not of the form $\chi\wedge X$). So $A^C_e$ is decided by: find the readings, and for each run the enumeration for exactly $m-1$ steps and check whether $\chi$ appears at step $m-1$. This halts on every input, also when $\mathsf W_e$ is finite. $\chi^{\wedge m}$ is logically equivalent to $\chi$, so $\Cn(A^C_e)=\Cn(\mathsf W_e)$. A fixed $r_2$ gives $V(r_2p)$ = an index of this decider for $e=V(p)$; the map is injective and adds $|r_2|$. Both maps preserve consistency and what is proved, so summing over programs and taking shortest programs gives (a) with $c:=\max(|r_1|,|r_2|)$. This is the argument of AI-paper Thm 6.3 (proof in its App. E.1), here between two kinds of axiom hypotheses rather than between axiom and labeller hypotheses.
(b) TF Thm 2.2(b) proves $2^{-c}W_{\rm FI}\le W_{{\rm AI}^{\rm gen}_t}\le2^cW_{\rm FI}$ with $W_{\rm FI}$ the coin-free consistent-labeller mixture ($W^{\rm cons}$ here), and AI-paper Thm 6.3 proves $W_{\rm FI}\asymp W_{\rm AI}=W_A^{\rm dec}$; combine with (a). Directly: an enumerator $p$ of $A$ is mapped to TF's padded enumerator $e_q$ of the semi-decider $q$ of $A$ ("yes on $\chi$ iff $\chi$ is enumerated"); TF Lemma 2.1(a),(c),(d) give $\Cn(\mathrm{Pad}(q))=\Cn(A)$, the delay form, and $|e_q|\le|p|+c$ injectively. The converse inclusion is trivial (a polynomial-delay enumerator is an enumerator). The translation between TF's $U$-programs and our $V$-programs printing indices costs a constant. TF proves Lemma 2.1 under its assumption (S) on efficient self-simulation; (S) was not re-derived here.
(c) If $\psi$ witnesses $K_{\rm fin}(D)$, a fixed prefix $r_3$ turns a shortest program for $\psi$ into a program for an index of $\{\psi\}$, consistent with $B$ and proving $\psi_D$; so $K_A\le K_{\rm fin}+|r_3|$. For consistent $D$, $\psi:=\psi_D$ is a candidate for $K_{\rm fin}$. If $D$ is inconsistent, no consistent set proves $\psi_D$.
(d) $T:=\{R(c_j):j\in\mathbb N\}\cup\{\exists x\neg R(x)\}$ is r.e. with an index of complexity $O(1)$ and is consistent (domain $\mathbb N\cup\{*\}$, $c_j\mapsto j$, $R:=\mathbb N$), so $K_A(D_n)\le c$. *Upper bound on $K_{\rm fin}$:* for $N\ge n$ with $K(N)=\Kpad(n)$, the sentence $\bigwedge_{j<N}R(c_j)\wedge\exists x\neg R(x)$ is consistent, proves $\psi_{D_n}$, and has complexity $\le K(N)+c$. *Lower bound:* let $\psi$ be consistent with $\psi\vdash\psi_{D_n}$. If some $c_j$, $j<n$, does not occur in $\psi$, then $\psi\vdash R(c_j)$ and the theorem on constants give $\psi\vdash\forall xR(x)$, which contradicts $\psi\vdash\exists x\neg R(x)$ and the consistency of $\psi$. So $\psi$ mentions $c_0,\dots,c_{n-1}$, and $N(\psi):=1+\max\{j:c_j\text{ occurs in }\psi\}\ge n$ is computable from $\psi$; hence $K(\psi)\ge K(N(\psi))-c\ge\Kpad(n)-c$. $\Kpad$ is nondecreasing by definition and unbounded because only finitely many $N$ have $K(N)\le k$. ∎

So finite axiomatisability is a genuinely different simplicity measure: a simple r.e. theory can do a job for which every single sentence must name $n$ objects. *Credit and coordination.* The same $\Kpad$ phenomenon appears in scout N1's result 3 and in `terms` Thm 6.2(b): on the data "there are at least $n$ things" (pure equality), every consistent finite monadic theory has $K_{\rm fin}=\min_{m\ge n}K(m)\pm c$, while one unary function symbol gives $O(1)$. Prop 1.8(d) is the version with named objects; the paper should state the separation once, with `terms` Thm 6.2 as the main instance (it also shows what a theoretical function symbol buys) and Prop 1.8(d) as a one-line variant.

### 1.6 Complexities of theories

**Definition 1.9.** For an r.e. theory $T$ (NOTATION-draft §3.3): $[T]:=\{e:\Cn(\mathsf W_e)=\Cn(T)\}$; $K_\equiv(T):=\min_{e\in[T]}K(e)$ (complexity up to deductive equivalence); $\mm_\equiv(T):=\sum_{e\in[T]}\mm(e)$; $\Ext(T):=\{e:B\cup\mathsf W_e\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(\mathsf W_e)\supseteq\Cn(T)\}$ and $\mm(\Ext T):=\sum_{e\in\Ext T}\mm(e)$. The one-tape versions $\Unif_A[T]$, $\Unif_A(\Ext T)$ are in Def 1.4. **If $B\cup T$ is consistent**, then $[T]\subseteq\Ext T$, so $2^{-K_\equiv(T)}\le\mm_\equiv(T)\le\mm(\Ext T)$, and $\Unif_A[T]\ge2^{-c}\mm_\equiv(T)$, $\Unif_A(\Ext T)\ge2^{-c}\mm(\Ext T)$ (Thm 4.1(i)). The hypothesis is needed: for $T=\{\bot\}$, $\Unif_A[T]=0$ and $\Ext T=\emptyset$, while $\mm_\equiv(T)\ge2^{-K_\equiv(T)}>0$.

### 1.7 Variants

**Definition 1.10.**
* **Consistency-requiring (CR), 0/1** (Hänni's "do not contradict"; AI-paper $S_{\rm nc}$): $M_A^{\rm nc}(D):=\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\cup D^\pm\text{ consistent}\}$. Its posterior is the law of $A_\omega$ given that event; its predictor uses the hypothesis plus the data: $\Bel^{\rm nc}_D(\varphi):=\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\cup D^\pm\text{ consistent and }\vdash\varphi\}/M_A^{\rm nc}(D)$, $\Dis^{\rm nc}_D(\varphi):=\Bel^{\rm nc}_D(\neg\varphi)$.
* **Graded CR, grade ½**: for a consistent $A$, $\nu^{1/2}_A(D):=\ind[B\cup A\cup D^\pm\text{ consistent}]\prod_{\varphi\in\dom D}g_A(\varphi)$ with $g_A(\varphi):=1$ if $B\cup A\vdash\varphi^{D(\varphi)}$ and $\frac12$ otherwise; $M_A^{1/2}(D):=\int_{C}\nu^{1/2}_{A_\omega}(D)\,d\Unif(\omega)$. This is the *symmetric form* of the AI-paper's graded score $S_g$ (AI-paper Def 2.12) with $g\equiv1$ on proved data and $g_\infty=\frac12$: $S_g$ grades only the true-labelled data $D_+$, while $\nu^{1/2}$ grades false-labelled data too, through $\varphi^{D(\varphi)}$. The results of §7.3 and §11.2 use only true-labelled data, where the two agree.
* **Generative** (AI-paper L1, Def 2.9): a theory $A$ has a law $P_A$ on sentences (derivation grammar with citation weights on the axioms); data are unlabelled sentences $X_1,X_2,\dots$; the universal law of data streams is $\Xi(X_{1:n}):=\int\prod_iP_{A_\omega}(X_i)\,d\Unif(\omega)$, with the sampling coins read from separate parts of the tape. Details and results in §9.

## 2. Structure (target T2)

**Proposition 2.1 (structure) [proved; computed: `c1` A0–A4].** Let $G$ be a theory machine (in particular $G_U$).
(a) $\nu_G(D)>0$ implies that $D$ is consistent. $M_A(D)>0$ iff $D$ is consistent; $M_A^3(D)>0$ iff $D$ is consistent and $B\cup D^\pm$ decides no $\iota$-labelled sentence.
(b) For a two-valued $D$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$:
$$\nu_G(D+(\varphi,1))+\nu_G(D+(\varphi,0))+\nu^3_G(D+(\varphi,\iota))=\nu_G(D).$$
So $\nu_G$ is a weak semimeasure on data sets (P1 Def 3.2), and for $M_A$ the defect at $\varphi$ divided by $M_A(D)$ is $\Indep_D(\varphi)$. By (g), $\nu_G$ is moreover a (sub-probability) containment functional (P1 Def 3.4), a strictly stronger property (P1 Ex 3.5, and Prop 7.3(e) below for the graded weight).
(c) $\nu_G(D)$ depends on $D$ only through $\Cn(D^\pm)$. If $B\cup D^\pm\vdash\varphi^b$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$, then $\nu_G(D+(\varphi,b))=\nu_G(D)$ and $\nu_G(D+(\varphi,1-b))=0$: entailed data cost nothing and contradicting data are fatal.
(d) *Order independence.* For every consistent reveal sequence, in any order and with repeats, $\sum_{t\le n}-\log M_A(y_t\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)=L_A(D_n)-L_A(\emptyset)$, which depends only on $\Cn(D_n^\pm)$.
(e) $0<M_A(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0<1$.
(f) For a three-valued $D$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$, $\sum_{y\in Y_3}\nu^3_G(D+(\varphi,y))=\nu^3_G(D)$, and $\nu^3_G(\emptyset)=\nu_G(\emptyset)$. So the conditionals of $M_A^3$ are probability distributions on $Y_3$.
(g) On the consistency event $C^G$ put $\mathrm{tri}^G_\omega(\varphi):=1$ if $B\cup A^G_\omega\vdash\varphi$, $0$ if $B\cup A^G_\omega\vdash\neg\varphi$, and $\iota$ otherwise. Then $\nu^3_G(D)=\Unif\{\omega\in C^G:\mathrm{tri}^G_\omega\supseteq D\}$ and, for two-valued $D$, $\nu_G(D)=\Unif\{\omega\in C^G:\mathrm{tri}^G_\omega\supseteq D\}$. So, when $\nu_G(\emptyset)>0$, $\nu^3_G/\nu_G(\emptyset)$ is the containment functional (P1 Def 3.4) of the random total function $\mathrm{tri}^G_\omega:\Sent\to Y_3$ under $\Unif(\cdot\mid C^G)$, and $\nu_G/\nu_G(\emptyset)$ is that of the random partial function obtained by reading $\iota$ as "undefined". For $G_U$ write $\mathrm{tri}_\omega:=\mathrm{tri}^{G_U}_\omega$ (the trichotomy function of the random theory; NOTATION-draft reserves $f_\omega$ for $f^{F_U}_\omega$).

*Proof.* (a) If $D$ is inconsistent, a set proving $\psi_D$ is inconsistent, so $E^G_D=\emptyset$. If $D$ is consistent, the deterministic machine $G_D:=\{(\epsilon,\psi_D)\}$ has $\nu_{G_D}(D)=1$, so $M_A(D)\ge2^{-K(G_D)}>0$ by Thm 4.1. Three-valued: $B\cup\{\psi_D\}$ decides exactly what $B\cup D^\pm$ decides, so if $B\cup D^\pm$ decides no $\iota$-labelled sentence then $\nu^3_{G_D}(D)=1$; conversely, if $B\cup D^\pm$ decides an $\iota$-labelled sentence, so does every set proving $\psi_D$.
(b) $O^G_\chi$ depends only on the $B$-equivalence class of $\chi$, and $\psi_{D+(\varphi,b)}$ is equivalent to $\psi_D\wedge\varphi^b$. On $E^G_D$ the random theory is consistent, so exactly one of three things happens: it proves $\varphi$, it proves $\neg\varphi$, it decides neither. The three events are $E^G_{D+(\varphi,1)}$, $E^G_{D+(\varphi,0)}$ and $E^{3,G}_{D+(\varphi,\iota)}$. For $G_U$, divide by $\Unif(E_D)$: the third term is $1-\Bel_D(\varphi)-\Dis_D(\varphi)$.
(c) $B\cup A\vdash\psi_D$ iff $\Cn(D^\pm)\subseteq\Cn(A)$. If $B\cup D^\pm\vdash\varphi^b$, then $\Cn(D^\pm\cup\{\varphi^b\})=\Cn(D^\pm)$, and $D+(\varphi,1-b)$ is inconsistent, so (a) applies.
(d) At a step with $\varphi_t\notin\dom D_{t-1}$ the factor is $M_A(D_t)/M_A(D_{t-1})$; at a repeat (necessarily with the stored label, the sequence being consistent) it is $1$ and $D_t=D_{t-1}$. The product telescopes to $M_A(D_n)/M_A(\emptyset)$. If some $M_A(D_t)=0$, the first such step has factor $0$ and both sides are $+\infty$, by the monotonicity in (b). Dependence only on $\Cn(D_n^\pm)$ is (c).
(e) $M_A(\emptyset)=\Unif(C)$. Tapes beginning with a $V$-program for an index of the empty machine have $A_\omega=\emptyset$, consistent since $B$ is. Tapes beginning with a program for $\{(\epsilon,\bot)\}$, with $\bot:=\neg\forall x\,x=x$, are inconsistent. Both sets have positive measure.
(f) As (b), with $E^{3,G}_D$ in place of $E^G_D$; $\nu^3_G(\emptyset)=\Unif(C^G)=\nu_G(\emptyset)$.
(g) By definition, $E^{3,G}_D=\{\omega\in C^G:\mathrm{tri}^G_\omega\supseteq D\}$, and for two-valued $D$ this is $E^G_D$. ∎

**Proposition 2.2 (proof-requiring updating is geometric conditioning) [proved; computed: `c1` A5–A7; the names of the rules: known (secondary sources), §14].**
(a) For every $D$ with $M_A(D)>0$ and every $\chi$: $\Bel_D(\chi)=\Bel_0(\psi_D\wedge\chi)/\Bel_0(\psi_D)=\widehat\Bel_0(\psi_D\wedge\chi)/\widehat\Bel_0(\psi_D)$. This is the *geometric rule of conditioning* of the belief function $\widehat\Bel_0$ (Suppes and Zanotti 1977): keep the focal sets inside the conditioning event and renormalise.
(b) $\Bel_0$ and every $\Bel_D$ are totally monotone on the Lindenbaum algebra of $B$: for all $\chi_1,\dots,\chi_k$,
$$\Bel_D\Big(\bigvee_{i\le k}\chi_i\Big)\ \ge\ \sum_{\emptyset\ne I\subseteq[k]}(-1)^{|I|+1}\Bel_D\Big(\bigwedge_{i\in I}\chi_i\Big).$$
So $\Bel_D$ is a belief function in Shafer's sense, with random focal set $\mathrm{Mod}(B\cup A_\omega)$ and plausibility $\Pl_D(\chi)=1-\Bel_D(\neg\chi)$; $\Bel_0$ is the "probability of provability" reading of a belief function (Pearl 1988, ch. 9).
(c) There is a probability $P_D$ on the complete consistent extensions of $B$ (a Borel probability on the Stone space) with $\Bel_D(\chi)\le P_D(\chi)\le\Pl_D(\chi)$ for every $\chi$.

*Proof.* (a) A theory proves $\psi_D$ and $\chi$ iff it proves $\psi_D\wedge\chi$, so $E_D\cap O_\chi=O_{\psi_D\wedge\chi}\setminus O_\bot$, and both sides equal $\Unif(O_{\psi_D\wedge\chi}\setminus O_\bot)/\Unif(O_{\psi_D}\setminus O_\bot)$. The normalising constant cancels.
(b) Let $P:=\Unif(\cdot\mid E_D)$ and $Q_i:=O_{\chi_i}$. Then $\bigcap_{i\in I}Q_i=O_{\bigwedge_{I}\chi_i}$ (one tape: a theory proves each $\chi_i$ iff it proves their conjunction) and $\bigcup_iQ_i\subseteq O_{\bigvee_i\chi_i}$. Inclusion–exclusion for $P(\bigcup_iQ_i)$ gives the inequality. $\Bel_0$ is the unnormalised case ($P=\Unif|_C$).
(c) Fix an enumeration $(\sigma_k)$ of $\Sent$. For $\omega\in C$ let $T_0(\omega):=B\cup A_\omega$ and $T_{k+1}:=T_k\cup\{\sigma_k\}$ if that is consistent, $T_k\cup\{\neg\sigma_k\}$ otherwise; $T_\infty(\omega):=\bigcup_kT_k$ is a complete consistent extension (consistent by compactness; complete and deductively closed because it is maximal consistent). Each choice depends on whether $B\cup A_\omega\cup F$ is consistent for a finite $F$, i.e. on membership of $\omega$ in the open class $O_{\neg\bigwedge F}$, so $\omega\mapsto T_\infty(\omega)$ is Borel. Let $P_D$ be its law under $\Unif(\cdot\mid E_D)$. If $B\cup A_\omega\vdash\chi$ then $\chi\in T_\infty$; if $\chi\in T_\infty$ then $B\cup A_\omega\nvdash\neg\chi$. So $\Bel_D\le P_D\le\Pl_D$. ∎

(b) and (c) are the one-tape forms of AI-paper Props 2.18 and 2.19, which are stated there for countable classes; the proofs above do not need countability.

**Proposition 2.3 (consistency-requiring updating is Dempster conditioning) [proved; computed: `c1` A8; the name of the rule: known (secondary sources)].** Let $\psi:=\psi_D$ and $M_A^{\rm nc}(D)>0$. Then $M_A^{\rm nc}(D)=\Pl_0(\psi)$ and
$$\Bel^{\rm nc}_D(\varphi)=\frac{\Bel_0(\varphi\vee\neg\psi)-\Bel_0(\neg\psi)}{\Bel_0(\top)-\Bel_0(\neg\psi)},$$
which, written with $\widehat\Bel_0$, is Dempster's rule of conditioning $\widehat\Bel(\varphi\mid\psi)=[\widehat\Bel(\varphi\vee\neg\psi)-\widehat\Bel(\neg\psi)]/[1-\widehat\Bel(\neg\psi)]$ (Dempster 1967; Shafer 1976).

*Proof.* For consistent $B\cup A$: $B\cup A\cup\{\psi\}$ is consistent iff $B\cup A\nvdash\neg\psi$, and $B\cup A\cup\{\psi\}\vdash\varphi$ iff $B\cup A\vdash\varphi\vee\neg\psi$ (deduction theorem; $\psi$ is a sentence). So $M_A^{\rm nc}(D)=\Unif(C)-\Unif(C\cap O_{\neg\psi})=\Bel_0(\top)-\Bel_0(\neg\psi)$. Since $B\cup A\vdash\neg\psi$ implies $B\cup A\vdash\varphi\vee\neg\psi$, the numerator is $\Unif(C\cap O_{\varphi\vee\neg\psi})-\Unif(C\cap O_{\neg\psi})$. ∎

*Random-set reading.* With focal set $F_\omega:=\mathrm{Mod}(B\cup A_\omega)$: proof-requiring updating keeps the focal sets contained in $\mathrm{Mod}(\psi)$ and renormalises; Dempster updating replaces each $F_\omega$ by $F_\omega\cap\mathrm{Mod}(\psi)$, drops the empty ones and renormalises (the "cookie-cutter" description of Dempster's rule in the secondary literature, §14). In the first, a datum must be *explained* by the hypothesis; in the second it is *added* to the hypothesis.

## 3. Computability (target T3)

**Proposition 3.1 [proved].** $M_A(D)=\Unif(O_{\psi_D})-\Inc_0$, where $\Unif(O_{\psi_D})$ is left-c.e. uniformly in $D$ and $\Inc_0=\Unif(O_\bot)$ is left-c.e. (Lemma 1.3). The correction that turns the lsc function $D\mapsto\Unif(O_{\psi_D})$ (which is not a semimeasure: it gives an inconsistent theory credit for every datum) into $M_A$ is one real number.

**Theorem 3.2 (the inconsistency probability is an Ω-like real) [proved; a new application of the standard argument that $\Omega$ is Solovay complete, to the event "the random theory is inconsistent"].**
(a) $\Inc_0$ is not computable. Hence $M_A(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0$ is right-c.e. and not left-c.e., and $D\mapsto M_A(D)$ is not lower semicomputable.
(b) For every left-c.e. $\alpha\in[0,1]$ there are a $V$-program $p_\alpha$ and a left-c.e. real $\gamma_\alpha$ with $\Inc_0=2^{-|p_\alpha|}\alpha+\gamma_\alpha$. Consequently $\alpha\le_S\Inc_0$ for every left-c.e. $\alpha$: $\Inc_0$ is Solovay complete.
(c) **[proved, given Chaitin's theorem that $\Omega_V$ is Martin-Löf random and the Levin–Schnorr characterisation (known, standard; §14)]** $\Inc_0$ is Martin-Löf random.

*Proof.* *The machine $G_\alpha$.* Let $\alpha_s\uparrow\alpha$ be a computable nondecreasing sequence of rationals and $G_\alpha:=\{(w,\bot):\exists s\ 0.w+2^{-|w|}\le\alpha_s\}$, with $0.w$ the dyadic rational with binary digits $w$. Then $A^{G_\alpha}_\omega\subseteq\{\bot\}$, and $\bot\in A^{G_\alpha}_\omega$ iff some prefix $w$ of $\omega$ has $[w]\subseteq[0,\alpha_s]$ for some $s$, read as reals. This holds for every $\omega$ with $0.\omega<\alpha$ (take $s$ with $\alpha_s>0.\omega$ and a long prefix) and fails for every $\omega$ with $0.\omega>\alpha$. So the inconsistency probability of $G_\alpha$ is $\alpha$.
*The decomposition.* Let $p_\alpha$ be a $V$-program for an index of $G_\alpha$. Splitting tapes by their first block in $\dom V$ (tapes with no block have $A_\omega=\emptyset$, consistent),
$$\Inc_0=2^{-|p_\alpha|}\alpha+\gamma_\alpha,\qquad \gamma_\alpha:=\sum_{p\in\dom V\setminus\{p_\alpha\}}2^{-|p|}\Unif(O^{G_{V(p)}}_\bot),$$
and $\gamma_\alpha$ is left-c.e. because $\dom V\setminus\{p_\alpha\}$ is c.e. and each term is left-c.e. uniformly.
(a) Let $K_0$ be the halting set and $\alpha:=\sum_{n\in K_0}2^{-n-1}$, left-c.e.; its binary expansion is the characteristic sequence of $K_0$ (unique, since $K_0$ is infinite and co-infinite), so $\alpha$ is not computable. If $\Inc_0$ were computable, $\alpha=2^{|p_\alpha|}(\Inc_0-\gamma_\alpha)$ would be right-c.e.; being also left-c.e. it would be computable. A real that is left-c.e. and right-c.e. is computable, so $1-\Inc_0$ (right-c.e.) is not left-c.e.; as $M_A(\emptyset)$ is a value of $M_A$, $M_A$ is not lsc.
(b) *Solovay reducibility* (Solovay 1975; definition as in Downey–Hirschfeldt 2010): $\alpha\le_S\beta$ iff there are a constant $k$ and a partial computable $f$ on rationals such that for every rational $q<\beta$, $f(q)$ is defined, $f(q)<\alpha$ and $\alpha-f(q)\le2^k(\beta-q)$. Let $\beta:=\Inc_0$, with approximations $\beta_s:=2^{-|p_\alpha|}\alpha_s+\gamma_{\alpha,s}\uparrow\beta$. If $\alpha$ is computable, the reduction is trivial (given $q<\beta$, find $s$ with $\beta_s>q$ and output a rational in $(\alpha-(\beta_s-q),\alpha)$). Otherwise $\alpha_s<\alpha$ for all $s$; let $f(q):=\alpha_s$ for the first $s$ with $\beta_s>q$. Then, since $\gamma_\alpha-\gamma_{\alpha,s}\ge0$,
$$\alpha-\alpha_s=2^{|p_\alpha|}\big[(\beta-\beta_s)-(\gamma_\alpha-\gamma_{\alpha,s})\big]\le2^{|p_\alpha|}(\beta-\beta_s)<2^{|p_\alpha|}(\beta-q).$$
(c) Apply (b) with $\alpha:=\Omega_V$, and Lemma 3.3 with $k=|p_\alpha|$, $\gamma=\gamma_\alpha$. ∎

**Lemma 3.3 (randomness passes up) [known: Demuth 1975 (if $\alpha$ is left-c.e. and random and $\beta$ is left-c.e., then $\alpha+\beta$ is random; converse by Downey, Hirschfeldt and Nies 2002), verified through secondary sources; proved here for completeness, given the Levin–Schnorr characterisation].** Let $\alpha,\gamma$ be left-c.e., $\alpha$ Martin-Löf random, $k\ge0$, and $\beta:=2^{-k}\alpha+\gamma<1$. Then $K(\alpha_{1:n})\le K(\beta_{1:n})+k+c$ for all $n$, where $c$ depends on $k$ and on fixed approximating programs for $\alpha$ and $\gamma$; so $\beta$ is Martin-Löf random. (In the application, $\alpha=\Omega_V$, $k=|p_\alpha|$ and $\gamma=\gamma_\alpha$ are fixed by $V$, so $c$ is a machine constant.)

*Proof.* Let $\alpha_s\uparrow\alpha$, $\gamma_s\uparrow\gamma$ be computable rational approximations and $\beta_s:=2^{-k}\alpha_s+\gamma_s$. Given the string $u:=\beta_{1:n}$ (which also gives $n$), put $q:=0.u-2^{-n}<\beta$ and find the first $s$ with $\beta_s>q$. Then $\beta-\beta_s<\beta-q\le2^{1-n}$, hence $\alpha-\alpha_s\le2^k(\beta-\beta_s)<2^{k+1-n}$ (as $\gamma-\gamma_s\ge0$). $\alpha$ is irrational (random), so $\alpha_{1:n}$ is the index of the dyadic interval of length $2^{-n}$ containing $\alpha$, one of the at most $2^{k+1}+1$ such intervals meeting $[\alpha_s,\alpha_s+2^{k+1-n})$. So $\alpha_{1:n}$ is determined by $u$ and a $(k+2)$-bit index: $K(\alpha_{1:n})\le K(\beta_{1:n})+k+c$. By Levin–Schnorr, $K(\alpha_{1:n})\ge n-c'$ for all $n$, hence $K(\beta_{1:n})\ge n-k-c-c'$ for all $n$, and $\beta$ is random by Levin–Schnorr again. ∎

*Remarks.* (i) By Calude–Hertling–Khoussainov–Wang (2001) and Kučera–Slaman (2001), the Solovay-complete left-c.e. reals are exactly the random left-c.e. reals and the halting probabilities of universal prefix machines (known, unverified as to theorem numbers). So $\Inc_0$ is an Ω-number; the proofs above do not use this. (ii) *Related work.* Reals of the form "the probability that a universal machine has a given behaviour" are studied by Becher, Figueira, Grigorieff and Miller ("Randomness and halting probabilities", *JSL* 71(4):1411–1430, 2006: the randomness of $\Omega_U[X]$, the probability of halting with output in $X$) and by Barmpalias, Cenzer and Porter ("Random numbers as probabilities of machine behaviour", arXiv:1605.05838). $\Inc_0$ is such a probability, for the behaviour "the emitted theory is inconsistent", a $\Sigma_1$ event of the tape; Thm 3.2 is the standard argument applied to it.

**Proposition 3.4 [proved].** (a) $M_A(D)$ and the conditionals $M_A(b\mid D,\varphi)$, $M_A^3(y\mid D,\varphi)$, $\Bel_D(\chi)$ are computable relative to $\emptyset'$, uniformly. (b) $D\mapsto M_A(D)+\Inc_0$ is lsc; so $M_A$ is lsc relative to the single real $\Inc_0$ (given as an oracle).

*Proof.* (a) Every left-c.e. real is $\emptyset'$-computable (decide "$\exists s\,a_s>q$" for rationals $q$), and differences and ratios of $\emptyset'$-computable reals are $\emptyset'$-computable once the denominator is known to be positive; $M_A(D)>0$ iff $D$ is consistent (Prop 2.1(a)), a $\Pi^0_1$ question, decidable in $\emptyset'$. The three-valued events are Boolean combinations of effectively open classes, with $O_a\cap O_b=O_{a\wedge b}$, so their measures are sums and differences of left-c.e. reals by inclusion–exclusion. (b) Prop 3.1. ∎

*Contrast with P1.* Every machine law of P1 is lsc (P1 Prop 3.7) and $M_F$ is lsc with $\emptyset'$-computable conditionals (P1 Prop A.10). $M_A$ shares the second property and loses the first, by exactly one real: the probability of inconsistency. P1's function machines are consistent by construction, while a theory machine's consistency is a $\Pi^0_1$ property of its output. (`sfi` Prop 6.7 adds that, when consistency of finite sets of sentences with $B$ is not r.e., none of $W_A$, $M_A$, $W^{\rm cons}$, $M_F^{\rm cons}$ is lsc, because their supports are not r.e.; Thm 3.2(a) holds in every setting.)

## 4. Universality and invariance (target T4)

**Theorem 4.1 (mixture identity and dominance) [proved].** For every two-valued $D$,
$$M_A(D)=\sum_{p\in\dom V}2^{-|p|}\nu_{G_{V(p)}}(D)+(1-\Omega_V)\ind[B\vdash\psi_D]=\sum_i\mm(i)\,\nu_{G_i}(D)+(1-\Omega_V)\ind[B\vdash\psi_D],$$
with $\Omega_V:=\sum_{p\in\dom V}2^{-|p|}$, and the same holds for $M_A^3$, $\nu^3_{G_i}$ and the indicator that $B$ proves $\psi_D$ and decides no $\iota$-labelled sentence. Hence $M_A\ge2^{-K(\nu)}\nu$ for every theory-machine law $\nu$, and $M_A^3\ge2^{-K(G)}\nu^3_G$ for every theory machine $G$. In particular:
(i) $M_A(D)\ge2^{-K(e)-c}\ind[B\cup\mathsf W_e\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D]$ for every index $e$; consequently, **for every r.e. $T$ with $B\cup T$ consistent**, $\Unif_A[T]\ge2^{-c}\mm_\equiv(T)\ge2^{-K_\equiv(T)-c}$ and $\Unif_A(\Ext T)\ge2^{-c}\mm(\Ext T)$;
(ii) $M_A(D)\ge2^{-K(f)-c}\ind[f\supseteq D]$ for every partial computable labeller $f:\Sent\rightharpoonup\{0,1\}$ with $B\cup\Gamma_f$ consistent;
(iii) for every lsc semimeasure $v$ on indices, $M_A(D)\ge2^{-K(v)-c}\sum_ev(e)\ind[B\cup\mathsf W_e\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D]$; in particular $M_A\ge2^{-c}W_A$;
(iv) for an r.e. $T$ and a computable family $(\alpha_j)$ of sentences, the law of "$T$ plus fair-coin facts $\alpha_j^{\omega_j}$" (Example (iii) of §1.2) is dominated with constant $2^{-K(T,(\alpha_j))-c}$.

*Proof.* $\dom V$ is prefix-free, so the cylinders $[p]$, $p\in\dom V$, are disjoint, and for $\omega=p\omega'$ we have $A_\omega=A^{G_{V(p)}}_{\omega'}$ with $\omega'$ uniform given $[p]$. Hence $\Unif(E_D\cap[p])=2^{-|p|}\nu_{G_{V(p)}}(D)$. The tapes with no prefix in $\dom V$ have measure $1-\Omega_V$ and $A_\omega=\emptyset$. Grouping the $p$ with $V(p)=i$ gives weight $\mm(i)\ge2^{-K(i)}$. The three-valued case is identical.
(i) The deterministic machine $G'_e:=\{(\epsilon,\chi):\chi\in\mathsf W_e\}$ has an index $g(e)$ with $g$ computable and injective, so $\mm(g(e))\ge2^{-c}\mm(e)$ (the image of $\mm$ under $g$ is an lsc semimeasure, dominated by $\mm$). For $e\in[T]$, the tapes beginning with a program for $g(e)$ have $A_\omega=\mathsf W_e$, with $\Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn(T)$, and $B\cup A_\omega$ consistent because $B\cup T$ is. These tape sets are disjoint for distinct $e$; sum. Likewise for $\Ext T$.
(ii) $G_f:=\{(\epsilon,\varphi^b):f(\varphi)=b\}$ enumerates $\Gamma_f$, which is consistent with $B$ and proves $\psi_D$ when $f\supseteq D$; $K(G_f)\le K(f)+c$.
(iii) The map $i\mapsto\sum_{g(e)=i}v(e)$ is an lsc semimeasure of complexity $\le K(v)+c$, so $\mm(g(e))\ge2^{-K(v)-c}v(e)$. With $v=\mm$: $M_A\ge2^{-c}W_A$.
(iv) The machine of Example (iii) has an index computable from indices of $T$ and of the family. ∎

*Credit.* The mixture identity and the dominance argument are the standard ones for universal mixtures (P1 Thm 3.8; Solomonoff, Levin); what is new is the object they are applied to, the single-draw proof-requiring law. A dominance property of the same kind is stated for the modified Demski prior (§10). `sfi` Lemma 1.2 states the same identity (its eq. (1.2)).

**Proposition 4.2 (choice of the universal machine) [proved].** Call a theory machine $G'$ *universal* if for every theory machine $G$ there is a string $p_G$ with $A^{G'}_{p_G\omega}=A^G_\omega$ for all $\omega$ ($G_U$ is universal, with $p_G$ a $V$-program for an index of $G$). If $G'$ is universal, then $2^{-c}M_A\le\nu_{G'}\le2^cM_A$ and the same for the three-valued laws, with $c$ depending only on $G_U$ and $G'$. So posteriors change by at most a constant factor and semimeasure-convention losses by an additive constant.

*Proof.* $M_A\ge2^{-K(G')}\nu_{G'}$ by Thm 4.1. Conversely, the tapes $p_{G_U}\omega'$ give $G'$ the axiom set $A_{\omega'}$, so $\nu_{G'}(D)\ge2^{-|p_{G_U}|}M_A(D)$. ∎

**Proposition 4.3 (the background is a prior shift) [proved].** Let $B,B'$ be decidable and consistent with $B\subseteq\Cn_\emptyset(B')$ (plain logical consequence), and write $M_A^B$, $M_A^{B'}$ for SAI with these backgrounds. Then $M_A^B(D)\ge2^{-K(B')-c}M_A^{B'}(D)$ for every $D$, with $K(B')$ the complexity of an index of $B'$; likewise $W_A^B\ge2^{-K(B')-c}W_A^{B'}$ and $K_A^B\le K_A^{B'}+K(B')+c$. The converse fails: a $D$ consistent with $B$ and inconsistent with $B'$ has $M_A^{B'}(D)=0<M_A^B(D)$.

*Proof.* The machine $G_{B'}:=\{(\epsilon,\chi):\chi\in B'\}\cup G_U$ emits $B'\cup A_\omega$ on $\omega$. Under background $B$, $B\cup B'\cup A_\omega$ is equivalent to $B'\cup A_\omega$, so $\nu^B_{G_{B'}}(D)=M_A^{B'}(D)$; apply Thm 4.1. (Both $M_A^B$ and $M_A^{B'}$ use the same $G_U$, which does not depend on the background.) The same map on single programs gives the other two. ∎

So building knowledge into the background gains at most $K(B')+c$ bits on any data.

**Theorem 4.4 (faithful translations) [proved].** Let $(\mathcal L,B)$ and $(\mathcal L',B')$ be two settings and $\tau:\Sent_{\mathcal L}\to\Sent_{\mathcal L'}$ computable with
* (T1) for all $\Gamma\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ and $\varphi$: $B\cup\Gamma\vdash\varphi$ iff $B'\cup\tau[\Gamma]\vdash\tau(\varphi)$;
* (T2) $B'\vdash\tau(\neg\varphi)\leftrightarrow\neg\tau(\varphi)$ for all $\varphi$.

For a data set $D$ on $\mathcal L$ let $M'_A(\tau D):=\Unif\{\omega:B'\cup A'_\omega\text{ consistent and }\vdash\tau(\varphi)^{D(\varphi)}\ \forall\varphi\in\dom D\}$, with $A'$ the universal theory machine of $\mathcal L'$. Then for every $D$
$$2^{-K(\tau)-c}M_A(D)\le M'_A(\tau D)\le2^{K(\tau)+c}M_A(D),$$
the same holds for $W_A$, and $|K'_A(\tau D)-K_A(D)|\le K(\tau)+c$. Here $c$ depends on both settings.

*Proof.* *A preliminary fact:* $B'\vdash\neg\tau(\bot)$. By (T1) with $\Gamma=\emptyset$, $B\vdash\neg\bot$ gives $B'\vdash\tau(\neg\bot)$, and (T2) converts this.
*Push forward.* The machine "run $G_U$ and emit $\tau(\chi)$ for every emitted $\chi$" emits $\tau[A_\omega]$. By (T1), $B'\cup\tau[A]\vdash\tau(\bot)$ iff $B\cup A\vdash\bot$; with the preliminary fact, $B'\cup\tau[A]$ is consistent iff $B\cup A$ is. By (T1) and (T2), $B'\cup\tau[A]\vdash\tau(\varphi)^b$ iff $B\cup A\vdash\varphi^b$ (for $b=0$: $B'\cup\tau[A]\vdash\neg\tau(\varphi)$ iff $\vdash\tau(\neg\varphi)$ iff $B\cup A\vdash\neg\varphi$). So this machine's event for $\tau D$ is exactly $E_D$, and Thm 4.1 in $\mathcal L'$ gives $M'_A(\tau D)\ge2^{-K(\tau)-c}M_A(D)$.
*Pull back.* For $A'\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L'}$ let $\tau^*(A'):=\{\varphi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L}:B'\cup A'\vdash\tau(\varphi)\}$. The set $G^*:=\{(w,\varphi):B'\cup A'_w\vdash\tau(\varphi)\}$ is c.e. and, by compactness as in Lemma 1.3, $A^{G^*}_\omega=\tau^*(A'_\omega)$. If $B'\cup A'$ is consistent, so is $B\cup\tau^*(A')$: if $B\cup\Gamma\vdash\bot$ for a finite $\Gamma\subseteq\tau^*(A')$, then $B'\cup\tau[\Gamma]\vdash\tau(\bot)$ by (T1), and $B'\cup A'$ proves every member of $\tau[\Gamma]$, so $B'\cup A'\vdash\tau(\bot)\wedge\neg\tau(\bot)$. If $B'\cup A'\vdash\tau(\varphi)^b$, then $\varphi^b\in\tau^*(A')$ (for $b=0$ via (T2)). So $G^*$'s event for $D$ contains the event for $\tau D$, and $M_A(D)\ge2^{-K(\tau)-c}M'_A(\tau D)$.
The same two maps act on single enumerator programs with a prefix of length $K(\tau)+c$, which gives the statements for $W_A$ and $K_A$. ∎

**Corollary 4.5 (adding vocabulary) [proved].** Let $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal L'$, $B'=B$ and $\tau$ the inclusion. (T1) holds because every $\mathcal L$-structure expands to an $\mathcal L'$-structure (with completeness, $B\cup\Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal L'}\varphi$ iff $B\cup\Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal L}\varphi$ for $\mathcal L$-sentences), and (T2) is trivial. Hence for data in the old vocabulary, $M_A^{\mathcal L'}(D)$ and $M_A^{\mathcal L}(D)$ agree up to a factor $2^{\pm c}$, $W_A$ likewise, $K_A^{\mathcal L'}(D)=K_A^{\mathcal L}(D)\pm c$, and predictive probabilities of $\mathcal L$-sentences agree up to $2^{\pm2c}$.

*Reading.* In the unbounded, universal setting, theoretical vocabulary buys at most a constant: the pull-back of a theory is its set of observational consequences, an r.e. set, and the universal machine enumerates it. This is the effective content of Craig's theorem inside the prior (Hempel's horn of the theoretician's dilemma). `terms` Thm 2.1 extends this to a theoretical background, to class-level posterior odds and to penalised versions, and shows that tape by tape the observational projection has exactly the same law; its projection machine is the pull-back machine $G^*$ above for the inclusion. Where vocabulary does pay (finite theories, resource bounds) is `terms` §3–§6; Prop 1.8(d) shows that $K_{\rm fin}$ is not invariant in this way.

## 5. Loss bounds (target T5)

**Theorem 5.1 (loss against a theory that decides the data) [proved].** Let $(\varphi_t,b_t)_{t\le n}$ be any consistent reveal sequence, chosen in any way (in advance, adaptively, adversarially, non-computably; any order; repeats allowed), and let $T$ be an r.e. theory with $B\cup T$ consistent and $B\cup T\vdash\varphi_t^{b_t}$ for all $t\le n$. Then the cumulative loss in the semimeasure convention satisfies
$$L_A(D_n)-L_A(\emptyset)\ \le\ L_A(D_n)\ \le\ -\log\Unif_A(\Ext T)\ \le\ -\log\mm(\Ext T)+c\ \le\ -\log\mm_\equiv(T)+c\ \le\ K_\equiv(T)+c,$$
and also $L_A(D_n)\le-\log W_A(D_n)+c\le K_A(D_n)+c$.

*Proof.* Every consistent extension of $T$ proves every datum, so $E_{D_n}\supseteq\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(A_\omega)\supseteq\Cn(T)\}$. Then Thm 4.1(i), $[T]\subseteq\Ext T$ (as $B\cup T$ is consistent) and $\mm_\equiv(T)\ge2^{-K_\equiv(T)}$. $L_A(\emptyset)\ge0$; the left side is the cumulative loss by Prop 2.1(d). The last chain is Thm 4.1(iii) and $W_A\ge2^{-K_A}$. ∎

*Which complexity appears.* Not the complexity of an axiom set, and not a property of the data's syntax, but the algorithmic probability of the class of all consistent extensions of $T$, bounded by $K_\equiv(T)$, the complexity of $T$ *up to deductive equivalence*. Three consequences:
* the bound favours the *weakest* theory that decides the data ($\Cn T\subseteq\Cn T'$ implies $\Ext T'\subseteq\Ext T$);
* a non-finitely-axiomatisable theory such as $\PA$ has small $K_\equiv$ although every axiomatisation is infinite;
* if a theory has a complicated finite axiomatisation and a simple equivalent one, the simple one counts.

Compared with P1 Thm 5.1(b), where the loss on data from a partial computable $\varphi$ is at most $K(\varphi)+c$: a program is replaced by a theory up to equivalence, and the theory need not be complete, only decide the sentences asked.

**Proposition 5.2 (laws plus exceptions) [proved].**
(a) For every theory-machine law $\nu$: $L_A(D_n)\le-\log\nu(D_n)+K(\nu)$.
(b) For every r.e. $T$ and every $E\subseteq D_n$ (a sub-data-set) with $B\cup T\cup E^\pm$ consistent and $B\cup T\cup E^\pm\vdash\psi_{D_n}$: $L_A(D_n)\le K_\equiv(T)+K(E)+c$. Taking for $E$ the data $T$ does not prove gives a two-part, law-plus-exceptions bound.
(c) Let $(\alpha_j)_{j\ge1}$ be a computable family of sentences and $T$ r.e. such that $B\cup T\cup\{\alpha_j^{x_j}:j\ge1\}$ is consistent for every $x\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}$. If every datum of $D_n$ is proved by $T$ or is one of the sentences $\alpha_j$ with some label, and $m$ distinct $j$ occur, then $L_A(D_n)\le K(T,(\alpha_j))+m+c$.

*Proof.* (a) Thm 4.1. (b) For $e\in[T]$, the set $\mathsf W_e\cup\{\psi_E\}$ has an index computable from $(e,E)$, is consistent with $B$ and proves $\psi_{D_n}$; $K(e,E)\le K(e)+K(E)+c$; minimise over $e$. (c) The machine "emit $T$ and $\alpha_j^{\omega_j}$ for all $j$" always has a consistent theory (the hypothesis forces $T$ to decide no $\alpha_j$), which proves $D_n$ whenever $\omega$ agrees with the $m$ labels of the $\alpha_j$-data: probability $2^{-m}$. Apply (a). ∎

In (c) the exceptions cost one bit each, without paying for *which* atoms were exceptional; in (b) one pays $K(E)$, which includes their positions. (c) is the setting of `laws` (laws against initial conditions; `laws` Props 1.4–1.9 use (a)–(c) and extend (c) from atoms the law is silent about to answers the law only constrains).

**Proposition 5.3 (mistake bounds).**
(a) **[proved]** Under the hypotheses of Thm 5.1, the predictor that outputs the most probable of $1,0,\iota$ under $(\Bel,\Dis,\Indep)_{D_{t-1}}(\varphi_t)$ (ties broken arbitrarily; the stored label at a repeat) makes at most $-\log\big(\Unif_A(\Ext T)/M_A(\emptyset)\big)\le K_\equiv(T)+c$ mistakes, a mistake being any output other than $b_t$ ($\iota$ included).
(b) **[proved; computed: `c3`; `rb5` R5.1]** *The Kolmogorov predictor.* Fix a well-order of $\dom V$ that refines length (ties in any fixed way). At step $t$ let $p_t$ be the first program $p$ with $B\cup\mathsf W_{V(p)}$ consistent and $\vdash\psi_{D_{t-1}}$; predict $1$ (resp. $0$) if $\mathsf W_{V(p_t)}$ proves $\varphi_t$ (resp. $\neg\varphi_t$), and abstain otherwise. If $k:=\sup_nK_A(D_n)<\infty$ (for instance $k\le K_\equiv(T)+c$ under Thm 5.1's hypotheses), it errs or abstains at most $2^k$ times.
(c) **[proved; computed: `rb5` R5.1, R5.2] The order hypothesis in (b) is needed.** "The $2^k$ bound holds for every well-order of $\dom V$" is **refuted**: for every $m$ there are a well-order $\prec$ of $\dom V$ of type $\omega$ and a data set $D$ with $K_A(D)\le K(m)+c\le2\log m+c'$ on which the Kolmogorov predictor with $\prec$ errs or abstains $2^m-1$ times, which exceeds $2^{K_A(D)}$ for all large $m$. With an arbitrary well-order the count is bounded only by the number of programs that precede the final one.

*Proof.* (a) The posterior trichotomy sums to $1$, so if $M_A(b_t\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)=\Bel_{D_{t-1}}(\varphi_t^{b_t})>\frac12$ then $b_t$ is the unique maximiser. Each mistake therefore costs at least one bit of cumulative loss, which Thm 5.1 bounds by $L_A(D_n)-L_A(\emptyset)\le-\log\Unif_A(\Ext T)+\log M_A(\emptyset)$. This is the halving argument (P1 Cor 5.4).
(b) At an error or abstention, $p_t$ does not prove $\varphi_t^{b_t}$, so it is incompatible with $D_t$ and with every later $D_{t'}$, and is never chosen again. Each $p_t$ has length $K_A(D_{t-1})\le k$, because the order refines length. Kraft's inequality for the prefix-free set $\dom V$ gives at most $2^k$ programs of length $\le k$.
(c) Work with fresh atoms $a_1,\dots,a_m$ and $\mathrm{desc}(w):=\bigwedge_{i\le m}a_i^{w_i}$ as in Thm 11.3. For each $w\ne0^m$ fix a program $q_w\in\dom V$ for an index of $\{\mathrm{desc}(w)\}$. Let $\prec$ list the $2^m-1$ programs $q_w$ first (in any order) and then the rest of $\dom V$ by length. A consistent theory proves at most one $\mathrm{desc}(w)$, so $\pi(w)=q_w$ for $w\ne0^m$ (notation of Thm 11.3), and $\pi(0^m)$ lies in the tail; hence $w^*=0^m$ in the proof of Thm 11.3, which holds for every well-order. It gives $2^m-1$ errors or abstentions on a data set $D$ whose conjunction is equivalent to $\mathrm{desc}(0^m)$, so $K_A(D)\le K(\mathrm{desc}(0^m))+c\le K(m)+c'$. *Computed:* in referee B's exhaustive finite tests, random orders gave one more failure than the number of programs of length $\le k$ (`rb5` R5.1), and on the sequences of Thm 11.3 the failures exceeded $2^{K_A(D)}$ in 4 of 36 random-order runs (R5.2). ∎

The bound in (b) is essentially attained (Thm 11.3): the single shortest theory can be exponentially worse than the Bayes predictor of (a). This is the logical analogue of P1 Thm 9.10 (the conjunction ladder), and, as there, it is the selection rule that fails, not the prior.

**Theorem 5.4 (merging for three-valued data) [proved; computed: `c1` A15].** Let $G$ be a theory machine with $\nu_G(\emptyset)=1$, $\omega\sim\Unif$ and $A:=A^G_\omega$. Let $(\xi_t)$ be random variables independent of $\omega$ (any joint law), and let the questions be $\varphi_t:=s_t(h_{t-1},\xi_t)$ for arbitrary measurable rules $s_t$, where $h_{t-1}=(\varphi_1,y_1,\dots,\varphi_{t-1},y_{t-1})$ and $y_t\in Y_3$ is $A$'s trichotomy label of $\varphi_t$. Let $D_t:=\{(\varphi_s,y_s):s\le t\}$ and
$$d_t:=\KL\big(\nu^3_G(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)\,\big\|\,M_A^3(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)\big)\ \text{bits}\qquad(d_t:=0\text{ at a repeat}).$$
Then for every $n$
$$\sum_{t\le n}\Ex d_t=\Ex\log\frac{\nu^3_G(D_n)}{M_A^3(D_n)}+\log M_A(\emptyset)\ \le\ K(G)+\log M_A(\emptyset)\ <\ K(G).$$
Hence $\sum_t\Ex d_t\le K(G)$, $d_t\to0$ almost surely, and $\sum_t\Ex\|\nu^3_G(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)-M^3_A(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)\|_1^2\le2\ln2\cdot K(G)$.

*Proof.* *Step 1 (the conditional law of the next label).* Histories take countably many values. For a history value $h$ and a value of $\xi_{<t}$, the event $\{h_{t-1}=h\}$ is the intersection of an event in $\xi_{<t}$ (the rules produce $h$'s questions from $h$'s labels) with the event $\{\mathrm{tri}^G_\omega\supseteq D_h\}$, where $\mathrm{tri}^G_\omega$ is the trichotomy function of $A^G_\omega$ (Prop 2.1(g)); $\nu_G(\emptyset)=1$ makes $\mathrm{tri}^G_\omega$ defined (the theory consistent) on almost every tape. Since $\xi$ is independent of $\omega$, given $(h_{t-1},\xi_{\le t})$ the tape has law $\Unif(\cdot\mid \mathrm{tri}^G\supseteq D_{t-1})$, and $y_t=\mathrm{tri}^G_\omega(\varphi_t)$ has law $\nu^3_G(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)$. (Almost surely $\nu^3_G(D_{t-1})>0$.)
*Step 2 (telescoping).* Let $Z_t:=\log\nu^3_G(y_t\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)-\log M_A^3(y_t\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)$ ($0$ at a repeat) and $S_n:=\sum_{t\le n}Z_t$. Since $\nu^3_G(\emptyset)=1$ and $M_A^3(\emptyset)=M_A(\emptyset)$, $S_n=\log\nu^3_G(D_n)-\log M_A^3(D_n)+\log M_A(\emptyset)$.
*Step 3 (integrability).* By Thm 4.1, $M^3_A\ge2^{-K(G)}\nu^3_G$, so $S_n\le K(G)+\log M_A(\emptyset)$ pointwise. Since $-\log M^3_A(D_n)\ge0$, $S_n\ge\log\nu^3_G(D_n)+\log M_A(\emptyset)$. And $\Ex[-\log\nu^3_G(D_n)]=\sum_{t\le n}\Ex[H_t]\le n\log3$, where $H_t$ is the entropy of $\nu^3_G(\cdot\mid D_{t-1},\varphi_t)$, a distribution on three points: the summands $-\log\nu^3_G(y_t\mid\cdots)$ are nonnegative, so the tower property applies by Step 1 without further conditions (Tonelli). Hence $S_n$ is integrable, and so is each $Z_t=S_t-S_{t-1}$.
*Step 4.* By Step 1, $\Ex[Z_t\mid h_{t-1},\xi_{\le t}]=d_t$, which is $\ge0$ because the conditionals of $M^3_A$ are probability distributions (Prop 2.1(f)). Taking expectations and summing, $\sum_{t\le n}\Ex d_t=\Ex S_n$, which is the displayed identity and bound. Pinsker's inequality in bits, $\KL\ge\|P-Q\|_1^2/(2\ln2)$, gives the last claim, and $\Ex\sum_td_t<\infty$ gives $d_t\to0$ a.s. ∎

With two-valued data the rule would have to see $A$ (ask only questions $A$ decides) for the two-valued conditionals to be the data law, and the expected bound can fail, as in P1's remark after its Thm 5.3. The pathwise bound Prop 5.2(a) still holds.

**Corollary 5.5 (three-valued loss bound) [proved].** If $D_n$ is three-valued and labelled by the trichotomy of an r.e. $T$ with $B\cup T$ consistent, then in any reveal order the three-valued cumulative loss is $-\log M_A^3(D_n)+\log M_A(\emptyset)\le-\log\Unif_A[T]\le K_\equiv(T)+c$.

*Proof.* Tapes with $\Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn(T)$ (and $B\cup A_\omega$ consistent) lie in $E^3_{D_n}$; order independence is Prop 2.1(d) for $M_A^3$, with the same proof; then Thm 4.1(i). ∎

Here $[T]$ replaces $\Ext T$: three-valued data penalise theories that decide more than $T$.

**Proposition 5.6 (lower bound) [proved].** For every data set $D$, $L_A(D)\ge K(b_D\mid\dom D)-c$, where $b_D$ is the vector of two-valued labels in canonical order. So on fair-coin labels of any fixed $n$ sentences, $\Ex L_A\ge n-c$, whatever the sentences.

*Proof.* Prop 6.1(b). For the expectation: $\sum_b2^{-K(b\mid\dom D)}\le1$, so Gibbs' inequality gives $\Ex_bK(b\mid\dom D)\ge n$ for uniform $b\in\{0,1\}^n$. With logically dependent sentences, inconsistent labellings have $L_A=\infty$, and the bound holds trivially for them. ∎

## 6. Versions: coding bounds and no coding theorem (target T6)

**Proposition 6.1 (coding lower bound and the chain) [proved].**
(a) Let $S$ be a finite set of pairwise $B$-incompatible sentences and $\sigma\in S$. Then $-\log\Bel_0(\sigma)=-\log M_A(\{(\sigma,1)\})\ge K(\sigma\mid S)-c$.
(b) For every data set $D$, $L_A(D)\ge K(b_D\mid\dom D)-c$.
(c) For every consistent $D$ and every finite set $S$ of pairwise $B$-incompatible sentences with $\psi_D\in S$,
$$2^{-K_A(D)}\le W_A(D)\le2^cM_A(D)\le2^{K(S)+c}\,2^{-K_A(D)} .$$
With $S$ the $2^{|\dom D|}$ label conjunctions on the two-valued part of $\dom D$, $K(S)\le K(\dom D)+c$.

*Proof.* (a) Let $Q_S(\sigma)$ be the probability, over $\omega$, that the following procedure outputs $\sigma$: at stage $s$, read $\omega_{1:s}$, run $s$ steps of the enumeration of $G_U$ on it, and test all derivations of size $\le s$ from $B$ and the axioms emitted so far, for every member of $S$ in a fixed order; output the first member found. The output events are disjoint open sets, so $\sum_{\sigma\in S}Q_S(\sigma)\le1$, and $Q_S(\sigma)$ is lsc uniformly in $(S,\sigma)$. (So the coding theorem is applied to $Q_S$, which is lsc, not to $M_A$, which is not; Thm 3.2.) If $B\cup A_\omega$ is consistent and proves $\sigma$, it proves no other member of $S$, so the procedure outputs $\sigma$: $\Bel_0(\sigma)\le Q_S(\sigma)$. The conditional coding theorem (P1 §3.1: $K(a\mid z)\le-\log P(a\mid z)+K(P)+c$ for uniformly lsc $P$ with $\sum_aP(a\mid z)\le1$) gives $K(\sigma\mid S)\le-\log Q_S(\sigma)+c$.
(b) Apply (a) with $S$ the label conjunctions on $\dom D$ (pairwise logically incompatible, computable from $\dom D$) and $\sigma:=\psi_D$, so that $M_A(D)=\Bel_0(\psi_D)$ (Prop 2.1(c)); from $\dom D$ and $\psi_D$ one computes $b_D$, so $K(b_D\mid\dom D)\le K(\psi_D\mid S)+c$.
(c) First inequality: one term of $W_A$. Second: Thm 4.1(iii). Third: $K_A(D)\le K(\psi_D)+c$ (Prop 1.8(c)) $\le K(S)+K(\psi_D\mid S)+c'$, and (a) bounds $K(\psi_D\mid S)$ by $L_A(D)+c$. ∎

The slack $K(S)$ is the complexity of a set of pairwise incompatible sentences containing the data's conjunction (for instance the direct answers of a *question* in the sense of NOTATION-draft §3.2, though $S$ need not be exhaustive), in place of P1's $K(\dom D)$ (P1 Thm 4.4). It can be much smaller. For $S=\{\psi_D,\neg\psi_D\}$ the slack is $K(\psi_D)$, which is useless. For "which of these $m$ incompatible laws holds", the slack is $K(S)$, which is $K(m)+O(1)$ when $S$ is computable from $m$.

**Theorem 6.2 (no coding theorem for axiom systems) [proved; computed: `c4`].** Work in the literal setting (Def 1.1′). For every $d\ge2$ there are distinct $x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}^d$ such that $D:=\{(R(t_{x_1}),0),(R(t_{x_2}),1)\}$ satisfies
(i) $K_A(D)\ge\ell_d>\log d-2$, with $\ell_d:=\lceil\log(d/\log3)\rceil-1$, and $W_A(D)\le\Omega_V^{\ge\ell_d}:=\sum\{2^{-|p|}:p\in\dom V,\ |p|\ge\ell_d\}$;
(ii) $-\log W_A(D)\le2+K(\lceil\log(d+1)\rceil)+c$;
(iii) $-\log M_A(D)\le2+c$.
Parts (ii) and (iii) hold for every data set of two literals on distinct words of length $\le d$. Hence $K_A(D)+\log W_A(D)\ge\log d-K(\lceil\log(d+1)\rceil)-c-4\to\infty$, and $M_A(D)/W_A(D)\ge2^{-c-2}/\Omega_V^{\ge\ell_d}\to\infty$: no two of $2^{-K_A}$, $W_A$, $M_A$ agree up to a constant factor.

**Lemma 6.2a (literals of a fresh predicate) [proved].** In the literal setting: (L1) every set $\Lambda$ of literals $R(t_w)^{b}$ with at most one literal per word is consistent with $B$; (L2) if $B\cup\Lambda$ is consistent, then $B\cup\Lambda\vdash R(t_y)^b$ only if $R(t_y)^b\in\Lambda$. The same holds for finitely many fresh predicates, and for sets of sentences built from fresh atoms by propositional connectives in the obvious way (a propositional valuation of the atoms is realised by interpreting $R$).

*Proof.* (L1) Expand the model of $B$ in which the $t_w$ are distinct by $R:=\{t_w^{\mathfrak M}:R(t_w)\in\Lambda\}$; $B$ does not mention $R$, so it stays true. (L2) If $R(t_y)^{1-b}\in\Lambda$, proving $R(t_y)^b$ would make $B\cup\Lambda$ inconsistent. If neither literal of $t_y$ is in $\Lambda$, then $\Lambda\cup\{R(t_y)^{1-b}\}$ is consistent by (L1). For the last sentence: every valuation of finitely many fresh atoms is realised in an expansion of the model of $B$, so a propositional combination of fresh atoms is consistent with $B$ iff it is propositionally satisfiable, and $B$ together with such combinations proves another such combination iff it does so propositionally. ∎

*Proof of Theorem 6.2.* (i) Let $\ell:=\ell_d$, so $2^\ell<d/\log3$ and $3^{2^\ell}<2^d$. There are fewer than $2^\ell$ programs of length $<\ell$. Colour each $x\in\{0,1\}^d$ by the vector, over these programs $p$, of: "+" if $B\cup\mathsf W_{V(p)}$ is consistent and proves $R(t_x)$; "−" if it is consistent and proves $\neg R(t_x)$; "∗" otherwise. There are at most $3^{2^\ell-1}<2^d$ colours, so two distinct words $x_1,x_2$ share a colour. A program $p$ with $|p|<\ell$ compatible with $D$ would colour $x_1$ "−" and $x_2$ "+". So every compatible program has length $\ge\ell$, which gives both claims of (i); and $\ell\ge\log(d/\log3)-1>\log d-2$ since $\log\log3<0.67$.
(ii) Let $j(x)$ be the position of $x$ in the length-lexicographic order ($j(\epsilon)=0$), so $j(x)\le2^{d+1}-2$ for $|x|\le d$. Let $s:=\lceil\log(d+1)\rceil$ and $L:=2^{2^s}\ge2^{d+1}$. For $u\in\{0,1\}^L$ let $\Lambda_u:=\{R(t_x)^{u_{j(x)+1}}:j(x)<L\}$, consistent by (L1), and proving $R(t_x)^b$ iff $u_{j(x)+1}=b$ by (L2). A fixed prefix machine reads a $V$-program for $s$, computes $L$, reads $L$ further bits $u$ and outputs an index of $\Lambda_u$; by universality of $V$ a fixed string $r$ makes $r\,q\,u\in\dom V$ a program for that index, for $q$ a shortest program for $s$. These $2^L$ programs are distinct (and form a prefix-free family, since $q$ fixes $L$) and have length $|r|+K(s)+L$. For a two-point $D$ on distinct words of length $\le d$, exactly a quarter of the $u$ give a compatible $\Lambda_u$. So $W_A(D)\ge2^L\cdot\frac14\cdot2^{-|r|-K(s)-L}$.
(iii) The theory machine $G_{\rm coin}:=\{(w,R(t_x)^{w_{j(x)+1}}):|w|=j(x)+1\}$ emits $\{R(t_x)^{\omega_{j(x)+1}}:x\in\{0,1\}^*\}$, consistent by (L1); it proves $D$ iff $\omega$ agrees with $D$'s labels at two positions (L2), with probability $\frac14$. Apply Thm 4.1.
The consequences: $K_A+\log W_A\ge\ell_d-2-K(s)-c$; $\Omega_V^{\ge\ell}$ is the tail of a convergent series and $\ell_d\to\infty$. ∎

This is P1 Thm 4.9 for axiom systems, proved directly with theory machines, so it does not depend on the transfer theorem `sfi` Thm 5.1 (which gives the same statement as `sfi` Cor 5.2(a)). The mechanism is the same as in P1: deterministic programs (here r.e. axiom sets) cannot separate atoms cheaply, while a coin can.

**Remark 6.3 (what else transfers) [proved, given `sfi` Thm 5.1 and the cited P1 results].** By `sfi` Thm 5.1 (on positive literals of a fresh predicate, $K_A$, $W_A$, $M_A$ agree with P1's $K_F$, $W$, $M_F$ on the corresponding string data, up to constants; scout S Thm 2.7, scout F's F9) and `sfi` Cor 5.2(c), P1 Cor 4.10 (two-point rate: $\log d-O(1)\le\max(K_A-L_A)\le\log d+O(\log\log d)$) and P1 Prop 4.7 (shared randomness is not a mixture of private coin facts) hold for $M_A$, $W_A$, $K_A$ on literal data. *Large gaps have no computable witnesses* [proved directly]: along a computable family $k\mapsto D_k$ of consistent data sets, $K_A(D_k)\le K(\psi_{D_k})+c\le K(k)+c'$ while $L_A\ge0$, so $2^{-K_A}$, $W_A$ and $M_A$ agree up to $K(k)+c'$ bits.

**Proposition 6.4 (coding for whole theories).** For an r.e. set $X\subseteq\Sent$ let $K_{\rm enum}(X):=\min\{|p|:p\in\dom V,\ \mathsf W_{V(p)}=X\}$ and $\mm_{\rm enum}(X):=\sum\{2^{-|p|}:p\in\dom V,\ \mathsf W_{V(p)}=X\}$.
(a) **[proved]** For every r.e. $T$: $K_\equiv(T)=K_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)\pm c$ and $2^{-c}\mm_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)\le\mm_\equiv(T)\le2^c\mm_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)$.
(b) **[proved, given Solovay 1977 (known: abstract level, through Vereshchagin 2007 and Shekhovtsov–Zakharov 2023)]** $-\log\mm_\equiv(T)\le K_\equiv(T)\le3\,(-\log\mm_\equiv(T))+O\big(\log(-\log\mm_\equiv(T))\big)+c$.

*Proof.* (a) Let $\kappa$ be computable and injective with $\mathsf W_{\kappa(e)}=\Cn(\mathsf W_e)$ (s-m-n with padding). Every $e'$ with $\mathsf W_{e'}=\Cn T$ lies in $[T]$, so $K_\equiv(T)\le K_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)+c$ and $\mm_\equiv(T)\ge2^{-c}\mm_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)$ (grouping programs by the index they print). Conversely $\kappa$ maps $[T]$ into $\{e':\mathsf W_{e'}=\Cn T\}$, with $K(\kappa(e))\le K(e)+c$ and $\mm(\kappa(e))\ge2^{-c}\mm(e)$ (the image of $\mm$ under $\kappa$ is an lsc semimeasure). (b) The left inequality is one term of the sum. For the right one, Solovay's theorem is $K_{\rm enum}(X)\le3I(X)+O(\log I(X))$ for every r.e. $X$, where $I(X)$ is the negative logarithm of the probability that a machine with a random input tape enumerates exactly $X$ (the "probabilistic reading", which the abstracts of Vereshchagin 2007 and Shekhovtsov–Zakharov 2023 support: §14). That probability is at least $2^{-c}\mm_{\rm enum}(X)$ (a deterministic program is a randomised algorithm that ignores its tape), so the inequality also holds with $I(X):=-\log\mm_{\rm enum}(X)$; apply it to $X=\Cn T$ and use (a). ∎

**Remark 6.5 (factor 2) [known: abstract level; error term unverified].** Vereshchagin (*IPL* 103(1):34–39, 2007) replaces Solovay's constant 3 by 2 for finite sets, and Shekhovtsov and Zakharov ("Enumerating complexity revisited", arXiv:2312.04187, ECCC TR23-201) show that any such bound for finite sets transfers to all sets up to logarithmic terms, so the constant 2 holds for every set. ## 7. The consistency-requiring variants (target T7)

Let $H_\omega:=B\cup A_\omega\cup D^\pm$ (the hypothesis plus the data) and $\varepsilon_\emptyset:=\Unif\{\omega:A_\omega=\emptyset\}\ge2^{-|p_\emptyset|}>0$, with $p_\emptyset$ a $V$-program for an index of the empty machine (the tapes with no program prefix also count). When $M_A^{\rm nc}(D)>0$ write $\Pr_D:=\Unif(\cdot\mid H_\omega\text{ consistent})$; by definition $\Bel^{\rm nc}_D(\varphi)=\Pr_D[H\vdash\varphi]$.

**Proposition 7.1 (the 0/1 CR weight is a plausibility and does not discriminate) [proved; computed: `c1` A9, A10].**
(a) For $\varphi\notin\dom D$: $M_A^{\rm nc}(D+(\varphi,1))+M_A^{\rm nc}(D+(\varphi,0))=M_A^{\rm nc}(D)+\Unif\{H_\omega\text{ consistent and decides neither }\varphi\text{ nor }\neg\varphi\}\ge M_A^{\rm nc}(D)$. So $M_A^{\rm nc}$ is not a semimeasure; it is the plausibility $\Pl_0(\psi_D)$ (Prop 2.3), the dual of a belief function.
(b) $M_A^{\rm nc}(D)\ge\varepsilon_\emptyset$ for every consistent $D$: $-\log M_A^{\rm nc}\le\log(1/\varepsilon_\emptyset)$ on all consistent data. The coin-free CR weight is $\ge2^{-|p_\emptyset|}$, and the Kolmogorov CR version $K_A^{\rm nc}(D):=\min\{|p|:B\cup\mathsf W_{V(p)}\cup D^\pm\text{ consistent}\}$ is $\le|p_\emptyset|$ on all consistent $D$.
(c) The CR posterior after $D$ is the prior restricted to the tapes whose theory is consistent with $D$: data eliminate hypotheses but never change the odds between survivors (one-tape form of AI-paper Prop 2.22(b) and pa Prop 5.2).

*Proof.* (a) For consistent $H$, $H\cup\{\varphi^b\}$ is consistent iff $H\nvdash\varphi^{1-b}$, so both labels are possible iff $H$ decides neither, and exactly one otherwise; for inconsistent $H$ neither. (b) If $A_\omega=\emptyset$, $H_\omega=B\cup D^\pm$ is consistent. (c) By definition. ∎

**Proposition 7.2 (the CR predictor).**
(a) **[proved; computed: `c2` B1]** For every consistent $D$ and $\varphi\notin\dom D$ with $B\cup D^\pm\nvdash\varphi^b$: $\Bel^{\rm nc}_D(\varphi^b)\le1-\varepsilon_\emptyset/M_A^{\rm nc}(D)\le1-\varepsilon_\emptyset$. So on every consistent reveal sequence the CR cumulative loss $\sum_t-\log\Bel^{\rm nc}_{D_{t-1}}(\varphi_t^{b_t})$ is at least $\log\frac1{1-\varepsilon_\emptyset}$ times the number of steps whose datum is not entailed by the earlier data. Example: for fresh atoms $\alpha_j$ (Def 1.1′), the stream $(\alpha_j,1)_{j\ge1}$ has no entailed datum (L2), so CR loses at least $n\log\frac1{1-\varepsilon_\emptyset}$ on $n$ data, while $T:=\{\forall x\,R(x)\}$ decides the stream, so proof-requiring SAI loses at most $K_\equiv(T)+c=O(1)$ (Thm 5.1).
(b) **[proved; computed: `c5` R1, R2; `rb4` R4.4, `rb8` R8.2] The CR cumulative loss of $M_A$ itself depends on the reveal order, with a linear gap.** In the literal setting let $a_i$ and $s_{i,j}$ ($i,j\ge1$) be fresh atoms (all words pairwise distinct, computable in $(i,j)$), and put
$$e_{i,j}:=\neg s_{i,1}\wedge\dots\wedge\neg s_{i,j-1}\wedge s_{i,j},\qquad b_{i,j}:=a_i\vee e_{i,j}.$$
  * (b1) For $D_j:=\{(a_1,1),(b_{1,j},1)\}$: for all but at most $1/(\Bel_0(a_1)\,\varepsilon_\emptyset)$ indices $j$, the CR probability of $D_j$ is strictly larger in the order $(a_1,b_{1,j})$ than in the order $(b_{1,j},a_1)$.
  * (b2) Let $c:=|p_R|$ for a $V$-program $p_R$ of an index of the deterministic machine emitting $\forall x\,R(x)$. For every $n$ there are indices $j_1,\dots,j_n\le\lceil2^{c+1}/\varepsilon_\emptyset\rceil$ such that, for $D_n:=\{(a_i,1),(b_{i,j_i},1):i\le n\}$, the CR cumulative loss in the order $(b_{1,j_1},a_1,b_{2,j_2},a_2,\dots)$ exceeds that in the order $(a_1,b_{1,j_1},a_2,b_{2,j_2},\dots)$ by at least $n\log(1+2^{-c}\varepsilon_\emptyset/2)$ bits.
(c) **[proved; computed: `c2` B2; `rb4` R4.3]** *An explicit law.* For $G:=\{(1,a)\}$ (theory $\{a\}$ on tapes beginning with 1, $\emptyset$ otherwise), $a,c$ fresh atoms and $D:=\{(a,1),(a\vee c,1)\}$, the order $(a,a\vee c)$ has CR probability $\frac12$ and the order $(a\vee c,a)$ has $\frac14$.

*Proof.* (a) The tapes with $A_\omega=\emptyset$ are in the denominator of $\Bel^{\rm nc}_D$ and not in its numerator.

(b) *Step 1 (one pair, after any revealed set).* Let $D$ be consistent with $M_A^{\rm nc}(D)>0$, let $a,e$ be sentences, $b:=a\vee e$, and write $\Pr:=\Pr_D$, $H:=H_\omega$. Define
$$\Pl(b):=\Pr[H\cup\{b\}\text{ consistent}],\quad T:=\Pr[H\cup\{b\}\text{ consistent},\ H\vdash b\to a],\quad\rho:=\Pr[H\cup\{e\wedge\neg a\}\text{ consistent}],\quad\delta:=\Pr[H\vdash b,\ H\nvdash a].$$
The CR probability of revealing $(a,1)$ then $(b,1)$ after $D$ is $P_{(a,b)}:=\Bel^{\rm nc}_D(a)\Bel^{\rm nc}_{D+(a,1)}(b)=\Pr[H\vdash a]$, because $a\vdash b$, so $H\cup\{a\}$ proves $b$ whenever it is consistent. The CR probability of the other order is $P_{(b,a)}:=\Bel^{\rm nc}_D(b)\Bel^{\rm nc}_{D+(b,1)}(a)=\Pr[H\vdash b]\cdot T/\Pl(b)$: by the deduction theorem $H\cup\{b\}\vdash a$ iff $H\vdash b\to a$, and the ratio of $\Unif$-measures of events inside $\{H\text{ consistent}\}$ equals the ratio of their $\Pr$-measures. Two identities hold:
* $\Pl(b)-T=\rho$: $H\nvdash b\to a$ iff $H\cup\{b,\neg a\}$ is consistent, which implies that $H\cup\{b\}$ is consistent; and $\{b,\neg a\}$ is equivalent to $\{e\wedge\neg a\}$.
* $\Pr[H\vdash b]=\Pr[H\vdash a]+\delta$, since $H\vdash a$ implies $H\vdash b$.

If $\Pr[H\vdash a]>0$, then $T\ge\Pr[H\vdash a]>0$ and $\Pr[H\vdash b]>0$, and substituting,
$$\frac{P_{(a,b)}}{P_{(b,a)}}=\frac{\Pr[H\vdash a](T+\rho)}{(\Pr[H\vdash a]+\delta)\,T}=1+\frac{N}{\Pr[H\vdash b]\,T},\qquad N:=\Pr[H\vdash a]\,\rho-\delta\,T\ \ge\ \Pr[H\vdash a]\,\rho-\delta .$$
*Step 2 (disjointness).* For fixed $i$ and $D$, the events $\{H\vdash b_{i,j},\ H\nvdash a_i\}$ are pairwise disjoint in $j$: $e_{i,j}$ and $e_{i,j'}$ ($j<j'$) are incompatible ($e_{i,j}$ asserts $s_{i,j}$, $e_{i,j'}$ denies it), so $H\vdash a_i\vee e_{i,j}$ and $H\vdash a_i\vee e_{i,j'}$ give $H\vdash a_i\vee(e_{i,j}\wedge e_{i,j'})$, hence $H\vdash a_i$. So $\sum_j\delta_{i,j}\le1$.
*Step 3 (lower bounds).* Let $D$ be $\emptyset$ (for (b1)) or $D_{i-1}:=\{(a_{i'},1),(b_{i',j_{i'}},1):i'<i\}$ (for (b2)); $D_{i-1}^\pm$ is implied by $\{a_{i'}:i'<i\}$.
* $\rho_{i,j}\ge\varepsilon_\emptyset$. On the tapes with $A_\omega=\emptyset$ we have $H=B\cup D^\pm$, and $H\cup\{e_{i,j}\wedge\neg a_i\}$ is consistent by (L1): it is implied by the literal set $\{a_{i'}:i'<i\}\cup\{\neg a_i\}\cup\{\text{the literals of }e_{i,j}\}$, one literal per word. These tapes have $\Pr$-mass $\varepsilon_\emptyset/M_A^{\rm nc}(D)\ge\varepsilon_\emptyset$.
* $\Pr[H\vdash a_i]\ge2^{-c}$ for (b2): on the tapes beginning with $p_R$, $H=B\cup\{\forall xR(x)\}\cup D^\pm$ is consistent ($B$ does not mention $R$; interpret $R$ as the whole domain; $D^\pm$ consists of $a_{i'}$ and disjunctions containing them) and proves $a_i$; their $\Pr$-mass is $2^{-c}/M_A^{\rm nc}(D)\ge2^{-c}$.
* For (b1), $D=\emptyset$, $\Pr=\Unif(\cdot\mid C)$ and $\Pr[H\vdash a_1]=\Bel_0(a_1)/(1-\Inc_0)\ge\Bel_0(a_1)>0$ (the one-axiom theory $\{a_1\}$).

*Conclusion.* (b1) By Steps 1 and 3, $N_j\ge\Bel_0(a_1)\varepsilon_\emptyset-\delta_{1,j}$, which is positive unless $\delta_{1,j}\ge\Bel_0(a_1)\varepsilon_\emptyset$; by Step 2 that happens for at most $1/(\Bel_0(a_1)\varepsilon_\emptyset)$ indices. For the others $P_{(a,b)}/P_{(b,a)}>1$. (b2) Build $j_1,j_2,\dots$ in turn. At step $i$, with $D=D_{i-1}$ and $J:=\lceil2^{c+1}/\varepsilon_\emptyset\rceil$, Step 2 gives some $j_i\le J$ with $\delta_{i,j_i}\le1/J\le2^{-c}\varepsilon_\emptyset/2$, and then $N_i\ge2^{-c}\varepsilon_\emptyset-2^{-c}\varepsilon_\emptyset/2=2^{-c}\varepsilon_\emptyset/2$ by Step 3. Since $0<\Pr[H\vdash b]\,T\le1$, $\log(P_{(a,b)}/P_{(b,a)})\ge\log(1+N_i)\ge\log(1+2^{-c}\varepsilon_\emptyset/2)$. The CR predictor's conditionals depend on the revealed data only as a set, and after $i-1$ completed pairs both orders have revealed $D_{i-1}$; so the loss of the order "$b$ first" minus the loss of the order "$a$ first" is $\sum_{i\le n}\log(P^{(i)}_{(a,b)}/P^{(i)}_{(b,a)})\ge n\log(1+2^{-c}\varepsilon_\emptyset/2)$.

(c) Order $(a,a\vee c)$: $\Bel^{\rm nc}_\emptyset(a)=\frac12$ (only $\{a\}$ proves $a$), and then $a\vee c$ is entailed. Order $(a\vee c,a)$: $\Bel^{\rm nc}_\emptyset(a\vee c)=\frac12$; given $a\vee c$, both theories remain consistent and only $\{a\}\cup\{a\vee c\}$ proves $a$, so the second factor is $\frac12$. ∎

**Proposition 7.3 (graded CR) [proved; computed: `c1` A12, `c5` R3; `rb2` R2.7, `rb8` R8.1].**
(a) For consistent $A$, $\nu^{1/2}_A$ is a weak semimeasure on data sets (P1 Def 3.2) with $\nu^{1/2}_A(\emptyset)=1$, and is order independent (a product over the set). Hence $M_A^{1/2}$ is a weak semimeasure with $M_A^{1/2}(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0$, and the conditionals of $M_A^{1/2}/M_A^{1/2}(\emptyset)$ form an order-independent predictor (P1 Prop 3.3, which assumes value $1$ at $\emptyset$).
(b) $M_A^{1/2}\ge M_A$.
(c) It charges for entailed data: for fresh atoms $p,q$ and $D:=\{(p,1)\}$, $M_A^{1/2}(D+(p\vee q,1))\le M_A^{1/2}(D)-\varepsilon_\emptyset/4$, although $B\cup D^\pm\vdash p\vee q$. So $M_A^{1/2}$ is not the law of any theory machine (Prop 2.1(c)).
(d) It depends on how the data are packaged: $M_A(\{(p\wedge q,1)\})=M_A(\{(p,1),(q,1)\})$ (Prop 2.1(c)), but $M_A^{1/2}(\{(p\wedge q,1)\})-M_A^{1/2}(\{(p,1),(q,1)\})\ge\varepsilon_\emptyset/4$.
(e) **(new)** $M_A^{1/2}/M_A^{1/2}(\emptyset)$ is not a containment functional (P1 Def 3.4), not even of a random partial function on $\Sent$ that ignores logic. For every sentence $\varphi$, on the two inputs $\varphi,\neg\varphi$, the Möbius sum $\mathrm{Mob}_{\{\varphi,\neg\varphi\}}(\emptyset)$ of P1 Prop A.2 equals $-\frac12\,\Unif\{\omega\in C:A_\omega\text{ decides neither }\varphi\text{ nor }\neg\varphi\}$ for $M_A^{1/2}$, which is $\le-\frac12\varepsilon_\emptyset<0$ when $B$ does not decide $\varphi$; for $M_A$ the same sum equals $+\Unif\{\omega\in C:A_\omega\text{ decides neither}\}\ge0$.

*Proof.* (a) Let $B\cup A\cup D^\pm$ be consistent (otherwise every term is $0$). If $B\cup A\vdash\varphi^{b^*}$, then $D+(\varphi,b^*)$ keeps the value (consistent, factor $1$) and $D+(\varphi,1-b^*)$ is inconsistent with $A$. If $A$ decides neither, each extension has factor at most $\frac12$. Integrating over $C$ preserves the inequality; dividing by $M_A^{1/2}(\emptyset)>0$ gives a weak semimeasure with value $1$ at $\emptyset$, to which P1 Prop 3.3 applies. (b) For consistent $A$ proving $\psi_D$, $\nu^{1/2}_A(D)=1$. (c) For each consistent $A$ the new datum keeps consistency (it is entailed) and multiplies by $1$ or $\frac12$; for $A=\emptyset$ the value drops from $\frac12$ to $\frac14$. (d) Per consistent theory: if it proves $p\wedge q$, both values are $1$; if it proves exactly one of $p,q$ and is consistent with the other, both are $\frac12$; if it proves neither and is consistent with $p\wedge q$, they are $\frac12$ and $\frac14$; otherwise both are $0$. Integrate; the empty theory contributes $\varepsilon_\emptyset/4$. (e) The Möbius sum is $\nu(\emptyset)-\sum_b\nu(\{(\varphi,b)\})-\sum_b\nu(\{(\neg\varphi,b)\})+\sum_{b,b'}\nu(\{(\varphi,b),(\neg\varphi,b')\})$, computed per consistent theory $A$. If $A$ decides $\varphi$, say $A\vdash\varphi^{b^*}$: the singletons consistent with $A$ are $(\varphi,b^*)$ and $(\neg\varphi,1-b^*)$, both proved (value $1$); the only pair consistent with $A$ is $\{(\varphi,b^*),(\neg\varphi,1-b^*)\}$ (value $1$); the sum is $1-1-1+1=0$. If $A$ decides neither: all four singletons are consistent and unproved ($\frac12$ each); of the four pairs, $\{(\varphi,1),(\neg\varphi,0)\}$ and $\{(\varphi,0),(\neg\varphi,1)\}$ are consistent and unproved ($\frac14$ each), and the other two are inconsistent; the sum is $1-2+\frac12=-\frac12$. Integrate over $C$. The empty-theory tapes decide nothing that $B$ does not decide. For $M_A$, the same computation gives $0$ and $1-0-0+0=1$ (no singleton datum is proved by an undeciding theory), so the sum is the undecided mass. By P1 Prop A.2, a weak $\nu$ with $\nu(\emptyset)=1$ is a containment functional iff all its Möbius sums are nonnegative. ∎

**Proposition 7.4 (the CR predictor decomposes; N1's N10) [proved; computed: `c1` A11].** For every $D$ with $M_A^{\rm nc}(D)>0$ and every $\varphi$,
$$M_A^{\rm nc}(D)\,\Bel^{\rm nc}_D(\varphi)=M_A(D)\,\Bel_D(\varphi)+\Unif\{\omega:H_\omega\text{ consistent},\ B\cup A_\omega\nvdash\psi_D,\ H_\omega\vdash\varphi\}.$$
So the CR predictor is the mixture, with weight $\lambda_D:=M_A(D)/M_A^{\rm nc}(D)$, of the proof-requiring predictor and a "weak theory plus the data" predictor.

*Proof.* Split the CR event by whether $B\cup A_\omega\vdash\psi_D$. If it does, $H_\omega$ is equivalent to $B\cup A_\omega$: consistency of $H_\omega$ is that of $B\cup A_\omega$ and $H_\omega\vdash\varphi$ iff $B\cup A_\omega\vdash\varphi$, which gives $\Unif(E_D\cap O_\varphi)=M_A(D)\Bel_D(\varphi)$. ∎

The weak component contains the empty theory (mass $\ge\varepsilon_\emptyset$), which predicts from the data alone; that is the source of Prop 7.2(a).

### 7.5 Why the proof-requiring variant is primary

The precise reasons, each a result above:
1. **It is the law of a random object.** $M_A$ is the probability that a random consistent theory proves the data. It is a containment functional (Prop 2.1(b),(g)), its cumulative loss is order independent and depends only on the content $\Cn(D^\pm)$ of the data (Prop 2.1(c),(d)), and its predictions are posterior averages of what individual theories prove.
2. **It has the learning guarantees.** Dominance over every theory-machine law (Thm 4.1), loss at most $K_\equiv(T)+c$ against every consistent r.e. theory deciding the data (Thm 5.1), mistake bounds and merging (Prop 5.3, Thm 5.4).
3. **The 0/1 CR weight does not discriminate.** It is bounded below by $\varepsilon_\emptyset$ on all consistent data (Prop 7.1(b)): data only eliminate theories and never reweight the survivors, so the inductive work is done by adding the data to the hypothesis. Its proper predictor (Dempster) pays at least a constant on every datum not entailed by earlier data (Prop 7.2(a)), which on streams decided by a simple consistent theory is linearly many bits where PR pays $O(1)$; and its cumulative loss depends on the reveal order, already for $M_A$ itself, with a linear gap (Prop 7.2(b)).
4. **Graded CR is not a law of theories.** It dominates PR, but it charges for entailed data, depends on how the data are packaged, and is not even a containment functional (Prop 7.3(c)–(e)); its advantage over PR can come within $2|D|+O(\log)$ bits of $K(\dom D)+|D|$ (Prop 11.2), which measures packaging, not better induction.

In one sentence: under PR a theory must *explain* every datum; under CR it need only *tolerate* it, and toleration is too weak to discriminate between theories. (For complete theories the two coincide, Prop 10.1(f); the distinction exists because the random theory of $M_A$ is incomplete.)

## 8. Limits and identification (target T8)

**Lemma 8.0 (majority vote) [proved].** Let $Z$ be a decidable set of sentences, $\Phi$ an oracle machine that on tape $\omega$ and input $\sigma\in Z$ outputs $\Phi^\omega(\sigma)\in\{0,1\}$ or diverges, $S\subseteq Z$, $g:S\to\{0,1\}$, and $\mathcal G$ a Borel set of tapes with $\Unif(\mathcal G)>0$ such that for every $\omega\in\mathcal G$, $\Phi^\omega(\sigma)$ is defined for all $\sigma\in Z$ and equals $g(\sigma)$ for $\sigma\in S$. Then some total computable $h:Z\to\{0,1\}$ extends $g$.

*Proof.* By outer regularity there is an open $U\supseteq\mathcal G$ with $\Unif(U)<\frac43\Unif(\mathcal G)$; writing $U$ as a disjoint union of cylinders, some cylinder $[u]$ has $\Unif(\mathcal G\cap[u])>\frac34\,2^{-|u|}$. For $b\in\{0,1\}$ let $O_b(\sigma):=\{\omega\in[u]:\Phi^\omega(\sigma)=b\}$: effectively open uniformly in $\sigma$ (a halting computation reads a finite prefix), and disjoint for $b=0,1$; their measures are left-c.e., with computable approximations from below. Let $h(\sigma)$ be the $b$ with the larger approximated measure at the first stage where the two approximations sum to more than $\frac12\,2^{-|u|}$. That stage exists for every $\sigma\in Z$, because $\Unif(O_0(\sigma)\cup O_1(\sigma))\ge\Unif(\mathcal G\cap[u])>\frac34\,2^{-|u|}$. For $\sigma\in S$ the wrong set $O_{1-g(\sigma)}(\sigma)$ lies in $[u]\setminus\mathcal G$, of measure $<\frac14\,2^{-|u|}$; so at that stage the right set's approximation exceeds $\frac14\,2^{-|u|}$, more than the wrong one's. ∎

This is the averaging argument behind de Leeuw, Moore, Shannon and Shapiro (1956), in the form needed below. It replaces scout F's use of that theorem (F26(a)) and of the PA-degree facts (F27). `logic` uses it as its Prop 2.3 and identifies the lottery (`logic` Thm 2.2) as exactly the place where majority vote over consistency constraints of unbounded arity breaks.

Throughout, $D_\infty$ is a consistent infinite stream, $E_n:=E_{D_n}$ (decreasing), $E_\infty:=\bigcap_nE_n=\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(A_\omega)\supseteq D_\infty^\pm\}$, $a_n:=\Unif(E_n)$, $a:=\Unif(E_\infty)$, $P_n:=\Unif(\cdot\mid E_n)$.

**Proposition 8.1 (limit posterior) [proved; computed: `c1` A13].**
(a) If $a>0$, then $\sup_{\mathcal X}|P_n(\mathcal X)-P_\infty(\mathcal X)|\le(a_n-a)/a_n\to0$ with $P_\infty:=\Unif(\cdot\mid E_\infty)$. Hence $\sup_\chi|\Bel_{D_n}(\chi)-\Bel_\infty(\chi)|\to0$ with $\Bel_\infty(\chi):=P_\infty(O_\chi)$, likewise for $\Dis$ and $\Indep$, and the cumulative loss converges to $-\log(a/M_A(\emptyset))$.
(b) If $a=0$, then $M_A(D_n)\to0$ and the cumulative loss tends to $\infty$.
(c) $a\ge\Unif_A(\Ext T)\ge2^{-K_\equiv(T)-c}$ whenever some r.e. $T$ with $B\cup T$ consistent proves every datum of $D_\infty$.
(d) For every set $\Gamma$ of sentences with $\Cn(\Gamma)=\Cn(D_\infty^\pm)$, complete or not: $E_\infty=\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(A_\omega)\supseteq\Cn(\Gamma)\}$, so, if $a>0$, $P_\infty$ is the prior restricted to the tapes whose theory is a consistent extension of $\Gamma$.

*Proof.* (a) For a Borel $\mathcal X$ let $x:=\Unif(\mathcal X\cap E_\infty)\le a$ and $y_n:=\Unif(\mathcal X\cap(E_n\setminus E_\infty))\le a_n-a$. Then $|(x+y_n)/a_n-x/a|=|y_na-x(a_n-a)|/(a\,a_n)\le\max(y_na,\,x(a_n-a))/(a\,a_n)\le(a_n-a)/a_n$, and $a_n\downarrow a$ by continuity from above. The loss statement is Prop 2.1(d). (b) Continuity from above. (c) As in Thm 5.1. (d) A theory contains $D_\infty^\pm$ in its consequences iff it contains $\Cn(D_\infty^\pm)=\Cn(\Gamma)$. ∎

**Question Q1** (scout F). Does $a>0$ imply that some consistent r.e. theory proves $D_\infty^\pm$? By Prop 8.6(a) below, yes when $\Cn(D_\infty^\pm)$ is complete (then it is decidable and is itself such a theory). **In general no**: `logic` Cor 2.5, via the lottery of `logic` Thm 2.2 (re-verified there from scout N1's Thm E1; proved in the `logic` development record and found correct by its referee A), gives a stream with $a>0$ on which no consistent r.e. theory proves the stream; `logic` Thm 2.9(b) gives one inside true arithmetic with true data. The answer is yes for bounded inconsistency arity (`logic` Prop 2.3), when true random theories have positive mass (`logic` Prop 2.4), for co-r.e. streams over block-decidable classes (`logic` Prop 2.7) and for $\Pi_1$ streams with a true background $\supseteq\Q$ (`logic` Cor 3.5).

**Proposition 8.2 (complete decidable truths are identified up to equivalence) [proved].** Let $T^*$ be consistent and r.e. with $B\cup T^*$ complete (hence decidable), and $\Cn(D_\infty^\pm)=\Cn(T^*)$ (for instance, every sentence appears with its $T^*$-label, or $T^*$'s axioms appear labelled true). Then $P_n(\{\omega:\Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn(T^*)\})\to1$, $\sup_\chi|\Bel_{D_n}(\chi)-\ind[T^*\vdash\chi]|\to0$, $\sup_\chi\Indep_{D_n}(\chi)\to0$, and the cumulative loss converges to $-\log(\Unif_A[T^*]/M_A(\emptyset))\le K_\equiv(T^*)+c$. If $T^*$ is axiomatised by one sentence $\tau$ and $(\tau,1)\in D_n$, then $P_n([T^*])=1$ from then on.

*Proof.* A complete consistent theory has no proper consistent extension, so $E_\infty=\{\omega:\Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn(T^*)\}$ (Prop 8.1(d)), of positive measure by Thm 4.1(i); on it the trichotomy is $\ind[T^*\vdash\chi]$ for all $\chi$ at once. Apply Prop 8.1. If $\psi_{D_n}\vdash\tau$, then $E_n=E_\infty$. ∎

*Examples* (completeness and decidability standard; references not re-verified): Presburger arithmetic, dense linear orders without endpoints, real closed fields, algebraically closed fields of fixed characteristic.

**Proposition 8.3 (incomplete r.e. truths: two-valued against three-valued data) [proved; computed: `c1` A14].** Let $T^*$ be consistent and r.e. with $B\cup T^*$ incomplete.
(a) *Two-valued data.* If $\Cn(D_\infty^\pm)=\Cn(T^*)$, then $P_\infty=\Unif(\cdot\mid\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(A_\omega)\supseteq\Cn(T^*)\})$, the prior restricted to the consistent extensions of $T^*$ (Prop 8.1(d)); this is not identification. For every $\psi$ independent of $B\cup T^*$:
$$\lim_n\Bel_{D_n}(\psi)=\frac{\Unif_A(\Ext(T^*+\psi))}{\Unif_A(\Ext T^*)}\in(0,1),\quad\lim_n\Dis_{D_n}(\psi)=\frac{\Unif_A(\Ext(T^*+\neg\psi))}{\Unif_A(\Ext T^*)}\in(0,1),\quad\lim_n\Indep_{D_n}(\psi)\ge\frac{\Unif_A[T^*]}{\Unif_A(\Ext T^*)}>0 .$$
(b) *The most probable class can be a strict extension.* In the literal setting, with fresh atoms $\alpha_i:=R(t_{w_i})$ for a computable sequence of distinct words, let $U:=\{\alpha_i:i\in\mathbb N\}$ and $T_j:=U\setminus\{\alpha_j\}$. Then $\sum_j\Unif_A[T_j]\le1$, so $\Unif_A[T_j]\to0$, while $\Unif_A[U]\ge2^{-c}$. Hence for all but finitely many $j$, on data presenting $T_j$ (e.g. $\alpha_i$ labelled true for all $i\ne j$), the limit posterior gives the class $[U]$, a strict extension of $T_j$ that decides $\alpha_j$, more probability than $[T_j]$. Moreover a most probable class exists, and it is a strict extension of $T_j$.
(c) *Three-valued data.* If every sentence appears with its $T^*$-trichotomy label, then $\Unif(\{\Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn(T^*)\}\mid E^3_{D_n})\to1$. A three-valued presentation of $T^*$ on a set $S$ of sentences leaves the prior restricted to the theories whose trichotomy agrees with $T^*$'s on $S$.

*Proof.* (a) $E_\infty$ is the event of the display by Prop 8.1(d). $T^*+\psi$, $T^*+\neg\psi$ and $T^*$ are consistent r.e. theories, so all three ratios are positive by Thm 4.1(i); each of the first two is $<1$ because the other is positive; the classes $\{\Cn(A)=\Cn(T^*)\}$ lie in the independent part. (b) $T_j\nvdash\alpha_j$ by (L2) while $T_{j'}\vdash\alpha_j$ for $j'\ne j$, so the classes $[T_j]$ are pairwise distinct and their events disjoint; hence $\sum_j\Unif_A[T_j]\le1$. $U$ is r.e. with an index of complexity $O(1)$, consistent by (L1). The limit posterior odds of $[U]$ against $[T_j]$ are $\Unif_A[U]/\Unif_A[T_j]$ by (a). *Existence of a most probable class:* under $P_\infty$ the classes partition the event $E_\infty$, so only finitely many classes have limit probability above the positive number $\Unif_A[U]/\Unif_A(\Ext T_j)$, the limit probability of $[U]$; a maximiser exists among them, lies in $\Ext(T_j)$, and is not $[T_j]$ (which is beaten by $[U]$), so it is a strict extension. (c) A consistent theory that proves every $T^*$-theorem and decides no $T^*$-independent sentence has $\Cn(A)=\Cn(T^*)$: a theorem $\sigma\notin\Cn(T^*)$ of $A$ would be $T^*$-refutable (then $A$ is inconsistent) or $T^*$-independent (then labelled $\iota$ and decided). The class has positive measure by Thm 4.1(i); apply Prop 8.1 to $M_A^3$, whose proof is the same. ∎

(b) settles scout F's F24(a) remark, which was a proof sketch needing an upper bound on $\Unif_A[T^*]$ for a complicated subtheory of $\PA$. The disjointness argument gives such bounds for all but finitely many members of any infinite family of pairwise inequivalent theories with a common simple consistent extension. The $\PA$ version (every r.e. $T^*$ with $\Q\subseteq T^*\subseteq\PA$ and $K_\equiv(T^*)$ large) follows from Prop 11.5(c), given Solovay's theorem in its probabilistic reading.

**Proposition 8.4 (text versus informant) [proved; known in substance: Gold 1967 (secondary sources)].** In the literal setting, with fresh atoms $\alpha_i$ as in Prop 8.3(b), let $\Lambda_k:=\{\alpha_i:i<k\}$ and $\Lambda_\infty:=\{\alpha_i:i\in\mathbb N\}$. Call a sequence $(\sigma_t)$ of sentences a *text* for $T$ if $\Cn(\{\sigma_t:t\})=\Cn(T)$ (two-valued data $(\sigma_t,1)$; a datum $(\varphi,0)$ is the datum $(\neg\varphi,1)$). No function $F$ from finite sequences of sentences to deductive classes (computable or not) identifies in the limit, on every text, every member of $\{[\Lambda_k]:k\ge1\}\cup\{[\Lambda_\infty]\}$. In particular no learner identifies all consistent r.e. theories from two-valued data, while three-valued data (an informant) identify every consistent r.e. $T^*$ in the limit posterior (Prop 8.3(c)), non-computably.

*Proof.* Build a text for $\Lambda_\infty$ in stages. Stage $k\ge1$ appends $\alpha_{k-1}$ repeatedly until $F$ outputs $[\Lambda_k]$; this happens, because the sequence built so far continued by $\alpha_{k-1}$ forever is a text for $\Lambda_k$, on which $F$ converges to $[\Lambda_k]$. The resulting infinite sequence contains every $\alpha_i$, so it is a text for $\Lambda_\infty$, and $F$ outputs $[\Lambda_k]\ne[\Lambda_\infty]$ for infinitely many $k$ (by (L2), $\Lambda_k\nvdash\alpha_k$). ∎

So the failure of two-valued SAI to identify incomplete truths (Prop 8.3(a)) is not an artefact of its prior: no learner can do it. This is Gold's text/informant distinction (N1's N12): Gold (1967) shows that a class containing all finite languages and one infinite language is not identifiable from text, by the same locking argument. The paper should cite it as the learning-theoretic shadow of Prop 8.3, not as new.

**Proposition 8.5 (computable structures; the basic one-tape ω-gap) [proved].** Let $\mathfrak M$ be a computable structure in which every element is the value of a closed term, $\mathrm{Diag}(\mathfrak M)$ its atomic diagram (closed atomic sentences and their negations true in $\mathfrak M$; decidable), and let the data list $\mathrm{Diag}(\mathfrak M)$ in a computable order, labelled true.
(a) $L_A(D_n)\le K_\equiv(\mathrm{Diag}\,\mathfrak M)+c\le K(\mathfrak M)+c$, with $K(\mathfrak M)$ the complexity of an index of $\mathfrak M$ (for $B$ true in $\mathfrak M$).
(b) $P_\infty$ is the prior restricted to the consistent extensions of $\mathrm{Diag}(\mathfrak M)$ (Prop 8.1(d)). For $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$, $\mathfrak M=(\mathbb N,0,S,+,\cdot,<)$, **$B=\emptyset$ (or any $B$ true both in $\mathbb N$ and in the model $\mathbb N\sqcup\{a\}$ of the proof)**, and $\theta:=\forall x\,(x+0=x)$, $\lim_n\Bel_{D_n}(\theta)$ and $\lim_n\Dis_{D_n}(\theta)$ both lie in $(0,1)$: instances never settle the universal sentence. (If instead $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$, then $B\vdash\theta$ and $\lim\Bel_{D_n}(\theta)=1$.)
(c) If $\Th(\mathfrak M)$ is decidable and the data present it, Prop 8.2 applies.

*Proof.* (a) Thm 5.1 with $T=\mathrm{Diag}(\mathfrak M)$. (b) Prop 8.3(a), with $\psi=\theta$; it needs $\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbb N)\cup\{\theta\}$ and $\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbb N)\cup\{\neg\theta\}$ consistent with $B$. The first holds in $\mathbb N$. For the second take $\mathbb N\sqcup\{a\}$ with $S(a):=a$, $a+y:=0$ for every $y$ (including $y=a$), $y+a:=a$ for $y\in\mathbb N$, $a\cdot y=y\cdot a:=a$ for every $y$, and $a$ $<$-incomparable with everything. Closed terms take values in $\mathbb N$, which is closed under the operations, so the diagram holds; and $a+0=0\ne a$. ∎

This is the one-tape form of AI-paper Prop B.8 and of the Bayesian ω-gap (AI-paper Thm 3.9). Its anatomy and a uniform Gödelian form are `logic` Props 5.1–5.2 and Thm 5.3.

**Proposition 8.6 (non-decidable complete truths) [proved].**
(a) If $X:=\Cn(D_\infty^\pm)$ is complete and not decidable (for example $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$, $B\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)$ decidable, and every sentence labelled by truth in $\mathbb N$), then $a=0$: $M_A(D_n)\to0$ and the loss is unbounded.
(b) If $(\sigma_t)$ is a computable enumeration of all sentences and $D_n$ labels $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_n$ by a complete consistent $X$, then $K(b_{1:n}\mid n)-c\le L_A(D_n)\le n+K(n)+c$.

*Proof.* (a) On $E_\infty$ the theory is consistent and contains the complete $X$, so $\Cn(A_\omega)=X$. Let $\Phi^\omega(\sigma)$ be the first of $\sigma,\neg\sigma$ found provable from $B\cup A_\omega$. On $E_\infty$ it is total and decides $X$. If $a>0$, Lemma 8.0 with $Z=S:=\Sent$, $\mathcal G=E_\infty$ and $g$ the characteristic function of $X$ gives a computable decision procedure for $X$, a contradiction. That $\Th(\mathbb N)$ is undecidable follows, for instance, from the inseparability step of Prop 8.7. (b) Lower bound: Prop 6.1(b), as $\dom D_n$ is computable from $n$. Upper bound: $K_A(D_n)\le K(\psi_{D_n})+c\le K(n)+n+c'$ (given $n$, the labels are $n$ bits), and Thm 5.1's last chain. ∎

The coin-free versions are AI-paper Prop 7.11 and TF Lemma 1.6. The growth rate on $\Th(\mathbb N)$ in natural enumerations (scout F's F26(c), N1's N7) is `logic` Thm 6.1 ($L_A(D_n)\ge C'^{-\delta}n^\delta-K(n)-c$), which uses the lower bound in (b).

**Proposition 8.7 (random theories above $\Q$ are almost surely incomplete) [proved, given that $\Q$ strongly represents every computable set and the diagonal lemma for $\Q$ (standard textbook facts); a new application of the fact that separating sets of effectively inseparable pairs have measure zero].** Let $\mathcal L\supseteq\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$.
(a) For every theory machine $G$: $\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A^G_\omega\text{ is consistent, proves every axiom of }\Q,\text{ and decides every }\mathcal L_{\rm ar}\text{-sentence}\}=0$. In particular, if $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$, the consistent random theory of $M_A$ is almost surely incomplete, and every posterior gives probability $0$ to complete theories.
(b) For every function machine $F$ over $X=\Sent_{\mathcal L_{\rm ar}}$ with labels $\{0,1\}$ (P1 Def 3.6): $\Unif\{\omega:f^F_\omega\text{ is total, labels every }\Q\text{-provable sentence }1\text{ and every }\Q\text{-refutable sentence }0\}=0$; likewise with $\PA$ or any consistent r.e. extension of $\Q$ in place of $\Q$.

*Proof.* *Inseparability.* No total computable $h:\Sent_{\mathcal L_{\rm ar}}\to\{0,1\}$ is $1$ on the theorems of $\Q$ and $0$ on its refutable sentences. Otherwise $\{n:h(n)=1\}$ (via Gödel numbers) is strongly represented in $\Q$ by some $R_h(x)$, and the diagonal lemma gives $\sigma$ with $\Q\vdash\sigma\leftrightarrow\neg R_h(\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner)$. If $h(\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner)=1$, then $\Q\vdash R_h(\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner)$, so $\Q\vdash\neg\sigma$ and $h$ is $1$ on a refutable sentence. If $h(\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner)=0$, then $\Q\vdash\neg R_h(\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner)$, so $\Q\vdash\sigma$ and $h$ is $0$ on a theorem. A separator for a consistent r.e. $T\supseteq\Q$ separates $\Q$'s pair too.
(a) On the event, $\Phi^\omega(\sigma):=$ the first of $\sigma,\neg\sigma$ found provable from $B\cup A^G_\omega$ is total on $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-sentences and separates $\Q$'s theorems from its refutable sentences (a consistent theory proving $\Q$ proves every $\Q$-theorem and refutes every $\Q$-refutable sentence). Lemma 8.0 (with $Z:=\Sent_{\mathcal L_{\rm ar}}$, $S$ the $\Q$-decided sentences) would give a computable separator. (b) $\Phi^\omega(\sigma):=f^F_\omega(\sigma)$, computed by running $F$ on $\sigma$ with tape $\omega$. ∎

**Corollary 8.8 (a theory-machine law above $\Q$ has a local defect) [proved; computed: `c5` R5 (finite analogue)].** Let $\mathcal L\supseteq\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$ and let $G$ be a theory machine with $\nu_G(\emptyset)>0$ whose random theory proves $\Q$ almost surely on its consistency event $C^G$ (for instance, any $G$ when $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$). Then $\nu_G$ has positive local defect at $D=\emptyset$ for some $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-sentence: $\nu_G(\{(\varphi,1)\})+\nu_G(\{(\varphi,0)\})<\nu_G(\emptyset)$. In particular "a theory plus a fair coin on every sentence it leaves undecided", read as a theory-machine law with zero local defect everywhere, does not exist above $\Q$.

*Proof.* By Prop 2.1(b) at $D=\emptyset$, the defect at $\varphi$ is $\nu^3_G(\{(\varphi,\iota)\})=\Unif\{\omega\in C^G:A^G_\omega\text{ decides neither }\varphi\text{ nor }\neg\varphi\}$. If it were $0$ for every $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-sentence, then, by the countable union, almost every $\omega\in C^G$ would have a theory deciding every $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-sentence; with the hypothesis, the event of Prop 8.7(a) would have measure $\Unif(C^G)=\nu_G(\emptyset)>0$, a contradiction. ∎

*Consequences and related work.* (i) With $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$, the trichotomy of a sentence can tend to $(1,0,0)$ or $(0,1,0)$, sentence by sentence, but never through a complete random theory. (ii) Prop 8.7(b) is the random-labeller form of the observation in Hänni's working notes that no total computable labeller gets every $\PA$-provable and $\PA$-refutable sentence right: a random tape does not help, almost surely. (iii) Cor 8.8 is one reason why Q6 (§11.4) is not trivial. (iv) Prop 8.7 also follows from two known facts: separators of effectively inseparable pairs (such as $\Q$'s) have PA degree, and the PA degrees form a null class (Jockusch–Soare 1972; secondary sources). Lemma 8.0 makes the proof self-contained; the result is a new application, not a new theorem. A quantitative relative is Levin's "Forbidden information" (*J. ACM* 60(2), 2013), which bounds the probability that a randomised algorithm completes $\PA$ on formulas of size $n$ by about $2^{-n}$ (abstract level, as recorded by `logic` §3.4).

## 9. The generative variant (target T9)

*Setting.* Fix AI-paper L1 (Def 2.9): a derivation grammar with positive rule probabilities, a full-support instantiation grammar and admissible parameters. For a set $A$ of axioms with an enumeration $(\chi_1,\chi_2,\dots)$, let the citation weights be $\propto2^{-j}$ on $\chi_j$ (normalised; none if $A=\emptyset$), and let $P_A$ be the resulting law on sentences. One-tape form: split the tape into $\omega^{(0)},\omega^{(1)},\dots$ by a computable interleaving; $A_\omega:=A^{G_U}_{\omega^{(0)}}$ in emission order; the $i$-th datum $X_i$ is sampled from $P_{A_\omega}$ with the coins $\omega^{(i)}$. The universal law of unlabelled data streams is $\Xi(X_{1:n}):=\int\prod_{i\le n}P_{A_\omega}(X_i)\,d\Unif(\omega)$, and the generative posterior has density $\propto\prod_iP_{A_\omega}(X_i)$ with respect to $\Unif$.

**Proposition 9.1.**
(i) **[proof sketch]** *Supports.* With $B=\emptyset$ (or $B$ included among the axioms), $\operatorname{supp}P_A=\Th(A)$ for every $A$. AI-paper Lemma 2.10(c) proves this for finite template theories; for an axiom set in which every axiom has positive citation weight the same argument applies (each derivation tree citing finitely many axioms has positive probability). Not written out.
(ii) **[proved, given (i)]** *Reweighting.* On data $X_1,\dots,X_n$, a consistent $A$ has positive likelihood iff $A\vdash X_i$ for all $i$, the proof-requiring compatibility condition for $D:=\{(X_i,1)\}$. So on consistent theories the generative posterior is the PR posterior reweighted by $\prod_iP_A(X_i)\le1$. Inconsistent theories have positive likelihood (their support is everything) and pay the size principle: a theory with mass $\varepsilon$ outside the support of the data law loses at least $\log\frac1{1-\varepsilon}$ bits per datum in expectation (AI-paper Lemma 2.21).
(iii) **[proved, given (i)]** *Question conditioning.* For a yes/no question $q=\{\varphi,\neg\varphi\}$ and consistent $A$: $P_A(q)>0$ iff $A$ decides $\varphi$, and then $P_A(\varphi^b\mid X\in q)=\ind[A\vdash\varphi^b]$. Hence the generative predictive probability that the next datum answers $q$ with $\varphi^b$, given that it answers $q$, is $\int\pi_n(d\omega)P_{A_\omega}(q)\ind[A_\omega\vdash\varphi^b]\big/\int\pi_n(d\omega)P_{A_\omega}(q)$ over consistent theories (inconsistent theories, which pay the size principle, contribute their own $P_A(\varphi^b\mid X\in q)$): PR's $\Bel$ under a posterior reweighted by how likely each theory was to have *asked* $q$. "Independent" gets probability $0$: the model presupposes that asked questions are answered.
(iv) **[proof sketch, given Doob's consistency theorem (known; AI-paper Thm 4.2)]** *Identification.* If $X_1,X_2,\dots$ are i.i.d. from $P_{T^*}$, the law of the deterministic machine emitting a consistent r.e. $T^*$, the generative posterior probability of $\{\omega:\Th(A_\omega)=\Th(T^*)\}$ tends to $1$ almost surely; the PR posterior on two-valued data presenting $T^*$ only reaches the prior on $\Ext(T^*)$ (Prop 8.3(a)). Sketch: $\theta(\omega):=\operatorname{supp}P_{A_\omega}=\Th(A_\omega)$ is almost surely a function of the infinite sample (the set of sentences that occur), so Doob's theorem gives posterior consistency for $\theta$ at $\Unif$-almost every $\omega$; the tapes whose theory has law exactly $P_{T^*}$ form a set of positive measure, so the statement holds under $P_{T^*}^\infty$. Missing: measurability of $\omega\mapsto P_{A_\omega}$, and (i) for r.e. axiom sets.

*Proof of (ii) and (iii).* (ii) By (i), $P_A(X_i)>0$ iff $X_i\in\Th(A)$. (iii) $\operatorname{supp}P_A=\Th(A)$ contains at most one of $\varphi,\neg\varphi$ (consistency) and contains one iff $A$ decides $\varphi$; Bayes' rule. ∎

*Reading.* The generative variant buys identification of the theorem set with a model of which sentences get stated, the size principle. This is the bridge to the AI-paper's inducer (its §4). `logic` §5.4 discusses the generative closure of the ω-gap.

## 10. Comparison with Demski-type priors (target T10)

Two constructions from the literature, known here only through secondary descriptions (§14):
* **Demski's prior** (Demski 2012): draw sentences i.i.d. from a measure $\mu$ with full support on $\Sent$, and keep a drawn sentence iff it is consistent with $B$ and the sentences kept so far (one description also skips sentences already implied, which does not change the outcome). Let $S_t$ be the kept set after $t$ draws, $T_\infty:=\Cn(\bigcup_tS_t)$ and $P_\mu(\varphi):=\Pr[\varphi\in T_\infty]$.
* **The modified Demski prior** (Fallenstein, Demski and Garrabrant, Intelligent Agent Foundations Forum posts, c. 2015; the basis of Garrabrant, Fallenstein, Demski and Soares, *Inductive Coherence*, arXiv:1604.05288, 2016): at each step, run a fixed universal machine on fresh random bits and read its output as a set of sentences (a theory, possibly infinite, such as $\PA$ with its schema); keep the drawn theory iff it is consistent with $B$ and with what has been kept. In our notation each draw is $A_{\omega^{(t)}}$ for an independent tape $\omega^{(t)}$. Let $T'_\infty$ be the deductive closure of everything kept.

The search snippets (§14) also report, for the modified prior, positive probability for computable infinite sets of sentences and a dominance property over the laws of machine outputs ("Strict dominance for the modified Demski prior": dominance of the universal semimeasure on a bit sequence encoded by a fresh predicate). These are the counterparts of Thm 4.1 and of `sfi` Cor 5.2(d) ($M_A$ on unary literal data is Solomonoff's $M$ up to constants).

**Proposition 10.1.**
(a) **[proved; computed: `c5` R4, `ra1` B]** $T_\infty$ is consistent and complete with probability $1$. Hence $P_\mu$ is a probability on the Lindenbaum algebra of $B$ (coherent; $P_\mu(\varphi)+P_\mu(\neg\varphi)=1$), and $\Pr[\Cn(T_\infty)=\Cn(T)]=0$ for every incomplete $T$.
(b) **[proved]** Under $M_A$: for every $\varphi$ undecided by $B$, the prior independent mass $\Bel_0(\top)-\Bel_0(\varphi)-\Bel_0(\neg\varphi)$ is at least $\varepsilon_\emptyset>0$; every r.e. $T$ with $B\cup T$ consistent, complete or not, has $\Unif_A[T]\ge2^{-K_\equiv(T)-c}>0$; and above $\Q$ the random theory is almost surely incomplete (Prop 8.7).
(c) **[proved, given Church's theorem with Kalmár's reduction to one binary relation symbol (known)]** For $B=\emptyset$ and a language with a binary relation symbol, the map $(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_t)\mapsto S_t$ is not computable (already $t=1$ asks whether $\sigma_1$ is satisfiable), and it is computable relative to $\emptyset'$ (consistency of a finite set is $\Pi^0_1$). The same holds for the modified process (a single draw can output one sentence). A theory machine emits sentences unconditionally; consistency enters $M_A$ only as the event measured, so $M_A$ is defined without a consistency test during sampling, and its values are $\emptyset'$-computable (Prop 3.4), though $M_A$ is not lsc (Thm 3.2).
(d) *Remark, not a theorem.* $P_\mu(\varphi)\ge\mu(\varphi)$ for every $B$-consistent $\varphi$ (first draw), so under Demski's original prior finite theories get weight from the sentence measure, while an infinite theory enters only through infinitely many draws. Under the modified prior and under $M_A$ an infinite decidable schema costs the length of its generator (Thm 4.1(i)). No quantitative comparison of the weights of complete theories is claimed.
(e) **(new) [proved; computed: `c5` R4, `ra1` B′]** $T'_\infty$ (the modified process) is consistent and complete with probability $1$; so the modified prior is also a coherent probability over completions, and gives every incomplete theory class probability $0$. (The variant that keeps the sentences of a drawn theory one by one, as long as they are consistent with what is kept, has the same property, by the same proof.)
(f) **(new) [proved; computed: `c5` R4]** For a complete consistent theory $T$ (i.e. $B\cup T$ complete and consistent) and every data set $D$: $B\cup T\vdash\psi_D$ iff $B\cup T\cup D^\pm$ is consistent. Hence for any prior concentrated on complete theories, such as those of (a) and (e), proof-requiring and consistency-requiring compatibility coincide, the trichotomy has no independent outcome, and conditioning on $D$ restricts to the complete theories that contain $D^\pm$.

*Proof.* (a) Fix $\varphi$. With probability $1$ it is drawn at some step, since $\mu(\varphi)>0$. At the first such step $t$, either $S_{t-1}\cup\{\varphi\}$ is consistent and $\varphi$ is kept, or $S_{t-1}\vdash\neg\varphi$. Either way $T_\infty$ decides $\varphi$. There are countably many $\varphi$. Every $S_t$ is consistent, so their union is (compactness). (b) Thm 4.1, and the empty-theory tapes (Prop 7.1). (c) Church's theorem with Kalmár's reduction: satisfiability of first-order sentences in a language with one binary relation symbol is undecidable. (e) Fix $\varphi$. At each step, the drawn theory is $\{\varphi\}$ with probability at least $2^{-K(\varphi)-c}>0$ (the tapes beginning with a program for the machine emitting $\varphi$), independently across steps; so with probability $1$ some step draws $\{\varphi\}$. At the first such step, either it is consistent with what was kept, and is kept, or what was kept proves $\neg\varphi$. Either way $T'_\infty$ decides $\varphi$; take the countable union; consistency by compactness as in (a). For the one-by-one variant, the same step decides $\varphi$. (f) A complete consistent $B\cup T$ proves $\psi_D$ or $\neg\psi_D$, and is consistent with $\psi_D$ iff it does not prove $\neg\psi_D$. ∎

*What this says.* Demski's prior, the modified prior and $M_A$ are algorithmic-probability-like priors over first-order theories. **The modified prior and $M_A$ share the sampling stage** (a universal machine on random bits emits a set of sentences), and with it program-length credit for infinite schemas and dominance over machine laws; that stage is not new here. They differ in the following ways, each a result above:
1. *Completion.* The Demski-type priors iterate draws until the theory is complete (Prop 10.1(a),(e)). $M_A$ uses a single draw, so its random theory is incomplete with positive probability (almost surely above $\Q$, Prop 8.7) and incomplete r.e. theories get positive class probability (Prop 10.1(b)).
2. *What a prediction is.* A coherent probability of truth over completions, against a belief function of provability with an independent outcome (Prop 2.2), whose defect is the semimeasure defect (Prop 2.1(b)).
3. *What is conditioned on.* For complete theories, proof-requiring and consistency-requiring conditioning coincide (Prop 10.1(f)); for $M_A$ they differ, and the proof-requiring one is the one with learning guarantees (§7.5). The distinction between explaining and tolerating data exists only because the hypotheses are incomplete.
4. *Where consistency enters.* At every step of the Demski-type processes (an oracle is needed to run them, Prop 10.1(c)), against once, as the event measured, in $M_A$ (Prop 3.1).

So what is new in $M_A$ is the single-draw proof-requiring law and the results about it (§2–§11), not the sampling of theories by a universal machine. The related-work section of the paper must cite the modified prior next to Demski (2012).

### 11.1 Q5: the normalised loss of $M_A$ is order dependent

**Theorem 11.1 (order dependence in the normalised convention) [proved; computed: `c2` B3, B4; `ra1` A; `rb4` R4.1, R4.2].**
(a) Let $a,c$ be sentences with $B\cup\{a\}$ consistent and $B\cup\{\neg a\}$ consistent and deciding neither $c$ nor $\neg c$. Let $D:=\{(a,1),(a\vee c,1)\}$, and for $x,y\in Y_3$ let $p_{xy}:=\Unif\{\omega\in C:\mathrm{tri}_\omega(a)=x,\ \mathrm{tri}_\omega(a\vee c)=y\}$ (Prop 2.1(g)). Then $p_{10}=p_{1\iota}=p_{\iota0}=0$, and the normalised probabilities of $D$ in the orders $(a,a\vee c)$ and $(a\vee c,a)$, $N_{a\to b}$ and $N_{b\to a}$, satisfy
$$\frac{N_{b\to a}}{N_{a\to b}}=1+\frac{(p_{11}+p_{01})\,p_{0\iota}+p_{\iota1}\,(p_{00}+p_{0\iota})}{(p_{11}+p_{01}+p_{\iota1}+p_{00})\,(p_{11}+p_{01})}\ >\ 1 .$$
So the normalised cumulative loss of $M_A$ on $D$ is strictly smaller in the order $(a\vee c,a)$, while the semimeasure-convention loss is the same in both orders.
(b) *A linear gap.* Let $a_i,c_i$ ($i\ge1$) be fresh atoms (Def 1.1′; all words pairwise distinct). For $y\in\{0,1\}^n$ put $\alpha_i:=a_i^{y_i}$, $\beta_i:=\alpha_i\vee c_i$ and $D_n(y):=\{(\alpha_i,1),(\beta_i,1):i\le n\}$. Compare the orders $\mathrm A=(\alpha_1,\beta_1,\alpha_2,\beta_2,\dots)$ and $\mathrm B=(\beta_1,\alpha_1,\beta_2,\alpha_2,\dots)$. For every $y$ the normalised loss of order A exceeds that of order B by at least $\sum_{i\le n}\log(1+v_i(y))\ge0$, where $v_i(y)$ is the posterior probability, after the first $i-1$ pairs, that the random theory refutes $\alpha_i$ and does not decide $c_i$. The average of this difference over $y\in\{0,1\}^n$ is at least $2^{-c_1-1}n$, with $c_1$ the length of a $V$-program for the machine $G_{ca}:=\{(w,a_j^{w_j}):|w|=j\}$ (fair coins on the $a$'s). So for every $n$ some data set with $2n$ data has two reveal orders whose normalised losses differ by at least $2^{-c_1-1}n$ bits.

*Proof.* (a) If the random theory proves $a$, it proves $a\vee c$; if it refutes $a\vee c$, it refutes $a$. This gives the three zero cells. In the order $(a,a\vee c)$, the first normalised factor is $\Bel_\emptyset(a)/(\Bel_\emptyset(a)+\Dis_\emptyset(a))=p_{11}/(p_{11}+p_{01}+p_{00}+p_{0\iota})$, and the second is $1$, since $a\vee c$ is then entailed (Prop 2.1(c)). In the order $(a\vee c,a)$, the first factor is $(p_{11}+p_{01}+p_{\iota1})/(p_{11}+p_{01}+p_{\iota1}+p_{00})$; given $(a\vee c,1)$ the posterior is $\Unif|_C$ restricted to $f(a\vee c)=1$, on which $\Bel(a)\propto p_{11}$ and $\Dis(a)\propto p_{01}$, so the second factor is $p_{11}/(p_{11}+p_{01})$. (The normalising constant $1-\Inc_0$ cancels in each factor.) Dividing,
$$\frac{N_{b\to a}}{N_{a\to b}}=\frac{(p_{11}+p_{01}+p_{\iota1})(p_{11}+p_{01}+p_{00}+p_{0\iota})}{(p_{11}+p_{01}+p_{\iota1}+p_{00})(p_{11}+p_{01})},$$
and expanding, numerator minus denominator is $(p_{11}+p_{01})p_{0\iota}+p_{\iota1}(p_{00}+p_{0\iota})$. Strictness: $p_{11}>0$ (the tapes beginning with a program for $\{a\}$), and $p_{0\iota}>0$ (the tapes beginning with a program for $\{\neg a\}$: consistent, refuting $a$, and not deciding $a\vee c$, because $B\cup\{\neg a\}\vdash a\vee c$ iff $B\cup\{\neg a\}\vdash c$, and $B\cup\{\neg a\}\vdash\neg(a\vee c)$ iff $B\cup\{\neg a\}\vdash\neg c$).
(b) After the first $i-1$ pairs both orders have revealed the same set $D_{i-1}(y)$, and the normalised conditionals depend only on the current set and the queried sentence. So the difference is the sum over $i$ of $\log(N^{(i)}_{b\to a}/N^{(i)}_{a\to b})$, computed as in (a) under the posterior $P_{i-1}:=\Unif(\cdot\mid E_{D_{i-1}})$ in place of $\Unif|_C$; the zero cells of (a) hold for every consistent theory. Write $X:=p_{11}+p_{01}$, $Y:=p_{\iota1}$, $Z:=p_{00}$, $W:=p_{0\iota}$ for the cell masses under $P_{i-1}$; they sum to at most $1$, and $X>0$ (the $G_{ca}$-tapes with $\omega'_{\le i}=y_{\le i}$). The ratio is $1+[XW+Y(Z+W)]/[(X+Y+Z)X]\ge1+W/(X+Y+Z)\ge1+W=1+v_i(y)$. Next, $\log(1+v)\ge v$ for $v\in[0,1]$. The tapes $p\,\omega'$, with $p$ the program for $G_{ca}$, $\omega'_{<i}=y_{<i}$ and $\omega'_i\ne y_i$, lie in $E_{D_{i-1}(y)}$ (their theory proves $\alpha_j$, hence $\beta_j$, for $j<i$), refute $\alpha_i$, and do not decide $c_i$ (L2); they have measure $2^{-c_1-i}$. So $v_i(y)\ge2^{-c_1-i}/M_A(D_{i-1}(y))$. Over uniform $y$, Jensen's inequality gives $\Ex[1/M_A(D_{i-1})]\ge1/\Ex[M_A(D_{i-1})]$. Moreover $M_A(D_{i-1}(y))=\Bel_0(\bigwedge_{j<i}\alpha_j(y))$, and these conjunctions are pairwise incompatible for distinct $y_{<i}$, so $\sum_{y_{<i}}M_A(D_{i-1}(y))\le1$ and $\Ex[M_A(D_{i-1})]\le2^{-(i-1)}$. Hence $\Ex\,v_i\ge2^{-c_1-1}$; sum over $i\le n$. ∎

*Remarks.*
* This answers scout F's Q5 positively for $M_A$, with an explicit two-datum witness and a linear-gap family. The mechanism is the failure of P1's two-point criterion $(\star)$ (P1 Prop A.7): whether the random theory decides $a\vee c$ depends on its answer to $a$ (a theory proving $a$ always decides $a\vee c$; one refuting $a$ need not).
* The witnesses use logical structure: in one order the second datum is entailed. Referee B's symbolic sign search (`rb4` R4.5) supports this: over generic positive masses on the 15 nonempty focal sets on 4 valuations, the order difference for two-datum sets $\{(x,1),(y,1)\}$ has a constant sign exactly on the 36 entailment pairs ($x\subseteq y$) and mixed sign on all 30 others, logically independent pairs included. Whether the normalised loss is order dependent on *logically independent* data (positive literals of a fresh predicate, where $M_A\asymp M_F$) is P1's Question A.8 in another guise and remains **[open]**; the transfer `sfi` Thm 5.1 holds only up to constant factors, which do not control normalised conditionals. So the result does not answer the SOI papers' question; it shows only that for sentence data the answer is yes.
* Consequence for the paper: none of the normalised convention's lower bounds transfer from the semimeasure convention automatically (as in P1), and its losses are not functions of the data set.

### 11.2 Q4: how much graded CR can exceed PR

**Proposition 11.2 [proved; computed: `c2` B5, `rb6` R6.6].**
(a) For every consistent $D$: $0\le\log\big(M_A^{1/2}(D)/M_A(D)\big)\le K(\dom D)+|D|+c$.
(b) Let $m\ge3$ and $k\ge1$, let $a_{j,i}$ ($j\le k$, $i<m$) be fresh atoms (pairwise distinct words), and put $\chi^{(j)}_i:=\neg a_{j,1}\wedge\dots\wedge\neg a_{j,i-1}\wedge a_{j,i}$ for $1\le i<m$ and $\chi^{(j)}_m:=\neg a_{j,1}\wedge\dots\wedge\neg a_{j,m-1}$. For $\vec\imath\in[m]^k$ let $E_{\vec\imath}:=\{(\chi^{(j)}_{i_j},1):j\le k\}$. Then some $\vec\imath$ has
$$\log\frac{M_A^{1/2}(E_{\vec\imath})}{M_A(E_{\vec\imath})}\ \ge\ k\log\frac m2+\log\varepsilon_\emptyset,\qquad |E_{\vec\imath}|=k,\qquad K(\dom E_{\vec\imath})\le k\log m+K(k,m)+c .$$
For $m=3$ the gap is $k\log\frac32-c$, linear in $|D|$; for $k=1$ it is $\log m-1-c$ on a single datum. The lower bound is within $2|D|+K(k,m)+c$ of the upper bound (a).

*Proof.* (a) The lower bound is Prop 7.3(b). For the upper bound, $M_A^{1/2}\le1$ and $M_A(D)\ge2^{-K(\psi_D)-c}$ (the one-axiom theory $\{\psi_D\}$), with $K(\psi_D)\le K(\dom D)+|D|+c$ (the labels are $|D|$ bits given $\dom D$).
(b) Each $E_{\vec\imath}$ is consistent (each $\chi$ is a consistent conjunction of literals, and the blocks use disjoint atoms; L1), and $B$ proves none of its data (L2 applied to $\Lambda=\emptyset$). The empty theory gives $\nu^{1/2}_\emptyset(E_{\vec\imath})=2^{-k}$, so $M_A^{1/2}(E_{\vec\imath})\ge\varepsilon_\emptyset2^{-k}$. Two different $\vec\imath$ differ in some block, where their sentences are incompatible; so the $m^k$ conjunctions $\psi_{E_{\vec\imath}}$ are pairwise incompatible, $\sum_{\vec\imath}M_A(E_{\vec\imath})\le M_A(\emptyset)\le1$, and some $\vec\imath$ has $M_A(E_{\vec\imath})\le m^{-k}$. Its gap is at least $\log(\varepsilon_\emptyset2^{-k}m^k)$. $\dom E_{\vec\imath}$ is computable from $(k,m)$ and the index of $\vec\imath$ among $m^k$ values. ∎

This confirms scout F's conjecture (linear gap, as in P1 Thm 3.12(iii)), and more: the gap can come close to the maximum $K(\dom D)+|D|$. The source is the per-datum grading of Prop 7.3(d): a datum whose content is a long conjunction costs one bit under the graded weight and its full content under PR. The construction is the logical counterpart of P1's three-input weak semimeasure (P1 Ex 3.5(b)): $m$ pairwise incompatible single data, each worth $\frac12$ to the empty theory, total mass $m/2>1$. (The development round's `c2` B5 had a weight of the empty theory that made five of its seven lower bounds nonpositive; the revised B5 uses $\varepsilon_\emptyset=\frac34$, and every bound is positive and met, with margins between 3.1 and 5.1 bits; referee B's R6.6 made the same test.)

### 11.3 Q7: the Kolmogorov predictor can be exponentially worse than Bayes

**Theorem 11.3 [proved; computed: `c3`; `rb5` R5.2, R5.3].** Let $a_1,\dots,a_m$ be fresh atoms (Def 1.1′), and for $w\in\{0,1\}^m$ let $\mathrm{desc}(w):=\bigwedge_{i\le m}a_i^{w_i}$. Fix any **well-order** $\prec$ of $\dom V$ (for instance of type $\omega$), and let the Kolmogorov predictor of Prop 5.3(b) use the $\prec$-first compatible program. There are a data set $D$ of $2^m-1$ sentences, all of the form $\neg\mathrm{desc}(v)$ labelled true, and a reveal order of $D$, such that:
(i) the Kolmogorov predictor errs or abstains at every one of the $2^m-1$ steps;
(ii) $K_A(D)\le m+K(m)+c$, and the Bayes predictor of Prop 5.3(a) makes at most $K_A(D)+c\le m+K(m)+c'$ mistakes on the same sequence.
If $\prec$ refines length, then $m\le K_A(D)\le m+K(m)+c$, so with $k:=K_A(D)$ the number of errors or abstentions is at least $2^{k-K(m)-c}-1\ge2^{k-2\log k-c'}-1$, while the Bayes predictor makes at most $k+c$ mistakes; the upper bound of Prop 5.3(b) is $2^k$.

*Proof.* For each $w$ let $\pi(w)$ be the $\prec$-first $p\in\dom V$ with $B\cup\mathsf W_{V(p)}$ consistent and proving $\mathrm{desc}(w)$; it exists, since $\{\mathrm{desc}(w)\}$ is consistent (L1), and it exists as a $\prec$-first element because $\prec$ is a well-order. A consistent theory proves at most one $\mathrm{desc}(w)$, so $\pi$ is injective. Let $w^*$ be the $w$ whose $\pi(w)$ is $\prec$-last (a maximum among $2^m$ elements). The adversary repeats the following, starting from $D=\emptyset$, as long as the $\prec$-first program $p$ compatible with the current data does not prove $\mathrm{desc}(w^*)$:
* $p$ is consistent and $B\vdash\bigvee_v\mathrm{desc}(v)$, so the set $\mathrm{Open}(p)$ of $v$ with $B\cup\mathsf W_{V(p)}\cup\{\mathrm{desc}(v)\}$ consistent is nonempty. It contains some $v\ne w^*$: otherwise $p$ proves $\neg\mathrm{desc}(v)$ for all $v\ne w^*$, hence $\mathrm{desc}(w^*)$.
* Present $(\neg\mathrm{desc}(v),1)$ for such a $v$.

Then: (1) every presented sentence is true in the valuation $w^*$, so the data stay consistent and $\{\mathrm{desc}(w^*)\}$ proves them all. (2) $p$ does not prove $\neg\mathrm{desc}(v)$ ($v$ is open for $p$), so the predictor errs (if $p$ proves $\mathrm{desc}(v)$) or abstains. (3) The sentence $\neg\mathrm{desc}(v)$ was not presented before, since $p$ is compatible with the earlier data. (4) For $w\ne w^*$, $\pi(w)$ proves $\neg\mathrm{desc}(v)$ for every $v\ne w$, so it stays compatible until $\neg\mathrm{desc}(w)$ is presented. While some $\pi(w)$ with $w\ne w^*$ is compatible, the $\prec$-first compatible program is $\preceq\pi(w)\prec\pi(w^*)$, and, being consistent and $\prec$-before $\pi(w^*)$, does not prove $\mathrm{desc}(w^*)$; so the loop continues. By (3) the loop runs at most $2^m-1$ times, and by (4) it does not stop before all $\neg\mathrm{desc}(w)$, $w\ne w^*$, have been presented. So it runs exactly $2^m-1$ times, and by (2) every step is an error or abstention.
(ii) $\bigwedge_{v\ne w^*}\neg\mathrm{desc}(v)$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{desc}(w^*)$, so $K_A(D)\le K(\mathrm{desc}(w^*))+c\le m+K(m)+c'$. For the Bayes predictor, apply Prop 5.3(a) with $T:=\mathsf W_{V(p)}$ for a shortest program $p$ compatible with $D$: $K_\equiv(T)\le|p|+c=K_A(D)+c$, and $T$ proves every datum. (Equivalently, Thm 5.1's last chain $L_A(D)\le K_A(D)+c$ and the halving argument.)
If $\prec$ refines length, every program compatible with $D$ proves $\mathrm{desc}(w^*)$, and the $\prec$-first of them is $\pi(w^*)$, so $K_A(D)=|\pi(w^*)|$; the $2^m$ distinct programs $\pi(w)$ all have length $\le|\pi(w^*)|$, and Kraft's inequality gives $2^m\le2^{|\pi(w^*)|}$. Finally $K(m)\le2\log m+c\le2\log k+c$. ∎

*Remarks.* The stream is not computable (it depends on $\pi$). The construction is logical: in SOI on strings the analogous decoys would be beaten by the constant labeller "true", which here is unavailable because $\{\neg\mathrm{desc}(v):v\in\{0,1\}^m\}$ is inconsistent. As in P1's conjunction ladder (P1 Thm 9.10, with the remark that it is the selection rule, not the prior, that fails), the single simplest theory compatible with the data is a poor predictor although the Bayes mixture over the same hypotheses is good. The choice of $w^*$ as the $w$ with $\prec$-last $\pi(w)$ is needed: with the $\prec$-first instead, the loop stops early in all 90 of referee B's test lists (`rb5` R5.3). Prop 5.3(c) uses the theorem with a non-length order to show that the order hypothesis of Prop 5.3(b) is needed.

### 11.4 Q6: does $M_A$ dominate every lsc probability of provability?

Call a Borel random variable $\Theta$ with values in the consistent, deductively closed sets of sentences a *random consistent theory*, and $\beta(\chi):=\Pr[\chi\in\Theta]$ its provability function. Q6 asks whether $\Bel_0\ge2^{-c_\beta}\beta$ whenever $\beta$ is lsc.

**Proposition 11.4.**
(a) **[proved]** For every lsc $\beta$ of this kind, every finite set $S$ of pairwise $B$-incompatible sentences and every $\sigma\in S$: $\Bel_0(\sigma)\ge2^{-K(\beta)-K(S)-c}\beta(\sigma)$. In particular $M_A(D)\ge2^{-K(\beta)-K(\dom D)-c}\beta(\psi_D)$.
(b) **[proved]** If $\Theta$ is the closure of the output of a theory machine $G$ (almost surely consistent), then $\Bel_0\ge2^{-K(G)}\beta$ (Thm 4.1).
(c) **[proved, given `sfi` Thm 5.1]** Q6 restricted to random sets of literals of a fresh predicate (with lsc containment functional on literal data, and domination required on literal data) is equivalent to P1 Question 3.14 for labels $\{0,1\}$.

*Proof.* (a) $\Theta$ contains at most one member of $S$ (consistency, as "consistent" means consistent with $B$), so $\sum_{\sigma\in S}\beta(\sigma)\le1$ and $\beta|_S$ is a semimeasure, lsc uniformly in $S$; the conditional coding theorem gives $K(\sigma\mid S)\le-\log\beta(\sigma)+K(\beta)+c$. If $\beta(\sigma)>0$, $\sigma$ is consistent with $B$, and the one-axiom theory gives $\Bel_0(\sigma)\ge2^{-K(\sigma)-c}\ge2^{-K(S)-K(\sigma\mid S)-c'}$. For $D$ take $S$ the label conjunctions on $\dom D$. (c) A random literal set $\Lambda$ with at most one literal per word corresponds to the random partial function $F(x):=b$ iff $R(t_x)^b\in\Lambda$, with $\Pr[\Lambda\supseteq D^\pm]=\Pr[F\supseteq D^{\rm str}]$ ($D^{\rm str}$ the string data set of $D$); and $M_A(D)\asymp M_F(D^{\rm str})$ on literal data (`sfi` Thm 5.1; scout S Thm 2.7). ∎

So Q6 is at least as hard as the binary case of P1's question, and for general sentences it is **[open]**. The obstacle is P1's: an lsc law of a random object need not be realisable by a machine reading one tape (P1 App A.6). Note that $\Bel_0$ itself is not lsc (Thm 3.2), while $\Bel_0+\Inc_0$ is.

### 11.5 Q2: does the random tape help for whole theories?

For an r.e. set $X$ of sentences let $\Unif_{\rm enum}(X):=\Unif\{\omega:A_\omega=X\}$, the probability that the universal theory machine emits exactly $X$ (a universal probabilistic enumerator), and recall $K_{\rm enum}$, $\mm_{\rm enum}$ from Prop 6.4. Q2 asks whether $\Unif_A[T]\asymp\mm_\equiv(T)$ for consistent r.e. $T$, and whether $K_\equiv(T)\le-\log\mm_\equiv(T)+O(1)$.

**Proposition 11.5.**
(a) **[proved]** For r.e. $T$ with $B\cup T$ consistent: $2^{-c}\Unif_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)\le\Unif_A[T]\le2^c\Unif_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)$, and $\Unif_A[T]\ge2^{-c}\mm_\equiv(T)$.
(b) **[proved]** Suppose $\mathcal L$ is in the literal setting (Def 1.1′). Then $\Unif_A[T]\asymp\mm_\equiv(T)$ for all consistent r.e. $T$ iff $\Unif^{\rm str}_{\rm enum}(X)\asymp\mm^{\rm str}_{\rm enum}(X)$ for all r.e. $X\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ (the same quantities for a universal probabilistic enumerator of sets of strings); and $K_\equiv\le-\log\mm_\equiv+O(1)$ iff $K^{\rm str}_{\rm enum}\le-\log\mm^{\rm str}_{\rm enum}+O(1)$.
(c) **[proved, given Solovay 1977 in the probabilistic reading (known: abstract level)]** $-\log\Unif_A[T]\le-\log\mm_\equiv(T)+c\le K_\equiv(T)+c\le3\,(-\log\Unif_A[T])+O\big(\log(-\log\Unif_A[T])\big)+c$. With Rem 6.5 the factor 3 becomes 2 (abstract level).

*Proof.* (a) $\{A_\omega=\Cn T\}\subseteq\{\Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn T\}$, so $\Unif_A[T]\ge\Unif_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)$. Conversely the machine $G_{\rm cl}$ ("run $G_U$, emit $\Cn(A_\omega)$") emits exactly $\Cn T$ on $\{\Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn T\}$, and the tapes $p_{G_{\rm cl}}\omega'$ show $\Unif_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)\ge2^{-|p_{G_{\rm cl}}|}\Unif_A[T]$. The last claim is Thm 4.1(i).
(b) (⇐) By (a) and Prop 6.4(a), $\Unif_A[T]\asymp\Unif_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)$ and $\mm_\equiv(T)\asymp\mm_{\rm enum}(\Cn T)$, and coding sentences as strings changes the enumeration quantities by constant factors. (⇒) For $X\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ let $T_X:=\{R(t_x):x\in X\}$. By (L2), $X=\{x:R(t_x)\in\Cn(T_X)\}$. A probabilistic enumerator of exactly $X$ yields one of exactly $\Cn(T_X)$ (close under $\Cn$) and conversely (extract the $x$), with constant overhead, on probabilistic and on deterministic programs alike; distinct $X$ give distinct $\Cn(T_X)$. So $\Unif^{\rm str}_{\rm enum}(X)\asymp\Unif_{\rm enum}(\Cn T_X)\asymp\Unif_A[T_X]$ and $\mm^{\rm str}_{\rm enum}(X)\asymp\mm_\equiv(T_X)$; likewise for $K$.
(c) The first two inequalities are (a) and Prop 6.4. Solovay's theorem bounds $K_{\rm enum}(X)$ by $3I(X)+O(\log I(X))$ with $I(X)$ the negative logarithm of the probability that a machine with a random input tape enumerates exactly $X$, which is $-\log\Unif_{\rm enum}(X)\pm c$; apply it to $X=\Cn T$ and use (a) and Prop 6.4(a). ∎

So Q2 is exactly the question whether randomness helps to enumerate a set, which Solovay (1977), Vereshchagin (2007) and Shekhovtsov–Zakharov (2023) study through the constant in $K_{\rm enum}\le c\cdot I+O(\log)$; whether the constant can be $1$ (a coding theorem for enumeration) was not found stated in the abstracts, and Q2 stays **[open]**. Its relevance: the limit loss on a complete presentation is $-\log\Unif_A[T^*]+\log M_A(\emptyset)$ (Prop 8.2), and Q2 asks whether this equals $-\log\mm_\equiv(T^*)$ up to $O(1)$. For true $\Pi_1$-axiomatised theories the extension version is answered up to $K(k)$ by `logic` Prop 3.7 (from scout N1's N11); Q2 for the exact class $[T]$ stays open there too. (c) also gives the $\PA$ version of Prop 8.3(b): if $\Unif_A[T^*]\ge\Unif_A[\PA]\ge2^{-K_\equiv(\PA)-c}$, then $K_\equiv(T^*)\le3K_\equiv(\PA)+c_{\PA}$, with $c_{\PA}$ a constant (the logarithmic term is $O(\log K_\equiv(\PA))$). So every r.e. $T^*$ with $\Q\subseteq T^*\subseteq\PA$ and $K_\equiv(T^*)>3K_\equiv(\PA)+c_{\PA}$ has $\Unif_A[T^*]<\Unif_A[\PA]$, and on data presenting $T^*$ the limit posterior prefers $[\PA]$ to $[T^*]$ (given Solovay; with Rem 6.5, $2K_\equiv(\PA)$ in place of $3K_\equiv(\PA)$ at abstract level).
