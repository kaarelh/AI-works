**The Kepler example (§4).**
* **Prop 4.3 [proved].** In a *geocentric* direction language Kepler's third law has content: K12 + K3 entails "Mars is not permanently in opposition", K12 does not. (In a heliocentric direction language it has none: Prop 4.2.)
* **Prop 4.4 [proved; computed: c4 B].** Given the other elements, K3 does exactly one job in the geocentric language: it replaces the size element $\rho=a_{\rm Ma}/a_{\rm Ea}$, worth 11.3, 12.3, 13.4 and 14.7 bits at 8′, 4′, 2′ and 1′ (for $\rho$ ranging over $[0,4)$). Whether compression alone selects K3 depends on the code for K3: it pays once it fixes the sizes of about 25–40 bodies with brackets counted (375–450 bits), 11–15 in Polish notation (165 bits), 8–10 with a power symbol (115 bits) (c9 (8)); the five planets other than the Earth would give about 55–75 bits (extrapolated), so in none of these codes does compression select K3 at Kepler's precision. K3's own error is invisible at that precision: exact K3 misplaces Jupiter and Saturn geocentrically by at most 0.23′ and 0.19′ over 1580–1600 (c9 (9)).
* **The historical Mars pool [computed: c4, c9, c10; c4's S1/S3 law texts corrected].** Minimax errors over 1580–1600 against plan94: circle 640.79′, eccentric 23.05′, bisected equant 8.17′, vicarious hypothesis 1.41′, ellipse 1.07′ (upper bounds witnessed by elements; reproduced to four decimals by referee B's independent search). Against an exact Kepler ellipse the first three are 22.34′, 7.43′ and 0.62′ (c9 (7)): the equant's 8′ is intrinsic, while more than half of the vicarious hypothesis's 1.41′ is perturbation error it shares with the ellipse; plan94's own error for Mars is at most about 0.43′ in this period, so the sub-arcminute ordering of the last two forms is established against plan94, not against the sky. With noise-free interval data at one common tolerance δ (the guaranteed-tolerance regime), the data alone (no text prior) prefer the ellipse at every δ ≤ 60′, by 0.2–15 bits (c9 (5)); law texts differ by 100–800 bits except for one pair (the epicyclic syntax's equant and vicarious texts, 18 bits), so in that regime the text prior decides which compatible form is preferred. With graded fit (Gaussian noise, or honest tolerance theories inside $M_A$), fit differences grow linearly with the number of observations: pairwise, the ellipse overcomes each shorter law text within 2–41 observations at σ = 2′, except the epicyclic syntax's advantage of the vicarious hypothesis over the ellipse, which longitudes alone overcome only after about 5,000 observations at σ = 2′ (1,300 at 1′); a full noisy-data simulation agrees (c9 (3), (4), (6)). Distance data decide that last step, syntax-free: at a distance tolerance of 1% the vicarious hypothesis remains compatible down to a longitude tolerance of at most 5.12′ (the least found by two independent searches; c8 had reported 6.31′ as a least tolerance, which referee B refuted), while the ellipse fits both kinds of data at 1.07′; so below the vicarious hypothesis's least tolerance only the ellipse is compatible, in every syntax (c10). This reproduces the abandonment of circular orbits, not Kepler's last step (oval to ellipse), which is outside the pool.

**Limitations (§5).** Statistical confrontation of theory with data is the default in physics and SAI's proof-requiring core has no native noise model (L0, promoted to a headline limitation). The textbook point splits into a bridge gap, a computational gap (`shape`'s theorems, which need a cost on the written theory and hold in `shape`'s model) and a tacit gap; approximate truth and domains are partly formal (`laws`); the condensation essay's list is assessed item by item. Verdict: the axiom view models the declarative, inferential core of theories and misses their procedural and dynamic sides.

The two candidates of the development record (the domain-of-applicability note's "no nice formal criterion"; the condensation essay's "nothing fundamentally right about these information-theoretic losses") were reassessed after referee A's issue 7: each attacked a reading the author's text does not make, and on the natural reading the author is right. ## 1. The problems and the argument map (T1)

### 1.1 Two problems

* **P1 (what a theory does).** What is a scientific theory for? Candidate answers in the literature: predicting observations (the raw-data view behind Solomonoff induction [known: solomonoff1964formal]); answering questions posed in some language, including why-questions [known: vanfraassen1980scientific, ch. 5; content: secondary]; explaining [known: hempel1948studies]; solving problems [known: laudan1977progress; content: secondary].
* **P2 (what a theory is).** A program that prints or predicts observations; a set of sentences closed under consequence (the "received" view [known: suppe1977structure; content: secondary]); a class of models (the semantic view [known: suppes1960comparison, vanfraassen1980scientific]); a core of models with intended applications (structuralism [known: sneed1971logical, balzer1987architectonic; content: secondary]); a practice of exemplars and skills [known: kuhn1970structure, kuhn1977second, polanyi1958personal; content: secondary].

The axiom view answers P1 with "answering questions in a given language" and P2 with "a logical theory"; the raw-data view answers "predicting observations" and "a program".

### 1.2 The descriptive observation and the explanatory question

The brief fixes the stance (agreed with the author on 2026-10-15); this record develops it, with the scope narrowed after referee A (issue 4).

* **Observation (descriptive) [assessment].** The textbook presentations of fundamental theories are *declarative*: laws, definitions, principles, models given by equations over mathematical structures (state spaces, dynamics); not procedures that print predictions. We agree; it is an observation about texts, needing no theory of why. Two qualifications:
  * *Declarative is not sentential.* What physics texts write is closer to a class of models specified by equations (Suppes's set-theoretic predicates [known: suppes1960comparison]; his hierarchy of models of data [known, unverified here: Suppes 1962, "Models of data"; content via referee A's search]) than to a list of first-order axioms. Turning Kepler's laws into first-order axioms over a background $B$ of "enough true analysis" (§4.1) is a reconstruction. So the observation supports "declarative"; "first-order sentences" is a modelling choice, made because questions and data are sentences and SAI's prior is over texts (§1.4 item 4).
  * *Scope.* Theories *in use* have procedural parts: methods attached to concepts (integration; the linearity-of-expectation trick), numerical schemes, perturbation series, simulation codes. In some sciences "theory" names a procedure outright: in quantum chemistry a "level of theory" is a computational method (Hartree–Fock, coupled cluster, a density functional); Feynman rules and renormalisation schemes are recipes [observation; no citation]. Even in §4, deriving one Mars position from K12 is a root-finding computation for Kepler's equation. So the observation is about the textbook presentation of fundamental theories, not about theories in all their uses.
* **Question (explanatory) [open].** Why that shape is good, and whether an ideal inducer should produce it. Craig's theorem rules out an answer based only on what is representable (§1.4); any answer must be relative to a reasoner with costs, or to a community of reasoners who communicate, check and extend theories piece by piece.

### 1.3 Claim D: a theory answers questions posed in a given language

**Statement and sources.** A theory answers questions stated in a given language ("what is the period of Jupiter?", "where will Mars be at time $t$, to within 2′?"); it does not generate or predict a raw data stream. Sources: the author's request (brief); `phil-sci-scientific-theories-are-not-for-generating-or-predicting-arbitrary-data.md`; `phil-sci-science-predicts-only-very-particularly.md`; `predicting-specific-things.md`; the condensation essay ("it is intermediately correct, and also insightful, to think of concepts specifically as helping one answer questions and solve problems"; predictions are "already high-level" and "about extremely specific things"). The author clarified (brief, "Existing language") that the claim is comparative: answering in terms of existing abstractions is a step away from pixel prediction, not the end point.

**Arguments for** (scout P D1–D4, checked).
* (D1) Science is silent on almost everything; a raw-data predictor must model everything, so a theory's contribution is a vanishing *share* of its loss. *Formal version:* Prop 2.2(a), with the correction (Prop 2.2(d)) that the law's *absolute* value per step is the same in the two tasks when the question task's no-law baseline is the one the raw no-law model induces.
* (D2) Predictions are graded by purpose-made tolerances on high-level quantities. *Formal version:* interval sentences as data (§4.1); the purpose fixes which sentences are data (§2.2, remark on losses).
* (D3) Questions are partial and so are theories. *Formal version:* the trichotomy, the "unanswered" mass (Prop 2.1), the independent-label separation (`sfi` Props 6.3–6.5).
* (D4) Theories answer in many directions (K3 gives $P$ from $a$ and $a$ from $P$; Le Verrier asked Newton's theory where the perturber of Uranus is). *Formal version:* conditional questions $(\alpha,\ell)$ in every direction are answered by deduction; their cost for a bounded reasoner is `shape` §4 (in `shape`'s model: a factor of the number of directions without intermediate concepts or with one round of propagation, at most 9 in written length with intermediate concepts and unbounded rounds; nothing under program length).

**Objections.**
* (o1) The theory is in the raw-data predictor too. The author's own remark (`msgs to samuel on ML mind upload difficulty.md`, via scout P): Kepler's laws are "in" a video of the sky "in a deep way". *Formal support:* in the conservation world a Bayes mixture over raw data learns the law at one bit (Prop 2.2(c)), and the law's raw gain is exactly a question loss (Prop 2.2(d)). So the contrast is not one of content; it is whether the theory is a separable, queryable component, and what the inducer is asked.
* (o2) Formally SAI is still prediction: it predicts truth values of sentences, and SAI $\asymp$ consistent SOI on sentences in the coin-free, one-tape and Kolmogorov versions (`sfi` Thm 2.1, Thm 3.1 [proved there]). So "answering versus predicting" is not a difference in the type of inference. It is a difference in the *data type* and in the *coherence constraint*.
* (o3) Choosing the questions is half of science; SAI takes the questions and the language as given (§5, L4–L5).
* (o4) *The practice of physics* (referee A, issue 10). Large parts of current physics confront theory with *near-raw* data through generative forward models and information-theoretic losses: gravitational-wave searches match waveform templates against the strain time series with a Gaussian noise likelihood; cosmology fits likelihoods of microwave-background data (at low multipoles at the level of sky maps); particle physics simulates detector responses event by event [observation from standard practice; no citation]. There the theory, together with instrument and noise models, generates predictions for raw data. *Reply [assessment]:* these pipelines exist in order to answer a few questions (a mass, a spin, a cosmological parameter, whether a particle exists), and the raw-data likelihood is a means to those answers; so D in its comparative form survives. But the objection shows two things the paper must say: (i) theory-plus-instrument-plus-noise models are generative, and the theory is only one part of them; (ii) SAI's proof-requiring core, with exact or interval data and no native noise model, sits far from this practice (L0, §5.0); §4.4's showcase is the noise-free special case, and its reading changes when noise is added (Part A′).

**Verdict [assessment].** D is true in its comparative form. The formal content is: (i) the data type (sentences of a chosen language, labelled true/false, with questions as sets of sentences); (ii) logical coherence (hypotheses are consistent and closed under consequence); (iii) partiality; (iv) the choice of target: which losses are counted (Prop 2.2); (v) many-directional use. (i)–(ii) are definitional; (iii) is where SAI and SOI separate (`sfi` §6); (v) matters only relative to a reasoner and a cost on the written theory (`shape` Thm 1.11, Thm 4.3). The paper should not present D as a new inference principle.

### 1.4 Claim S: a theory is a logical theory, not a program

**Statement and sources.** The brief; `phil-sci-some-disanalogies-between-solomonoff-induction-and-science.md`, first bullet ("a system of talking which involves some sorts of things … about which some questions can be asked … with some relations between the answers"); the condensation essay (concepts are not code snippets; inferential relations are constitutive).

**Arguments for** (scout P S1–S4). (S1) the descriptive observation (§1.2); (S2) modularity: axioms have individual content and can be added, dropped, tested and combined across vocabularies, whereas "this program contains that law" has no canonical meaning; (S3) inferential role: theoretical terms get their meaning from their role in the theory, as meaning postulates [known: carnap1952meaning; content: source (first page)] and Ramsey–Lewis functional definitions [known: ramsey1931theories, lewis1970define; content: secondary / source (first page)]; (S4) many-directional use.

**What the formal results say.**
1. **The idealised formalism cannot see shape.** Coin-free: $W_A\asymp W_A^{\rm dec}\asymp W^{\rm cons}\asymp W^{\rm cl}$ and $K_A=K_F^{\rm cons}\pm c$ (`sfi` Thm 2.1 [proved there]; the construction is Craig's [known: craig1953axiomatizability]). One-tape: $M_A\asymp M_F^{\rm cons}$ on every finite $D$, the empty one included (`sfi` Thm 3.1 [proved there]). Vocabulary: adding symbols changes old-vocabulary weights by $O(1)$ (`fundamentals` Cor 4.5 [proved there]; class-level, tape-by-tape and penalised versions: `terms` Thm 2.1 [proved there]). Even the difference between an r.e. axiom set and a *finite* theory disappears in the coin-free version: r.e. axiom induction is finite-theory induction in a three-sorted extension, up to $O(1)$ (`terms` Thm 6.5 [proved there], an effective many-sorted form of the Kleene–Craig–Vaught theorem; not one-tape, `terms` Rem 6.6). Time penalties on checking or generating axioms keep the equivalence on the axiom side, coin-free (`sfi` Thm 7.1, under the self-simulation assumption (S)) and one-tape (`sfi` Thm 3.4, under (S$_{\rm tape}$)) [proved there]; the diagonal separations under time bounds come from free deduction, not from axioms (`sfi` Prop 7.6(a), (b) [proved there]; its part (c) is a proof sketch there). So "a logical theory rather than an arbitrary program" is not a difference the posterior can register in this setting [proved there; the reading is an assessment]. The author agrees (brief).
2. **Where shape does matter, it is relative to a reasoner and a vocabulary, and only when the written theory is charged; this holds in `shape`'s model.** `shape`'s model: Boolean laws (linear over GF(2), parity, the implication chain), conditional questions in every direction, reasoners that are unit propagation with a given number of rounds, forward chaining or Gaussian elimination, and "program shape" taken as a direction-complete set of sound directed rules (`shape` Def 4.1) [all statements below proved there]:
   * *Under program-length priors (SAI's own) neither the reasoner nor the vocabulary is visible:* on the question data of an affine law, $K_A^{\mathcal R}=K(\mathcal S)\pm c$ for every named reasoner, with or without intermediate concepts, because a fixed program writes out the prime-implicate library (`shape` Thm 1.11, Thm 1.12(c)).
   * *With a cost on the written theory* (`shape` Thm 1.12(a), (b)): a Gaussian-elimination reasoner needs only the equations; a unit-propagation reasoner with clauses over the base variables needs exactly the clauses of the indecomposable circuits, at least $a_*2^{d_*-1}$ clauses when $d_*\ge3$, where $d_*$ is the least weight of a nonzero derived equation and $a_*$ the number of derived equations of that weight (`shape` Thm 2.6, Cor 2.7(a)); for the simplex family this is $m2^{(m-1)/2}$ clauses against $\log_2(m+1)$ equations (Cor 2.8).
   * *Intermediate concepts help only a reasoner that can chain through them:* one round of propagation cannot use them (every one-round-usable theory contains all prime implicates, `shape` Prop 3.1a); a fixed number $k$ of rounds needs $2^{\Omega(m^{1/(2k-1)})}$ clauses for one parity law with any intermediate concepts (Prop 3.8(c), given Håstad); with about $2\log_2m$ rounds one parity law needs only $4m-6$ clauses (Prop 3.1, Prop 3.8(b)), and the simplex family $m(2m-4)$ with unbounded propagation (Rem 3.2a). For a *system* of laws (the hidden-state law) every unit-propagation-usable theory, at any depth and with any intermediate concepts, has written length $2^{\Omega(m^{\varepsilon/3})}$, while elimination uses a linear-size theory (`shape` Thm 3.6, given Göös–Kamath–Robere–Sokolov 2019). So the exponential separations are between *pairs* (reasoner, vocabulary) (`shape` Cor 3.9).
   * *Directionality is real but cheap:* a direction-complete rule base is exactly $m$ times the clausal law for one parity law, $m$ the number of query directions, also for one round of reasoning with any vocabulary (`shape` Prop 4.2); with intermediate concepts and unbounded rounds the factor is at most 9 in written length (3 for the chain encoding), and for every round budget at most the clause width (`shape` Thm 4.3).

   **Reading [assessment].** In `shape`'s model, the large separations are between pairs (reasoner, vocabulary), not between declarative and procedural shapes, and logical shape buys many-directional use only up to a constant factor once intermediate concepts are available and the reasoner can chain through them. Whether this extends beyond that model (real-valued laws, numerical solvers, probabilistic programs with conditioning, simulation codes) is **open**. (These results were first obtained, for one law and without the program-length and systems results, by scout N2, Props 2.4–2.8.)
3. **Question-answering competence does not require an explicit theory.** Khardon and Roth's "learning to reason" combines learning with a reasoning task and shows, for propositional classes, that answering queries can be learnable where learning an explicit representation and then reasoning is not [known: khardon1997learning; content: abstract: the AAAI 1994 abstract states a learning-to-reason algorithm for a class of propositional languages that has no efficient reasoning algorithm when represented as a formula-based knowledge base; the JACM paper's full statements were not seen]. The same point holds formally inside SAI: SOI's labellers, which carry no theory, are equivalent to theories (item 1). So D (theories are for answering questions) does not by itself support S as an *optimality* claim [assessment, with scout L §7].
4. **The semantic view (rewritten after referee A, issue 4).** In first-order logic a theory and its class of models determine each other up to equivalence; Halvorson argues that the semantic view, taken literally, misidentifies theories and cannot express relations between theories such as embeddability [known: halvorson2012what; content: abstract], and Lutz that the residual disagreement, about dependence on predicate logic, is illusory [known: lutz2017what; content: abstract]. Three points matter here [assessment]:
   * *Questions need not be sentences.* In partition semantics a question denotes a partition of logical space and an answer a union of cells [known, unverified here: Groenendijk and Stokhof's partition semantics; content via referee A's search (secondary)]; a theory answers a question when its class of models lies inside one cell. So the question-answering view is neutral between sentences and classes of models; the development record's "since questions are sentences, the syntactic view is the natural one" was wrong.
   * *Our own Kepler computation is mostly semantic.* Proof-requiring compatibility with interval data is a condition on a class of models (a region in element space), and the Occam factor (§4.4) is the measure of that region. The only syntactic ingredient of §4.4 is the law-text prior, and it is exactly what produces the language relativity found there. A semanticist would call that relativity an artefact of the syntactic prior; a presentation-free prior (uniform over the five model families, uniform volume on each family's elements) selects the ellipse at every δ ≤ 60′ from the data alone (c9 (5), §4.4).
   * *The rejoinder, which we think correct.* A prior over *infinitely many* model families needs an enumeration of them, that is, a presentation, so the relativity is relocated rather than removed; and a volume prior on elements depends on the parametrisation (a Bertrand-type relativity: halving the eccentricity ranges moves the vicarious hypothesis by one bit against the four-element forms, c9 (5)). So the paper should say: the question-answering view is neutral between sentences and model classes; the paper uses sentences because SAI's prior is over texts; §4.4 shows that this is where the language relativity enters, and §4.4's semantic computation shows how much of the verdict survives without it. Nothing in the formal results favours sentences over model classes as the *object*.
5. **Inconsistent theories in use.** Classical electrodynamics of point charges has been argued to be inconsistent as standardly formulated [known: frisch2004inconsistency; content: abstract; disputed in the literature]; Boltzmann's H-theorem with molecular chaos at all times conflicts with reversibility [known: brown2009boltzmann; content: abstract]. Proof-requiring SAI gives a globally inconsistent theory weight 0. Real theories are logical theories only after domains are restricted and approximations stated honestly (§5.3; `laws` Prop 4.3).

**Verdict [assessment].** We agree with the descriptive observation in the scope of §1.2. The explanatory claim is open; by item 1 any answer is relative to a reasoner (or a community of reasoners); item 2 is the best formal answer available, and in its model it does *not* show that logical form is what matters: what matters there is a reasoner that can do algebra or chain, plus intermediate concepts. The paper should say this, and list the formal differences that do exist (§1.7).

**A correction of the brief's own argument (S4) (not the author's claim).** "A program computes one direction cheaply" is true of a particular program, not of the class: a program may consist of a generic solver applied to a declarative law, at $O(1)$ extra length (scout P §1.3), and a straight-line program with internal variables answers all directions of parity with $O(m)$ operations (`shape` §4). The separation the brief looked for exists only relative to a fixed weak reasoner and a cost on the written theory (`shape` Thm 4.3); under program length it vanishes (`shape` Thm 1.11), which is the precise form of this correction.

### 1.5 Claim E: theories explain propositions

Developed in §3. Verdict: a theory entails answers; derivation from a short theory is explanation in the unification sense, with at least four known gaps (asymmetry; explanation by approximate correction; irrelevance; the conjunction problem).

### 1.6 Claim L: the ladder

Agreed with the author (brief, "Existing language"). Each step moves further from verificationism.
1. **Raw-data prediction** (pixels, observation sequences).
2. **Answering questions posed with existing abstractions:** SAI over a fixed language $\mathcal L$. The base language is whatever abstractions are already available, often highly theoretical: heliocentric longitudes presuppose Copernican geometry and the Earth's orbit (§4.1; theory-ladenness [known: hanson1958patterns; content: secondary]). No theory-neutral observation language is claimed, so the classical objections to one do not apply in the same way; but theory-ladenness still biases comparisons between theories that disagree about the base language (the strongest objection, below) [assessment].
3. **Theories that extend the language** (theoretical terms; data stay in the base language). Formal content, from `terms` (Thm 8.1 assembles it; [proved there], with the known results each part rests on named there):
   * with free deduction, theoretical vocabulary costs $O(1)$ bits in the one-tape, coin-free and Kolmogorov versions, class by class and also under penalties on checking or generating axioms; tape by tape the observational projection of a random theory has exactly the same law on observational data (`terms` Thm 2.1; `fundamentals` Cor 4.5);
   * when derivations are charged, Craig's construction and every run-padded observational axiomatisation fit exactly the polynomial-time regularities at polynomial budgets (`terms` Lemma 3.1, Thm 3.3, Cor 3.5); but theoretical *vocabulary* can be eliminated at polynomial cost, by storing the content in objects that no observational term names: per hypothesis under total material (reading (b)), and at the level of fit classes under the other readings, where already finite observational theories fit what all hypotheses fit (`terms` Prop 4.1, 4.3, Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7; Thm 8.1(iii));
   * a finite theory about the named objects with any unary theoretical predicates and constants, compatible with the first-order theory of the named objects, fits exactly the regular regularities; one binary theoretical relation, or quantification over unnamed objects (theoretical entities), restores what unrestricted hypotheses fit (every decidable regularity under the readings (a2), (b2); $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ under (b); $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ under (a1)); a finite observational theory that uses unnamed objects to fit a non-regular regularity must prove some $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-sentence false in the named structure, and for the theory built there that sentence is $\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$, true of no named object (`terms` Thm 5.1, 5.3, Thm 4.6, Prop 9.1(b)); with free deduction, the existence of a finite theory with one binary theoretical relation is the Kleene–Craig–Vaught theorem (`terms` §10), and what is new there is the derivation bounds and fit classes;
   * with free deduction and finite theories, theoretical functions or sorts pay without bound (infinity; PA) (`terms` Thm 6.2, 6.4);
   * for usability by a weak reasoner, under a cost on the written theory, intermediate concepts pay exponentially for one law, for a reasoner that can chain through them ($4m-6$ clauses against $2^{m-1}$), but not for every system of laws, where every usable theory stays exponential with any intermediate concepts (`shape` Prop 3.1, Prop 3.8, Cor 2.7(c), Thm 3.6 [proved there]; scout N2 Prop 2.5).

   So what pays, in the settings where anything theoretical pays, is theoretical *relations* and *entities*, not vocabulary as such; and in the idealised setting nothing theoretical is indispensable [the reading is `terms`' and ours; assessment].
4. **Theories that revise the base abstractions** (meaning change; questions shown ill-posed: simultaneity, mass). No formal handle in SAI [assessment].

**Hempel's dilemma and the replies to it (new; referee A, issue 6).** Hempel's dilemma: if theoretical terms serve their purpose of systematising observations, they are dispensable (Craig, Ramsey), and if they do not, they are pointless [known: hempel1958theoretician; content: secondary]. Three classical replies, and what the formal results say about each:
* *Impracticality.* Craig's replacement axiomatisation is infinite and useless in practice [known: craig1956replacement; content: secondary]. The formal results make this a theorem, but only about Craig's construction and run-padded axiomatisations under charged derivations; theoretical vocabulary as such is eliminable at polynomial cost (`terms` Thm 8.1(ii)–(iii) and its "What this does and does not show") [proved there]. This corrects the brief's seed (§6, last paragraph).
* *Inductive systematisation.* Hempel himself argued that theoretical terms may be indispensable for *inductive* systematisation: a Craigian replacement preserves the deductive connections among observation sentences but may lose inductive ones; the debate (Scheffler, Lehrer and Tuomela for it; Bohnert, Hooker and Stegmüller against) is surveyed by Niiniluoto [known: niiniluoto1972inductive; bibliographic data and abstract verified by scout L / `terms`; the attribution of the inductive reply to Hempel 1958 and the list of participants rest on referee A's search (Wikipedia "Craig's theorem"; Niiniluoto's abstract): secondary]. **Assessment.** SAI *is* an inductive systematisation, so this reply can be tested in it. In the universal mixture with free deduction, the posterior predictive probability of every observational sentence, given observational data, is the same up to $2^{\pm2c}$ whether or not hypotheses may use theoretical vocabulary (`terms` Thm 2.1 and Rem 2.2 [proved there]). So, in the idealised Bayesian setting, **Hempel's inductive-systematisation reply fails**: theoretical terms add no inductive power among observational sentences beyond a constant factor. This is a statement about the mixture, not about any single theory; and Hempel's own definition of inductive systematisation (relative to a single theory and a confirmation relation) was not checked against his text (`terms` says the same). It contrasts with Hintikka–Tuomela's monadic inductive logic, where theoretical concepts do change inductive probabilities [known: hintikka1970towards, niiniluoto1973theoretical; content: abstract level, scout L via `terms` §10]. Where something theoretical pays — finite theories (theoretical functions, sorts, binary relations, entities: `terms` Thm 6.2, 6.4, 4.6, 5.1) and charged derivations — the reply survives in a corrected form: what is indispensable there is theoretical relations or entities, not vocabulary.
* *Aims beyond prediction.* Putnam argued that the instrumentalist use of Craig's theorem rests on the misconception that science aims only at prediction [known: putnam1965craig; content: secondary (referee A's search)]. The question-answering view is a partial version of this reply: theories are scored on the questions they answer, which may be about unobservables only if the base language contains them (ladder steps 2–3).

**The strongest objection to the ladder** [assessment]: the "existing abstractions" of step 2 are themselves products of earlier theory (heliocentric longitudes; §4.1), and a theory that revises them (step 4) is then scored in a language it rejects; Kuhn's case of "earth" before and after Copernicus is the classic example [known: kuhn1970structure; content: secondary]. The ladder accepts this: it is why step 4 is listed and why SAI is placed on step 2 only.

SAI sits on step 2, with partial results on step 3. The paper should present step 2 as a step in the right direction and say plainly that steps 3–4 are needed.

## 2. What a theory does: questions against raw data (T2)

### 2.1 Questions

**Definition 2.0** (NOTATION §3.2, following the erotetic tradition [known: belnap1976logic, hintikka1999inquiry; content: secondary]). A *question* is a finite set $\mathcal Q=\{\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_k\}$ of pairwise $B$-incompatible sentences with $B\vdash\bigvee_i\varphi_i$ (its direct answers). A theory $T$ *answers* $\mathcal Q$ if $B\cup T\vdash\varphi_i$ for some $i$, and *answers it partially* if it proves $\bigvee_{i\in J}\varphi_i$ for some $J\subsetneq[k]$, $J\neq\emptyset$. A *conditional question* is a pair $(\alpha,\ell)$, "given $\alpha$, is $\ell$ the case?"; a theory $T$ is compatible with the datum "yes" when $B\cup T\cup\{\alpha\}$ is consistent and proves $\ell$ (`shape`'s encoding (E3), with `laws`; the two-valued encoding "$\alpha\to\ell$ is true" is degenerate, since a single world fits all question data: `shape` Props 1.8, 1.9).

**Proposition 2.1 (the answer distribution of SAI) [proved; computed: referee B's rb1 (4)].** Let $M_A(D)>0$ and $\mathcal Q$ a question. Put $\mathrm{Un}_D(\mathcal Q):=\Unif(E_D\setminus\bigcup_iO_{\varphi_i})/\Unif(E_D)$, the posterior probability that the random theory proves no direct answer. Then
(a) $\sum_{i\le k}\Bel_D(\varphi_i)+\mathrm{Un}_D(\mathcal Q)=1$;
(b) for $\emptyset\ne J\subsetneq[k]$, $\Bel_D(\bigvee_{i\in J}\varphi_i)\ge\sum_{i\in J}\Bel_D(\varphi_i)$, and the difference is the posterior mass of theories that answer $\mathcal Q$ partially through $J$ but prove no single answer in $J$; for $J=[k]$, $\Bel_D(\bigvee_i\varphi_i)=1$ and the difference is $\mathrm{Un}_D(\mathcal Q)$;
(c) for a yes/no question $\{\varphi,\neg\varphi\}$, (a) is the trichotomy $\Bel_D+\Dis_D+\Indep_D=1$.

*Proof.* On $E_D$ the theory $B\cup A_\omega$ is consistent. If it proved $\varphi_i$ and $\varphi_j$ with $i\ne j$, it would prove $\varphi_i\wedge\varphi_j$, and $B\vdash\neg(\varphi_i\wedge\varphi_j)$, a contradiction. So the events $E_D\cap O_{\varphi_i}$ are pairwise disjoint, and with $E_D\setminus\bigcup_iO_{\varphi_i}$ they partition $E_D$; divide by $\Unif(E_D)$. (b): $O_{\varphi_i}\subseteq O_{\bigvee_J\varphi_j}$ for $i\in J$, and the $O_{\varphi_i}\cap E_D$ are disjoint; the difference is the mass of $E_D\cap O_{\bigvee_J\varphi_j}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in J}O_{\varphi_i}$. For $J=[k]$, $B\vdash\bigvee_i\varphi_i$, so $O_{\bigvee_i\varphi_i}\supseteq E_D$, and the difference is $\mathrm{Un}_D(\mathcal Q)$ by (a). (c) is (a) with $k=2$. $\square$

*Computed (referee B).* On 279 random finite cases (propositional random theories, random backgrounds and data), (a) and (b) hold to $4\times10^{-16}$ (`referee_code/rb1_formal.out` (4); re-run in this round, identical).

**Reading [assessment].** Raw-data prediction answers one question, "what is the next observation?", and answers it fully: a total predictive distribution has no unanswered mass. SAI answers every question expressible in the base language, with an explicit "unanswered" mass. In SOI (labellers on sentences) the analogue of $\mathrm{Un}$ exists but mixes logical independence with laziness (`sfi` Prop 6.1(c)), and the answer masses of a $k$-way question need not respect the incompatibility of the answers unless the labeller is consistent.

### 2.2 The conservation-law world: what a law is worth to each task

This re-proves scout N2's Prop 5.1 and makes its reading exact; `checks/c5_conservation.py` re-implements N2's `n4` independently; `checks/c9_revision.py` part (1) adds the chain-rule identity (d) after referee A's issue 12. (Local notation, renamed from `notes.md` to avoid clashes: the dynamics is $\sigma$, the class size $Z$, the horizon $t_{\max}$.)

**Setting.** World $W_m$ ($m$ even): micro-states $x\in\{0,1\}^m$; the dynamics $\sigma$ is a uniformly random permutation of each weight class (so $|x|$ is conserved: the *law*); $x_0$ uniform on the class of weight $m/2$, of size $Z:=\binom m{m/2}$; there are $2^m$ states. Raw data: the trajectory $x_0,x_1,\dots$. Questions: "what is $|x_t|$?", $t=0,1,\dots$. Predictors (Bayes for the stated hypothesis): RAW-LAW ($\sigma$ a uniform random permutation of each weight class); RAW-NOLAW ($\sigma$ a uniform random permutation of $\{0,1\}^m$); QA-LAW ($|x_t|=|x_0|$, $|x_0|$ uniform on $\{0..m\}$); QA-NOLAW ($|x_t|$ i.i.d. uniform on $\{0..m\}$); QA-BIN ($|x_t|$ i.i.d. Binomial$(m,\frac12)$); MIX (prior ½ on each hypothesis of a task).

**Proposition 2.2 [proved; computed: c5, c9 (1); re-computed by referee B, rb1 (1)].**
(a) *Raw data.* Let $\ell$ be the length of the cycle of $x_0$. For $t<\ell$ the step-$t$ losses are $\log_2(Z-t)$ (RAW-LAW) and $\log_2(2^m-t)$ (RAW-NOLAW); for $t\ge\ell$ both are 0. The law's gain at step $t<\ell$ is $g_t=\log_2\frac{2^m-t}{Z-t}$, nondecreasing in $t$, and for $t\le Z/2$
$$\log_2\tfrac{2^m}Z\ \le\ g_t\ \le\ \log_2\tfrac{2^m}Z+1,\qquad \log_2\tfrac{2^m}Z=\tfrac12\log_2\tfrac{\pi m}2+o(1).$$
Its share of the step loss, $g_t/\log_2(2^m-t)$, is $O(\log m/m)$ for $t\le Z/2$.
(b) *Questions.* QA-LAW pays $\log_2(m+1)$ for $|x_0|$ and 0 afterwards; QA-NOLAW pays $\log_2(m+1)$ per question. Over $t_{\max}+1$ questions the law's share of the no-law loss is $t_{\max}/(t_{\max}+1)\to1$. (This is so by construction: the toy asks only about the conserved quantity. Choosing which questions matter is ladder steps 3–4 and L4; historically, the energy concept arose together with its conservation law.)
(c) *Learning.* In each task MIX pays at most 1 bit more than the better of its two hypotheses, on every trajectory.
(d) *Absolute value: the chain rule.* For $t<\ell$, RAW-NOLAW's step loss splits exactly as
$$\log_2(2^m-t)=\underbrace{\log_2\tfrac{2^m-t}{Z-t}}_{-\log_2P_{\rm NOLAW}(|x_{t+1}|=m/2\,\mid\,x_{0..t})}+\underbrace{\log_2(Z-t)}_{\text{the state within its class}},$$
and RAW-LAW's step loss is the second term. So **the law's raw gain $g_t$ is exactly the loss of the raw no-law model's own answer to the question "what is $|x_{t+1}|$?"**. Against the i.i.d. baselines: QA-BIN's realised loss per question is $-\log_2\binom m{m/2}2^{-m}=\log_2(2^m/Z)=g_0$ (so the law is worth $g_0$ per question, and $g_t\in[g_0,g_0+1]$ per raw step for $t\le Z/2$); QA-NOLAW's is $\log_2(m+1)$, about twice $g_0$.

*Proof.* (a) Under a uniformly random permutation of a set $X$, given a path $x_0,\dots,x_t$ of distinct elements, $x_{t+1}$ is uniform on $(X\setminus\{x_0,\dots,x_t\})\cup\{x_0\}$, a set of $|X|-t$ elements (the path is an initial segment of the cycle of $x_0$, and the rest of the permutation is uniform given it); once the path returns to $x_0$ the cycle is known and later steps are determined. For RAW-LAW, $X$ is the weight class ($|X|=Z$, and the true trajectory stays in it); for RAW-NOLAW, $|X|=2^m$. Since $2^m>Z$, $(2^m-t)/(Z-t)$ is increasing in $t$; for $t\le Z/2$, $\frac{2^m-t}{Z-t}\le\frac{2^m}{Z/2}$. Stirling: $Z=2^m\sqrt{2/(\pi m)}(1+o(1))$. The share bound: $g_t\le\log_2(2^m/Z)+1=O(\log m)$ and $\log_2(2^m-t)\ge m-1$. (b) Immediate. (c) $-\log_2(\frac12 2^{-a}+\frac12 2^{-b})\le\min(a,b)+1$. (d) Under RAW-NOLAW, $x_{t+1}$ is uniform on a set of $2^m-t$ states of which $Z-t$ have weight $m/2$ (the unvisited states of the class, plus $x_0$); so $P(|x_{t+1}|=m/2\mid x_{0..t})=(Z-t)/(2^m-t)$, and given the class $x_{t+1}$ is uniform on those $Z-t$ states, exactly RAW-LAW's predictive distribution. The binomial value is $P_{\rm Bin}(m/2)=Z2^{-m}$. $\square$

**Computed [c5; c9 (1)].** (A) Per-step values at the start of a trajectory agree with `n4` to its printed precision (largest difference 0.0005 bits): the gain is 1.871, 2.148, 2.348, 2.837, 3.331, 3.829, 5.326, 10.292 bits at $m=8,12,16,32,64,128,1024,10^6$, against $\frac12\log_2(\pi m/2)$ = 1.826, 2.118, 2.326, 2.826, 3.326, 3.826, 5.326, 10.292; shares 0.23, 0.18, 0.15, 0.089, 0.052, 0.030, 0.0052, $1.0\times10^{-5}$. (B) Exact expectations at $m=12$ ($Z=924$, $2^m=4096$), over the cycle length (uniform on $\{1..Z\}$): over $t_{\max}=300$ steps, raw loss 2415.6 bits (law) against 3004.9 (no law), share 0.196; mixture 2416.6; QA loss over 301 questions 3.70 (law) against 1113.8 (no law), share 0.997. Over a whole cycle the raw share is 0.232 and the mean per-step gain 2.75 bits. (C) 2000 simulated runs by sequential sampling: mean raw losses 2405.9 ± 18.3 and 2992.6 ± 22.9, within one standard error of (B); the cycle length passes a uniformity check (deciles 181–210 of expected 200; KS distance 0.020); MIX − LAW ≤ 1.0000 bits in every run. (D) For N2's single run (cycle length 275), the exact formula gives 2643.5 and 3286.4 bits, share 0.1956, as `n4` printed. (E, c9 (1)) Per question at $m=8,12,16,32,64,128,1024$: QA-BIN 1.871, 2.148, 2.348, 2.837, 3.331, 3.829, 5.326 bits $=g_0$; QA-NOLAW 3.170, 3.700, 4.088, 5.044, 6.022, 7.011, 10.001; $g_{Z/2}$ = 2.659, 2.976, 3.199, 3.732, 4.258, 4.777, 6.308. The identity (d) holds at $m=12$ for all $t<Z$ to $1.8\times10^{-15}$ bits. Referee B's rb1 (1) reproduces every number of (A)–(D).

*On referee A's baseline figure.* Referee A (issue 12, ra1 (1)) compared the raw gain with the *entropy* of Binomial$(m,\frac12)$, $\approx\frac12\log_2(\pi em/2)$, and found a gap tending to $\frac12\log_2e=0.72$ bit. That is the expected binomial loss in a world where $|x_t|$ is binomially distributed; in $W_m$ every $|x_t|$ equals $m/2$, and the realised binomial loss is exactly $g_0$. The referee's conclusion ("nearly equal; the factor of about 2 was the uniform baseline") is right, and (d) makes it exact.

**The exact reading [assessment].**
* The author's "this doesn't improve one's ability to predict pixels in an arbitrary video basically at all" (`phil-sci-science-predicts-only-very-particularly.md`) is true of the *share* of raw loss (it tends to 0) and false of the *absolute* gain, which is *equal* to the law's value for the corresponding question (d). What makes the share vanish is micro-detail that no theory addresses and that dominates the raw loss.
* Both tasks learn the law at the same price (c): the theory is "in" the raw predictor (objection o1 of §1.3).
* So the two tasks differ in their *target* (which losses are counted), not in what must be learned, nor in what the law is worth per step. This is the formal content of "a theory answers questions rather than predicting raw data" in this toy: it is a claim about what we score, and it is correct to score questions when what we want is answers.

**Remark (purpose-relative grading and logarithmic loss) [assessment].** This replaces the development record's "possible error" note §6.2 (see §6). The condensation essay says that predictions are graded by purpose-made metrics and that "there is really nothing fundamentally right about these information-theoretic losses". Two jobs should be kept apart. *Grading a prediction for use:* what is graded, and with which tolerance, is decided by the purpose; we agree, and the paper's interval data (§4.1) are built this way. *Scoring credences and comparing theories:* for ideal (well-specified) probabilistic beliefs every strictly proper score has the same optimum, the true distribution, and Bayesian updating compares theories by cumulative logarithmic loss; the logarithmic score is, up to positive affine transformation, the only strictly proper *local* scoring rule on more than two outcomes [known: bernardo1979expected; content: secondary], but which proper score is privileged for epistemic purposes is contested (accuracy-first epistemology argues for the Brier score [known, unverified here: Pettigrew 2016; Leitgeb and Pettigrew; Greaves and Wallace 2006 justify conditionalisation for strictly proper epistemic utilities generally; all via referee A's search]). For bounded or misspecified models, which is the essay's subject, different proper losses select different best-in-class models, and raw-data log loss spends capacity on detail no purpose asks for: that is the essay's point, and it is correct. SAI's answer is to let the purpose fix the propositions (which sentences are data, at what tolerance) and to score credences on those; and even inside SAI the price of a datum can depend on the loss convention by an unbounded amount (the "independent" value is scored in the semimeasure and three-valued conventions and not in the normalised one: `logic` Prop 3.11).

### 2.3 Certification and extraction

**Proposition 2.3.**
(a) **[proved; computed: referee B's rb1 (3)]** A deterministic procedure with black-box access to a map $\pi:\{0,1\}^m\to\{0,1\}^m$ that decides "$\forall x\ |\pi(x)|=|x|$" must query all $2^m$ points on some input. If $\pi$ is promised to be a permutation, it must query at least $2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}=2^m(1-\Theta(m^{-1/2}))$ points on some input, and this many suffice, so the bound is exact.
(b) **[proved; standard]** For dynamics on an infinite state space given by programs, "every state on the trajectory has the conserved value" is $\Pi_1$-complete.
(c) **[proved there]** The SAI posterior reports $\Bel_D(\text{law})$, the mass of theories that prove the law; a raw predictor reports the probability that all future data conform. These differ (the ω-gap: AI-paper Thm 3.9; `fundamentals` Prop 8.5; `logic` Props 5.1–5.2, Thm 5.3).

*Proof.* (a) Maps: on the identity, if a point $x$ is not queried, the map that agrees with the identity except $\pi(x):=x\oplus e_1$ answers every query identically and violates the law. Permutations: on the identity, let $U$ be the set of unqueried points. If $U$ contains $x,y$ with $|x|\ne|y|$, the transposition $(x\ y)$ answers every query identically and violates the law; so a correct procedure leaves $U$ inside one weight class, $|U|\le\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}$. Conversely, querying every point outside a largest weight class suffices: a permutation that preserves weight outside that class maps the class onto itself. (b) Reduction from non-halting: the state at step $t$ records the configuration of a machine at step $t$ and has the conserved value unless the machine has halted by step $t$. (c) As cited. $\square$

*Computed (referee B, rb1 (3)).* Exact minimax game-tree search: 2 queries for $m=2$ (24 permutations) and 5 for $m=3$ (40,320 permutations), equal to $2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}$; for arbitrary maps with $m=2$ (256 maps), $4=2^m$.

**Refuted claim (kept).** Scout N2's Prop 5.2(a) says that deciding the law from a black-box *permutation* "must, in the worst case, query all $2^m$ states" [refuted as stated]. *Counterexample:* $m=2$; a procedure that knows $\pi$ is a permutation queries $00$ and $11$ only; if both are fixed points, $\pi$ permutes $\{01,10\}$ and preserves weight; otherwise the law fails. Two queries, not four (confirmed by referee B's exhaustive search). The corrected bound is (a); the reading is unchanged, since $2^m-\binom m{m/2}=2^m(1-O(m^{-1/2}))$.

**Reading [assessment; rephrased after referee A, issue 24].** Certifying a universal law from black-box access to the *dynamics* needs exponentially many queries (a), and for program dynamics it is $\Pi_1$-complete (b); and the quantity a raw *predictor* reports is a different quantity from belief in a law (c). So a good raw predictor does not by itself deliver belief in the law it embodies: obtaining it is a search or proof problem. This agrees with the brief's conclusion that any difference between theories and predictors lies in what is cheap to obtain from each, relative to a reasoner.

### 2.4 Question answering never needs more description than raw modelling plus an extractor

Scout N2 §5.3 gave this as a proof sketch for deterministic generators; here it is a theorem, random generators included. (The sampler is written $\Psi$; `notes.md` wrote $\Phi$, which is an explicit theory in `shape`.)

**Proposition 2.4 [proved, given `fundamentals` Thm 4.1 and the standard sampling construction].** Let $X$ be a finite alphabet, $\mu$ a computable probability measure on $X^{\mathbb N}$ (the raw world), $(\varphi_q)_{q\in\mathbb N}$ a computable family of sentences (the questions), and $g$ a partial computable function (the *extractor*) with $g(q,x)\in\{0,1\}$ computed from a finite prefix of $x$. Assume (H): for $\mu$-almost every $x$ the labelling $f_x:\varphi_q\mapsto g(q,x)$ is consistent with $B$ (e.g. the labels are the truth values of the $\varphi_q$ in a model of $B$ determined by $x$). Put $\mu_g(D):=\mu\{x:f_x\supseteq D\}$. Then for every finite data set $D$ of question–answer pairs
$$M_A(D)\ \ge\ 2^{-K(\mu)-K(g)-c}\,\mu_g(D),\qquad\text{so}\qquad L_A(D)\le-\log\mu_g(D)+K(\mu)+K(g)+c .$$
In particular, for a deterministic computable world $x$ (i.e. $\mu=\delta_x$) with $f_x$ consistent with $B$ (which is (H) for $\mu=\delta_x$), $L_A(D)\le K(x)+K(g)+c$ on every $D$ with $f_x\supseteq D$.

*Proof.* There is a partial computable map $\Psi$ from tapes to $X^{\mathbb N}$, computable from an index of $\mu$, with $\Psi(\omega)$ defined for $\Unif$-almost every $\omega$ and $\Psi_*\Unif=\mu$ (the standard construction: read tape bits to refine a dyadic interval and output the next symbol $s$ as soon as the interval lies inside the $\mu$-interval of $s$ given the symbols already output; it fails only on the null set of tapes that hit interval boundaries). Let $G$ be the theory machine that, on tape $\omega$, computes $\Psi(\omega)$ as far as needed and emits $\varphi_q^{g(q,\Psi(\omega))}$ for every $q$ on which $g$ halts; it emits a label only once the tape prefix read so far fixes the prefix of $\Psi(\omega)$ that $g$ reads, so each emission depends on a finite prefix of $\omega$, as a theory machine requires. $G$ is c.e. and $K(G)\le K(\mu)+K(g)+c$. On every tape with $\Psi(\omega)$ defined and (H) holding, $A^G_\omega=\Gamma_{f_{\Psi(\omega)}}$ is consistent with $B$, and it proves $\psi_D$ whenever $f_{\Psi(\omega)}\supseteq D$. Hence $\nu_G(D)\ge\mu_g(D)$, and `fundamentals` Thm 4.1 gives $M_A(D)\ge2^{-K(\nu_G)}\nu_G(D)\ge2^{-K(G)-c}\nu_G(D)$. For $\mu=\delta_x$, $\mu_g(D)=1$ exactly when $f_x\supseteq D$. $\square$

**Reading [assessment].** SAI on questions never needs more description than a raw-data model plus the extractor that turns raw data into answers. The extractor is the bridge from raw data to the question language, and its cost $K(g)$ is not small in real cases (it contains, for instance, whatever recognises a cat or a planet in an image): this is the bridge gap of §5, L1(a), in formal dress. The converse fails badly: answers do not determine the raw data, and in the conservation world the raw loss exceeds the question loss by the whole micro-entropy (Prop 2.2).

### 2.5 Answering and predicting (scout N1's N9, assembled and completed)

**Proposition 2.5.**
(a) **[proved there: `fundamentals` Thm 5.1]** *Answering implies predicting.* If a consistent r.e. $T$ proves every datum of a consistent reveal sequence, the cumulative loss is at most $-\log\Unif_A(\Ext T)\le K_\equiv(T)+c$, in any order.
(b) **[proved there: `logic` Thm 2.2, scout N1 Thm E1]** *Predicting does not imply that any theory answers.* On the lottery stream $M_A$ has bounded loss while no consistent r.e. theory proves $D_\infty$, and $W_A(D_n)\to0$.
(c) **[proved there: `fundamentals` Prop 8.3]** *Predicting a text does not converge to answers on unasked questions.* On two-valued data from an incomplete $T^*$, the limit posterior is the prior on consistent extensions; on every $T^*$-independent question all three trichotomy values keep positive shares. Three-valued data identify $[T^*]$.
(d) **[proved; computed: rb1 (4)]** *Closure.* For all $D$ with $M_A(D)>0$ and all $\varphi,\psi$: $\Bel_D(\varphi\wedge\psi)\ge\Bel_D(\varphi)+\Bel_D(\psi)-1$; more generally $\Bel_D(\bigwedge_{i\le k}\varphi_i)\ge1-\sum_i(1-\Bel_D(\varphi_i))$; and if $B\cup\{\varphi\}\vdash\psi$ then $\Bel_D(\psi)\ge\Bel_D(\varphi)$. So the "δ-answers" (yes if $\Bel_D\ge1-\delta$) are closed under entailment and, with error $k\delta$, under $k$-fold conjunction.

*Proof of (d).* $O_\varphi\cap O_\psi\subseteq O_{\varphi\wedge\psi}$ (a theory proving both proves the conjunction); within $E_D$, $\Unif(O_\varphi\cap O_\psi\cap E_D)\ge\Unif(O_\varphi\cap E_D)+\Unif(O_\psi\cap E_D)-\Unif(E_D)$; divide by $\Unif(E_D)$; induct on $k$. If $B\cup\{\varphi\}\vdash\psi$ then $O_\varphi\subseteq O_\psi$. $\square$

**Reading [assessment].** (a)–(c) give a formal vocabulary for "answering questions ≫ predicting pixels": answering is the stronger achievement ((a) one way, (b) not the other way), and success at predicting a presentation of data does not yield answers to questions not asked (c). (d) is the coherence that a predictor of separate labels lacks: in consistent SOI the probability of labelling an entailed or unasked sentence never reaches 1 in the semimeasure convention (`sfi` Prop 6.1(c)).

## 3. Explanation (T3)

### 3.1 Derivation and unification

In the axiom view, $T$ explains $\varphi$ when $B\cup T\vdash\varphi$: the deductive-nomological pattern [known: hempel1948studies; content: source (opening seen) and secondary]. The quality of an explanation is measured by how short $T$ is relative to what it derives: explanation as unification [known: friedman1974explanation, kitcher1981explanatory, kitcher1989explanatory; content: abstract (Kitcher 1981), secondary (the others)]. The precise measure is the explanatory power of `laws` Def 5.2, $\mathrm{EP}_D(\mathcal A;T)=(\mathrm{Exc}_D(T\setminus\mathcal A)-\mathrm{Exc}_D(T))/\|\mathcal A\|$, bits of data explained per bit of axiom; on an edge of the steeper-prior hull it is the edge slope (`laws` Def 5.2, Prop 5.1; scout N2 Def 4.3, Prop 4.2 [proved there]). It is a per-bit log-likelihood ratio (`laws` Prop 5.8(a)), and Myrvold's Bayesian unification term [known: myrvold2003bayesian; content: abstract and secondary summaries] is zero when the parameters are stated and equals the bits of the shared unknown parameters when they are integrated out (`laws` Prop 5.8(b), (c) [proved there]). This record does not re-define EP. (NOTATION §6.4 writes $|\mathcal A|$; `laws` and this record write $\|\mathcal A\|$, the length of the module; §8 asks NOTATION to align.)

### 3.2 Asymmetry: the flagpole

Derivability is symmetric where explanation is not: a pole's height and the sun's elevation entail the shadow's length, and the shadow's length and the elevation entail the height [known: bromberger1966why, glymour1978two; content: the attribution of the flagpole example to Bromberger is Glymour's (opening of glymour1978two, seen in search results); Bromberger's text not seen]. Scout N2's C13 is made precise here. (Local symbols renamed from `notes.md`: height $\mathrm{ht}$, shadow $\mathrm{sh}$, number of poles $N_{\rm p}$; NOTATION reserves $h$ for derivation budgets and $N$ for $2^d$.)

**Setting.** Two sorts, poles and reals; $B$ = the theory of real closed fields (on the real sort). Pole constants $p_1,\dots,p_{N_{\rm p}}$; function symbols $\mathrm{ht}$ (height) and $\mathrm{sh}(p,u)$ (shadow length when the tangent of the sun's elevation is $u>0$). Data: sentences $\mathrm{sh}(p_i,u)=v$ and $\mathrm{ht}(p_i)=v$ with rational numerals $u,v$ (interval versions behave the same). Two equivalent formulations of the law:
$$\Lambda_h:=\forall p\,\forall u\,(u>0\to \mathrm{sh}(p,u)\cdot u=\mathrm{ht}(p)),\qquad \Lambda_s:=\forall p\,\forall u\,(u>0\to \mathrm{sh}(p,u)\cdot u=\mathrm{sh}(p,1))\ \wedge\ \forall p\,\mathrm{ht}(p)=\mathrm{sh}(p,1).$$
Theories, with $a_i$ the heights: $T_H:=\Lambda_h+\{\mathrm{ht}(p_i)=a_i\}_i$, $T_S:=\Lambda_h+\{\mathrm{sh}(p_i,1)=a_i\}_i$ (the shadow at 45° "explains" the height), $T_H':=\Lambda_s+\{\mathrm{ht}(p_i)=a_i\}_i$, $T_S':=\Lambda_s+\{\mathrm{sh}(p_i,1)=a_i\}_i$. Causal extensions with intervention vocabulary $\mathrm{sh}_H(p,u,v)$ (the shadow after the height of $p$ is set to $v$) and $\mathrm{ht}_S(p,v)$ (the height after the shadow $\mathrm{sh}(p,1)$ is set to $v$ by an intervention on the shadow, e.g. by an obstacle): $C_H:=T_H+\{\forall p\forall u\forall v\,(u>0\to \mathrm{sh}_H(p,u,v)\cdot u=v),\ \forall p\forall v\,\mathrm{ht}_S(p,v)=\mathrm{ht}(p)\}$ (height exogenous) and $C_S:=T_S+\{\forall p\forall u\forall v\,\mathrm{sh}_H(p,u,v)=\mathrm{sh}(p,u),\ \forall p\forall v\,\mathrm{ht}_S(p,v)=v\}$ (shadow exogenous).

**Derivation cost** (made precise after referee B, issue 13). The cost of a derivation is the number of closed instances it uses of the universally quantified *conjuncts* of the law axioms ($\Lambda_h$ has one, $\Lambda_s$ two); steps in $B$ and uses of the initial conditions are free. The least cost of a datum is the least number of such instances that, together with $B$ and the initial conditions, entail it (the semantic, Herbrand-style count the proof uses).

**Proposition 3.1 (the flagpole tie and what breaks it) [proved; computed: c6; re-computed by referee B, rb1 (5)].**
(a) $B\vdash\Lambda_h\leftrightarrow\Lambda_s$, and $T_H,T_S,T_H',T_S'$ are deductively equivalent over $B$. Hence they lie in one class $[T]$: every data set is compatible with all four or with none; $\Unif_A$ cannot distinguish them; in the coin-free version the posterior odds of any two programs for them equal their prior odds on every $D$; and $|K(T_S)-K(T_H)|\le c$, since a fixed computable translation maps either axiom list to the other.
(b) For a shadow datum $\mathrm{sh}(p_i,u)=a_i/u$ with $u\neq1$: $T_H$ and $T_S'$ need exactly one instance, $T_S$ and $T_H'$ exactly two. For a height datum $\mathrm{ht}(p_i)=a_i$: $T_H,T_H'$ need none, $T_S,T_S'$ one. For a reference-shadow datum $\mathrm{sh}(p_i,1)=a_i$: $T_H,T_H'$ need one, $T_S,T_S'$ none. So, under derivation charges, the theory whose initial conditions sit at the *hub* of the law's formulation wins, and which quantity is the hub is a property of the formulation, not of the world: the two hub theories $T_H$ and $T_S'$ have equal costs on all shadow data with $u\ne1$, and their texts differ by $|\Lambda_s|-|\Lambda_h|$, a constant independent of $N_{\rm p}$ and of the data.
(c) $C_H$ and $C_S$ have the same consequences in the language without $\mathrm{sh}_H,\mathrm{ht}_S$, and one interventional datum separates them: $C_H\vdash \mathrm{sh}_H(p_1,1,2a_1)=2a_1$ ("doubling the pole doubles its 45° shadow") while $C_S\vdash \mathrm{sh}_H(p_1,1,2a_1)=a_1$, which refutes it when $a_1\ne0$; likewise $C_H\vdash \mathrm{ht}_S(p_1,v)=a_1$ and $C_S\vdash \mathrm{ht}_S(p_1,v)=v$.

*Proof.* (a) At $u=1$, $\Lambda_h$ gives $\mathrm{sh}(p,1)=\mathrm{ht}(p)$; with this, $\Lambda_h$ and the first conjunct of $\Lambda_s$ are interderivable by field arithmetic; so $B\vdash\Lambda_h\leftrightarrow\Lambda_s$, and given either law the initial conditions $\mathrm{ht}(p_i)=a_i$ and $\mathrm{sh}(p_i,1)=a_i$ are interderivable. Compatibility in SAI depends only on $\Cn$ (`fundamentals` Prop 2.1(c)); the class probabilities are of classes; a coin-free posterior ratio of two compatible programs is the ratio of their weights. The translation replacing each $\mathrm{ht}(c)=v$ by $\mathrm{sh}(c,1)=v$ and conversely is computable and fixed. (b) *Upper bounds:* the derivations are explicit (instantiate the law at $(p_i,u)$, substitute the initial condition, do arithmetic; for $T_S$, first instantiate at $(p_i,1)$ to obtain $\mathrm{ht}(p_i)=a_i$). *Lower bounds:* with fewer instances, a model of $B$ plus the initial conditions plus those instances in which the datum fails exists, because the term $\mathrm{sh}(p_i,u)$ (resp. $\mathrm{ht}(p_i)$, $\mathrm{sh}(p_i,1)$) is then linked to no initial condition and can be given any value; `c6` checks this exhaustively over all subsets of the relevant instance pool by exact linear algebra. The text difference is the difference of the two law texts. (c) Without the intervention symbols, $C_H$ and $C_S$ are $T_H$ and $T_S$ plus axioms about fresh symbols; every model of $T_H$ (resp. $T_S$) expands (put $\mathrm{sh}_H(p,u,v):=v/u$, $\mathrm{ht}_S(p,v):=\mathrm{ht}(p)$, resp. $\mathrm{sh}_H:=\mathrm{sh}$, $\mathrm{ht}_S(p,v):=v$), so they are conservative, and by (a) their old-vocabulary consequences coincide. The two interventional consequences are instances of the added axioms. $\square$

**Computed [c6].** (1) On 2000 random data (500 random data sets with 1–4 poles, heights and elevations), $T_H,T_S,T_H',T_S'$ prove, refute or leave open exactly the same data (2000/2000; "open" only for elevations whose law instance is not in the finite pool). (2) Least instance counts by exhaustive search: shadow at $u=2$: 1, 2, 2, 1 (for $T_H,T_S,T_H',T_S'$); reference shadow: 1, 0, 1, 0; height: 0, 1, 0, 1 (referee B's own exact rational linear algebra, rb1 (5), gives the same, also at $u=3$). (3) In a literal syntax $T_S$ is longer than $T_H$ by one token per pole (the term $\mathrm{sh}(p,1)$ is longer than $\mathrm{ht}(p)$), so a *literal* code length also breaks the tie, again in favour of the theory whose primitive happens to be shorter; the hub theories $T_H$, $T_S'$ differ by 20 tokens independently of $N_{\rm p}$. (4) The interventional data: $C_H$ proves, $C_S$ refutes, both; both prove the observational data. **Kitcher's answer, formalised (new; referee A, issue 3).** Kitcher's unificationist answer to the asymmetry is not a choice of formulation of the shadow law: the best systematisation of *all* our beliefs contains one "origin and development" pattern that derives the dimensions of artefacts, mountains, stars and organisms from their origins and later history; a height-from-shadow pattern would add a pattern, and it fails for objects that cast no shadow [known: kitcher1989explanatory; content: secondary (SEP *Scientific Explanation* and Kitcher's text, via referee A's search)]. The development record's sentence "Prop 3.1(b) is a formal version of why such answers depend on the choice of patterns" misdescribed this. The toy of Prop 3.1 has no other law in which height figures, so it cannot represent Kitcher's answer. The following proposition adds such laws.

*Setting.* As in Prop 3.1, plus function symbols $g_1,\dots,g_k$ on poles (further quantities: weight, the height on the construction plan, …) with nonzero rational constants $c_j$, and data $g_j(p_i)=v$. Laws in height form $\Gamma_j^{\rm ht}:=\forall p\,g_j(p)=c_j\cdot\mathrm{ht}(p)$, in shadow form $\Gamma_j^{\rm sh}:=\forall p\,g_j(p)=c_j\cdot\mathrm{sh}(p,1)$. Theories: the height hub $T_H^k:=\Lambda_h+\{\Gamma_j^{\rm ht}\}_{j\le k}+\{\mathrm{ht}(p_i)=a_i\}_i$; the mixed theory $T_S^k:=\Lambda_h+\{\Gamma_j^{\rm ht}\}_j+\{\mathrm{sh}(p_i,1)=a_i\}_i$; the shadow hub $T_{SS}^k:=\Lambda_s+\{\Gamma_j^{\rm sh}\}_j+\{\mathrm{sh}(p_i,1)=a_i\}_i$. *Dark variant:* a predicate Sunlit; the shadow law only for sunlit poles, $\Lambda_h^{\rm D}:=\forall p\,(\mathrm{Sunlit}(p)\to\forall u\,(u>0\to\mathrm{sh}(p,u)\cdot u=\mathrm{ht}(p)))$; sunlit poles $p_1,\dots,p_{N_{\rm p}}$ and dark poles $q_1,\dots,q_{N_{\rm d}}$, with the axioms $\mathrm{Sunlit}(p_i)$, $\neg\mathrm{Sunlit}(q_l)$ in every theory; shadow-form laws must split cases, $\Gamma_j^{\rm sh,D}:=\forall p\,\big((\mathrm{Sunlit}(p)\to g_j(p)=c_j\,\mathrm{sh}(p,1))\wedge(\neg\mathrm{Sunlit}(p)\to g_j(p)=c_j\,\mathrm{ht}(p))\big)$; $T_H^{k,\rm D}:=\Lambda_h^{\rm D}+\{\Gamma_j^{\rm ht}\}_j+\{\mathrm{ht}(p_i)=a_i\}_i+\{\mathrm{ht}(q_l)=b_l\}_l$ and $T_{SS}^{k,\rm D}:=\Lambda_h^{\rm D}+\{\Gamma_j^{\rm sh,D}\}_j+\{\mathrm{sh}(p_i,1)=a_i\}_i+\{\mathrm{ht}(q_l)=b_l\}_l$ (plus the Sunlit axioms).

**Proposition 3.2 (the flagpole with a system of laws) [proved].**
(a) *Class tie.* $T_H^k$, $T_S^k$, $T_{SS}^k$ are deductively equivalent over $B$, and so are $T_H^{k,\rm D}$ and $T_{SS}^{k,\rm D}$. So for every $k$ the posterior odds of the classes never move.
(b) *Universal prior.* $|K(T_{SS}^k)-K(T_H^k)|\le c$ and $|K(T_{SS}^{k,\rm D})-K(T_H^{k,\rm D})|\le c$, with $c$ independent of $k$, $N_{\rm p}$, $N_{\rm d}$ and the numerals.
(c) *Literal codes* (a theory charged by the symbols it writes, each axiom separately, no definitions). $|T_{SS}^k|-|T_H^k|=(|\Lambda_s|-|\Lambda_h|)+(k+N_{\rm p})\Delta$, where $\Delta:=|\mathrm{sh}(x,1)|-|\mathrm{ht}(x)|$ is the extra length of the reference-shadow term in the syntax ($\Delta=0$ if the language has a primitive for the reference shadow). In the dark variant $|T_{SS}^{k,\rm D}|-|T_H^{k,\rm D}|=k\,d_0+\sum_{j\le k}|c_j|+N_{\rm p}\Delta$, with $d_0>0$ the length of the case-split frame and $|c_j|$ the length of the numeral $c_j$ (written twice in the split law), so the shadow hub loses by at least $k\,d_0$, an amount growing with $k$, even when $\Delta=0$. With a definition, the overhead is a constant: define once $z(p)$ as $\mathrm{sh}(p,1)$ on sunlit poles and $\mathrm{ht}(p)$ on dark ones and write every law in $z$; in every model of the theory $z$ is coextensive with $\mathrm{ht}$.
(d) *Derivation charges* (instances of universally quantified conjuncts, as above). For a datum $g_j(p_i)=c_ja_i$: $T_H^k$ and $T_{SS}^k$ need exactly one instance, the mixed $T_S^k$ exactly two. On shadow data with $u\ne1$ the two hub theories need one each; on heights $T_H^k$ needs none and $T_{SS}^k$ one; on reference shadows the reverse.

*Proof.* (a) Every theory listed contains a form of the shadow law, which gives $B\vdash\mathrm{ht}(p)=\mathrm{sh}(p,1)$ for every (sunlit) pole $p$ (Prop 3.1(a)); substituting, each law in shadow form is equivalent to the law in height form, and each reference-shadow initial condition to the height condition; in the dark variant the case split collapses to $g_j(p)=c_j\,\mathrm{ht}(p)$ because on sunlit poles $\mathrm{sh}(p,1)=\mathrm{ht}(p)$. (b) The syntactic translations (replace $\mathrm{ht}(t)$ by $\mathrm{sh}(t,1)$ in the $g$-laws and in the initial conditions of sunlit poles, $\Lambda_h$ by $\Lambda_s$; in the dark variant, replace each $\Gamma_j^{\rm ht}$ by $\Gamma_j^{\rm sh,D}$) and their inverses are fixed computable maps on axiom lists, so a program for one list yields a program for the other at a constant extra length. (c) Counting: each $\Gamma_j^{\rm sh}$ differs from $\Gamma_j^{\rm ht}$ by one occurrence of the replaced term, each sunlit initial condition likewise; each $\Gamma_j^{\rm sh,D}$ is longer than $\Gamma_j^{\rm ht}$ by a fixed frame of length $d_0>0$ (the connectives, the two Sunlit atoms, a second copy of "$g_j(p)=\cdot$" and the term $\mathrm{sh}(p,1)$; a symbol $g_j$ counts as one token) plus a second copy of the numeral $c_j$. The definition of $z$ is a single axiom of constant length. (d) Upper bounds: instantiate $\Gamma_j$ at $p_i$ and use the initial condition (for $T_S^k$, first instantiate $\Lambda_h$ at $(p_i,1)$). Lower bounds: as in Prop 3.1(b) — with no instance of $\Gamma_j$ the term $g_j(p_i)$ is unconstrained; for $T_S^k$ with an instance of $\Gamma_j$ but none of $\Lambda_h$, $\mathrm{ht}(p_i)$ is linked to no initial condition and can take any value. $\square$

**Reading [assessment; rewritten after referee A, issue 3].**
* SAI's explanation relation ($T\vdash d$) is direction-free, and its posterior cannot move between deductively equivalent theories (Props 3.1(a), 3.2(a)).
* Kitcher's answer is a *whole-theory simplicity* effect, and SAI reproduces it only under a cost on the written theory: with a system of $k$ laws in which height figures, the shadow-hub formulation is longer by an amount growing with $k$ in a literal code without definitions — relative to the primitives when every object has a shadow, and even with a reference-shadow primitive when some objects cast none (Prop 3.2(c)). Under derivation charges, only the *mixed* theory (laws about height, initial conditions about shadows) loses, and it loses on every $g$-datum (Prop 3.2(d)). Under SAI's universal prior the whole effect is $O(1)$, whatever $k$ (Prop 3.2(b)): a fixed translation makes the formulation invisible, exactly as program length makes the reasoner invisible (`shape` Thm 1.11). So the asymmetry is recovered from simplicity only for a listing code, which charges each law statement separately; with definitions allowed, even that reduces to a constant, and the hub becomes a quantity coextensive with height. This is the formal sense in which Kitcher's answer depends on counting argument patterns.
* Direction enters cleanly with *interventional questions*: once the base language can pose them, which causal theory is right is decided by data, by one datum (Prop 3.1(c)). The intervention semantics was written into $C_H$, $C_S$ by hand, and SAI then only learns which stipulated causal theory is right; so the toy is *consistent with* the interventionist account [known: woodward2003making; content: secondary] and gives it no support over rivals (temporal order; process or mark-transmission accounts [known, unverified here: Salmon 1984, *Scientific Explanation and the Causal Structure of the World*, via referee A's report]). Whether explanatory asymmetry in general is interventional is not settled here.

### 3.3 Explanation by approximate correction

Newton's theory does not entail Kepler's laws; it contradicts them [known: duhem1954aim; content: secondary]; what it derives, *together with masses and initial conditions*, is a corrected statement and the claim that Kepler's laws hold within stated tolerances on stated domains, as in Schaffner's model of reduction to a corrected theory [known: schaffner1967approaches; content: abstract and commentary]. §4.3 gives the sizes: a fixed Kepler ellipse misses Mars by 1.6–1.9′ over one to three decades and 3.6′ over a century, the Moon by 1.1° within a month; the third-law constant differs between planets at the $10^{-3}$ level, as Newton explains. The provable toy is `laws` Prop 7.1 [proved there] (scout N2 C19 was a proof sketch): in an exact rational model the successor theory proves the honest old law on every window $W$, with least tolerance $\varepsilon_{\min}(W)\le((1+\eta\|P\|)^W-1)|a|$, refutes the exact old law when $\eta Pa\ne0$ (Prop 7.1(c)), and among the uniform-tolerance forms $H^\varepsilon_W$ of the old law, the strongest one the successor proves is also the strongest one that both theories prove (Prop 7.1(b), (d)). (The development record said "the strongest honest consequence the old and new theories share"; `laws`' referee A (M2) showed that the full common content of the two theories is the disjunction $T_0\vee T_N$, which is not a tolerance law; only the restriction to tolerance forms is proved.) **The point for claim E [assessment]:** "$T$ explains $\varphi$" must include "$T$ derives an honest approximation of $\varphi$ with an error bound on a domain"; plain derivation does not cover the central historical case, and the honest (tolerance-weakened) form of an approximate law is the strongest uniform-tolerance form its successor can explain.

### 3.4 Unification without better prediction

Copernicus explained why superior planets retrogress exactly at opposition: retrograde motion is the Earth overtaking them; Ptolemy had to stipulate the coincidence planet by planet [the astronomy: secondary (lecture notes and arXiv 1301.2119, located by WebSearch in the development round: in Ptolemy's model the epicycle radius of a superior planet stays parallel to the Earth–Sun line, which is what makes retrogression fall at opposition; in Copernicus's it is the Earth overtaking the planet); kuhn1957copernican is the standard history, but its own passage was not seen]. Copernicus's system also fixed the order and the relative distances of the planets from observation, which Ptolemy's could not (Kuhn's "harmony") [known: kuhn1957copernican; content: secondary; referee A's issue 26]: a second unification with no gain in accuracy, and the heliocentric counterpart of §4.2's size element $\rho$.

*Bookkeeping (corrected after referee B, issue 11, and `laws`' final record §5.3).* `laws` compares three theories with a common part [proved there; the lengths are not measured]: $P_f$, Ptolemy with free epicycle phases, which pays the coincidence of each superior planet's retrogression with opposition in its fit, $a_p$ bits per planet; $P_s$, Ptolemy with the coincidences stated as axioms ($\sum_pa_p$ more law bits than $P_f$, the same fit as Copernicus); and $C$, Copernicus ($H$ more law bits than $P_f$, the heliocentric reorganisation). Then: $C$ against $P_s$ (same fit, same predictions): $C$ wins at every steepness $\lambda$ iff $H<\sum_pa_p$ — *unification without better prediction*; $C$ against $P_f$: $C$ is $P_f$ plus the heliocentric module, whose EP is $\sum_pa_p/H$, so $C$ wins iff $\lambda<\sum_pa_p/H$; in the marginal-likelihood account the same $\sum_pa_p$ bits are Copernicus's extra likelihood against $P_f$, the *total* support $I(h;D)$ of `laws` Prop 5.8, not Myrvold's unification term $U$. (The development record said that the module has EP $\sum_pa_p/H$ "relative to the coincidence axioms it replaces, with no change in fit", which mixed the two comparisons; and it identified the gain with $U$ (Prop 5.8(c): $U=k_C$ for two data sets sharing a $k_C$-bit constant). That identification is withdrawn: for $n$ planets sharing the Sun's $k_C$-bit parameter, the natural multi-set generalisation of Prop 5.8(c) gives about $(n-1)k_C$, which equals $\sum_pa_p-k_C$ only if each coincidence costs about $k_C$, which was not checked.) This is the cleanest case of unification tracking an explanatory gain with no gain in accuracy, and the paper's best illustration of unification [assessment].

### 3.5 Verdict on explanation

Write: *a theory entails answers; derivation from a short theory is explanation in the unification sense, with at least these known gaps: asymmetry (Props 3.1, 3.2: no direction at the level of classes or under the universal prior; under a listing code or derivation charges only relative to the formulation, the primitives and the system of laws; cleanly with interventional questions); explanation by approximate correction (§3.3); and the two older ones, irrelevance and the conjunction problem.* Do not claim that derivation is explanation outright [assessment].

*The older gaps (added after referee A, issue 3) [assessment].*
* *Irrelevance.* Derivations from premises that include irrelevant ones (the standard examples: hexed salt dissolving in water; a man taking birth-control pills not becoming pregnant [known, unverified here: Kyburg 1965 and Salmon 1971, the standard sources, via referee A's report]). SAI's length prior addresses this only for *whole theories*: a theory with an irrelevant axiom ("hexed salt dissolves") is longer than the same theory without it and is penalised by the prior, but the derivation relation does not mark which axioms an individual derivation used, so a derivation that uses an irrelevant premise is not penalised as an *explanation*.
* *The conjunction problem.* Hempel and Oppenheim noted that a law $L$ is trivially derived from the conjunction $L\wedge L'$ [known: hempel1948studies; content: secondary (referee A; the footnote itself not seen)]; this is why Friedman and Kitcher appeal to unification. SAI's length prior and the EP measure address it partly: adding $L'$ to an axiom costs $|L'|$ bits, so the module $\{L\wedge L'\}$ explains $L$'s data at a lower EP than $\{L\}$ (`laws` Def 5.2).

## 4. The worked example: Kepler's laws, the historical Mars pool, and what Newton adds (T4)

### 4.1 The language, the axioms, the data

Scout P §2.1–2.3, checked; the geocentric language is added. (Local symbols renamed from `notes.md`, §8: heliocentric longitude $\theta$, latitude $\beta$, distance $r$; NOTATION reserves $\lambda$ for P2's steepness. The north ecliptic pole is $\mathbf z$, the eccentric anomaly $\psi$, the elements of body $b$ are $\varepsilon_b$.)

* **Sorts.** Bodies $\mathfrak B$ and reals $\mathbb R$ (times in days from an epoch, angles in radians, lengths in AU).
* **Heliocentric data language $\mathcal L_{\rm obs}$.** Body constants Sun, Me, Ve, Ea, Ma, Ju, Sa, Mo; a predicate Planet; functions $\theta(b,t),\beta(b,t),r(b,t)$ (heliocentric ecliptic longitude, latitude, distance; for the Moon geocentric). $\mathcal L_{\rm dir}\subseteq\mathcal L_{\rm obs}$ omits $r$.
* **Geocentric data language $\mathcal L_{\rm geo}$.** The same constants and functions $\theta_g(b,t),\beta_g(b,t)$: the geocentric ecliptic direction $\mathrm{dir}_g(b,t)\in S^2$ of body $b$. This is a *modern idealisation of Tycho's language* (referee A, issue 20): Tycho measured mainly equatorial coordinates, and reducing them to ecliptic directions (refraction, precession, the equinox of date) is itself theory-laden; the computed data below are geometric plan94 positions, without aberration (about 0.34′) or light time, and geocentric from the Earth–Moon barycentre rather than the Earth (up to about 0.3′ for Mars at a close opposition) [these sizes from referee A's report; not recomputed]. Heliocentric longitudes are a further theoretical reconstruction (history from memory, not checked against a source).
* **Background $B$.** Real-closed-field axioms with $\sin,\cos,\pi$ and enough true analysis to prove the numerical bounds used (rational-function bounds of the kind MetiTarski proves [known: akbarpour2010metitarski; content: Paulson's pages via scout P]). The full theory of $(\mathbb R,+,\cdot,<,\sin)$ is undecidable ($\sin(\pi x)=0$ defines $\mathbb Z$) [proof sketch], so $B$ is a sound decidable fragment; statements below say which true sentences $B$ must contain.
* **Theoretical vocabulary.** Heliocentric positions $x(b,t)\in\mathbb R^3$ (for the geocentric language; in $\mathcal L_{\rm obs}$ they are definable from $\theta,\beta,r$) and the elements $a,e,i,\Omega,\varpi,M_0,P:\mathfrak B\to\mathbb R$.
* **K12** (Kepler's first two laws, via Kepler's equation). For Planet($b$) and all $t$, with $M:=M_0(b)+2\pi t/P(b)$ (the mean anomaly; a local letter), there is $\psi$ with $\psi-e(b)\sin\psi=M$, and $x(b,t)=\mathrm{Rot}(b)\big(a(b)(\cos\psi-e(b)),\ a(b)\sqrt{1-e(b)^2}\sin\psi,\ 0\big)$, $0\le e(b)<1$, $a(b),P(b)>0$; $x(\mathrm{Sun},t)=0$. For $\mathcal L_{\rm geo}$, K12 includes the **bridge**: $\mathrm{dir}_g(b,t)=(x(b,t)-x(\mathrm{Ea},t))/|x(b,t)-x(\mathrm{Ea},t)|$ if $x(b,t)\neq x(\mathrm{Ea},t)$, and the north ecliptic pole $\mathbf z$ otherwise; and the frame convention $i(\mathrm{Ea})=0$ (the ecliptic is the Earth's orbital plane).
* **K3.** For all planets $b,b'$: $P(b)^2a(b')^3=P(b')^2a(b)^3$.
* **Elements** $\varepsilon_b$ (initial conditions): numerals (or intervals) for the elements of body $b$.
* **Data** are true interval sentences $d(b,t,v,\delta)$: "the direction of $b$ at $t$ lies within angle $\delta$ of $v$" (heliocentric or geocentric), and, where available, "$r(b,t)\in[r_1,r_2]$". **Questions** are covers by such sentences ("where is Mars at $t$, to within 2′?"), and structural questions that the laws answer without elements: "is the orbit of $b$ planar?", "does $b$'s heliocentric direction return periodically?", "does the line from the Sun to $b$ sweep equal areas in equal times?". (The development record gave "the return period" and "the mean distance" as examples; without elements the laws fix neither; referee A, issue 23.)

### 4.2 What the laws say, in which language

**Proposition 4.1 (scout P Prop 2.1, re-checked) [proved].** (a) The Ramsey sentence $R(T)$ of a theory $T$ (its theoretical function symbols existentially quantified, second order) has the same $\mathcal L_{\rm obs}$-consequences as $T$, consequence being semantic (second-order entailment). (b) For every $B$ true in $\mathbb R$: $R(\mathrm{K12}+\mathrm{K3})\cup B$ entails no datum $d(b,t,v,\delta)$ with $\delta<\pi$.

*Proof.* (a) A model of $R(T)$ is an $\mathcal L_{\rm obs}$-structure that expands to a model of $T$; so the two have the same models in $\mathcal L_{\rm obs}$. (b) Fix $b,t,v$ and $\delta<\pi$. Keep $a(b)$ and $P(b)$ (so K3 is untouched) and choose the orbital plane and the phase so that the Keplerian direction of $b$ at $t$ is $-v$ (the plane can be any plane through the Sun and the phase can place the body anywhere on its orbit); define $\theta,\beta,r$ by K12. This is a model of $B\cup\mathrm{K12}\cup\mathrm{K3}$ on the standard reals in which the angle between the direction of $b$ at $t$ and $v$ is $\pi>\delta$. $\square$

So the laws alone answer only structural questions; laws plus initial conditions answer position questions. In the question format, initial conditions belong in the question antecedent, and laws answer conditional questions with no elements at all: this is the formal locus of the laws/initial-conditions distinction (scout N2 §4.2; `laws` Prop 5.9 [proved there]: in the question format an elements module has explanatory power 0, no λ-MAP contains elements, and the laws alone answer every conditional question).

**Proposition 4.2 (scout P Prop 2.2, re-checked) [proved].** K12 and K12 + K3 have the same $\mathcal L_{\rm dir}$-consequences over any $B$ containing the real-closed-field axioms.

*Proof.* A Kepler direction depends on $(e,i,\Omega,\varpi,M_0,P)$ and not on $a$: the formula for $x(b,t)$ is $a(b)$ times an expression free of $a$. Given a model of $B\cup\mathrm{K12}$, put $a'(b):=(k^2P(b)^2/4\pi^2)^{1/3}$ for a fixed $k>0$ (cube roots exist in a real closed field) and leave everything else unchanged; positions scale by $a'(b)/a(b)>0$, directions are unchanged, and K3 holds because $a'(b)^3/P(b)^2$ is constant. So every $\mathcal L_{\rm dir}$-sentence true in all models of K12 + K3 is true in all models of K12; the converse is trivial. $\square$

So in a *heliocentric* direction language K3 has no content. Scout P remarked that in a *geocentric* direction language it does; here is the proof.

**Proposition 4.3 (K3 has content in the geocentric language) [proved; computed: c7 (2); referee B's rb4 (2), (3)].** Let $\chi_{\rm opp}$ be the $\mathcal L_{\rm geo}$-sentence
$$\chi_{\rm opp}:=\exists t\ \ \mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Ma},t)\neq-\mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Sun},t)\qquad(\text{"Mars is not permanently in opposition"}).$$
(a) $\chi_{\rm opp}$ holds in every model of K12 + K3 (with the bridge and the frame convention) whose real sort is $\mathbb R$; hence $B\cup\mathrm{K12}\cup\mathrm{K3}\vdash\chi_{\rm opp}$ for every $B$ that contains the two true sentences of $\mathbb R$ named in the proof ($\theta_{\rm K3}$ and $\theta_{\rm pos}$).
(b) For every $B$ true in $\mathbb R$, $B\cup\mathrm{K12}\nvdash\chi_{\rm opp}$.

*Proof.* (b) The model $\mathcal M_0$: Earth with $a=1$, $e=0$, $i=0$, period $P_0$ and some phase; Mars with $a=\rho>1$, $e=0$, $i=0$, the same period $P_0$ and the same phase. Then $x(\mathrm{Ma},t)=\rho\,x(\mathrm{Ea},t)$ for all $t$, so $x(\mathrm{Ma},t)-x(\mathrm{Ea},t)=(\rho-1)x(\mathrm{Ea},t)\neq0$ and $\mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Ma},t)=x(\mathrm{Ea},t)/|x(\mathrm{Ea},t)|=-\mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Sun},t)$ for every $t$: $\mathcal M_0\models\neg\chi_{\rm opp}$. $\mathcal M_0$ satisfies K12 (each body moves on a Kepler orbit; K12 imposes no relation between bodies) and violates K3 (equal periods, unequal axes). It is a model of every $B$ true in $\mathbb R$.
(a) Let $\mathcal M$ be a model of K12 + K3 on the standard reals with $\mathcal M\models\neg\chi_{\rm opp}$. Earth moves in the ecliptic, so $u_E(t):=x(\mathrm{Ea},t)/|x(\mathrm{Ea},t)|$ lies in the ecliptic and differs from $\mathbf z$. By $\neg\chi_{\rm opp}$, $\mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Ma},t)=u_E(t)\neq\mathbf z$ for all $t$, so the bridge gives $x(\mathrm{Ma},t)\neq x(\mathrm{Ea},t)$ and $x(\mathrm{Ma},t)=x(\mathrm{Ea},t)+c(t)u_E(t)=(r_E(t)+c(t))\,u_E(t)$ with $c(t)>0$: Mars is always on the ray from the Sun through the Earth, beyond the Earth. So Mars moves in the ecliptic, its heliocentric direction equals the Earth's at every $t$, and its distance is $r_M(t)=r_E(t)+c(t)>r_E(t)$. Take continuous polar angles $\theta_M=\theta_E$ in the ecliptic. Kepler's second law, a consequence of K12 (from $r=a(1-e\cos\psi)$, $\tan\frac\nu2=\sqrt{\tfrac{1+e}{1-e}}\tan\frac\psi2$ and $\dot M=2\pi/P$ one gets $r^2\dot\theta=2\pi a^2\sqrt{1-e^2}/P$; standard), gives $r_b(t)^2\dot\theta_b(t)=h_b$, a nonzero constant for each body. Hence $r_M^2/r_E^2=h_M/h_E$ is constant: $r_M(t)=\kappa r_E(t)$ with a constant $\kappa>1$, so $x(\mathrm{Ma},t)=\kappa\,x(\mathrm{Ea},t)$ for all $t$. A Kepler orbit's period $P(b)$ is the least period of $t\mapsto x(b,t)$, and its semi-major axis is half the sum of its largest and smallest distances ($r=a(1-e\cos\psi)$); so $P(\mathrm{Ma})=P(\mathrm{Ea})$ and $a(\mathrm{Ma})=\kappa\,a(\mathrm{Ea})\neq a(\mathrm{Ea})$, contradicting K3. Let $\theta_{\rm K3}$ be the sentence of the real-field language with $\sin,\cos$:
$$\forall\vec\varepsilon_{\rm Ea}\,\forall\vec\varepsilon_{\rm Ma}\Big(\mathrm{Adm}(\vec\varepsilon_{\rm Ea},\vec\varepsilon_{\rm Ma})\ \to\ \exists t\ \forall \psi_{\rm Ea}\,\forall \psi_{\rm Ma}\big(\mathrm{Kep}_{\rm Ea}(t,\psi_{\rm Ea})\wedge\mathrm{Kep}_{\rm Ma}(t,\psi_{\rm Ma})\ \to\ \Delta(t,\psi_{\rm Ea},\psi_{\rm Ma})\big)\Big),$$
where $\vec\varepsilon_b$ are the seven elements of body $b$, $\mathrm{Adm}$ says $0\le e<1$, $a,P>0$ for both bodies, $i_{\rm Ea}=0$ and K3 for the pair, $\mathrm{Kep}_b(t,\psi)$ is Kepler's equation $\psi-e_b\sin\psi=M_{0,b}+2\pi t/P_b$, and $\Delta$ says that the positions $\xi_b:=\mathrm{Rot}_b(a_b(\cos\psi_b-e_b),a_b\sqrt{1-e_b^2}\sin\psi_b,0)$ either coincide or satisfy $(\xi_{\rm Ma}-\xi_{\rm Ea})/|\xi_{\rm Ma}-\xi_{\rm Ea}|\neq\xi_{\rm Ea}/|\xi_{\rm Ea}|$. Let $\theta_{\rm pos}$ be the true sentence $\forall a\,\forall e\,\forall\psi\,(a>0\wedge0\le e<1\to a(1-e\cos\psi)>0)$ (added after referee B, issue 13: it guarantees $x(\mathrm{Ea},t)\ne0$, so that $-\mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Sun},t)=x(\mathrm{Ea},t)/|x(\mathrm{Ea},t)|$ also in a non-standard model). $\theta_{\rm K3}$ is true in $\mathbb R$: given standard element values satisfying $\mathrm{Adm}$, Kepler's equation has exactly one solution for each $t$ (its left side is strictly increasing in $\psi$ when $0\le e<1$), so the antecedent of the inner implication fixes the true positions, and the argument above gives a $t$ at which they coincide or the directions differ. Now let $\mathcal M$ be any model of $B\cup\mathrm{K12}\cup\mathrm{K3}$ with $\theta_{\rm K3},\theta_{\rm pos}\in B$ (its real sort may be non-standard). Instantiate $\theta_{\rm K3}$ at the elements of Earth and Mars in $\mathcal M$ ($\mathrm{Adm}$ holds by K12, K3 and the frame convention) and obtain $t$. K12 supplies, for this $t$, values $\psi_{\rm Ea},\psi_{\rm Ma}$ solving Kepler's equations with $x(b,t)=\xi_b$; the universal quantifier over $\psi$ applies to them, so $\Delta$ holds of the actual positions. By $\theta_{\rm pos}$, $|x(\mathrm{Ea},t)|=a_{\rm Ea}(1-e_{\rm Ea}\cos\psi_{\rm Ea})>0$. If the positions coincide, the bridge gives $\mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Ma},t)=\mathbf z$, which is not $-\mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Sun},t)=x(\mathrm{Ea},t)/|x(\mathrm{Ea},t)|$ because the latter has third coordinate $0$ ($i_{\rm Ea}=0$); otherwise the bridge gives $\mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Ma},t)\ne x(\mathrm{Ea},t)/|x(\mathrm{Ea},t)|=-\mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Sun},t)$. Either way $\mathcal M\models\chi_{\rm opp}$. By completeness, $B\cup\mathrm{K12}\cup\mathrm{K3}\vdash\chi_{\rm opp}$. (The universal quantifier over $\psi$ is what makes the instantiation valid in a model where Kepler's equation might have several solutions; the first run of the development round stated it with "$\exists\psi$", which leaves that case open.) $\square$

*Computed [c7 (2); rb4].* In the circular coplanar case with $\rho=1.5237$, $\mathcal M_0$ keeps Mars at opposition to rounding ($1.2\times10^{-6}$° in c7's floating point; $10^{-14}$° in referee B's rb4 (2)), while the K3-model with the same Earth, the same $\rho$ and the same initial phase leaves opposition by 1.3° within a day, 13° within 10 days and 87° within 100 days. Referee B's rb4 (3): over 300 random K12 + K3 models (outer planet, Earth in the ecliptic, Mars's plane random) the largest angle of Mars from opposition over 20 years is at least 175° in every model; over 50 random $\mathcal M_0$ models it never exceeds $6\times10^{-14}$°. (Whether finitely many interval data true in $\mathcal M_0$ refute every K3-model was not examined.)

*Remarks.* (i) The bridge needs a convention at coincidence; without it, a K3-model in which Mars coincides with the Earth would leave $\mathrm{dir}_g(\mathrm{Ma},t)$ unconstrained and could imitate $\mathcal M_0$. (ii) For an *inner* co-moving planet ($\rho<1$) the analogue fails: a K3-model with Mars on the Earth's orbit diametrically opposite ("behind the Sun") reproduces "always in the direction of the Sun", so $\chi_{\rm opp}$ was chosen with an outer planet. (iii) $\chi_{\rm opp}$ is an odd sentence (its negation is a fantastic world); its role is only to show that the geocentric data language can register K3. The natural form of the content is Prop 4.4.

**Proposition 4.4 (given the other elements, K3 replaces exactly one element in the geocentric language) [proved; computed: c4 B, c7 (1); referee B's rb4 (1), rb5].** Let $\mathcal E^-$ be numerals for all elements of Earth and Mars except $a(\mathrm{Ma})$, with $a(\mathrm{Ea})=1$. Write $F_M(t)$ for Mars's heliocentric position computed from $\mathcal E^-$ with $a=1$, so that $x(\mathrm{Ma},t)=\rho F_M(t)$ with $\rho:=a(\mathrm{Ma})$.
(a) $\mathrm{K12}\cup\mathrm{K3}\cup\mathcal E^-$ fixes $\rho=(P(\mathrm{Ma})/P(\mathrm{Ea}))^{2/3}$ and hence every geocentric direction of Mars: it proves $d_g(\mathrm{Ma},t,v,\delta)$ whenever the computed direction lies within $\delta$ of $v$ (with $B$ proving the numerical bounds, as in scout P §2.3).
(b) $\mathrm{K12}\cup\mathcal E^-$ leaves $\rho$ free. At every $t$ at which $x(\mathrm{Ea},t)$ and $F_M(t)$ are not parallel, the geocentric directions of Mars at $t$ over the models of $\mathrm{K12}\cup\mathcal E^-$ (with $\rho$ ranging over $(0,\infty)$) form the open great-circle arc from $-u_E(t)$ (the Sun's direction) to $F_M(t)/|F_M(t)|$, of length $\alpha(t)\in(0,\pi)$, the angle between these two directions; so $\mathrm{K12}\cup\mathcal E^-$ proves no datum $d_g(\mathrm{Ma},t,v,\delta)$ with $\delta<\alpha(t)/2$.

*Proof.* (a) K3 with $a(\mathrm{Ea})=1$ gives $a(\mathrm{Ma})^3=P(\mathrm{Ma})^2/P(\mathrm{Ea})^2$; then K12 determines $x(\mathrm{Ma},t)$ and the bridge the direction. (b) The geocentric vector is $g(\rho)=\rho F_M(t)-x(\mathrm{Ea},t)$; as $\rho$ runs over $(0,\infty)$, $g(\rho)$ runs along the open ray from $-x(\mathrm{Ea},t)$ in the direction $F_M(t)$, which does not pass through the origin when the two are not parallel; seen from the origin, its direction moves monotonically along the great circle through $-u_E(t)$ and $F_M(t)/|F_M(t)|$, from the first towards the second, covering the open arc between them. Every $\rho>0$ gives a model of K12 (K12 constrains $a$ only by $a>0$). An arc of length $\alpha$ is contained in no cap of radius $<\alpha/2$. $\square$

*Computed [c7 (1); rb4 (1)].* Over 1580–1600 (daily, plan94), the arc length $\alpha(t)$ has median 90° (5th–95th percentiles 9.5°–170°; minimum 0.11°, so no sampled day is exactly at conjunction or opposition); along the arc the direction moves monotonically as $\rho$ grows (checked on 21 sampled days; referee B on 60 seeded days, with the great-circle identity to $9\times10^{-15}$ rad), and the sensitivity of the geocentric direction to $\rho$ at the true $\rho$ has median 969′ and maximum 3556′ per unit of $\rho$. Referee B reproduces every figure.

So, given the other elements, K3's content in the geocentric language is exactly the element $\rho$: the bits needed to state $\rho$ to the precision the data require (Prop 4.3 shows that K3 also has content without the other elements). §4.4 Part B measures those bits.

### 4.4 The proof-requiring posterior over the historical Mars pool

**Setting [computed: c4, c4b].** Data: the 7306 daily heliocentric longitudes of Mars over 1580.0–1600.0 (c2's data), each stated as the interval sentence "$\theta(t_i)$ lies within $\delta$ of $v_i$" with $v_i$ the plan94 value: the guaranteed-tolerance regime with zero measurement error, *the noise-free special case* (Part A′ treats noisy and honest data). The pool: circle (2 elements), eccentric (4), bisected equant (4), vicarious hypothesis (5), ellipse (4), each with and without K3. A theory is a law text plus element numerals; it proves every datum iff its longitudes are within $\delta$ of every $v_i$, and it is then consistent with the data, so its proof-requiring weight is $2^{-|\text{text}|}$ or 0.

**The written-down syntax.**
* *Element numerals:* each element is a fixed-length 64-bit binary numeral for a cell midpoint of its range ($e,e_1,e_2\in[0,1)$; $w$, $L$ in $[0,2\pi)$; $n\in[0,0.1)$ rad/day; $L$ is the mean longitude at the window's midpoint). Then the total weight of the compatible numerals of a form is $\mathrm{Vol}_F(\delta)/\prod(\text{ranges})$ up to grid error $2^{-64}$, where $\mathrm{Vol}_F(\delta)$ is the volume of the compatible region in element space. Write $\mathrm{Occ}_F(\delta):=-\log_2(\mathrm{Vol}_F(\delta)/\prod\text{ranges})$, the form's *Occam factor* in bits (`notes.md` wrote $V_F$, $C_F$, which clash with the universal machine and other uses of $C$). The term and the reading "posterior volume over prior volume" are MacKay's, and Jefferys and Berger used Bayes factors with this reading on Mercury's perihelion [known: mackay1992bayesian, jefferys1992ockham; bibliographic data and abstracts confirmed by referee A's search in this thread, not re-searched in the revision round; content: abstract and secondary]. The posterior of form $F$ is proportional to $2^{-(|\mathrm{LAW}_F|_S+\mathrm{Occ}_F(\delta))}$.
* *Law texts* (written out as s-expressions in the script; length = tokens × $\lceil\log_2|\text{alphabet}|\rceil$, with one opening and one closing bracket per compound counted as tokens), in three syntaxes: **S1** arithmetic ($+,-,\cdot,/,\sin,\cos,\mathrm{atan2},\sqrt{}$, numerals 0, 1, 2, $\forall,\exists$; the equant and vicarious constructions and Kepler's equation are implicit definitions with $\exists$); **S2** epicyclic (S1 plus a unit-vector primitive, a direction primitive, and "the point where the ray from $Q$ at angle $\theta$ meets the unit circle about $C$"); **S3** Keplerian (S1 plus the true anomaly as a function of $e$ and the mean anomaly).

With the same elements the old equant text's longitudes differed from the bisected equant's by up to 327′, and the family it described has minimax error 3.50′, not 8.17′ (referee B's rb2, rb3). **What the data decide and what the syntax decides in this regime [computed: c4, c9 (5); reading: assessment; rewritten after referee A, issue 1, and referee B, issue 3].**
* *The data decide compatibility.* The forms drop out as $\delta$ falls below their minimax errors (640.8′, 23.05′, 8.17′, 1.41′, 1.07′): circle, eccentric, equant, vicarious, ellipse. This is syntax-free, and it is the historical order, as expected, since each successor was adopted because it fitted better. (The development record said the posterior "reproduces the evaluation side of the history exactly"; that overclaims: the forms are heliocentric reconstructions evaluated on modern heliocentric longitudes in the best plane, while the historical geocentric models also represented the Earth's orbit — for a superior planet by the epicycle, moving uniformly — which the heliocentric reconstruction removes; their errors in geocentric longitude were larger [history: from referee A's report, issue 2; not checked here].)
* *Among compatible forms the data contribute only the Occam factors, and on their own they point to the true form.* With no text prior (uniform over the five forms, c4's uniform element prior: a presentation-free, "semantic" prior, §1.4 item 4), the MAP is the eccentric at 720′ and 120′ (margins 0.69 and 0.46 bits) and the ellipse at every $\delta\le60′$, with margins 0.20, 0.70, 1.62, 4.48, 4.97, 5.77, 6.70, 7.65, 10.15 and 14.61 bits at 60′, 30′, 20′, 12′, 9′, 6′, 4′, 3′, 2′, 1.6′ [computed: c9 (5); referee A's ra3 gives the same table]. They depend on the parametrisation of the element boxes: halving the eccentricity ranges moves the vicarious hypothesis by one bit against the four-element forms and the circle by one bit the other way (c9 (5)).
* *The text prior decides the rest in this regime.* Law-text differences are 100–800 bits for every pair of forms except one: the S2 equant and vicarious texts differ by 18 bits (and, after the correction, the S1/S3 equant and vicarious texts have equal length, but both lose to the ellipse by more than 400 bits there). For that pair, at 20′–9′ where the equant is S2's MAP, the Occam factors move the margin by up to 5.6 bits (from 2.1 bits in the equant's favour at 20′ to 5.6 bits in the vicarious hypothesis's favour at 9′), so the 18-bit text difference still decides, with a margin of 12.4 bits at 9′; text and data contributions are of the same order there (referee B, issue 3). Everywhere else the text differences are an order of magnitude larger than any Occam-factor difference. So in this regime the sequence of preferred theories is set by the syntax: with epicyclic primitives (S2) the posterior runs through circle → eccentric → equant → vicarious hypothesis → ellipse, switching only when a form becomes incompatible; with plain arithmetic (S1) it goes eccentric → ellipse at 23′; with a Keplerian primitive (S3) circle → ellipse at 641′.
* *Robustness to the numeral code.* A variable-length numeral code (an Elias-gamma length prefix per element) adds about 5–11 bits per element; this moves the vicarious-against-4-element comparisons by 6–11 bits and the circle against 4-element forms by 12–20 bits, which changes none of the MAP rows above.
* *Robustness to the pool* (referee A, issue 22; [computed there: ra2, re-run in this round, identical]). A natural form outside the pool, the equation-of-centre series truncated at second or third order (four elements, like the ellipse), has S1 texts longer than the ellipse's (680 and 1345 bits against 545; S1 has only the numerals 0, 1, 2) and minimax errors 4.11′ and 1.31′; so the S1 MAP rows are unchanged by this extension. The conclusions are about the pool; this one natural extension does not change them.

**Part A′: graded fit (new; referee A, issues 1 and 10) [computed: c9 (2)–(6); referee A's ra1 (3) gives the same expected-penalty figures].** The guaranteed-tolerance regime is special: every datum is an interval of one half-width $\delta$ around a noise-free value, a form proves all of them or none, and so the data's discriminating power among compatible forms cannot grow with the number of data (the Occam factor depends on $\delta$, not on $n$). In any regime where fit is graded it does grow with $n$. Two such regimes, both inside $M_A$:
* *Gaussian noise.* A theory may be a sampler: the law plus a Gaussian error model with stated σ, randomised by the tape; on data "the observed longitude lies in this cell" its likelihood is the Gaussian probability of the cell. (This is a randomised theory of $M_A$ in the sense of `laws` §1.3; the error model's own text is the same for every form and cancels.) Least-squares fits to the 7306 longitudes have RMS residuals 454.4′ (circle), 15.81′ (eccentric), 5.26′ (equant), 0.628′ (vicarious), 0.446′ (ellipse). The expected log-likelihood penalty per independent observation relative to the ellipse, $(\mathrm{RMS}_F^2-\mathrm{RMS}_{\rm ell}^2)/(2\sigma^2\ln2)$, is at σ = 2′ (about Tycho's precision) 45.05 bits (eccentric), 4.96 (equant) and 0.035 (vicarious). A form's text advantage over the ellipse is overcome after (text advantage)/(penalty) observations: the eccentric's in S1 (100 bits) after 2, in S2 (312) after 7; the equant's in S2 (204) after 41; the vicarious hypothesis's in S2 (186) after about 5,300 (1,300 at σ = 1′, 21,000 at σ = 4′). In S1 and S3 the ellipse is already the shorter text against the equant and the vicarious hypothesis (by 445 and 730 bits), and in S3 against the eccentric.
* *A full simulation* (c9 (4); 20 seeds per cell; days drawn at random from 1580–1600, plan94 longitude plus Gaussian noise; each form fitted by least squares; Laplace approximation of the evidence under c4's uniform element prior). At σ = 2′: in S1 and S3 the MAP is the ellipse in 20 of 20 runs for every $n\ge10$ (at $n=5$, about the number of elements, S1's MAP is the eccentric); in S2 the MAP is the vicarious hypothesis in 20 of 20 runs for $n$ = 20 to 2560, in 10 of 20 at $n=5120$, and the ellipse in 20 of 20 at $n=7306$. At σ = 1′ the S2 switch to the ellipse comes between 640 (vicarious 20 of 20) and 2560 (ellipse 20 of 20). The median evidence ratio of the ellipse over the vicarious hypothesis, without text prior, grows from $2^{2.2}$ at $n=5$ to $2^{276}$ at $n=7306$ (σ = 2′). The simulation agrees with the expected-penalty estimates, which omit the parameter-fitting terms.
* *Honest uniform-tolerance theories* (randomised honest laws in the sense of `laws` Prop 1.8, each with tolerance equal to its form's minimax error). `laws`' final record (Prop 3.6a, §3.6) shows that the uniform band is not what SAI's mixture prefers: it prefers the law together with an error distribution shaped like its residuals (a calibrated error model, of which the Gaussian regime above is a crude instance); the uniform band is the simplest honest form and gives a conservative estimate here. For cells much narrower than the tolerances, the per-datum cost difference against the ellipse is about $\log_2(\varepsilon_F/\varepsilon_{\rm ell})$: 4.43 bits (eccentric), 2.93 (equant), 0.40 (vicarious). The text advantages are then overtaken after 23 (S1) and 71 (S2) data for the eccentric, 70 for the equant (S2) and about 470 for the vicarious hypothesis (S2) [computed: c9 (6); an estimate from `laws`' pricing, not a separate posterior computation].

So in graded-fit regimes **the data, not the syntax, decide every step within tens of observations, except the longitude-only choice between the vicarious hypothesis and the ellipse in the epicyclic syntax**, which needs thousands of longitudes at Tycho-level noise (hundreds with honest tolerance theories). Part C shows that distance data decide that step.

**Reading [assessment; rewritten].**
1. The proof-requiring posterior refutes the forms in the historical order as precision improves; this is syntax-free, and expected.
2. In the noise-free, common-tolerance regime, which compatible form is preferred is decided by the text prior whenever text differences exceed about 15 bits, which they do here; the data on their own point to the true form, weakly. So "the simplest theory compatible with the data" is, in this regime, relative to the language in which laws are written. Best-system accounts face the same relativity, and the analogy should be stated carefully (referee A, issue 5): Lewis treats language relativity as a *problem* — with a predicate $F$ true exactly where the world's system holds, "$\forall x\,Fx$" would axiomatise everything — and meets it by requiring the system to be stated with predicates for perfectly natural properties [known: lewis1983new; content: secondary]; Cohen and Callender *accept* relativity to a chosen set of kinds [known: cohen2009better; content: abstract/secondary]. SAI inherits the relativity through the choice of base language: the universal prior bounds it only relative to a fixed reference language, by the translation cost $K(\tau)$ (`fundamentals` Thm 4.4), here of the order of the definitions of the extra primitives in S1, a few hundred bits (an estimate, not computed). Lewis's trivialising predicate buys nothing when the data are base-language answers (`laws` Prop 5.7 [proved there]: its connection to the data must be stated in bridge axioms that pay for every bit), so in SAI the worry moves into the choice of the base language, where a base language that has the trivialising predicate as a primitive simply contains it. And best-system accounts are a metaphysics of laws (the best system of the whole history), while SAI is inference from finite data: the relation is an analogy.

**Reading [computed; reading: assessment].**
1. Given the other elements, K3 does one job in the geocentric language (Prop 4.4), and the data put a price on it: 11.3–14.7 bits at 8′–1′, about one more bit per halving of δ. This is what any real-valued element costs when it is stated to the precision the data require.
2. K3's text is paid once, however many bodies it relates, and it saves $\Delta_{\rm K3}(\delta)$ bits for each body whose size it fixes. By the criterion of `laws` Prop 5.5(b) [proved there] (with the law in place, K3 pays iff $\Delta_{\rm K3}|S|>|\mathrm{K3}|$, $|S|$ the number of such bodies, at exchange rate 1; this compares law choices and is not membership of the λ-MAP, where the empty theory may win, `laws` §10 item 11), K3 pays once it fixes the sizes of about 25–33 bodies (375 bits), 11–15 (165 bits) or 8–10 (115 bits), at 11.3–14.7 bits per body. The five planets other than the Earth give about 55–75 bits if each saves about as much as Mars (an extrapolation; not computed for the other planets). So **in none of these three codes does compression alone select K3 at Kepler's precision**; it would under a code in which K3 costs less than about 55–75 bits [estimate]. The conclusion is conditional on the code; under the universal prior the relevant cost is the complexity of K3 given the rest of the theory, which these computations do not determine.
3. This makes scout N2's "K3 is a law about initial conditions" quantitative: K3 is worth what it saves on the elements of the bodies it relates. Two qualifications. *It depends on the parametrisation* (referee A, issue 21): it holds because $P$ and $a$ are both taken as free elements (Kepler's kinematic parametrisation); in a state-space parametrisation (positions and velocities as initial conditions, with a dynamics) K3 is the dynamical relation fixing the mean motion from the energy, and whether it "pays" is a different question; this is the same relativity as reading 2 of Part A. *Its later standing has another source:* in Newton's theory the third law, with its mass correction, is a consequence of the one law that does all the other work (§4.5), so there it costs nothing extra [assessment].

### 4.5 What Newton adds (rewritten after referee A, issue 11)

Newton's theory extends the language with masses $m:\mathfrak B\to\mathbb R$, the constant $G$ and accelerations (a sort of trajectories or an axiomatised derivative in $B$); the law is $m_b\ddot x_b=\sum_{b'\ne b}Gm_bm_{b'}(x_{b'}-x_b)/|x_{b'}-x_b|^3$. The data language is unchanged: this is ladder step 3, theoretical terms with no new data vocabulary. Masses are $T$-theoretical in Sneed's sense, measurable only by using the theory [known: sneed1971logical, balzer1987architectonic; content: secondary]. Most of what follows is the achievement of Newtonian mechanics as developed over about 150 years (Newton to Laplace and Le Verrier), not of Newton alone: Newton accounted for about half of the lunar apsidal motion and Clairaut for the rest [history: secondary, via referee A's search; not checked here]; philosophers of Newtonian method stress this [known: harper2011newton; content: publisher's description]. With the figures of §4.3 [computed: scout P c1–c3, re-run identical]:
1. **Parameters and fit on the old data.** Newton does not reduce the number of parameters: per body he needs six initial conditions, equivalent to osculating elements (as Kepler does), *plus* a mass, and the law (an ODE system with $G$) is much longer than K12 + K3. On planetary longitudes at Kepler-era precision the extra parameters buy nothing measurable over 20 years: the Kepler ellipse fits Mars to 1.07′ (§4.3), inside Tycho's precision. So an SAI posterior on planetary data alone at that precision would not prefer Newton [assessment; consistent with Part A′ of §4.4, where the ellipse is already the best form].
2. **Unification across domains** (where Newton's gain lies). One law covers the planets, the Moon (the Moon test: terrestrial gravity and the lunar acceleration under one law), falling bodies, tides, comets, and the third law for satellite systems with a mass-dependent constant: the two-body form is $n^2a^3=G(M+m)$, i.e. $q=1+m/M$. The fitted $q$ of Jupiter (1.00075–1.00099, windows ≥ 30 yr) matches its two-body value 1.00095; Saturn's (1.0013–1.0019) does not (two-body 1.00029) and is explained by the orbit-averaged pull of Jupiter, which acts like an interior ring (ring estimate 1.00154; a secular, circular, coplanar heuristic, not a perturbation theory). So the "constant" of K3 differs between planets at the $10^{-3}$ level, and Newtonian mechanics says why. (Some of these domains need more base vocabulary, so the language grows too.)
3. **Corrections inside the old domain.** Mercury's apsidal line turns 575.4″ per century in the fixed J2000 frame [computed]; in the classical decomposition of the motion relative to the equinox (5599.74″ per century), general precession accounts for about 5025.6″, leaving about 574.1″ in a fixed frame (our fit: 575.4″), of which Newtonian planetary perturbations account for about 531.5″, and the remainder of about 43″ is general relativity [known: clemence1947relativity; content: secondary: the total 5599.74″ and Newtonian 5557.18″ per century, leaving 42.56″, are Clemence's figures as analysed in arXiv 1008.1811; the split ≈574″ / ≈531″ / ≈43″ is from course material (LBL) located by WebSearch in the development round; 5025.6″ is the difference 5599.74 − 574.1. Referee B recalls, without a search, that Clemence's decomposition uses a general precession of 5025.645″, which would make the planetary part 5557.18 − 5025.645 = 531.54″ derivable from Clemence's own figures; this could not be verified in this round]. The great inequality of Jupiter and Saturn (period about 900 yr, amplitudes 18.3′ and 43.8′ in our fits) is the classic case explained by Laplace [known: wilson1985great for the history; period and amplitude not checked against it].
4. **New questions.** In the *old* language: where will Neptune be (Le Verrier's prediction from Uranus's residuals, also an example of reference fixed by a description [known: kripke1980naming; content: secondary]); when will Halley's comet return. In a *new* language (ladder step 3): the masses of the Sun and of the planets with satellites — masses are $T$-theoretical, so "the mass of Jupiter" is a question the old language cannot pose.
5. **No deductive entailment of Kepler.** Newton's axioms do not entail K12 or K3; together with masses and initial conditions they entail corrected statements and "K12 holds within $\varepsilon(b,W)$ on window $W$", with $\varepsilon$ as in §4.3 (§3.3; `laws` Prop 7.1 for the provable toy). A formal derivation of even one such bound needs an existence-and-uniqueness theorem and a validated integration or perturbation estimate: a very long derivation that no textbook writes out. This is the computational gap of the textbook point in a concrete case (§5.1(b)) [assessment].

### 4.6 Approximate laws: what to cite

The selection theorems about approximate laws are `laws`' and are not re-proved here [proved there; numbering of `laws`' final record, which keeps its development numbers]:
* *Strength is rewarded over deterministic hypotheses.* Proof-requiring compatibility is monotone in logical strength, so data never move the odds towards a hedge, and the false exact law beats its true tolerance-weakened version on every data set the latter fits (`laws` Prop 2.1, Cor 2.2; this is scout P's Prop 2.3).
* *Inside $M_A$ this is qualified.* After the exact law is refuted, randomised theories keep positive weight. Within the pool {no law, exact law with exceptions, uniform honest band} the honest band wins under SAI's pricing on the real Kepler residuals of seven bodies; but SAI's mixture prefers the law *together with an error distribution shaped like its residuals*, and even the two-point form "exact except in a share, then within $\varepsilon$" beats the uniform band iff $\Ex\ln(1+|e|/\varepsilon)<2\ln2-1$, which holds for 34 of the 39 Kepler body–window pairs (`laws` Prop 3.6a, §3.6 [proved there; computed there]). So under SAI's pricing an approximately true law is *a law with a calibrated error model*, neither a silent partial theory nor an exact law with listed exceptions (`laws` §3.6). (The development record said "the honest law wins for every body, window and precision"; that holds only within the smaller pool. The peakedness values it quoted, 1.9–4.0, were ratios across two fits; per fit they are 1.60–3.95 for the minimax fit and 1.89–7.08 for the mean fit: `laws`' referee A, M6, and its final record.)
* *Precision.* Under guaranteed tolerances the switch point is sharp at $\varepsilon^*+\delta_0$ (`laws` Prop 2.4); under dithered rounding a false exact law is refuted after about $\Delta/\Ex|e|$ data however coarse the cells (`laws` Prop 2.5).
* *Honest laws against laws with exceptions.* Under deterministic patches the honest law has the lower rate iff $\mathrm{pk}(e)<\gamma_{\rm ex}/(2\gamma_{\rm si})$, the price of an exception against twice the price of silence (`laws` Prop 3.4). Scout N2's "spread errors favour honesty, concentrated errors favour exceptions" is pricing-dependent: true under deterministic patches, false under literal pricing with random exceptions, and under SAI's pricing a peakedness criterion survives with thresholds about 2 (1.79 necessary, 2.12 sufficient; `laws` Prop 3.6a(d), §10 item 2).

§4.4's posterior is the guaranteed-tolerance regime with exact interval centres; under rounding every form in the pool, the ellipse included, is eventually refuted by enough data (plan94's residuals from every fixed ellipse are nonzero), and randomised theories with error models take over (Part A′).

## 5. Limitations of the axiom view (T5)

Each item: the claim and its source in the notes; a sharp statement; the formal status, either **partially addressable** (a formal handle exists or is a research question, with its pointer) or **a genuine limit** of the declarative view. All verdicts are [assessment] unless a theorem is cited.

### 5.0 L0. Statistical confrontation of theory with data (promoted to a headline limitation after referee A, issue 10)

**Sharp statement.** In physics the default contact between theory and data is statistical: theory plus instrument and noise models assign likelihoods to data, often near-raw data (objection o4 of §1.3); statistical mechanics, quantum mechanics and population genetics answer with probabilities, and their data are frequencies. Hempel and Oppenheim flagged statistical explanation as open [known: hempel1948studies; content: quoted in a citing source]. SAI's proof-requiring core, with exact or interval data, has no native noise model: a datum is proved or not, and graded fit enters only through randomised theories (honest bands and calibrated error models inside $M_A$: `laws` Props 1.8, 3.6a; Gaussian samplers: §4.4 Part A′) or through the generative variant (`fundamentals` Prop 9.1, parts proved and parts sketched there) or sentences about probabilities, whose relation to finite frequency data is not deductive.

**Formal status:** partially addressable, and it matters for the headline computations: §4.4 shows that the reading of the Mars computation changes when noise is added (Part A′). The paper should say that the proof-requiring core is the noise-free special case, and that its natural extension inside $M_A$ is a law with a calibrated error model (`laws` §3.6).

### 5.1 L1. Written theories are far from sufficient for a simple reasoner (the textbook point)

**Sources.** The author's request ("our written theories are not actually remotely sufficient for solving problems when hooked to some simple reasoner"); `phil-sci-our-science-is-very-human-software.md` ("those predictions are very much not just made by formal inferences from the letter of the theory, but also using your background understanding"); `phil-sci-scientific-theories-are-not-for-generating-or-predicting-arbitrary-data.md` (the Feynman and olympiad problems); `phil-sci-some-disanalogies…`, third bullet; the condensation essay ("the notions of newtonian mechanics alone are inert"). We agree with the claim; the work is to say what it consists of.

**Three gaps** (scout P's split, kept).
* **(a) The bridge gap.** A problem posed in ordinary terms ("a rope over a pulley…") becomes a formal problem only through idealisations and bridge principles that the written theory does not contain. For the problem *as posed*, the theory is logically insufficient. Literature: preparing the description, and models as autonomous mediators [known: cartwright1983how, morgan1999models; content: secondary]. *Formal status:* partially addressable. Bridges are more axioms in a larger language; Prop 2.4 prices the bridge from raw data to questions as the extractor's complexity $K(g)$, which in real cases is large. A measurable question is how large bridge and method parts are compared with the "physical picture".
* **(b) The computational gap.** For the formalised problem, theory plus mathematics is logically sufficient, but finding the answer needs methods, lemmas and approximation schemes. §4.5's case: deriving "Kepler within $\varepsilon$ on window $W$" from Newton needs an existence theorem and a validated perturbation bound, a long derivation no textbook writes out. *Formal status:* partially addressable, and a theorem in `shape`'s model, with a precondition that matters: the gap is visible only when the *written* theory is charged. Under SAI's program-length prior neither the reasoner nor the vocabulary is visible, because a short program can generate the missing lemmas (`shape` Thm 1.11, Thm 1.12(c) [proved there]). With a cost on the written theory (`shape` Thm 1.12(a), (b)), the written size a reasoner needs depends exponentially on the reasoner's algebra (Gaussian elimination needs a linear-size theory for the hidden-state law; unit propagation at any depth and with any intermediate concepts $2^{\Omega(m^{\varepsilon/3})}$: `shape` Thm 3.6, given GKRS 2019), on its depth (one round of propagation needs all prime implicates whatever the vocabulary, Prop 3.1a; $k$ rounds need $2^{\Omega(m^{1/(2k-1)})}$ for one parity law, Prop 3.8(c)), and on the vocabulary for a reasoner deep enough to use it (one parity law: $2^{m-1}$ clauses over the base variables against $4m-6$ with intermediate quantities; the simplex family $m2^{(m-1)/2}$ against $m(2m-4)$: Cor 2.7(c), Prop 3.8(b), Cor 2.8, Rem 3.2a); declarative against procedural shape matters only by a constant factor once intermediate concepts are available and the reasoner can chain (Thm 4.3). The AI-paper's charged-deduction readings, extended to conditional data, charge the full lemma library no more than a time-bounded description of the law (`shape` Thm 5.2) and so cannot express the textbook point; the know-how lives in the reasoner, in written lemmas, or in a computation the cost model does not charge (`shape` Prop 5.1). A weak reasoner without intermediate concepts also *learns* its know-how pattern by pattern, while an algebraic reasoner learns the law at once (`shape` Thm 6.2). In first-order logic the gap between a lemma-free theory and the same theory with its lemmas can be non-elementary for a cut-free reasoner [known there: Statman, Orevkov], and adding valid lemma sentences, which change the written theory but not its content, closes it (`shape` §8; one step a proof sketch there).
  * **What the theorems show [assessment].** "Insufficient for a simple reasoner" is a property of the pair (theory, reasoner) and of the vocabulary, under a cost on the written theory, and it can be exponential: the know-how must live in the reasoner, in explicit lemmas, or in an uncharged computation. So the author's textbook claim holds in the refined form "the written theory is insufficient for a simple reasoner, as a property of the pair (theory, reasoner) and of the vocabulary, when what is written is what is charged". `shape`'s recommended sentence for the paper, which we adopt: *"Whether a written theory suffices depends on the reader. Paired with a reasoner that can do algebra, a linear law's few equations suffice; paired with one that only applies rules, the theory must spell out exponentially many derived laws, unless it is written with new intermediate quantities and the reader can chain many steps through them. Even then, some systems of laws need exponentially long written theories for such a reader whatever quantities are introduced. The idealised inducer cannot see any of this, because it charges a theory by the length of a program that may compute the missing lemmas."*
  * **What they do not show [assessment].** They say nothing about the content of physics textbooks: the laws are linear or implicational, the reasoners are unit propagation, forward chaining and elimination, and the gap closes completely for a reasoner that can do algebra. They do not touch the bridge gap or the tacit gap. They cannot be stated for SAI as defined, whose prior charges a theory by the length of a program that may compute the missing lemmas (`shape` Thm 1.11): to model the textbook point, the paper must use a listing or time-charged prior. They do not show that humans solve problems by carrying exponentially many lemmas; they show that a *declarative* account of a weak reasoner's competence must include them.
* **(c) The tacit gap.** Perceptual and manual skills (reading instruments, recognising situations [known: polanyi1958personal, polanyi1966tacit; content: secondary / source (first sentence, via quotations)]), and exemplars, through which new problems are seen as like solved ones [known: kuhn1970structure, kuhn1977second; content: secondary]. *Formal status:* a genuine limit of a declarative view. Exemplars enter SAI only as data.

### 5.2 L2. Approximate truth

**Sources.** The author's request ("kepler's laws not really giving you correct predictions but giving approximately correct predictions"); `hanni-solomonoff-axiom-induction.md` ("we should have a metric on values grade generated sentences"); the condensation essay (purpose-made metrics). **Sharp statement.** Kepler's laws as stated are false; they are compatible with data at precision $\delta$ exactly when $\delta$ exceeds their error on the domain (§4.3–4.4, computed). **Formal status:** partially addressable, and largely done in `laws` (§4.6 lists the results): over deterministic theories SAI rewards the false exact law over its true honest version (`laws` Cor 2.2); inside $M_A$, randomised theories survive its refutation, and SAI's mixture prefers the law with a calibrated error model (`laws` Prop 3.6a, §3.6) [proved there; computed there]. Whether an approximate law is held honestly, with exceptions, or with an error model depends on how exceptions and silence are priced, which is a modelling choice (`laws` Props 3.4, 3.6a, §10 item 2). *For deterministic theories, honest approximate theories are partial theories, and the trichotomy is the formal home of humility; for SAI's randomised theories, humility is the calibration of an error distribution* (`laws` Prop 3.10 and its reading in the final record). **What remains open:** a canonical metric (none exists; it is purpose-relative) and a general comparative notion of truthlikeness, which is known to be hard (Popper's definition fails for false theories [known: popper1963conjectures, miller1974popper, tichy1974popper; content: secondary/abstract]; the similarity approach needs a metric [known: niiniluoto1987truthlikeness; content: secondary]); our notion is deliberately local: question, tolerance, domain.

### 5.3 L3. Domains of applicability, several models, inconsistent theories in use

**Sources.** `phil-sci-the-domain-of-applicability-of-a-scientific-model.md` (flat earth 100 m against spherical earth 99.9 m, "then we use 100=99.9 in some algebraic manipulations to do some nonsense"; "how come we usually avoid this sort of catastrophic reasoning? * i don't think any nice "formal criterion" is going to be right?"; the Stosszahlansatz with Newtonian mechanics; "the vibe of physics is imo more like: oh here's a way of talking that works. these bad things? don't do them, silly!"); `phil-sci-some-disanalogies…` ("having multiple ways to think about something"); the condensation essay (the Earth as a sphere, an oblate spheroid, rugged). **Sharp statement.** Practice uses mutually inconsistent models, each in its own domain; a single consistent axiom set must relativise each to a domain predicate or drop one; proof-requiring SAI gives a globally inconsistent theory weight 0. **Formal status:**
* *A sound formal device exists* (this replaces the development record's "possible error" note §6.1; see §6): state each approximate model in its honest form, $|q-f|\le\varepsilon$ on its domain, and reason classically. Two exact approximations that differ anywhere on a common domain are jointly inconsistent and prove everything ("$100=99.9$" gives everything); honest versions of true models are jointly consistent and everything derived from them is true; for a scalar quantity (or box tolerances) joint consistency is a pairwise check that the tolerances overlap, for Euclidean tolerances in $\mathbb R^m$ an $(m+1)$-wise check; every derived conclusion carries a propagated tolerance (`laws` Prop 4.3 [proved there], over a background that decides the relevant comparisons, which holds for semialgebraic models over RCF; scout N2 Prop 3.3, Cor 3.4). A probabilistic error model is formal too and less conservative: after 100 steps of random-sign errors the mean deviation is exactly $7.96\varepsilon$ against the worst case $100\varepsilon$, and after 1000 steps $25.2\varepsilon$ against $1000\varepsilon$ (`laws` Remark 4.4 [computed there]; referee B's rb1 (2): 7.9589ε and 25.2250ε exactly).
* *It does not reproduce practice, and its rigorous domains can be far narrower than the domains practice rightly uses.* Physicists rarely state or propagate tolerances explicitly; worst-case propagation becomes vacuous over long chains and for ill-conditioned or chaotic dynamics, where reliable conclusions rest on probabilistic error models or stability results specific to classes of problems; and the rigorous honest-domain version of the note's own example, the Boltzmann equation, is Lanford's theorem, which derives it only for a fraction of the mean free time in the low-density limit, while practice uses the equation far beyond that domain, and rightly [known, unverified here: Lanford's theorem as described in arXiv expositions found by referee A's search]. So the note is right that no nice formal criterion *explains* how practice avoids catastrophic reasoning; a sound device that blocks the catastrophe exists, but it does not say how to choose a model, a domain or a tolerance, and it would forbid much correct practice. The gap between the sound device and practice is part of what written theories leave to tacit understanding [assessment].
* *Domains in SAI.* Domains are formalisable as restricted axioms or structuralist intended applications [known: balzer1987architectonic; content: secondary]; a domain restriction pays iff the law, even with an honest tolerance or with exceptions, describes the excluded system worse than no law; under SAI's pricing that never happens asymptotically for a system the law confines to within a band of about 66° of longitude (a sufficient bound, not a threshold; the development records of `laws` and of this thread said about 24°, which `laws`' referee A (m4) improved), so the Moon is kept with a tolerance and an error distribution rather than excluded (`laws` Prop 6.1 [proved there]; scout N2's "the Moon is excluded" holds only under another pricing, `laws` §10 item 4); learning domains is partial (`laws` §8: tolerances and error distributions per body and window are learned, rather than sharp domains). So in SAI the formal counterpart of a domain of applicability is usually a tolerance (with an error model) that varies with the system, not a sharp boundary [assessment].
* *Knowing when a model applies* ("don't do that, silly!") is a recognizer (§5.4) and is a genuine limit of the declarative view.

**The other limitations, briefly** (scout P L4–L12, checked against the threads).
* **L4. Science predicts very particular things and makes the set-ups in which it can predict** (`phil-sci-science-predicts-only-very-particularly.md`; `predicting-specific-things.md`). SAI captures half: the data are a selected set of sentences and theories are partial. It does not capture which questions are worth asking, where the predictands come from, or the building of set-ups that make questions answerable [known: hacking1983representing; content: abstract (ch. 13 "The creation of phenomena")]. Partially addressable (value-weighted questions); the making of phenomena is a genuine limit.
* **L5. Concept formation and language change** (ladder steps 3–4; `phil-sci-some-disanalogies…`, fourth bullet: Faraday's lines of force, Lorentz–Poincaré–Einstein; "the bayesian conception basically completely fails to model gaining scientific understanding"). Step 3 partially addressable (`terms` Thm 8.1, `shape` Cor 3.9); step 4 a genuine limit for now. We agree with the quoted judgement read as a claim about *discovery and understanding*; Bayesian selection over a fixed hypothesis space does model *evaluation* (§4.4) [assessment].
* **L6** is the table above.
* **L7. Discovery is not selection.** SAI is an ideal of evaluation over a fixed hypothesis space; §4.4 shows the evaluation side working on Mars, but finding the ellipse took Kepler years of search (history from memory, not checked). Genuine limit of the idealised inducer; resource-bounded search is a handle on search, not on understanding. Computational discovery systems exist and are the nearest formal work [known: langley1987scientific, evans2021making; content: publisher description / abstract].
* **L8. Statistical theories** — promoted to L0 (§5.0).
* **L9. Theory-laden and fallible data.** Kepler's "data" were heliocentric positions computed from Tycho's geocentric angles through a model of the Earth's orbit (history from memory); §4.2's geocentric language is the more honest data language (itself a modern idealisation, §4.1), and there K3 has content (Prop 4.3). SAI takes labels as correct: one mislabelled datum forces an exception axiom (the AI-paper's corruption-channel noise model handles errors). Partially addressable. Theory-ladenness at ladder step 2 is not the classical objection to a neutral observation language, but it biases comparisons between theories that disagree about the base language (§1.6; rewritten after referee A, issue 25).
* **L10. Purposes, technology, the creation of phenomena** (`phil-sci-some-disanalogies…`, bullets 5–6: thermodynamics, circuits, lenses). Genuine limit.
* **L11. Science as a social structure** (same note, last bullet). Genuine limit as stated. It may be where the explanatory question of §1.2 gets its answer: declarative theories can be communicated, split among people, checked piece by piece and extended. A formal version would ask which hypothesis shapes are cheap to *verify jointly* by several bounded agents. Speculative; rejected as a thread at selection (`select.md` §4) and listed here as an open direction [open].
* **L12. The mathematics is free, and huge** (`some points against the world modeling story.md`: "you needed to invent real numbers"). In §4 the background $B$ (ordered fields, trigonometry, for Newton analysis and ODE theory) is free. Partially addressable: building $B'$ into the background gains at most $K(B')+c$ bits on any data (`fundamentals` Prop 4.3 [proved there]), so in the universal setting charging $B$ changes little; in derivation-charged settings it changes a lot. The other side of free mathematics: with free deduction, data that the background already entails carry no information, so checking instances $P(0),\dots,P(N)$ of a decidable ($\Delta_0$) property, or more generally instances that $B$ decides, leaves $\Bel(\forall x\,P(x))$ at its prior value for every $N$ (`logic` Prop 4.6 [proved there]; corrected after referee B, issue 11: for a $\Pi_1$ property the instances are not $B$-decided and do carry information); this is the problem of old evidence and logical omniscience [known: garber1983old; content: secondary]. Induction in mathematics, which working scientists and mathematicians do, needs a reasoner for whom deduction is costly; the ideal inducer cannot model it.

### 5.5 Verdict on the limitations

The axiom view captures the *static, declarative, inferential* side of theories and concepts: contents, inferential relations, composition of contents, learning by adding and weighing axioms (globally, not concept by concept), pointers, partiality, approximate truth in honest or error-model form. It misses the *procedural* side (recognizers, methods, the search for derivations, action), the *statistical* side of contact with data in its proof-requiring core (L0), and the *dynamic* side (purposes, softness, clarification, meaning change, discovery, the social process). This is less than a theory of concepts asks for; but a theory of concepts is not what the paper offers. The paper should say, in these words, that it offers a model of the declarative core of scientific theories, and that the procedural side reappears formally as the reasoner- and vocabulary-relativity of `shape` [assessment].
