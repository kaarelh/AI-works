## 0. Summary

### 0.1 What this record establishes

The thread studies what happens to the inducer *because its data are sentences of a logic*. Four phenomena, none of which has an analogue in plain SOI on strings (the first one has an analogue in *consistent* SOI, by `sfi` Cor 4.2):

1. **The lottery (§2).** There is a stream on which the one-tape inducer $M_A$ has bounded loss while no consistent r.e. theory proves the stream; the coin-free and Kolmogorov versions then have unbounded loss (semimeasure convention), and the limit posterior lives on non-r.e. random theories (Thm 2.2). The lottery also exists **inside true arithmetic with true data** of the lowest complexity beyond $\Sigma_1\cup\Pi_1$ (Boolean combinations of $\Sigma_1$ sentences), and on that stream the limit posterior is concentrated on *false* theories although every datum is true (Thm 2.9, Cor 2.10). The phenomenon is exactly a failure of the majority vote of de Leeuw et al. for consistency constraints of unbounded arity, the formal form of Kyburg's lottery paradox, and its survival condition is the convergence condition of bounded diagonally non-computable functions (§7). It cannot happen for bounded arity (the threshold $1/(r+1)$ is exact for the argument), for $\Pi_1$ data, for co-r.e. streams over lottery-type classes, or when a positive-measure set of true random theories succeeds (Props 2.3, 2.4, 2.7, Cor 3.5). On the lottery streams the coin-free loss diverges more slowly than any computable unbounded nondecreasing function (Cor 2.6′).
2. **$\Pi_1$ derandomisation (§3).** With a true background $\supseteq\Q$, every $\Pi_1$ sentence proved with prior probability $2^{-k}$ is proved by one true theory of complexity $k+O(\log k)$ (Thm 3.1), and on $\Pi_1$ data the universal mixture equals, up to a constant factor, **one** random true theory: a uniformly random threshold on the prior probability of provability (Thm 3.2). Consequences: a coding theorem for $\Pi_1$ data (Cor 3.3) and Chaitin's theorem for random theories (Cor 3.6): one true datum "$K(x)>m$" costs $m\pm O(\log m)$ bits **in the semimeasure and three-valued conventions**. In the normalised convention, with $B\supseteq\PA$, the same datum costs almost nothing for all but a $2^{2m+K(m)+O(1)-n}$ fraction of the strings $x$ of length $n$ (Prop 3.11, new): the $m$ bits are the price of *deciding* the sentence, not of predicting its truth value. **The boundary of the coding theorem is exact:** true Boolean combinations of $\Sigma_1$ sentences already break it, with two data (Prop 3.9) and bounded against unbounded on a stream (Cor 2.10).
3. **Gödel phenomena (§4).** "Independent" on a $\Pi_1$ question certifies truth (Prop 4.1); soundness is never learned coin-free (Thm 4.2) and never learned one-tape from $\Pi_1$ data, with a uniform constant (Thm 4.4); in general, one-tape confidence in soundness requires a large coding gap (Prop 4.3); given data from a theory $T$ with $B\cup T$ true, the direction of $\Con(B\cup T)$ is a choice of universal machine (Prop 4.5, now with the truth hypothesis that the development round had omitted); $\Q$-entailed instance checks confirm nothing, so induction in mathematics needs a bounded deducer (Prop 4.6).
4. **The ω-gap (§5).** Its anatomy (Prop 5.1); $\Bel$ satisfies the Gaifman condition exactly when the instances deductively *prove* the universal (Prop 5.2; the provability analogue of AI-paper Prop B.10); a uniform Gödelian form: true $\Pi_1$ sentences with $\Q$-provable instances and belief below any $2^{-k}$ (Thm 5.3); closures (the ω-rule, full second-order consequence; for the generative variant only with the universal sentence among the data); line budgets and a Kreisel-type property, with a non-degenerate counterexample (Prop 5.4, Example 5.4(ii)).

Also: on $\Th(\mathbb N)$ in a length-lexicographic enumeration the loss grows at least like $n^\delta$, with $\delta\ge0.0823$ **proved** for a stated syntax by an exact certificate (Thm 6.1), and every true r.e. theory leaves about $n^\delta$ of the first $n$ sentences undecided (Cor 6.2); whether the loss is $o(n)$ reduces to generic decidability of arithmetic and stays open (Prop 6.3).

### 1.1 Objects

The definitions are those of `fundamentals` §1 (final record), unchanged: a language $\mathcal L$ with equality and computable syntax; a decidable consistent background $B$; data sets $D$ (finite partial functions $\Sent\rightharpoonup\{0,1\}$), $D^\pm$, $\psi_D$; theory machines $G$ and their laws $\nu_G$; the universal theory machine $G_U$, $A_\omega$; $O_\chi=\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\vdash\chi\}$; $\Inc_0=\Unif(O_\bot)$; $E_D=O_{\psi_D}\setminus O_\bot$; $M_A(D)=\Unif(E_D)$; $L_A=-\log M_A$; $\Bel_D,\Dis_D,\Indep_D$; $\Bel_0(\chi)=\Unif(O_\chi)-\Inc_0$, unnormalised, with $\Bel_\emptyset=\widehat\Bel_0=\Bel_0/(1-\Inc_0)$; $W_A$, $K_A$, $K_{\rm fin}$. Class probabilities are as in `fundamentals` Def 1.4: $\Unif_A[T]:=\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(A_\omega)=\Cn(T)\}$ and $\Unif_A(\Ext T):=\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(A_\omega)\supseteq\Cn(T)\}$; $\mm(\Ext T)$ as in NOTATION §3.3. For an infinite stream, $E_\infty:=\bigcap_nE_{D_n}$ and $P_\infty:=\Unif(\cdot\mid E_\infty)$ when $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$.

Facts from `fundamentals` used throughout (all proved there):
* **(Dom)** $M_A\ge2^{-K(\nu)}\nu$ for every theory-machine law $\nu$, tape by tape: the tapes $p\omega'$ with $p$ a shortest program for $G$ have $A_{p\omega'}=A^G_{\omega'}$, so every event about the random theory of $G$ is matched, at a cost of $2^{-K(G)}$, by the same event for $G_U$. In particular $M_A(D)\ge2^{-K(e)-c}$ whenever $B\cup\mathsf W_e$ is consistent and proves $\psi_D$ (Thm 4.1).
* **(Chain)** $2^{-K_A(D)}\le W_A(D)\le2^cM_A(D)$ (Prop 6.1(c)).
* **(Lim)** If $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$, the posterior converges to $P_\infty$ in total variation and the cumulative loss is bounded; if $\Unif(E_\infty)=0$, it tends to $\infty$ (Prop 8.1(a),(b)). For every $\Gamma$ with $\Cn(\Gamma)=\Cn(D_\infty^\pm)$, complete or not, $E_\infty=\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \Cn(A_\omega)\supseteq\Cn(\Gamma)\}$, so $P_\infty$ is the prior restricted to the consistent extensions of $\Gamma$ (Prop 8.1(d)).

### 1.2 Arithmetic

* $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}=\{0,S,+,\cdot,<\}$, numerals $\underline n=S^n0$. $\Delta_0,\Sigma_1,\Pi_1$ as usual; $\Pi_1^*$ = finite conjunctions of $\Pi_1$ sentences and negations of $\Sigma_1$ sentences (NOTATION §3.1). These classes are syntactic; where a sentence is only $\Q$-equivalent to a member of a class, this record says "$\Q$-equivalent to".
* "$B$ **true**" means: $B$ has a model whose $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-reduct is $\mathbb N$ (for $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$: $B\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)$); likewise "$B\cup T$ true". "$B\supseteq\Q$" means $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$, likewise for $\PA$.
* A theory is **$\Sigma_1$-sound** if it proves no false $\Sigma_1$ sentence.
* Binary strings are coded by numbers through their **length-lexicographic rank** (the empty string is $0$, then $0,1,00,01,\dots$); this coding is injective across lengths and the length of a string is computable from its code. The strings of length $\le m$ are exactly the codes $\le\mathrm{rk}_m:=2^{m+1}-2$.

Standard facts used [known: textbook, e.g. Smoryński 1977 and Boolos, Burgess and Jeffrey, *Computability and Logic*, chs. 16–18; section numbers not re-checked; `sfi` §6.2 uses (O) with the same reference]:
* **($\Sigma_1$C)** $\Q$ proves every true $\Sigma_1$ sentence (in particular every true $\Delta_0$ sentence) and refutes every false $\Delta_0$ sentence.
* **($\Pi_1$T)** Consequently, if $B\supseteq\Q$ and $B\cup A$ is consistent, every $\Pi_1^*$ sentence that $B\cup A$ proves is true: if a conjunct were false, its negation would be a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence, provable in $\Q$.
* **(O)** For every $n$, $\Q\vdash\forall z\,(z\le\underline n\to z=\underline0\vee\dots\vee z=\underline n)$; so $\Q$ unfolds bounded quantifiers with a numeral bound into finite conjunctions and disjunctions.
* **(G2)** For every consistent r.e. presentation $Z$ with $\PA\subseteq\Cn(Z)$, $Z\nvdash\Con(Z)$, where $\Con(Z)$ is the canonical consistency statement of the presentation (a $\Pi_1$ sentence).
* **(Diag)** For every formula $\Theta(z,x)$ there is a formula $\Phi(x)$ with $\Q\vdash\forall x\,(\Phi(x)\leftrightarrow\Theta(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner,x))$.
* **(Uniq)** For Kleene's predicate $\mathrm T(p,t,y)$ ("$t$ is the code of the halting computation of $V$ on input $p$, with output $y$"), each halting computation has a unique code, and $\PA\vdash\forall p,t,t',y,y'\,(\mathrm T(p,t,y)\wedge\mathrm T(p,t',y')\to t=t'\wedge y=y')$ (determinism, provable by induction on the computation).

### 1.3 Conventions for losses (new in the revision)

As NOTATION §7 requires, every loss statement names its convention.
* Unless stated otherwise, losses are in the **semimeasure convention**: the cumulative loss of $D_n$ is $L_A(D_n)-L_A(\emptyset)$, and for the coin-free version $-\log W_A(D_n)+\log W_A(\emptyset)$. In particular the lower bounds Thm 2.2(c) ($W_A(D_n)\to0$), Cor 2.6′, Cor 3.6(a) and Thm 6.1 are semimeasure-convention statements.
* Upper bounds transfer to the **normalised convention** (each normalised conditional is at least the raw one); lower bounds do not. Prop 3.11 shows that the lower bound of Cor 3.6 fails in the normalised convention for most strings.
* In the **three-valued convention** the conditional probability of the label $1$ on a query $\varphi$ is $\Bel_D(\varphi)$, so a lower bound on $-\log\Bel_D$ is a lower bound on the three-valued cost of a true label.
* Statements about $K_A$, $K_{\rm fin}$ and about the posterior ($P_\infty$, $\Bel$, $\Dis$, $\Indep$) do not depend on a loss convention.

## 2. The lottery (target T1)

### 2.1 A density lemma

**Lemma 2.1 (Lebesgue density on Cantor space) [proved].** Let $X\subseteq\{0,1\}^\infty$ be Borel with $\Unif(X)>0$ and $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$. Then there is a finite string $w$ with $\Unif(X\cap[w])>(1-\varepsilon)2^{-|w|}$, i.e. $\Pr_w(X)>1-\varepsilon$.

*Proof.* By outer regularity there is an open $O\supseteq X$ with $\Unif(O)<\Unif(X)/(1-\varepsilon)$. Write $O=\bigsqcup_i[w_i]$ with $\{w_i\}$ prefix-free. If $\Unif(X\cap[w_i])\le(1-\varepsilon)2^{-|w_i|}$ for every $i$, summing gives $\Unif(X)\le(1-\varepsilon)\Unif(O)<\Unif(X)$. ∎

### 2.2 The lottery theorem

**Setting.** $\mathcal L$ contains $0$, $S$ and a binary relation symbol $\mathrm{Win}$; $B$ is decidable, consistent, does not mention $\mathrm{Win}$, and has a model in which the numerals denote pairwise distinct elements (e.g. $B=\emptyset$, $\Q$ or $\PA$ with $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}\cup\{\mathrm{Win}\}$). For $k\ge0$:
$$\mathrm{Lot}_k:=\exists x\,\mathrm{Win}(\underline k,x)\ \wedge\ \forall x\,\big(\mathrm{Win}(\underline k,x)\to x=\underline0\vee\cdots\vee x=\underline{N_k}\big),$$
"lottery $k$ has a winner, and every winner is one of its tickets".

**The random lottery theory $G_{\rm lot}$.** It reads the tape in consecutive blocks of $k+1$ bits, $k=0,1,2,\dots$; block $k$ is read as a number $J_k\in\{0,\dots,N_k\}$. After reading block $k$ it emits $\mathrm{Lot}_k$, $\mathrm{Win}(\underline k,\underline{J_k})$ and $\neg\mathrm{Win}(\underline k,\underline j)$ for every $j\le N_k$, $j\ne J_k$. Under $\Unif$ the $J_k$ are independent and uniform.

*Every $A^{G_{\rm lot}}_\omega$ is consistent with $B$*: take a model $\mathfrak M$ of $B$ with distinct numerals and put $\mathrm{Win}^{\mathfrak M}:=\{(\underline k^{\mathfrak M},\underline{J_k}^{\mathfrak M}):k\in\mathbb N\}$. Because the numerals are distinct, for each $k$ the only $\mathrm{Win}$-partner of $\underline k^{\mathfrak M}$ is $\underline{J_k}^{\mathfrak M}$, so every emitted sentence holds; $B$ does not mention $\mathrm{Win}$.

**Theorem 2.2 (the lottery) [proved; computed: `c1` A1, A2; `rb1` A, B].** There is an infinite stream $D_\infty$ of labelled sentences, computable from $\emptyset'$, such that:
* (a) **Bounded one-tape loss.** For every $n$, $M_A(D_n)\ge2^{-K(G_{\rm lot})-c}\prod_{k\ge0}(1-2^{-(k+1)})$, and the product equals $0.288788\ldots$ So $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$, the cumulative loss $L_A(D_n)-L_A(\emptyset)$ is bounded by $K(G_{\rm lot})+\log(1/0.2887)+c$, and the posterior converges in total variation to $P_\infty$.
* (b) **No theory.** No consistent r.e. theory proves every member of $D_\infty^\pm$ (together with $B$).
* (c) **The other versions diverge.** $W_A(D_n)\to0$ and $K_A(D_n)\to\infty$, while $L_A(D_n)$ stays bounded. Also $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L'}(D_n)\to\infty$ for every language $\mathcal L'\supseteq\mathcal L$ **with computable syntax**.
* (d) **The posterior abandons theories.** The posterior probability that the tape's program is a deterministic machine (one emitting only under the empty prefix) tends to $0$; and $E_\infty\cap\{\omega:\Cn(B\cup A_\omega)\text{ is r.e.}\}=\emptyset$, so the limit posterior lives entirely on random theories whose deductive closure is not r.e.

*Proof.* **The stream.** Let $\mathsf W_0,\mathsf W_1,\dots$ enumerate the r.e. sets of $\mathcal L$-sentences. For each $e$ define $j^*_e\in\{0,\dots,N_e\}$:
* if $B\cup\mathsf W_e$ is consistent and proves $\mathrm{Lot}_e$, let $j^*_e$ be the least $j\le N_e$ with $B\cup\mathsf W_e\nvdash\neg\mathrm{Win}(\underline e,\underline j)$;
* otherwise $j^*_e:=0$.

In the first case such a $j$ exists: if $B\cup\mathsf W_e$ proved $\neg\mathrm{Win}(\underline e,\underline j)$ for all $j\le N_e$, then with the second conjunct of $\mathrm{Lot}_e$ and the equality axioms it would prove $\forall x\,\neg\mathrm{Win}(\underline e,x)$, contradicting the first conjunct and its consistency. Put
$$D_\infty:=(\mathrm{Lot}_0,1),\ (\mathrm{Win}(\underline0,\underline{j^*_0}),0),\ (\mathrm{Lot}_1,1),\ (\mathrm{Win}(\underline1,\underline{j^*_1}),0),\ \dots$$
"$B\cup\mathsf W_e$ is consistent" is $\Pi_1$, "$B\cup\mathsf W_e\vdash\mathrm{Lot}_e$" and "$B\cup\mathsf W_e\vdash\neg\mathrm{Win}(\underline e,\underline j)$" are $\Sigma_1$, uniformly; so $e\mapsto j^*_e$ and the stream are computable from $\emptyset'$.

**(a)** Let $\mathrm{Surv}:=\{\omega:J_k(\omega)\ne j^*_k\ \forall k\}$. By independence and continuity from above, $\Unif(\mathrm{Surv})=\prod_k(1-2^{-(k+1)})$, which is positive because $\sum_k2^{-(k+1)}<\infty$. For $\omega\in\mathrm{Surv}$ the theory $B\cup A^{G_{\rm lot}}_\omega$ is consistent, emits every $\mathrm{Lot}_k$, and emits $\neg\mathrm{Win}(\underline k,\underline{j^*_k})$ because $j^*_k\le N_k$ and $j^*_k\ne J_k$. So $\nu_{G_{\rm lot}}(D_n)\ge\Unif(\mathrm{Surv})$ for all $n$, and (Dom) gives the bound. The rest is (Lim); $M_A(\emptyset)\le1$ gives the bound on the cumulative loss.

**(b)** If $B\cup\mathsf W_e$ is consistent and proves $D_\infty^\pm$, it proves $\mathrm{Lot}_e$, so $j^*_e$ was chosen with $B\cup\mathsf W_e\nvdash\neg\mathrm{Win}(\underline e,\underline{j^*_e})$; but that sentence is in $D_\infty^\pm$.

**(c)** $W_A(D_n)=\sum_e\mm(e)\ind[B\cup\mathsf W_e\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_{D_n}]$. Each indicator is non-increasing in $n$ and, by (b), eventually $0$; dominated convergence ($\sum_e\mm(e)\le1$) gives $W_A(D_n)\to0$, and $K_A\ge-\log W_A$ (Chain). For a single sentence $\psi$ of $\mathcal L'$ with $B\cup\{\psi\}$ consistent, its $\mathcal L$-content $\mathrm{Cont}(\psi):=\{\varphi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L}:B\cup\{\psi\}\vdash\varphi\}$ is r.e. (this uses the computable syntax of $\mathcal L'$) and $B\cup\mathrm{Cont}(\psi)$ is consistent, so by (b) $\mathrm{Cont}(\psi)\not\supseteq D_\infty^\pm$: every consistent $\psi$ fails some datum. Only finitely many $\psi$ have $K(\psi)\le j$, so $K^{\mathcal L'}_{\rm fin}(D_n)\to\infty$.

**(d)** Let $P_{\rm det}$ be the set of $p\in\dom V$ such that $G_{V(p)}\subseteq\{\epsilon\}\times\Sent$, and $X_p$ the set it emits. Then $\Unif(\{\omega\in E_{D_n}:\omega\text{ begins with some }p\in P_{\rm det}\})=\sum_{p\in P_{\rm det}}2^{-|p|}\ind[B\cup X_p\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_{D_n}]\to0$ by (b) and dominated convergence; divide by $M_A(D_n)$, which is bounded below by (a). If $\omega\in E_\infty$ and $\Cn(B\cup A_\omega)$ were r.e., it would be a consistent r.e. theory proving $D_\infty^\pm$, contrary to (b). ∎

### 2.3 Remarks on Theorem 2.2

* **The stream must be non-r.e. [proved].** If $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$ and $D_\infty^\pm$ is r.e., then $D_\infty^\pm$ is itself a consistent r.e. theory proving the stream. So every stream answering scout F's Q1 negatively has a set of literals that is not r.e. For lottery-type sentence classes it is not co-r.e. either (Prop 2.7). The streams constructed here are $\Delta_2$; an arbitrary stream with these properties need not be arithmetical (for instance, a diagonal against a non-arithmetical list).
* **Only for proof-requiring compatibility [proved].** The r.e. theory $\{\mathrm{Lot}_k:k\in\mathbb N\}$ is consistent with every finite part of $D_\infty$, so under the consistency-requiring variant it remains compatible forever. The phenomenon needs compatibility to demand that each reported loser be *derived*.
* **Where the randomness sits.** Every random lottery theory has the same law ($\mathrm{Lot}_k$ for all $k$) and differs only in randomly drawn contingent facts (the winners). A deterministic theory that derives losers must commit to winners once, and the diagonal reports exactly a ticket it fails to refute; a random theory commits at random and dies only if it happens to have picked the reported ticket. This is a forced version of the split between laws and randomly drawn initial conditions (P2's model–randomness split): here no deterministic hypothesis at all succeeds.
* **Consequence for consistent SOI (one sentence).** Since $M_F^{\rm cons}\asymp M_A$ and $W^{\rm cons}\asymp W_A$ (`sfi` Thm 3.1 and Thm 2.1), the stream of Theorem 2.2 separates one-tape consistent SOI (bounded loss) from coin-free consistent SOI (unbounded loss, semimeasure convention), while plain SOI is not separated (Prop 2.3(b); `sfi` Cor 4.2). So the lottery has no analogue in plain SOI, and it has one in consistent SOI.
* **For `terms`.** On this stream every random *finite* theory, in any extended language with computable syntax, fails (its content is r.e.), so random r.e. theories strictly beat random finite theories (scout N1's B4, `terms` Rem 6.6).
* **Computed illustration [computed: `c1` A1, A2; `rb1` B].** Exhaustive enumeration over winner vectors confirms the survival probabilities $\prod_{k<K}(1-2^{-(k+1)})$ for $K\le5$ (single lottery per index) and the batch version's bound (§2.5) for $K\le3$. What `c1` A1 tests is the product identity only (its reported tickets are drawn at random); the diagonal itself is tested against random lists in `c1` A1 and against an adversarial list (each listed theory survives until its own lottery) in referee B's `rb1` B, where one listed theory dies per lottery and $M(D_n)\ge\frac14\prod$ throughout.

### 2.4 Where the lottery cannot happen: bounded inconsistency arity

**Proposition 2.3 (majority vote) [proved; computed: `c2` A, B; `c6` R6; `rb2`].**
(a) *Axiom induction.* Let $S\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ be decidable and $r\ge1$, and suppose every $B$-inconsistent finite subset of $S$ contains a $B$-inconsistent subset with at most $r$ elements. Let $D_\infty$ be a stream with $D_\infty^\pm\subseteq S$ and $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$. Then there is a consistent r.e. $T\subseteq S$ with $D_\infty^\pm\subseteq T$; hence $\inf_nW_A(D_n)\ge\mm(\text{index of }T)>0$, and the coin-free loss is bounded too.
(b) *SOI.* For P1's one-tape $M_F$ with inputs $X$ and labels $Y$: if $D_\infty:X\rightharpoonup Y$ is infinite with $\lim_nM_F(D_n)>0$, some partial computable $f$ extends $D_\infty$, so P1's coin-free $W$ also has bounded loss on $D_\infty$.

*Proof.* (a) Put $\varepsilon:=1/(r+1)$ and choose $w$ by Lemma 2.1 with $\Pr_w(E_\infty)>1-\varepsilon$. Let $T:=\{\varphi\in S:\Pr_w(O_\varphi)>1-\varepsilon\}$, r.e. because $\Pr_w(O_\varphi)$ is left-c.e. uniformly in $\varphi$ (`fundamentals` Lemma 1.3). For $\varphi\in D_\infty^\pm$, $O_\varphi\supseteq E_\infty$, so $\varphi\in T$. Suppose $B\cup T$ is inconsistent. By compactness and the hypothesis, some $\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_j\in T$ with $j\le r$ are jointly $B$-inconsistent. Then $\Pr_w(\bigcap_iO_{\varphi_i})>1-j\varepsilon\ge1-r\varepsilon=\varepsilon$, and every tape in that intersection has an inconsistent theory, so $\Pr_w(O_\bot)>\varepsilon$. But $O_\bot\cap E_\infty=\emptyset$ gives $\Pr_w(O_\bot)<\varepsilon$. Contradiction.
(b) Choose $w$ with $\Pr_w\{\omega:f_\omega\supseteq D_\infty\}>\frac12$ and let $f(x):=y$ iff $\Pr_w\{f_\omega(x)=y\}>\frac12$. The events $\{f_\omega(x)=y\}$ are effectively open and disjoint in $y$, so $f$ is partial computable; for $x\in\dom D_\infty$ the event $\{f_\omega(x)=D_\infty(x)\}$ contains $\{f_\omega\supseteq D_\infty\}$. ∎

**The threshold is exact for the argument [proved; computed: `c6` R6; `rb2` A, B].** Let one $B$-inconsistent set $\{\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_s\}$ be minimal, and consider a law with inconsistent mass $a$ and mass $(1-a)/s$ on each of $s$ consistent types, type $i$ proving every $\varphi_j$ with $j\ne i$ (and nothing else). Then $\Pr(O_{\varphi_j})=a+(s-1)(1-a)/s$, so the majority set at threshold $1-\varepsilon$ contains all of $\{\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_s\}$ iff $a>1-s\varepsilon$, while the consistent mass exceeds $1-\varepsilon$ iff $a<\varepsilon$. Both hold for some $a$ iff $\varepsilon>1/(s+1)$. So $1/(r+1)$ is the largest threshold at which the argument is guaranteed to give a consistent set, and a family of minimal inconsistent sets of unbounded size defeats every threshold; referee B's LP over all laws on all 166 nonempty antichains on 4 sentences finds the same critical value.

**Instances.** Literal data of a fresh predicate ($r=2$; scout F's condition (Lit), `fundamentals` Def 1.1′). Plain SOI. $\Pi_1$ data over a true background, by a different argument (Cor 3.5). The lottery class has minimal inconsistent sets $\{\mathrm{Lot}_k\}\cup\{\neg\mathrm{Win}(\underline k,\underline j):j\le N_k\}$ of size $N_k+2$, so no $r$ works; for every threshold $1-\varepsilon$ the majority set refutes every ticket of each lottery with $2^{k+1}>1/\varepsilon$ and is inconsistent [computed: `c2` B].

**A truth version.** Truth is a constraint of arity one, so the majority argument transfers to it:

**Proposition 2.4 (majority over true theories) [proved; computed: `c2` C; `rb2` C].** Let $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$ and $B\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)$ decidable. If $\mathcal T_{\rm true}:=\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N),\ B\cup A_\omega\vdash D_\infty^\pm\}$ has $\Unif(\mathcal T_{\rm true})>0$, then some r.e. $T\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)$ has $B\cup T\vdash D_\infty^\pm$.

*Proof.* $\mathcal T_{\rm true}$ is Borel ($\{\omega:A_\omega\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)\}=\bigcap_{\chi\text{ false}}\{\omega:\chi\notin A_\omega\}$ is closed, and the proving condition is a countable intersection of open sets). Choose $w$ with $\Pr_w(\mathcal T_{\rm true})>\frac12$ and let $T:=\{\varphi:\Pr_w(O_\varphi)>\frac12\}$, r.e. It contains $D_\infty^\pm$. For $\varphi\in T$, $\Pr_w(O_\varphi\cap\mathcal T_{\rm true})>0$, so a true theory proves $\varphi$ and $\varphi$ is true. ∎

**Corollary 2.5 (scout F's Q1) [proved].** $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$ does not in general imply that a consistent r.e. theory proves $D_\infty$ (Thm 2.2, and Thm 2.9 below inside true arithmetic). It does when the data's inconsistency arity is bounded (Prop 2.3), for data of $\Pi_1$ type over a true background $\supseteq\Q$ (Cor 3.5), and for co-r.e. streams over block-decidable sentence classes, which include the lottery classes (Prop 2.7). If a positive-measure set of *true* random theories proves $D_\infty$, a true r.e. theory does (Prop 2.4).

### 2.5 How fast does the coin-free loss diverge? (extension E-Q2)

For a theory machine $G$ and a finite string $u$ let $A^G_u$ be the set of axioms emitted under prefixes of $u$.

**Proposition 2.6 (the proof horizon bounds the coin-free loss) [proved].** Let $G$ be a theory machine and $D_\infty$ a stream with
$$a_G:=\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A^G_\omega\text{ consistent},\ B\cup A^G_\omega\vdash D_\infty^\pm\}>0 .$$
Let the *proof horizon* $h_G(n)$ be the least $m$ with $\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A^G_\omega\text{ consistent},\ B\cup A^G_{\omega_{1:m}}\vdash\psi_{D_n}\}\ge a_G/2$. Then for every $n$,
$$-\log W_A(D_n)\ \le\ \Kpad(h_G(n))+K(G)+\log(2/a_G)+c .$$

*Proof.* $h_G(n)$ exists: a derivation of $\psi_{D_n}$ uses finitely many axioms, all emitted under some finite prefix, so the events in the definition increase in $m$ to a set containing the one defining $a_G$. Fix $m\ge h_G(n)$ and let $\mathrm{Good}_m$ be the set of $u\in\{0,1\}^m$ such that $B\cup A^G_u\vdash\psi_{D_n}$ and $[u]$ contains a tape $\omega$ with $B\cup A^G_\omega$ consistent. For $u\in\mathrm{Good}_m$, $A^G_u\subseteq A^G_\omega$, so $B\cup A^G_u$ is consistent and proves $\psi_{D_n}$; and $|\mathrm{Good}_m|2^{-m}\ge a_G/2$. The set $A^G_u$ is r.e. with an index $e(G,u)$ computable from $(G,u)$ and injective in $u$ (write $u$ into the index). Hence
$$W_A(D_n)\ge\sum_{u\in\mathrm{Good}_m}\mm(e(G,u))\ge\sum_{u\in\mathrm{Good}_m}2^{-K(G)-m-K(m)-c}\ge\tfrac{a_G}22^{-K(G)-K(m)-c},$$
using $K(e(G,u))\le K(G)+K(u)+c$ and $K(u)\le m+K(m)+c$ for $|u|=m$. Minimise $K(m)$ over $m\ge h_G(n)$. ∎

**Corollary 2.6′ (rate on the lottery stream) [proved; computed: `rb0` K2].** On the stream of Theorem 2.2:
* $-\log W_A(D_n)\le\Kpad(n)+c$ for all $n$. Since $\Kpad$ grows more slowly than every computable unbounded **nondecreasing** function, the coin-free loss (semimeasure convention) diverges, but more slowly than any computable unbounded nondecreasing function. (The restriction to nondecreasing functions is needed: a computable unbounded function that vanishes on the odd numbers is not eventually above $\Kpad$.)
* $K_A(D_n)\le\lceil n/2\rceil+O(\log n)$, and $K_A(D_n)\to\infty$.

*Proof.* For $G=G_{\rm lot}$ and $\omega\in\mathrm{Surv}$ (proof of 2.2(a)), the axioms needed for $D_n$ are emitted once the first $\lceil n/2\rceil$ blocks are read, i.e. by the prefix of length $m(n):=\sum_{k<\lceil n/2\rceil}(k+1)$; so $h_{G_{\rm lot}}(n)\le m(n)$, which is computable from $n$, whence $\Kpad(h(n))\le K(m(n))+c\le K(n)+c'$. As $D_n\subseteq D_{n'}$ for $n\le n'$, $W_A(D_n)\ge W_A(D_{n'})$, so $-\log W_A(D_n)\le\min_{n'\ge n}K(n')+c'=\Kpad(n)+c'$. *Slow growth*: for computable nondecreasing unbounded $g$, let $\eta(j)$ be the least $n'$ with $g(n')\ge j$ (computable); then $\eta(g(n)+1)>n$ and $\Kpad(n)\le K(\eta(g(n)+1))\le2\log(g(n)+1)+c$; apply this to $g:=\lfloor2^{f/3}\rfloor$ for a given computable unbounded nondecreasing $f$, which gives $\Kpad(n)\le\frac23f(n)+c$. *Upper bound on $K_A$*: with $K:=\lceil n/2\rceil$, the tickets $j^*_k$, $k<K$, are determined by the truth values of the $\Sigma_1$ facts "$B\cup\mathsf W_k\vdash\mathrm{Lot}_k$" and "$B\cup\mathsf W_k\vdash\neg\mathrm{Win}(\underline k,\underline j)$" ($j\le N_k$): $j^*_k$ is the least unrefuted ticket if $\mathsf W_k$ proves $\mathrm{Lot}_k$ and some ticket is unrefuted, and $0$ otherwise (an inconsistent $\mathsf W_k$ refutes all tickets). There are $2^{K+1}+K-2$ such facts (`rb0` K2); given $K$ and the number of true ones ($K+2$ bits), enumerate until that many are found. So $K(\psi_{D_n})\le K+O(\log K)$ and $K_A\le K(\psi_{D_n})+c$. $K_A\to\infty$ is 2.2(c). ∎

**The batch lottery [proved; computed: `c1` A2; `rb0` K2].** Give lottery $k$ the $2^{2k+1}$ tickets $0,\dots,2^{2k+1}-1$ (a block of $2k+1$ bits) and diagonalise in it against every $e<2^k$ at once: the data of lottery $k$ are $(\mathrm{Lot}_k,1)$ and $(\mathrm{Win}(\underline k,\underline{j^*_{k,e}}),0)$ for $e<2^k$, with $j^*_{k,e}$ the least ticket that $B\cup\mathsf W_e$ does not refute if $B\cup\mathsf W_e$ is consistent and proves $\mathrm{Lot}_k$, and $0$ otherwise. At most $2^k$ tickets are reported, so the random theory survives lottery $k$ with probability $\ge1-2^k/2^{2k+1}=1-2^{-(k+1)}$ and Theorem 2.2(a) holds with the same product; after lottery $k$ every $e<2^k$ has failed, so $W_A\le\sum_{e\ge2^k}\mm(e)$. The horizon after $n$ data is at most $(n+1)^2$ (at most $n$ lotteries are complete), so again $-\log W_A(D_n)\le\Kpad(n)+c$.

**Open (E-Q2, remaining part).** Is there a stream with bounded one-tape loss on which $-\log W_A(D_n)\ge f(n)$ for some computable unbounded nondecreasing $f$? By Prop 2.6 such a stream would need, for every machine $G$ with $a_G>0$, a proof horizon with $\Kpad(h_G(n))\ge f(n)-O(1)$. Then $h_G$ is not bounded by any computable function $b$: replacing $b$ by its running maximum, which is computable and nondecreasing, $\Kpad(h_G(n))\le\Kpad(b(n))\le\min_{n'\ge n}K(b(n'))\le\Kpad(n)+c$, and $\Kpad(n)<f(n)-O(1)$ for large $n$ by the slow-growth argument above.

### 2.6 Co-r.e. streams

**Proposition 2.7 (co-r.e. streams over block-decidable classes) [proved; computed: `c1` C; the block criterion of instance (1): `rb1` A].** Let $S\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ be decidable and computably partitioned into finite blocks $S_0,S_1,\dots$ (each given by a canonical index), such that
* (i) for every finite $X\subseteq S$: $B\cup X$ is consistent iff $B\cup(X\cap S_k)$ is consistent for every $k$;
* (ii) consistency of $B\cup X$ for finite $X\subseteq S_k$ is decidable, uniformly in $k$.

If $D_\infty^\pm\subseteq S$ is co-r.e. and $B\cup D_\infty^\pm$ is consistent, then some consistent decidable $T\subseteq S$ contains $D_\infty^\pm$.

*Proof.* $S_k\setminus D_\infty^\pm$ is r.e. uniformly in $k$; let $C^s_k$ be $S_k$ minus what has been enumerated by stage $s$. As $S_k$ is finite, $C^s_k=D_\infty^\pm\cap S_k$ for large $s$, and that set is consistent with $B$; so by (ii) the least $s_k$ with $B\cup C^{s_k}_k$ consistent exists and is computable. Put $T:=\bigcup_kC^{s_k}_k$ (decidable). $C^s_k\supseteq D_\infty^\pm\cap S_k$ for all $s$, so $T\supseteq D_\infty^\pm$, and $B\cup T$ is consistent by compactness and (i). ∎

**Instances.** (1) The lottery class $S=\{\mathrm{Lot}_k\}\cup\{\mathrm{Win}(\underline k,\underline j)^{b}:j\le N_k,b\in\{0,1\}\}$ with blocks by $k$: under the hypotheses of §2.2 a finite $X\subseteq S$ is consistent with $B$ iff no block contains a complementary pair or $\mathrm{Lot}_k$ together with all $N_k+1$ negative literals of lottery $k$ (choose the $\mathrm{Win}$-row of each $\underline k$ separately in a model with distinct numerals), so (i) and (ii) hold. Referee B's `rb1` A confirms the criterion against satisfiability in all structures with at most 5 elements, for $B=\emptyset$ and two blocks (all 16384 subsets). (2) The arithmetic lottery classes of §2.7: by Lemma 2.8(i) every propositionally satisfiable finite set of Boolean combinations of the tickets is consistent with $B$, so consistency is a propositional (decidable, blockwise) property. **So every stream of the lottery kinds that answers Q1 negatively is neither r.e. nor co-r.e.**; the streams constructed here are $\Delta_2$.

**Open.** Whether *some* co-r.e. stream (over a sentence class where consistency is not decidable) has $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$ and no consistent r.e. theory proving it. Prop 2.7's adversary waits, in each block, for a stage at which the remaining data are certifiably consistent; a counterexample must deny it any such certificate at every finite stage.

### 2.7 Lotteries inside arithmetic (extension E-Q1)

The lottery needs a family of sentences that behave, with respect to consistency with $B$, like independent atoms. Inside arithmetic with a true background they are provided by self-referential ("flexible") formulas. Lemma 2.8 is a self-contained proof of the weak form needed, in a version that also prescribes the truth values in $\mathbb N$.

**Lemma 2.8 (flexible formulas with prescribed truth) [proved, given (Diag), ($\Sigma_1$C)].** Let $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$ and $B$ decidable, true, $B\supseteq\Q$. Write $\mathrm{In}(y,e)$ for a $\Delta_0$ formula saying "$y$ is in the finite set with canonical index $e$", and for finite $F\subseteq\mathbb N$ with index $e_F$ write $y\in F$ for $\mathrm{In}(y,\underline{e_F})$. For every formula $\Psi(z,x)$ there is a formula $\Phi(x)$ such that
* (i) $B+\forall y\,(\Phi(y)\leftrightarrow y\in F)$ is consistent for every finite $F\subseteq\mathbb N$; hence any finite pattern $\{\Phi(\underline n)^{b_n}:n\in F'\}$ is consistent with $B$;
* (ii) $\mathbb N\models\Phi(\underline n)\iff\mathbb N\models\Psi(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner,\underline n)$ for every $n$;
* (iii) $\Q\vdash\forall x\,\big(\Phi(x)\leftrightarrow\mathcal A(x)\vee(\mathcal N\wedge\Psi(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner,x))\big)$ for a $\Sigma_1$ formula $\mathcal A(x)$ that is false of every number and a true $\Pi_1$ sentence $\mathcal N$.

*Proof.* Fix a computable enumeration $\tau_0,\tau_1,\dots$ of the theorems of $B$. For a code $z$ let $\varphi_z$ be the formula with code $z$, and call a sentence *of form $(z,e)$* if it is $\neg\forall y\,(\varphi_z(y)\leftrightarrow\mathrm{In}(y,\underline e))$; whether $\tau_s$ is of form $(z,e)$ for some $e$, and which $e$, is decidable from $(s,z)$. Let $\mathcal A_0(z,x)$ be the $\Sigma_1$ formula "there are $s$ and $e$ such that $\tau_s$ is of form $(z,e)$, no $\tau_{s'}$ with $s'<s$ is of form $(z,e')$ for any $e'$, and $\mathrm{In}(x,e)$" (the computation of $\tau_0,\dots,\tau_s$ is an existentially quantified witness, checked by a $\Delta_0$ formula), and $\mathcal N_0(z)$ the $\Pi_1$ formula "no $\tau_s$ is of form $(z,e)$ for any $e$". By (Diag) take $\Phi$ with
$$\Q\vdash\forall x\,\big(\Phi(x)\leftrightarrow\mathcal A_0(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner,x)\vee(\mathcal N_0(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner)\wedge\Psi(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner,x))\big).$$
As $\Q$ is true, the same equivalence holds in $\mathbb N$. Suppose some $\tau_s$ is of form $(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner,e)$; let $s_0$ be the least such $s$, with index $e_0$ and set $F_0$. In $\mathbb N$, $\mathcal A_0(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner,n)$ holds iff $n\in F_0$, and $\mathcal N_0$ fails; so $\mathbb N\models\forall y\,(\Phi(y)\leftrightarrow y\in F_0)$. But $\tau_{s_0}$, the negation of this sentence, is a theorem of the true theory $B$. Contradiction. Hence no theorem of $B$ is of form $(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner,e)$, which is (i) (the pattern follows from $\forall y(\Phi(y)\leftrightarrow y\in F)$ with $F:=\{n\in F':b_n=1\}$, since $\Q$ decides each $\underline n\in F$ correctly by ($\Sigma_1$C)); $\mathcal N_0(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner)$ is true and $\mathcal A_0(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner,\underline n)$ false for every $n$, which gives (ii) and (iii) with $\mathcal A(x):=\mathcal A_0(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner,x)$, $\mathcal N:=\mathcal N_0(\ulcorner\Phi\urcorner)$. ∎

*Literature.* This is the standard self-referential construction behind Kripke's flexible formulas (Kripke 1962) and Mostowski's independent sequences (Mostowski 1961); Woodin's universal algorithm (Woodin 2011; Blanck–Enayat 2017; Hamkins 2018) is a much stronger statement of the same kind. The version with a prescribed truth set (ii) is what the true lottery needs; it was not found stated in this form, but it is a routine variant and no novelty is claimed for it.

Let $\langle k,j\rangle$ be a $\Delta_0$ pairing with $k,j\le\langle k,j\rangle$, and write $\underline{\langle k,j\rangle}$ for the numeral of the pair code.

**Theorem 2.9 (lotteries in arithmetic) [proved, given Lemma 2.8; computed: (b)'s bookkeeping `c1` A3, exhaustively `rb3`].** Let $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$ and $B$ decidable, true, $B\supseteq\Q$. Let $\mathsf W_e$ range over r.e. sets of $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-sentences.

*Proof.* (i) Theorem 2.9(b) with 2.2(a),(c). (ii) If $\Unif\{\omega\in E_\infty:B\cup A_\omega\text{ true}\}>0$, Prop 2.4 gives an r.e. true theory proving the stream, contrary to 2.2(b). ## 3. $\Pi_1$ data: derandomisation, a coding theorem, Chaitin (target T2)

### 3.1 Setting

Throughout §3: $\mathcal L\supseteq\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$, $B$ decidable, true, $B\supseteq\Q$. $\Inc_0$ is left-c.e. and not computable (`fundamentals` Thm 3.2(a)), hence irrational. $I'_k:=\lfloor2^{k+2}\Inc_0\rfloor2^{-(k+2)}$, so $I'_k\le\Inc_0<I'_k+2^{-(k+2)}$; $I'_k$ is given by an integer below $2^{k+2}$, i.e. by $k+2$ bits once $k$ is known.

**Data of $\Pi_1$ type.** A data set $D$ is **of $\Pi_1$ type** if each literal in $D^\pm$ is $B$-equivalent to a $\Pi_1^*$ sentence or to a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence (in particular, if it is $\Q$-equivalent to one). Then $\psi_D$ is $B$-equivalent to $\pi_D\wedge\sigma_D$ with $\pi_D\in\Pi_1^*$ (the class is closed under conjunction) and $\sigma_D$ a conjunction of true $\Sigma_1$ sentences; $B\vdash\sigma_D$ by ($\Sigma_1$C); so $B\vdash\psi_D\leftrightarrow\pi_D$, $O_{\psi_D}=O_{\pi_D}$ and $M_A(D)=\Bel_0(\pi_D)$. The definition is invariant under $B$-equivalence of literals, as it must be: $M_A$, $W_A$ and $K_A$ depend on $D$ only through $\Cn(D^\pm)$ (Old).

### 3.2 Derandomisation

**Theorem 3.1 (threshold theories; scout N1's A1) [proved; computed: `c3` T1, `rb4` R1].** There is $c$ such that for every $k\ge0$ the set
$$T_k:=\{\pi\in\Pi_1^*:\Unif(O_\pi)>I'_k+2^{-(k+2)}\}$$
(i) is r.e. with an index $e_k$ of complexity $K(e_k)\le k+K(k)+c$; (ii) contains every $\pi\in\Pi_1^*$ with $\Bel_0(\pi)\ge2^{-k}$; (iii) consists of true sentences of positive prior belief, so $B\cup T_k$ is true, consistent and $\Sigma_1$-sound.

*Proof.* (i) $\Unif(O_\pi)$ is left-c.e. uniformly in $\pi$, and $\Pi_1^*$ is decidable, so $T_k$ is r.e. uniformly in $(k,I'_k)$; a program consists of a self-delimiting description of $k$, the $k+2$ bits of $I'_k$, and a fixed code. (ii) $\Unif(O_\pi)=\Inc_0+\Bel_0(\pi)\ge I'_k+2^{-k}>I'_k+2^{-(k+2)}$. (iii) If $\Unif(O_\pi)>I'_k+2^{-(k+2)}>\Inc_0$, then $\Bel_0(\pi)>0$: some tape has $B\cup A_\omega$ consistent and proving $\pi$, and $\pi$ is true by ($\Pi_1$T). ∎

The threshold can also be left random. This gives a cleaner statement, which also drives §4.3.

**Theorem 3.2 (the threshold machine) [proved; computed: `c3` T2, `rb4` R2].** Let $G_{\rm thr}$ be the theory machine that, after reading $s$ tape bits $u$, emits every $\pi\in\Pi_1^*$ for which some lower approximation of $\Unif(O_\pi)$ exceeds $0.u+2^{-s}$ ($0.u$ the dyadic rational with digits $u$). For a tape $r$ that is not a dyadic rational (read as a real),
$$A^{G_{\rm thr}}_r=\{\pi\in\Pi_1^*:\Unif(O_\pi)>r\};$$
it is inconsistent if $r<\Inc_0$ and true if $r>\Inc_0$. For every data set $D$ of $\Pi_1$ type,
$$M_A(D)\ \le\ \nu_{G_{\rm thr}}(D)\ \le\ 2^{K(G_{\rm thr})+c}M_A(D).$$

*Proof.* $\pi$ is emitted iff $\Unif(O_\pi)>0.r_{1:s}+2^{-s}$ for some $s$; as $0.r_{1:s}+2^{-s}$ decreases strictly to $r$ (non-dyadic $r$), this is iff $\Unif(O_\pi)>r$. If $r<\Inc_0$, every $\pi\in\Pi_1^*$ has $\Unif(O_\pi)\ge\Inc_0>r$, including false ones such as $\neg(0=0)$. If $r>\Inc_0$, every emitted $\pi$ has $\Bel_0(\pi)>0$ and is true by ($\Pi_1$T); $B$ is true; so $B\cup A^{G_{\rm thr}}_r$ is true. For $D$ of $\Pi_1$ type: if $\Inc_0<r<\Unif(O_{\pi_D})$, the theory is consistent and contains $\pi_D$, and $B\vdash\psi_D\leftrightarrow\pi_D$; so $\nu_{G_{\rm thr}}(D)\ge\Unif(O_{\pi_D})-\Inc_0=\Bel_0(\pi_D)=M_A(D)$ (the dyadic tapes are a null set). The upper bound is (Dom), with $K(\nu_{G_{\rm thr}})\le K(G_{\rm thr})+c$. ∎

*Reading.* On data of $\Pi_1$ type the universal random theory can be replaced, up to a constant factor, by **one** random theory: "every $\Pi_1^*$ sentence whose prior probability of being proved exceeds a uniformly random level". It is true whenever it is consistent, and its theories form a decreasing chain as the level rises. Theorem 3.1 is the special case in which $k+2$ bits of the level are fixed just above $\Inc_0$. The random tape helps on $\Pi_1$ data only by choosing the level, and choosing the level costs only what the probability of the datum already charges.

### 3.3 Corollaries

**Corollary 3.3 (a coding theorem for $\Pi_1$ data; scout N1's A2) [proved].** For every $D$ of $\Pi_1$ type,
$$L_A(D)\le-\log W_A(D)+c\le K_A(D)+c\le L_A(D)+K(\lceil L_A(D)\rceil)+c' .$$

*Proof.* The first two inequalities are (Chain). For the last, let $k:=\lceil L_A(D)\rceil$, so $\Bel_0(\pi_D)=M_A(D)\ge2^{-k}$; by Thm 3.1, $\pi_D\in T_k$, and $B\cup T_k$ is consistent and proves $\pi_D$, hence $\psi_D$. So $K_A(D)\le K(e_k)+c\le k+K(k)+c$. ∎

**Remark 3.4 (the coin-free version sums over levels) [proved; computed: `c6` R1, `rb4` R4].** For $D$ of $\Pi_1$ type, $-\log W_A(D)\le L_A(D)+\Kpad(\lceil L_A(D)\rceil+1)+c$.

*Proof (repaired in the revision).* For a pair $(j,L)$ with $L\ge0$ and $0\le j\le2^L$ let $Z_{j,L}:=\{\pi\in\Pi_1^*:\Unif(O_\pi)>j2^{-L}\}$. It is r.e. with an index $e(j,L)$ computable from the **pair** and injective in the pair (write the pair into the index), and $K(e(j,L))\le K(j,L)+c\le L+K(L)+c'$, since $j$ has $L+1$ bits given $L$. If $j2^{-L}\in(\Inc_0,\Unif(O_{\pi_D}))$, then every member of $Z_{j,L}$ has $\Bel_0>0$ and is true by ($\Pi_1$T), so $B\cup Z_{j,L}$ is true, and it contains $\pi_D$ and proves $\psi_D$. The indices $e(j,L)$ are distinct for distinct pairs, so

**Corollary 3.5 (bounded loss on a $\Pi_1$ stream is explained by one true theory; scout N1's A3) [proved].** If $D_\infty$ has all its finite parts of $\Pi_1$ type and $\sup_nL_A(D_n)\le k$, then $B\cup T_k$ is true, proves every datum, and has an index of complexity $\le k+K(k)+c$; so $\sup_n-\log W_A(D_n)\le k+K(k)+c$ and scout F's Q1 holds on such streams. *Proof.* Thm 3.1(ii) for each $\pi_{D_n}$; $T_k$ does not depend on $n$. ∎

**Corollary 3.6 (Chaitin's theorem for random theories; scout N1's A4) [proved].** Code strings by length-lexicographic rank (§1.2), so that the strings of length $\le m$ are the codes $\le\mathrm{rk}_m=2^{m+1}-2$. Let $\mathrm T(p,t,y)$ be Kleene's $\Delta_0$ predicate (§1.2 (Uniq): each halting computation has a unique code $t$), and
$$\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x):=\forall t\,\forall p\,\big(p\le\underline{\mathrm{rk}_m}\to\neg\mathrm T(p,t,\underline x)\big),$$
a $\Pi_1$ sentence, true iff $K(x)>m$. Then for every $m$ and $x$, **in the semimeasure convention**:
* (a) if $K(x)\le m$, $L_A(\{(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x),1)\})=\infty$; if $K(x)>m$, $L_A(\{(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x),1)\})\ge m-K(m)-K(\lceil L_A\rceil)-c\ge m-2\log m-O(\log\log m)$;
* (b) if $K(x)>m$, $L_A(\{(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x),1)\})\le K_A(\{(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x),1)\})+c\le m+K(m)+c'$, and one true theory $B\cup\{\beta_m\}$ of complexity $\le m+K(m)+c$ proves $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x)$ for every such $x$ at once.

The same bounds hold for the three-valued cost of the label $1$ on this single datum, which is $-\log\Bel_\emptyset(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x))=L_A(\{(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x),1)\})+\log(1-\Inc_0)$.

*Proof.* *(Chaitin, deterministic.)* If $B\cup\mathsf W_e$ is consistent and proves $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x)$, the program "given $e$ and $m$, enumerate the theorems of $B\cup\mathsf W_e$ and output the first $z$ with $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(z)$ among them" halts with some $z$, $K(z)\le K(e)+K(m)+c$; $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(z)$ is true by ($\Pi_1$T), so $K(z)>m$, whence $K(e)>m-K(m)-c$. So $K_A\ge m-K(m)-c$.
*(b)* Let $s_m$ be the largest code of a halting computation of $V$ on a program of length $\le m$ ($0$ if none); it exists because there are finitely many such programs and each halting computation has one code (Uniq). Let $\beta_m:=\forall t\,\forall p\,\forall y\,(p\le\underline{\mathrm{rk}_m}\wedge\mathrm T(p,t,y)\to t\le\underline{s_m})$, a true $\Pi_1$ sentence. For $x$ with $K(x)>m$, the $\Delta_0$ sentence $\forall p\le\underline{\mathrm{rk}_m}\,\forall t\le\underline{s_m}\,\neg\mathrm T(p,t,\underline x)$ is true, hence $\Q$-provable, and with $\beta_m$ it gives $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x)$ by pure logic. $s_m$ is computable from $m$ and the number $h_m\le2^m$ of halting programs of length $\le m$ (Kraft), an $(m+1)$-bit number: run them until $h_m$ have halted. So $K(\beta_m)\le K(m)+m+c$. (Without unique codes, a halting program could have infinitely many codes $t$ and $\beta_m$ would be false; referee B, issue 9.)
*(a)* For $K(x)\le m$ the datum is a false $\Pi_1$ sentence and $M_A=0$ by ($\Pi_1$T). Otherwise Cor 3.3 and the deterministic bound give $L_A\ge K_A-K(\lceil L_A\rceil)-c'\ge m-K(m)-K(\lceil L_A\rceil)-c''$; by (b), $\lceil L_A\rceil\le m+K(m)+c$, so $K(\lceil L_A\rceil)\le\log m+O(\log\log m)$, and $K(m)\le\log m+O(\log\log m)$. The three-valued statement is the identity $M_A^3(\{(\varphi,1)\})=M_A(\{(\varphi,1)\})$ for a single datum, divided by $M_A^3(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0$. ∎

**Proposition 3.11 (the normalised convention: deciding, not predicting; referee B, issue 1) [proved, given (Uniq); computed: bookkeeping in `c6` R3].** Let $B$ be decidable and true with $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B)$. There is $c$ such that for all $m$, $n$ and $j\ge0$, all strings $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ except at most $2^{2m+K(m)+j+c}+2^{m+1}$ of them satisfy $K(x)>m$ and
$$\frac{\Dis_\emptyset(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x))}{\Bel_\emptyset(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x))}\le2^{-j},\qquad\text{so the normalised loss of }(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x),1)\text{ is}\le\log(1+2^{-j})\le1.45\cdot2^{-j}.$$

*Proof.* *Step 1: few refutable strings per theory.* Let $N:=2^{m+1}$ and let $x_1,\dots,x_N$ be distinct strings. By (O), $\Q\vdash p\le\underline{\mathrm{rk}_m}\to\bigvee_{q\le\mathrm{rk}_m}p=\underline q$, so $\neg\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x_i)$ is $\Q$-equivalent to $\bigvee_{q\le\mathrm{rk}_m}\exists t\,\mathrm T(\underline q,t,\underline{x_i})$. Hence $\bigwedge_{i\le N}\neg\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x_i)$ is $\Q$-equivalent to the disjunction, over all maps $f:\{1,\dots,N\}\to\{0,\dots,\mathrm{rk}_m\}$, of $\bigwedge_i\exists t\,\mathrm T(\underline{f(i)},t,\underline{x_i})$. There are $\mathrm{rk}_m+1=2^{m+1}-1<N$ programs, so every $f$ has $f(i)=f(i')$ for some $i\ne i'$, and then (Uniq) gives $x_i=x_{i'}$, which $\Q$ refutes. So $\PA\vdash\neg\bigwedge_{i\le N}\neg\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x_i)$: every consistent extension of $B$ refutes $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x)$ for at most $2^{m+1}-1$ strings $x$. Integrating over the consistent tapes (Tonelli), $\sum_x\Bel_0(\neg\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x))\le2^{m+1}$.
*Step 2: a lower bound on belief.* For $K(x)>m$, Cor 3.6(b) and (Dom) give $\Bel_0(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x))\ge2^{-m-K(m)-c}$.
*Step 3: Markov's inequality.* By Step 1, fewer than $2^{m+1}\cdot2^{j+m+K(m)+c}$ strings have $\Bel_0(\neg\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x))>2^{-j}2^{-m-K(m)-c}$, and fewer than $2^{m+1}$ strings have $K(x)\le m$. For every other $x$, Step 2 gives $\Bel_0(\neg\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x))\le2^{-j}\Bel_0(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x))$; $\Dis_\emptyset$ and $\Bel_\emptyset$ are $\Bel_0(\neg\cdot)$ and $\Bel_0(\cdot)$ divided by the same $1-\Inc_0$. The normalised probability of the label $1$ is $\Bel_\emptyset/(\Bel_\emptyset+\Dis_\emptyset)\ge1/(1+2^{-j})$. ∎

So for $n\ge2m+K(m)+j+c'$, almost every $x$ of length $n$ costs essentially nothing in the normalised convention, while every $x$ with $K(x)>m$ costs $m\pm O(\log m)$ bits in the semimeasure and three-valued conventions (Cor 3.6). Consistent SOI behaves the same way: `sfi` Thm 3.1 transfers $M_A(\{(\varphi,b)\})$ to $M_F^{\rm cons}(\{(\varphi,b)\})$ up to factors $2^{\pm c}$, which leaves the semimeasure statement unchanged and shifts $j$ by a constant in the normalised one. With $B=\Q$ alone Step 1 needs $\Q$ to prove the uniqueness of outputs, which it may not; the normalised behaviour is then **[open]**.

*Reading (rewritten in the revision).* The datum $(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x),1)$ carries at most one bit of label information: unconstrained SOI predicts it at cost $O(1)$ ("always true"), and the general lower bound of `fundamentals` Prop 5.6 is $O(1)$ here. In the semimeasure convention SAI, and consistent SOI with it, pays $m\pm O(\log m)$ bits for it, with random or deterministic hypotheses alike; in the three-valued convention the same holds, because the label "true" gets probability $\Bel_\emptyset\le2^{-m+O(\log m)}$ (for most $x$ the inducer predicts "independent" with probability $1-2^{-m+O(\log m)}$, since $\sum_x\Dis_\emptyset(\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x))\le2^{m+1}/(1-\Inc_0)$ by Step 1 of Prop 3.11). In the normalised convention, with $B\supseteq\PA$, the cost is near $0$ for all but a $2^{2m+K(m)+O(1)-n}$ fraction of the $x$ of length $n$, because few consistent theories refute $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x)$ (Prop 3.11). **The $m$ bits are the price of *deciding* the sentence, not of predicting its truth value**: every $\Pi_1$ affirmation must be backed by a true theory, and a true theory that proves high complexity lower bounds contains the corresponding halting information. Paying once pays for all such data at level $m$: one $m$-bit $\Pi_1$ law $\beta_m$ explains every true lower bound at level $m$, a clean instance of "laws versus data". The statement is about the inducer's loss; it does not say that a theory of $k$ bits "contains at most $k$ bits" (scout L's caution, after Raatikainen 1998): the deterministic step is exactly Chaitin's upper bound $K(e)>m-K(m)-c$, which Raatikainen does not dispute (§7).

**Proposition 3.7 (scout F's Q2 for true $\Pi_1^*$-axiomatised theories; scout N1's A6) [proved].** Let $T$ be an r.e. set of true $\Pi_1^*$ sentences and $k:=\lceil-\log\Unif_A(\Ext T)\rceil$. Then
$$-\log\Unif_A(\Ext T)-c\le-\log\mm(\Ext T)\le-\log\Unif_A(\Ext T)+K(k)+c.$$

*Proof.* Right: each $\pi\in T$ has $\Bel_0(\pi)\ge\Unif_A(\Ext T)\ge2^{-k}$ (a consistent theory extending $\Cn(T)$ proves $\pi$), so $T\subseteq T_k$; $B\cup T_k$ is consistent, so $e_k\in\Ext(T)$ and $\mm(\Ext T)\ge\mm(e_k)\ge2^{-k-K(k)-c}$. Left: $\Unif_A(\Ext T)\ge2^{-c}\mm(\Ext T)$ (`fundamentals` Def 1.9, for consistent $B\cup T$). ∎ Q2 for the exact class $[T]$, and for general $T$, stays open (`fundamentals` Prop 11.5 reduces it to probabilistic against deterministic enumeration, with the factor-2 chain of `fundamentals` Rem 6.5 at abstract level).

### 3.4 Relation to the literature (novelty; rewritten in the revision)

* **Bienvenu, Romashchenko, Shen, Taveneaux, Vermeeren (2014)** add to $\PA$ axioms "$C(x)\ge n-c$" for randomly chosen strings $x$. Their abstract says that such random axioms do not help prove new interesting theorems, can shorten proofs (unless NP = PSPACE), that adding all true statements of this type proves every true $\Pi_1$ sentence, and that one carefully chosen statement per $n$ suffices [known: abstract and search snippets; full text not read, §11]. Scout L and the development round also report, from snippets, probabilistic proof strategies with an initial error "capital" and a soundness corollary (a statement proved with probability above the capital is true); that corollary already contains the **threshold idea**: the statements proved with probability above a known error level form an r.e. set of true statements. Shen (2011) is the preliminary note ("Are random axioms useful?").
* **What is plausibly new here** (to be checked against their §2 and Shen's Prop 2 once the full texts can be read): (1) the threshold placed just above an *unknown* error level, the inconsistency probability $\Inc_0$ of the universal random theory, and paid for by its $k+2$ bits (Thm 3.1); (2) the universal mixture over all theory machines, inconsistent ones included, as the random object, rather than a fixed scheme of complexity axioms; (3) the single random threshold theory $G_{\rm thr}$ (Thm 3.2), and with it the uniform soundness bound of Thm 4.4; (4) the coding theorem as a statement about an inductive inference rule (Cor 3.3), against its failure on literals (`fundamentals` Thm 6.2) and on Boolean combinations of $\Sigma_1$ sentences (§3.5).
* **What is known in substance.** The threshold device itself is the soundness argument of Bienvenu et al. (as reported) and the averaging argument of de Leeuw et al. (1956). The truth-certificate principle (a $\Pi_1$ sentence proved by a consistent extension of $\Q$ is true) is standard. The theory $\beta_m$ of Cor 3.6(b) ("the number of halting programs of length $\le m$") is the classical way to prove all true complexity bounds at level $m$, close to Bienvenu et al.'s "one carefully chosen statement per $n$"; Cor 3.6's deterministic step is Chaitin (1974).
* **Levin (2013)** ("Forbidden information") bounds the probability that a randomised algorithm produces a consistent completion of $\PA$ on formulas of size $n$ by $2^{-n}$ (up to factors) [known: abstract and search snippets]. It is a quantitative relative of `fundamentals` Prop 8.7 (random theories above $\Q$ are almost surely incomplete), which now cites it.

### 3.5 Where the coding theorem fails (extensions A5, A-Q1)

**Proposition 3.8 (one false $\Sigma_1$ datum; scout N1's A5) [proved, given Lemma 2.8; computed: constants `rb0` K3].** Let $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$, $B$ decidable, true, $\supseteq\Q$, and $\Phi_0$ the formula of Theorem 2.9(a) (false of every number, $\Q$-equivalent to a $\Sigma_1$ formula, every finite pattern of its instances consistent with $B$). For every $d\ge2$ there are distinct $x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}^d$ such that $D:=\{(\Phi_0(\underline{x_1}),0),(\Phi_0(\underline{x_2}),1)\}$ (strings coded by length-lexicographic rank) has $K_A(D)>\log d-2$ and $L_A(D)\le2+c$. The literal $\neg\Phi_0(\underline{x_1})$ is $\Q$-equivalent to a true $\Pi_1^*$ sentence; $\Phi_0(\underline{x_2})$ is $\Q$-equivalent to a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence.

*Proof.* *Upper bound.* The machine that, for every $n$, reads a fresh bit $b_n$ and emits $\Phi_0(\underline n)^{b_n}$ has a consistent theory on every tape (Lemma 2.8(i) and compactness) and proves $D$ with probability $\frac14$; apply (Dom). *Lower bound (colouring, as in `fundamentals` Thm 6.2).* Let $\ell:=\lceil\log(d/\log3)\rceil-1$, so $2^\ell<d/\log3$ and $3^{2^\ell-1}<2^d$. Fewer than $2^\ell$ programs have length $<\ell$. Colour each $x\in\{0,1\}^d$ by the vector, over these programs $p$, of "$+$" if $V(p)$ halts with an index $e$ such that $B\cup\mathsf W_e$ is consistent and proves $\Phi_0(\underline x)$, "$-$" if consistent and proves $\neg\Phi_0(\underline x)$, "$*$" otherwise. Two distinct $x_1,x_2$ share a colour; no program shorter than $\ell$ gives a consistent theory proving $\neg\Phi_0(\underline{x_1})\wedge\Phi_0(\underline{x_2})$. So $K_A(D)\ge\ell\ge\log d-\log\log3-1>\log d-2$. ∎

**Proposition 3.9 (two true data; A-Q1) [proved, given Lemma 2.8].** Under the same hypotheses there is a formula $\Phi_2$ such that for every $d\ge2$ some distinct $x_1,x_2\in\{0,1\}^d$ give **true** data $D_d:=\{(\Phi_2(\underline{x_1}),0),(\Phi_2(\underline{x_2}),1)\}$ with $K_A(D_d)>\log d-2$ and $L_A(D_d)\le2+c$. Each datum is $\Q$-equivalent to a Boolean combination of $\Sigma_1$ sentences (hence, by prenexing, both to a $\Sigma_2$ and to a $\Pi_2$ sentence).

*Proof.* *The colour formulas* (made explicit in the revision; referee A, m8). For a program $p$ (a numeral), put
* $H_p:\equiv\exists t\,\exists e\,\mathrm T(\underline p,t,e)$ ($\Sigma_1$: $V(p)$ halts);
* $\mathrm{Cons}_p:\equiv\forall t\,\forall e\,\big(\mathrm T(\underline p,t,e)\to\Con(B\cup\mathsf W_e)\big)$ ($\Pi_1$, with $\Con(B\cup\mathsf W_e)$ the canonical $\Pi_1$ formula in $e$);
* $\mathrm{Pr}^{b}_p(z,y):\equiv\exists t\,\exists e\,\big(\mathrm T(\underline p,t,e)\wedge\mathrm{Prov}_{B\cup\mathsf W_e}(\ulcorner\varphi_z(\dot y)^b\urcorner)\big)$ ($\Sigma_1$), for $b\in\{0,1\}$.

The colour entry of $y$ at $p$ is "$+$" iff $H_p\wedge\mathrm{Cons}_p\wedge\mathrm{Pr}^1_p(z,y)$, "$-$" iff $H_p\wedge\mathrm{Cons}_p\wedge\mathrm{Pr}^0_p(z,y)$, and "$*$" otherwise. In $\mathbb N$, where $V(p)$ has at most one output, these say exactly what the colouring of Prop 3.8 says (with $\varphi_z$ for $\Phi_0$); writing the consistency condition as "$\exists e\,(\mathrm T(p,t,e)\wedge\Con(\dots))$" would be $\Sigma_2$, which is why the universally quantified form is used. Let $\Psi(z,x)$ say: "$x$ is the code of a string of some length $d\ge2$, and $x$ is the second element of the lexicographically first pair of distinct strings of length $d$ that have the same colour" (colours over the programs of length $<\ell_d$), with all quantifiers outside the colour formulas bounded by $x$. Let $\Phi_2$ be Lemma 2.8's formula for $\Psi$ and $(x_1,x_2)$ that pair for $z=\ulcorner\Phi_2\urcorner$ (it exists by the pigeonhole count of Prop 3.8). By Lemma 2.8(ii), $\Phi_2(\underline{x_2})$ is true and $\Phi_2(\underline{x_1})$ false; the fair-coin machine of Prop 3.8 (with $\Phi_2$) gives $L_A(D_d)\le2+c$ by Lemma 2.8(i); the pair has the same colour for the actual $\Phi_2$, which gives $K_A(D_d)\ge\ell_d>\log d-2$ as in Prop 3.8. For a numeral argument, (O) and ($\Sigma_1$C) unfold the bounded quantifiers of $\Psi(\ulcorner\Phi_2\urcorner,\underline x)$ and decide its $\Delta_0$ parts in $\Q$, leaving a Boolean combination of the $\Sigma_1$ sentences $H_p$, $\mathrm{Pr}^b_p$ and the $\Pi_1$ sentences $\mathrm{Cons}_p$; with Lemma 2.8(iii) each datum is $\Q$-equivalent to a Boolean combination of $\Sigma_1$ sentences. ∎

**Answer to A-Q1.** No: true data do not admit a coding theorem beyond $\Sigma_1\cup\Pi_1$. Already two true data that are $\Q$-equivalent to Boolean combinations of $\Sigma_1$ sentences give the $\log d$ gap (Prop 3.9), and a stream of such data gives bounded one-tape loss with $K_A\to\infty$ (Cor 2.10). The boundary is exact at the first level: data of $\Pi_1$ type admit the coding theorem (Cor 3.3); Boolean combinations of $\Sigma_1$ sentences do not.

## 4. Gödel phenomena (target T3)

### 4.1 The arithmetical shape of the trichotomy

**Proposition 4.1 (scout N1's D1) [proved; computed: `c4`; `rb5` A].** Let $B$ be decidable and consistent with $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$, and $D$ consistent.
* (a) If $\sigma$ is a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence, $\Bel_D(\sigma)=1$.
* (b) If $\pi\in\Pi_1^*$ is false, $(\Bel_D,\Dis_D,\Indep_D)(\pi)=(0,1,0)$. Equivalently: $\Bel_D(\pi)>0$ or $\Indep_D(\pi)>0$ implies that $\pi$ is true.
* (c) If $B\cup D^\pm$ is true (has a model whose $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-reduct is $\mathbb N$), $\pi\in\Pi_1^*$ is true and $B\cup D^\pm\nvdash\pi$, then $\Bel_D(\pi),\Dis_D(\pi),\Indep_D(\pi)$ are all positive.
* (d) If $B\cup D^\pm$ is true, $\sigma$ is a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence and $B\cup D^\pm\nvdash\neg\sigma$, all three values of $\sigma$ are positive.

*Proof.* (a) The posterior lives on consistent extensions of $\Q$, all of which prove $\sigma$. (b) Some conjunct of $\pi$ is false, its negation is a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence, so every consistent extension of $\Q$ proves $\neg\pi$. (c) The r.e. theories $D^\pm\cup\{\pi\}$ (true in the model of $B\cup D^\pm$ with $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-reduct $\mathbb N$, so consistent with $B$; proves $\pi$), $D^\pm\cup\{\neg\pi\}$ (consistent with $B$ as $B\cup D^\pm\nvdash\pi$, by the deduction theorem for the finite set $D^\pm$; proves $\neg\pi$) and $D^\pm$ (proves neither: not $\pi$ by hypothesis, not $\neg\pi$ because $B\cup D^\pm$ is true and $\neg\pi$ false) all prove $\psi_D$ and have positive weight (Dom). (d) Apply (c) to the $\Pi_1^*$ sentence $\neg\sigma$. ∎

*Reading.* The inducer errs one-sidedly, in opposite directions on the two classes:

| class | positive "true" on a false sentence? | positive "false" on a true sentence? | what positive "independent" means |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\Sigma_1$ | possible ($\Sigma_1$-unsound theories) | **never** | the sentence is false |
| $\Pi_1$ ($\Con(T)$, Gödel sentences, $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x)$, Goldbach) | **never** | possible | the sentence is true |

So "independent" on a $\Pi_1$ question certifies truth: inside the inducer this is the content of "the Gödel sentence is true because it is unprovable". It also shows what the normalised convention throws away: $\Bel/(\Bel+\Dis)$ is $0$ on false $\Pi_1$ sentences, but it discards the $\Indep$ share, which only true $\Pi_1$ sentences carry (Prop 3.11 is a quantitative instance). *Extreme case [proved].* If the data present $T:=\PA+\neg\Con(\PA)$ (consistent and $\Sigma_1$-unsound), every consistent extension of $T$ proves $\neg\Con(T)$ (as $\PA\vdash\Con(T)\to\Con(\PA)$), so $\Dis(\Con(T))=1$ although $\Con(T)$ is true.

The check `c4` builds a finite model in which the only arithmetical input of Prop 4.1, ($\Sigma_1$C), is imposed (a world $0$ playing $\mathbb N$; true $\Sigma_1$ sentences hold in every world) and confirms (a)–(d) and the one-sided error pattern on 3000 random instances. Referee B's `rb5` A uses a stricter propositional model ($\Sigma_1$ sentences as monotone formulas over $\Sigma_1$ atoms, $\Q$ proving exactly the true atoms): 0 violations in 1616/1607/592/577 applicable instances of (a)–(d); the four impossible events never occur and the four possible ones occur over 1000 times each.

### 4.2 Soundness is never learned (coin-free), and a reduction

**Theorem 4.2 (coin-free; scout N1's D2) [proved, given (G2)].** Let $B$ be decidable and consistent with $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B)$. There is $c$ such that for every consistent $D$: $W_A(D;\text{$\Sigma_1$-unsound})\ge2^{-c}W_A(D;\text{$\Sigma_1$-sound})$, where $W_A(D;X):=\sum_{e\in X}\mm(e)\ind[B\cup\mathsf W_e\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D]$. Hence the coin-free posterior probability of $\Sigma_1$-soundness, and a fortiori of truth, is at most $1/(1+2^{-c})$ for every $D$, in particular for every finite part of true arithmetic; and in the Kolmogorov version the shortest compatible unsound theory is at most $c$ bits longer than the shortest compatible sound one.

*Proof.* By s-m-n there is an injective computable $g$ with $\mathsf W_{g(e)}=\mathsf W_e\cup\{\neg\Con(B\cup\mathsf W_e)\}$ (canonical consistency statement of the presentation $e$). If $B\cup\mathsf W_e$ is consistent, (G2) gives $B\cup\mathsf W_e\nvdash\Con(B\cup\mathsf W_e)$, so $B\cup\mathsf W_{g(e)}$ is consistent; its new axiom is a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence; it proves what $\mathsf W_e$ proves. As $\mm(g(e))\ge2^{-c}\mm(e)$, summing over the sound compatible $e$ (whose images are distinct) gives the claim. ∎

For a set $X$ of theories write $M_A(D;X):=\Unif(E_D\cap\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\in X\})$ and $P_{M_A}(X\mid D):=M_A(D;X)/M_A(D)$. The event "$B\cup A_\omega$ is $\Sigma_1$-unsound" is $\bigcup_{\sigma}O_\sigma$ over false $\Sigma_1$ sentences $\sigma$, an open set, so these quantities are defined.

**Proposition 4.3 (one-tape soundness is controlled by the coding gap) [proved, given (G2)].** Under the hypotheses of Thm 4.2, for every consistent $D$:
$$P_{M_A}(\text{$\Sigma_1$-unsound}\mid D)\ \ge\ 2^{L_A(D)-K_A(D)-c}.$$
*Proof.* Let $e$ witness $K_A(D)$. Its companion $g(e)$ is consistent, unsound, proves $\psi_D$, and $K(g(e))\le K(e)+c$; by (Dom) its tapes have measure $\ge2^{-K_A(D)-c}$; divide by $M_A(D)$. ∎

So one-tape SAI can become confident of soundness only on data where the coding theorem fails badly, which is where randomness dominates (Table 3.10).

### 4.3 One-tape soundness on $\Pi_1$ data: a uniform bound

Scout N1 proved $P_{M_A}(\text{unsound}\mid D)\ge2^{-K(\lceil L_A(D)\rceil)-c}$ for $D$ of $\Pi_1$ type (its D2′; it also follows from Prop 4.3 and Cor 3.3). That bound degrades as the loss grows. The threshold machine removes the degradation.

**Theorem 4.4 (soundness is never learned from $\Pi_1$ data) [proved, given (G2); computed: the dyadic and prefix facts in `c3` T3, `c6` R2 and `rb4` R3].** Let $B$ be decidable and true with $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B)$. There is $c$ such that for every $D$ of $\Pi_1$ type with $M_A(D)>0$:
$$M_A(D;\text{$\Sigma_1$-unsound})\ \ge\ 2^{-c}M_A(D),\qquad\text{so}\qquad P_{M_A}(\text{$\Sigma_1$-sound}\mid D)\le1-2^{-c}.$$

*Proof.* For a finite string $w$ let $U_w$ be the set of sentences that $G_{\rm thr}$ (Thm 3.2) emits on some tape extending $w$; it is r.e. uniformly in $w$, and $U_w=\{\pi\in\Pi_1^*:\Unif(O_\pi)>0.w\}$: the emissions under prefixes $v\sqsupseteq w$ have thresholds $0.v+2^{-|v|}$ with infimum $0.w$, not attained, and those under shorter prefixes $u\sqsubseteq w$ have thresholds $\ge0.w+2^{-|w|}$. Hence $U_w$ is true if $0.w>\Inc_0$ and is all of $\Pi_1^*$, inconsistent, if $0.w<\Inc_0$ (equality is impossible, $\Inc_0$ being irrational); and $U_{w'}\subseteq U_w$ for $w\sqsubseteq w'$, provably in $\PA$ from the definitions (every prefix of $w'$ is comparable with $w$). Let $G^{\rm u}_{\rm thr}$ emit everything $G_{\rm thr}$ emits and, for every prefix $w$ of the tape, $\chi_w:=\neg\Con(B\cup U_w)$ (scout N1's companion construction, applied to $G_{\rm thr}$).

*Consistency.* Let $r>\Inc_0$ be a non-dyadic tape and $F$ a finite subset of $A^{G^{\rm u}_{\rm thr}}_r$, with companion sentences $\chi_{w_1},\dots,\chi_{w_j}$; let $w^*$ be the longest $w_i$. If $0.w^*<\Inc_0$, every $U_{w_i}\supseteq U_{w^*}$ is inconsistent, every $\chi_{w_i}$ is a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence, provable from $B$, and the rest of $F$ lies in the true theory $A^{G_{\rm thr}}_r$. If $0.w^*>\Inc_0$, then $B\cup U_{w^*}$ is consistent, r.e. and contains $\PA$, so $B\cup U_{w^*}\cup\{\chi_{w^*}\}$ is consistent by (G2); it contains $F\cap A^{G_{\rm thr}}_r$ (as $A^{G_{\rm thr}}_r=\{\pi:\Unif(O_\pi)>r\}\subseteq U_{w^*}$, since $r>0.w^*$); and it proves each $\chi_{w_i}$, $w_i\sqsubseteq w^*$, because $U_{w^*}\subseteq U_{w_i}$ provably, so a proof of $\bot$ from $B\cup U_{w^*}$ is one from $B\cup U_{w_i}$.
*Unsoundness.* $r>\Inc_0$ has a prefix $w$ with $0.w>\Inc_0$ (the truncations increase to $r$); for it $\chi_w$ is a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence.
*Measure.* For $\Inc_0<r<\Unif(O_{\pi_D})$ the theory of $G^{\rm u}_{\rm thr}$ is consistent, $\Sigma_1$-unsound, contains $\pi_D$, and $B\vdash\psi_D\leftrightarrow\pi_D$. So $\nu_{G^{\rm u}_{\rm thr}}(D;\text{unsound})\ge M_A(D)$ (as in Thm 3.2), and (Dom), tape by tape, gives $M_A(D;\text{unsound})\ge2^{-K(G^{\rm u}_{\rm thr})-c}M_A(D)$. ∎

### 4.4 The one-tape question in general (D-Q1)

**Open (D-Q1).** Is $P_{M_A}(\text{$\Sigma_1$-sound}\mid D)$ bounded away from $1$ uniformly over all data $D$ (with $B$ true, $\supseteq\PA$)?

What is proved:
* data of $\Pi_1$ type: yes, uniformly (Thm 4.4);
* data with a bounded coding gap $K_A-L_A$: yes (Prop 4.3);
* on the $\mathrm{Win}$-lottery stream with $B$ true $\supseteq\PA$ and $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}\cup\{\mathrm{Win}\}$: yes [proved]. The machine "$G_{\rm lot}$ plus the axiom $\neg\Con(B)$" has the same survival event; its theories are consistent (a model of $B+\neg\Con(B)$ exists by (G2), has distinct numerals as every model of $\Q$ does, and is expanded by the winners) and $\Sigma_1$-unsound; so $P_{M_A}(\text{unsound}\mid D_n)\ge2^{-c}\cdot0.2887$ for every $n$;
* on the arithmetic lotteries (Thm 2.9): the random lottery theories are themselves unsound (Cor 2.10(iii) for (b); for (a) they affirm the false $\Sigma_1$ sentences $\mathrm{Tick}_{k,J_k}$);
* **(stated exactly in the revision; referee A, m5)** for every theory machine $G$ [proved; scout N1's companion, re-verified]: with $U^G_w$ the set of sentences $G$ emits on some tape extending $w$ and $\mathcal C^G:=\{\omega:B\cup U^G_w\text{ is consistent for some prefix }w\text{ of }\omega\}$,
$$M_A(D;\text{$\Sigma_1$-unsound})\ \ge\ 2^{-K(G)-c}\,\nu_G(D;\mathcal C^G)\quad\text{for every }D,$$
where $\nu_G(D;\mathcal C^G):=\Unif(\{\omega:B\cup A^G_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D\}\cap\mathcal C^G)$. *Proof.* The companion $G^{\rm u}$ emits what $G$ emits and $\chi_w:=\neg\Con(B\cup U^G_w)$ for every prefix $w$ of the tape; $K(G^{\rm u})\le K(G)+c$. On a tape $\omega$ where $B\cup A^G_\omega$ is consistent, the longest-prefix argument of Thm 4.4 shows that $B\cup A^{G^{\rm u}}_\omega$ is consistent ($A^G_\omega\subseteq U^G_{w^*}$ because $w^*$ is a prefix of $\omega$), and on $\mathcal C^G$ some $\chi_w$ is a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence. Apply (Dom) tape by tape. ∎ This answers D-Q1 for no class of data by itself: it bounds the unsound mass by the part of each machine's compatible mass that lies on tapes with a consistent continuation set. It fails for coin machines, all of whose sets $U^G_w$ are inconsistent.

Why the general case is hard: a companion must add a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence that the random theory $A_\omega$ does not refute, chosen from finitely much of the tape. $\neg\Con(A_\omega)$ is not a sentence, and $A_\omega$ may prove $\Con(Z)$ for every r.e. $Z$ read off a finite prefix (a machine can emit, at each stage, the consistency of everything it has emitted so far). Every attempt made here either needed a consistent $U_w$ (fails for coin machines) or a fixed false $\Sigma_1$ sentence that the random theory leaves open (fails for reflective machines).

### 4.5 $\Con(B\cup T)$ and Gödel sentences given data from $T$

**Proposition 4.5 (scout N1's D3; hypothesis corrected in the revision) [proved, given (G2); computed: the construction of (c), `rb5` B].** Let $T$ be an r.e. theory such that **$B\cup T$ is true** (has a model whose $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-reduct is $\mathbb N$) and $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B\cup T)$; let the data present $T$ ($\Cn(D_\infty^\pm)=\Cn(B\cup T)$), and let $\gamma_T$ be $\Con(B\cup T)$ (or the Gödel sentence of $B\cup T$, $\PA$-provably equivalent to it [known: Smoryński 1977]).
* (a) $\lim\Bel(\gamma_T)$, $\lim\Dis(\gamma_T)$, $\lim\Indep(\gamma_T)$ exist and all lie in $(0,1)$.
* (b) The $\Bel$ and $\Indep$ shares are truth certificates for $\gamma_T$ in the sense of Prop 4.1(b).
* (c) Which of "true" and "false" gets more mass is a choice of universal machine: for every universal prefix machine $V$ and every $j$ there are universal $V'$, $V''$ with $\lim\Bel(\gamma_T)/\lim\Dis(\gamma_T)\ge2^j-1$ under $V'$ and $\le1/(2^j-1)$ under $V''$.

*Proof.* (a) By (Lim) (`fundamentals` Prop 8.1(d)) the limit posterior is the prior restricted to the consistent extensions of $B\cup T$, which is incomplete by (G2). Three deterministic theories have positive weight: $T+\gamma_T$, which is true because $B\cup T$ is true, hence consistent, and $\gamma_T$ is a true $\Pi_1$ sentence; $T+\neg\gamma_T$, consistent with $B$ by (G2); and $T$, which decides neither (not $\gamma_T$ by (G2); not $\neg\gamma_T$ because $B\cup T$ is true and $\neg\gamma_T$ is false). (b) is Prop 4.1(b) applied to $\gamma_T\in\Pi_1$. (c) Let $V'(0^i1):=$ an index of the deterministic machine for $T+\gamma_T$ for $i<j$ and $V'(0^jp):=V(p)$; $V'$ is a universal prefix machine. The tapes beginning with some $0^i1$, $i<j$, have measure $1-2^{-j}$ and give a compatible theory proving $\gamma_T$; all tapes on which $\neg\gamma_T$ is proved begin with $0^j$. So the masses of the two events (proving $\gamma_T$, proving $\neg\gamma_T$, within $E_\infty$) are $\ge1-2^{-j}$ and $\le2^{-j}$; dividing both by the common normaliser $\Unif(E_\infty)$ gives the ratio. $V''$ is symmetric with $T+\neg\gamma_T$. ∎

*The hypothesis is needed* (referee A, M1). With $B:=\PA+\neg\Con(\PA)$ (decidable, consistent by (G2)), $T:=\emptyset$ (true) and $\gamma_T=\Con(B)$: $\PA\vdash\Con(B)\to\Con(\PA)$, so $B\vdash\neg\gamma_T$, every compatible theory refutes $\gamma_T$, and $\lim\Dis(\gamma_T)=1$; (a) and (c) fail. The development round's statement, which assumed only "$T$ true", is kept in §12 as refuted.

*Answer to the brief's question.* Given data from $T$ with $B\cup T$ true, the posterior on $\Con(B\cup T)$ or the Gödel sentence of $B\cup T$ keeps positive shares on all three answers forever; no data from $T$ move them in the limit; which of "true" and "false" is favoured is fixed by the choice of universal machine. Machine-independent: all three are positive; any "true" or "independent" mass certifies truth; and "the theory is sound" never exceeds $1/(1+2^{-c})$ coin-free (Thm 4.2), nor $1-2^{-c}$ one-tape on $\Pi_1$ data (Thm 4.4). If $B\cup T$ is consistent but false, the answer can be "false" with certainty (the extreme case after Prop 4.1).

### 4.6 Old evidence, and induction in mathematics

**Proposition 4.6 (scout N1's D4) [proved].** If $B\vdash\varphi^b$ for every $(\varphi,b)$ in $D$, then $M_A(D)=M_A(\emptyset)$ and the posterior equals the prior; the same holds for $W_A$ and $K_A$. In particular, with $\Q\subseteq\Cn(B)$ and a $\Delta_0$ formula $P(x)$, the data $\{(P(\underline n),1):n\le N\}$ (true, else they are inconsistent with $B$) leave $\Bel(\forall x\,P(x))$ at its prior value, for every $N$. *Proof.* (Old): $\Cn(D^\pm)=\Cn(\emptyset)$. ∎

*What this says about Hänni's working note on induction in mathematics.* The note asks whether checking $P(n)$ for all small $n$ licenses $\forall n\,P(n)$, or at least $P(n+1)$. SAI with free deduction says no, for a reason unrelated to the note's adversarial worries: the checks are entailed by $\Q$, so they are already "known" and carry no information. This is the problem of old evidence and logical omniscience (Garber 1983; Hacking 1967). Instances of a *fresh* predicate (empirical induction, $B=\emptyset$) do confirm, up to the ω-gap (§5). So the inference the note asks about needs a reasoner for whom computing $P(n)$ is costly: logical induction (Garrabrant et al. 2016) is one; a derivation-charged axiom inducer (AI-paper §6) is another. Whether a derivation-charged SAI posterior then concentrates on provers of $\forall x\,P(x)$ depends on competitors that prove instances cheaply without proving the universal (§5.5); this is not settled here. The note's other idea, committing in advance to a random stopping point, is a frequentist error-control argument; nothing here bears on it, and nothing here contradicts it. (`philosophy` L12 uses this proposition.)

## 5. The ω-gap (target T4)

### 5.1 Anatomy

**Proposition 5.1 (scout N1's C1) [proved].** Let $\mathcal L$ have a nonempty set $\mathrm{CT}$ of closed terms, $B$ decidable and consistent, $\varphi(x)$ a formula with one free variable, and suppose $I:=B\cup\{\varphi(t):t\in\mathrm{CT}\}$ is consistent. Let the stream list $(\varphi(t),1)$ for all $t\in\mathrm{CT}$, in any order. Then $\Unif(E_\infty)>0$, the posterior converges in total variation to $P_\infty=$ the prior restricted to the consistent extensions of $I$, and the limit trichotomy of $\forall x\,\varphi$ is:
* (i) $(1,0,0)$ if $I\vdash\forall x\,\varphi$;
* (ii) $(0,1,0)$ if $I\vdash\neg\forall x\,\varphi$ ($I$ is ω-inconsistent at $\varphi$);
* (iii) otherwise all three limits lie in $(0,1)$; $\lim\Dis=P_\infty(\text{the theory is ω-inconsistent at }\varphi)$ and $\lim\Indep=P_\infty(\text{the theory proves every instance and decides }\forall x\,\varphi\text{ neither way})$.

*Proof.* $I$ is an r.e. consistent theory proving the stream, so $\Unif(E_\infty)\ge2^{-K(I)-c}>0$ (`fundamentals` Prop 8.1(c)), and (Lim) applies; by `fundamentals` Prop 8.1(d), with $\Gamma=I$, $P_\infty$ is the prior restricted to the consistent extensions of $I$, whether or not $I$ is complete. In (i) and (ii) every consistent extension of $I$ proves what $I$ proves. In (iii), $I$, $I+\forall x\varphi$ and $I+\neg\forall x\varphi$ are consistent r.e. theories with positive weight. ∎

So **the Bayesian ω-gap is exactly the ω-incompleteness of the background plus the instances**, made quantitative by prior shares. Two standard cases: $B=\emptyset$ with an atomic diagram (`fundamentals` Prop 8.5; AI-paper Prop B.8); and $B\supseteq\Q$ with $\varphi\in\Delta_0$, where the instances are free (Prop 4.6) and the posterior does not move at all, case (iii) holding iff $B$ decides $\forall x\varphi$ neither way. For $\Sigma_1$-sound $B$ this is iff $B\nvdash\forall x\varphi$: with all instances true, $\neg\forall x\varphi$ is logically equivalent to the false $\Sigma_1$ sentence $\exists x\neg\varphi$, which a $\Sigma_1$-sound $B$ does not prove. Without $\Sigma_1$-soundness the remark fails (referee A, m10): for $B=\PA+\neg\Con(\PA)$ and $\varphi(x)$ the $\Delta_0$ formula "$x$ is not a $\PA$-proof of $\bot$", every instance is true and $\Q$-provable, so $I=\Cn(B)$, and $B\vdash\neg\forall x\varphi$: case (ii) holds although $B\nvdash\forall x\varphi$.

*Relation to the AI-paper's ω-gap theorem (Thm 3.9; corrected in the revision).* That theorem concerns the generative (derivation-likelihood) inducer over a countable class with i.i.d. data from a generator $T^*$. Its headline (a), (b): the posterior probability of "proves $\forall x\varphi$" tends to the prior share of provers in the generator class $\{T:P_T=P_{T^*}\}$. In the faithful regime with $\forall x\varphi$ in the data space it tends to $\ind[T^*\vdash_{\mathsf C}\forall x\varphi]$, whether the *generator* proves the universal (its (c1)), not to the truth value of $\forall x\varphi$. Prop 5.1 is the proof-requiring, one-tape counterpart, with the two non-proving shares identified (ω-inconsistent and ω-incomplete mass); `fundamentals` Prop 8.5 is its basic instance.

### 5.2 The Gaifman condition

**Proposition 5.2 (when $\Bel$ satisfies the Gaifman condition; sharpens scout L's Observation L1) [proved].** Under the hypotheses of Prop 5.1, enumerate $\mathrm{CT}=\{t_0,t_1,\dots\}$. Then $\lim_n\Bel_0(\bigwedge_{i\le n}\varphi(t_i))$ exists and
$$\lim_n\Bel_0\Big(\bigwedge_{i\le n}\varphi(t_i)\Big)-\Bel_0(\forall x\,\varphi)=\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent, proves every }\varphi(t_i),\ \nvdash\forall x\,\varphi\}.$$
This is $0$ iff $I\vdash\forall x\,\varphi$, and otherwise at least $2^{-K(I)-c}$, where $K(I)$ is the complexity of an index of $I$. The same holds for every posterior $\Bel_D$ with $D$ compatible with $I$ (divide by $M_A(D)$).

*Proof.* The events $\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega$ consistent and proves $\varphi(t_0),\dots,\varphi(t_n)\}$ decrease in $n$ to the event of proving every instance; continuity from above gives the limit. A theory proving $\forall x\varphi$ proves every instance, so the difference is the stated measure. If $I\vdash\forall x\varphi$, every consistent theory proving all instances proves $\forall x\varphi$. Otherwise $I$ itself is in the event, with weight $\ge2^{-K(I)-c}$ (Dom). ∎

Scout L's Observation L1 is the case $\mathcal L=\{0,S,R\}$, $\varphi=R(x)$, $B=\emptyset$. Note "prove", not "settle" (revision; referee A, m9): in the ω-inconsistent case (ii) of Prop 5.1 the instances refute the universal, yet the gap equals the whole consistent mass of $I$'s extensions.

*Reading.* The Gaifman condition, $P(\forall x\varphi)=\lim_nP(\bigwedge_{i\le n}\varphi(t_i))$, is a desideratum for credences about *truth* in models where the closed terms name everything. For a credence given by a probability $\mu$ on structures, the gap is $\mu\{\mathfrak M:\text{all closed instances hold},\ \mathfrak M\nvDash\forall x\varphi\}$ (AI-paper Prop B.10); Prop 5.2 is the provability analogue, with "theories that prove every instance but not the universal" in place of "structures with non-standard elements". Hutter, Lloyd, Ng and Uther (2013) impose the condition, and logical inductors do not satisfy it (Garrabrant et al. 2016, after Sawin and Demski 2013). $\Bel$ is a probability of *provability* and satisfies the condition exactly when deduction from the instances proves the universal. So Gaifman-type desiderata do not apply to it, and the paper should not present the failure as a defect.

### 5.3 The universal prior does not close the gap

**Theorem 5.3 (a uniform Gödelian ω-gap; scout N1's C2) [proved, given (G2)].** Let $B$ be decidable and true with $\PA\subseteq\Cn(B)$. For every $k$ there is a $\Delta_0$ formula $\zeta_k(x)$ such that $\forall x\,\zeta_k$ is true, every instance $\zeta_k(\underline n)$ is a true $\Delta_0$ sentence (so $\Q$-provable, and the instance data are free by Prop 4.6), and
$$2^{-k-K(k)-c}\ \le\ \Bel_0(\forall x\,\zeta_k)\ <\ 2^{-k}.$$
Hence after any instance data the posterior belief is $\Bel_0(\forall x\zeta_k)/(1-\Inc_0)<2^{-k+c'}$, so $\Dis+\Indep>1-2^{-k+c'}$, for every $n$.

*Proof.* Let $\forall x\,\zeta_k$ be the canonical consistency statement $\Con(B\cup T_k)$ of the presentation $e_k$ (Thm 3.1), rewritten with one universal quantifier over a code of a pair (derivation of $\bot$, enumeration stage), membership in $B$ and in $T_k$ certified inside the $\Delta_0$ matrix by computation witnesses, and the quantifiers contracted by $\Delta_0$ pairing. For such a natural rewriting $\PA\vdash\forall x\,\zeta_k\leftrightarrow\Con(B\cup T_k)$ [standard; referee A, m12]. $B\cup T_k$ is true (Thm 3.1(iii)), so $\forall x\,\zeta_k$ is true and its instances are true $\Delta_0$ sentences. If $\Bel_0(\forall x\,\zeta_k)\ge2^{-k}$, Thm 3.1(ii) puts $\forall x\,\zeta_k$ into $T_k$, so $B\cup T_k\vdash\forall x\,\zeta_k$, hence (by the $\PA$-provable equivalence) $B\cup T_k\vdash\Con(B\cup T_k)$, contradicting (G2) for the consistent r.e. presentation $B\cup T_k\supseteq\PA$. The lower bound is (Dom) for the true one-sentence theory $\{\forall x\,\zeta_k\}$, whose index is computable from $e_k$, of complexity $\le K(e_k)+c$. The posterior after $B$-entailed data is the prior normalised by $M_A(\emptyset)=1-\Inc_0$ ($\widehat\Bel_0=\Bel_\emptyset$, `fundamentals` Def 1.4). ∎

*Correction to scout N1's C2 (made in the development round).* N1 wrote "$\Bel_{D_n}(\forall x\varphi_k)=\Bel_0(\forall x\varphi_k)<2^{-k}$". $\Bel_0$ is unnormalised ($\Bel_0(\top)=1-\Inc_0$) while $\Bel_{D_n}$ is normalised, so the posterior is $\Bel_0/(1-\Inc_0)$; the bound becomes $2^{-k+c'}$.

*Reading.* For every confidence level there is a true universal statement, all of whose instances are trivially checkable, that the universal inducer believes below that level, uniformly over all random theory machines. This is Gödel's second theorem transported into the prior by the derandomisation of §3.

### 5.4 Versions where the gap closes

* **(a) The ω-rule [proved].** Let $\vdash_\omega$ be closure under first-order deduction and the ω-rule (from $\varphi(\underline n)$ for all $n$ infer $\forall x\varphi$). Then $\Q\vdash_\omega\chi$ for every true $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-sentence $\chi$: write $\chi$ in prenex form with a $\Delta_0$ matrix; true $\Delta_0$ sentences are $\Q$-provable; a true $\exists x\psi(x)$ has a true instance $\psi(\underline n)$, provable by induction on the prefix, and $\exists$-introduction applies; a true $\forall x\psi(x)$ has all instances true and provable, and the ω-rule applies. So with $B\supseteq\Q$ every $\vdash_\omega$-consistent random theory has $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-content exactly $\Th(\mathbb N)$ (it proves every truth and, being consistent, no falsehood). The gap closes because the inducer is omniscient about arithmetic a priori.
* **(b) Full second-order consequence [proved, given Dedekind's categoricity theorem (known; standard)].** Let hypotheses be second-order sentences, and let compatibility mean "has a full model, and every full model satisfies $\psi_D$". Every full model of second-order Peano arithmetic $\PA^2$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb N$, so $\{\PA^2\}$ is compatible with every finite correctly labelled data set; by the dominance argument (the tapes beginning with a program for $\{\PA^2\}$) the loss on any presentation of $\Th(\mathbb N)$, in any order, is at most $K(\PA^2)+c$, and the limit posterior has $\Bel(\varphi)=\ind[\mathbb N\models\varphi]$ for every $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-sentence (trivially, since every sentence is a datum; the content is the loss bound). This pins the unlearnability of $\Th(\mathbb N)$ (`fundamentals` Prop 8.6) on *effective* consequence, not on the complexity of arithmetical truth (scout N1's N6).
* **(c) The generative variant [known: AI-paper Thm 3.9(c1), (d1), (e), Prop 3.12(c); corrected in the revision].** With $\forall x\varphi$ in the data space and a faithful likelihood, the posterior of "proves $\forall x\varphi$" converges to $\ind[T^*\vdash_{\mathsf C}\forall x\varphi]$, whether the generator proves the universal (Thm 3.9(c1)); this closes the gap only in the sense that the universal sentence is itself observed. For filtered data the gap stays open: for quantifier-free $\varphi$ in the AI-paper's minimal class the limit is $0$ under its likelihood $\mathsf{L1}$ and the prior share under the selection-aware likelihood $\mathsf{L1}^{\rm sel}$ (Thm 3.9(d1)), and with an unknown filter it is a filter-prior mixture (Thm 3.9(e)). The only convergence to $1$ under $\mathsf{L1}^{\rm sel}$ reported there is Prop 3.12(c), a proof sketch, for unfiltered closed numeral data in a class with finitely many splits, in pure logic. So the generative variant is *not* a closure of the ω-gap on instance data; the development round's "converges to the truth value" was a misquotation (§12).
* **(d) Finite theories with line budgets.** See §5.5.

### 5.5 Line budgets and Kreisel's conjecture (extension C3)

*Setting.* Hypotheses are single sentences $\psi$; a datum $\chi$ is **$\ell$-compatible** with $\psi$ if $B\cup\{\psi\}$ is consistent and proves $\chi$ in a fixed Hilbert calculus by a derivation of at most $\ell$ lines (lines, not symbols). The data are the closed instances $\varphi(S^n0)$, $n\in\mathbb N$.

**Proposition 5.4 (scout N1's C3) [proved; elementary].** Assume that some sentence is $\ell$-compatible with every datum (for instance $\forall x\varphi$, when $B\cup\{\forall x\varphi\}$ is consistent and $\ell$ is at least the constant number of lines of instantiation; nonemptiness made a hypothesis in the revision, referee A, m13(a)). Then for any prior with full support on sentences, the limit posterior gives $\Bel(\forall x\varphi)=1$ iff every $\psi$ that is consistent with $B$ and proves every $\varphi(S^n0)$ within $\ell$ lines also proves $\forall x\varphi$ (a "Kreisel property" of $(B,\varphi,\ell)$). *Proof.* The compatible set decreases to the nonempty set of such $\psi$; a $\psi$ in it not proving $\forall x\varphi$ has positive weight. ∎

**Example 5.4(i) [proved; computed: `rb7` Part 1].** $B=\emptyset$, $\varphi(x):=x=0\vee\exists y\,x=Sy$. Each instance has a derivation of at most five lines, independently of $n$ (an equality axiom $S^n0=S^n0$, the axiom $\chi(t)\to\exists y\chi(y)$ with $t=S^{n-1}0$ and modus ponens, the axiom $\beta\to\alpha\vee\beta$ and modus ponens; three lines for $n=0$). But $\forall x\varphi$ is not valid: the structure $\mathbb N\sqcup\{a\}$ with $S(a):=0$ (or the finite structure $\{0,1,2,a\}$ with $S(0)=1$, $S(1)=2$, $S(2)=2$, $S(a)=0$) has $a\ne0$ and $a$ outside the range of $S$. So with $B=\emptyset$ even the trivial hypothesis keeps the gap open under a constant line budget. With $B=\Q$ the example closes ($\forall x\varphi$ is the axiom Q3). *This example is degenerate* (referee A, m13(b)): every instance is logically valid, so the data are old evidence (Prop 4.6), the posterior never moves, and the line budget plays no role.

**Example 5.4(ii) (non-degenerate; new in the revision, after referee B's issue 7) [proved; computed: `rb7` Part 2, `c6` R5].** $B=\emptyset$, $\mathcal L=\{0,S,R\}$ with $R$ a fresh unary predicate, data $R(S^n0)$ (not valid: the one-point structure with $R$ empty refutes $R(0)$). Let $\varphi(x):=x=0\vee\exists y\,x=Sy$ as in (i), and
$$\psi_1:=\forall x\,(\varphi(x)\to R(x)),\qquad\psi_2:=\forall x\,R(x).$$
$\psi_1$ derives $R(S^n0)$ in 9 lines (7 for $n=0$): the hypothesis, its instance at $S^n0$ by $\forall$-elimination and modus ponens, the 5-line derivation of $\varphi(S^n0)$ from (i), and modus ponens. $\psi_2$ derives each datum in 3 lines. The structure $\{0,1,2,a\}$ of (i) with $R:=\{0,1,2\}$ satisfies $\psi_1$ (the only element outside $R$ is $a$, and $\varphi(a)$ fails) and every $R(S^n0)$ (closed terms denote $0,1,2$), but not $\forall xR(x)$. So with $\ell=9$ both $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$ stay $\ell$-compatible with every datum, $\psi_1$ is consistent and does not prove $\forall xR(x)$, and under any full-support prior the limit belief in $\forall xR(x)$ lies strictly between $0$ and $1$: the Kreisel property of Prop 5.4 fails for $(\emptyset,R,9)$ with non-valid instances. Referee B's `rb7` checks the derivations line by line for $n\le60$ in a small Hilbert-proof checker, and both `rb7` and `c6` R5 check the countermodel.

**Literature [known; verification levels in §11].** Kreisel's conjecture: if $\PA$ proves $\varphi(S^n0)$ in at most $k$ lines for every $n$, then $\PA\vdash\forall x\varphi$. Parikh (1973) proved the corresponding statement for a version $\PA^*$ of $\PA$ with addition and multiplication as ternary relations (the $k$-line provability relation is decidable there); Baaz and Pudlák (1993) proved further cases ("Kreisel's conjecture for $L\exists_1$"); Hrubeš (2007) exhibited theories very close to $\PA$ for which the conjecture fails ($\PA$ with a minus function, the integers, $\PA$ with an arbitrary extra function symbol, $\PA$ with a predicate for "natural number"; earlier, Yukami 1978 for $\PA$ plus all true multiplication equations); Hrubeš (2009) proved it for $\PA$ axiomatised with the minimality principle; for the usual Hilbert-style $\PA$ it is open (Hrubeš's thesis; Santos and Kahle 2021 study variants). All these results concern *infinite* theories with schemata. Prop 5.4's property concerns finite extensions $B\cup\{\psi\}$, where a $k$-line derivation can use only the finitely many axioms of $\psi$ plus logical axioms; no result on it was found, so the link stays a reformulation. **[open]** whether, for $B=\Q$ and $\Delta_0$ formulas $\varphi$, every finite $\psi$ that proves all instances of $\varphi$ in boundedly many lines proves $\forall x\varphi$. (Over $\Q$ with $\varphi\in\Delta_0$ the instances are old evidence again; the question is about which finite theories the line-budget inducer keeps, not about learning from the instances.)

## 6. $\Th(\mathbb N)$: how fast does the loss grow? (target T5)

Setting: $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$, $B\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)$ decidable, $(\varphi_i)_{i\ge1}$ a computable enumeration of all sentences, $D_n$ the first $n$ sentences labelled by truth, $b_{1:n}$ the labels. `fundamentals` Prop 8.6: $K(b_{1:n}\mid n)-c\le L_A(D_n)\le n+K(n)+c$, and the loss is unbounded. All losses in this section are in the semimeasure convention (§1.3); $L_A(D_n)$ is nondecreasing in $n$, because $E_{D_{n+1}}\subseteq E_{D_n}$.

### 6.1 A polynomial lower bound

**Theorem 6.1 (scout F's F26(c); parts (a), (c) and the proved exponent added in the revision) [proved, given Chaitin's theorem that $\Omega_V$ is Martin-Löf random (known); computed: `c5`; the growth certificate `c6` R4 and `rb6` S3].** Suppose the enumeration lists sentences by length and, within a length, in a fixed order, and that (1) the number of sentences of length $\le L$ is at most $C\gamma^L$ for every $L$, and (2) there are terms $\underline k^{\rm b}$ ("binary numerals") with value $k$ and length $|\underline k^{\rm b}|\le c_1\log_2k+c_2$. Put $\delta:=1/(c_1\log_2\gamma)$ and $m(n):=\lfloor(n/C')^\delta\rfloor$, where $C'$ depends only on the syntax and on the fixed formula $\mathrm{Bit}$ below. Then for all $n$:
* (a) $K(b_{1:n}\mid n)\ \ge\ m(n)-K(n)-c$;
* (b) $L_A(D_n)\ \ge\ C'^{-\delta}\,n^\delta-K(n)-c$;
* (c) $L_A(D_n)\ \ge\ (2C')^{-\delta}\,n^\delta-2\log\log n-c$ for $n\ge4$.

For the 14-symbol Polish-notation syntax of `c5` (terms $0$, $St$, $+tt$, $*tt$, variables $x'^i$; formulas $=tt$, $<tt$, $\neg f$, $\wedge ff$, $\vee ff$, $\to ff$, $\forall vf$, $\exists vf$), with $\underline1^{\rm b}=S0$, $\underline{2m}^{\rm b}={\cdot}\,\underline m^{\rm b}\,SS0$, $\underline{2m+1}^{\rm b}=S{\cdot}\,\underline m^{\rm b}\,SS0$ (so $c_1=5$, $c_2=2$): counting all strings ($\gamma=14$) gives $\delta\ge1/(5\log_214)=0.0525$; counting formulas gives **$\delta\ge1/(5\log_25.385)=0.08234$ [proved; computer-checked certificate]**, by the following argument (referee B, issue 5). With $V(x)=x/(1-x)$ the generating functions of terms and formulas satisfy $T=x+V+xT+2xT^2$ and $F=2xT^2+xF+3xF^2+2xVF$, every term on the right carrying a factor $x$. If rationals $x_0,T^*,F^*>0$ satisfy both equations with "$\ge$" in place of "$=$" (a super-solution), then the iterates from $0$ have nonnegative coefficients, agree with the series up to degree $k$ after $k$ steps, and stay below $(T^*,F^*)$ at $x_0$; so $\sum_LF_Lx_0^L\le F^*$ and $\#\{\text{formulas of length}\le L\}\le\frac{F^*}{1-x_0}x_0^{-L}$ for every $L$. At $x_0=1000/5385$ a super-solution with $T^*\approx0.800042$, $F^*\approx0.609287$ is verified in exact rational arithmetic, giving $C=0.7482$, $\gamma=5.385$ (`c6` R4, independent code; referee B's `rb6` S3 found the same certificate; both compare the bound with exact counts up to $L=300$ resp. $400$; at $\gamma=5.38$ no certificate exists, the growth rate being about $5.383$).

*Proof.* Let $\Omega_s\uparrow\Omega_V$ be the computable approximations. Since $\Omega_V$ is irrational, its $k$-th binary digit is the eventual value of the $k$-th digit of $\Omega_s$. Let $\mathrm{Bit}'(y)$ be a formula with $\mathbb N\models\mathrm{Bit}'(\underline k)$ iff that digit is $1$: "$\exists s\,\forall s'\ge s$: the $k$-th digit of $\Omega_{s'}$ is $1$", with the digit predicate expressed by a $\Delta_0$ formula about a computation witness. Put $\mathrm{Bit}(x):=\exists y\,(y=x\wedge\mathrm{Bit}'(y))$, so that $x$ occurs once, and $\mathrm{Bit}_k:=\mathrm{Bit}(\underline k^{\rm b})$; then $|\mathrm{Bit}_k|\le|\mathrm{Bit}|+c_1\log_2k+c_2$, and the position of $\mathrm{Bit}_k$ in the enumeration is at most $C\gamma^{|\mathrm{Bit}|+c_2}\gamma^{c_1\log_2k}=C'k^{1/\delta}$. The first $n$ sentences therefore contain $\mathrm{Bit}_1,\dots,\mathrm{Bit}_{m(n)}$, at positions computable from $k$, and their labels are the digits $\Omega_{1:m(n)}$. So $\Omega_{1:m(n)}$ is computable from $n$ and $b_{1:n}$: $K(\Omega_{1:m(n)})\le K(b_{1:n}\mid n)+K(n)+c$. By Chaitin's theorem, $K(\Omega_{1:m})\ge m-c$. This is (a). (b) is (a) with `fundamentals` Prop 8.6(b) and $m(n)\ge(n/C')^\delta-1$. (c) For $n\ge4$ let $n':=2^{\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor}\in(n/2,n]$; then $K(n')\le K(\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor)+c\le2\log\log n+c'$, and by monotonicity and (b), $L_A(D_n)\ge L_A(D_{n'})\ge(n/(2C'))^\delta-2\log\log n-c''$. ∎

*Remarks.* (i) Only the numeral length and the growth of the syntax enter $\delta$; the (large) size of $\mathrm{Bit}$ enters only the constant. (ii) With unary numerals the position of $\mathrm{Bit}_k$ is at most $C''\gamma^k$, so $m$ is only $\lfloor\log_\gamma(n/C'')\rfloor$; then (b) is vacuous as written, since $K(n)$ can be about $\log_2n>\log_\gamma n$, but (c)'s monotonicity argument still gives a logarithmic bound $L_A(D_n)\ge\log_\gamma n-2\log\log n-c$ (referee A, m14(b)). (iii) The bound is weak in absolute terms: for $n=10^6$, $n^{0.0823}\approx3.1$. It is an asymptotic statement about the shape of the growth, not a usable estimate.

**Corollary 6.2 (quantitative incompleteness along the enumeration) [proved, given Theorem 6.1(a)].** Let $T$ be r.e. with $B\cup T\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)$, and $u_n(T)$ the number of $i\le n$ such that $B\cup T$ proves neither $\varphi_i$ nor $\neg\varphi_i$. Then $u_n(T)\ge C'^{-\delta}n^\delta/(2\log n)$ for all large $n$.

*Proof.* $b_{1:n}$ is computable from an index of $T$, $n$, the set of undecided positions and their labels (a true theory decides correctly): $K(b_{1:n}\mid n)\le K(e_T)+K(u_n\mid n)+\log\binom n{u_n}+u_n+c\le K(e_T)+u_n(\log n+3)+2\log n+c$, using $\log\binom nu\le u\log(en/u)$ (`rb0` K6) and $K(u\mid n)\le\log n+c$. Compare with Theorem 6.1(a), which is the inequality needed here (the statement (b) about $L_A$ would not suffice). ∎

*Reading.* Every true r.e. theory leaves undecided at least about $n^\delta$ of the first $n$ sentences. This is a Chaitin-type statement in the natural enumeration.

### 6.2 An upper bound by a reduction (scout N1's N7)

**Proposition 6.3 [proved].** For every r.e. $T$ with $B\cup T\subseteq\Th(\mathbb N)$ and every $n$,
$$L_A(D_n)\le K(e_T)+\log\binom n{u_n(T)}+u_n(T)+2\log n+O(\log\log n).$$
Hence if some such $T$ has $u_n(T)/n\to0$, then $L_A(D_n)=o(n)$.

*Proof.* $B\cup T$ plus the correctly labelled undecided sentences among the first $n$ is a consistent (true) r.e. theory proving $D_n$, with an index computable from $e_T$, $n$, $u_n$, the positions (given $n,u_n$: $\log\binom n{u_n}$ bits) and the $u_n$ labels; then (Chain). If $u_n/n\to0$, $\log\binom n{u_n}\le nH(u_n/n)=o(n)$. ∎

**Open (scout F's Q3).** Is $L_A(D_n)=o(n)$? By Prop 6.3 it suffices that some true r.e. theory decides a set of sentences of asymptotic density one in the enumeration, i.e. that the truth of arithmetic is *generically decidable* in the sense of generic-case complexity (Myasnikov and Rybalov 2008 for the framework). Known nearby facts:
* The halting problem is decidable on a set of asymptotic probability one for one model of Turing machines (Hamkins and Miasnikov 2006), which shows that such questions depend on the representation.
* **Calude and Jürgensen (2005)** (description corrected in the revision; referee A, m17): their abstract states that the probability that a true sentence of length $n$ is provable tends to $0$ (the arXiv v1 abstract: "an $n$-bit statement"), while the probability that a sentence of length $n$ is true stays positive; the probability space is the set of statements with $n$-bit codes under their Gödel numbering, and their complexity measure ($\delta$-complexity) is shown invariant under changes of the numbering [known: abstract, two versions]. Whether this transfers to the length enumeration here is a question about the encoding: in the syntax of §6.1 the sentences are a sparse subset of the strings (about $5.38^L$ formulas, and fewer sentences, against $14^L$ strings; `c5`, `rb6`). If a version held for the natural length enumeration, every true r.e. $T$ would leave a positive fraction of the sentences of each length undecided, so $u_n(T)\ge\rho n$ for large $n$ and Prop 6.3's sufficient condition would fail for every $T$. It would not settle Q3: $K(b_{1:n}\mid n)$ could still be $o(n)$, since truth values along the enumeration are highly redundant (for instance $\psi$ and $\neg\neg\psi$).
* Cor 6.2 gives only a polynomial lower bound on $u_n$.

The answer depends on the syntax conventions, and it is not known here.

*[Proof sketch, not used elsewhere]* A weaker improvement is within reach: if a decidable set of sentences of positive lower density $\rho$ in the enumeration is decided by $B$ (for instance the sentences of the form $\wedge\neg{=}00\,\psi$, i.e. $\neg(0=0)\wedge\psi$, which $\Q$ refutes), then $L_A(D_n)\le(1-\rho+o(1))n+K(n)+c$, because the labels of that set are computable from $n$. The missing step is a proof that the sentence counts $s(L)$ of the syntax satisfy $s(L-5)\ge\rho'\,s(L)$ for large $L$. Referee B's `rb6` S2 computed the exact sentence (closed formula) counts for $L\le70$, cross-checked by brute force ($L\le5$) and explicit generation ($L\le9$): $s(L-5)/s(L)$ decreases from $4.2\cdot10^{-4}$ at $L=20$ to $2.86\cdot10^{-4}$ at $L=70$, towards $\gamma^{-5}\approx2.2\cdot10^{-4}$, and $s(L)/F(L)$ falls from $0.056$ to $0.0094$. This supports the missing step numerically for $L\le70$ only. The gain would be a factor $1-\rho$ with $\rho\approx2\cdot10^{-4}$; the paper can drop the sketch without loss.

## 7. Relation to the literature (target T6; revised)

* **Probabilities on sentences.** Gaifman (1964) and Scott and Krauss (1966) assign coherent probabilities to sentences (measures on models). Hutter, Lloyd, Ng and Uther (2013) extend given probabilities of some sentences to all sentences of a higher-order logic under desiderata that include the Gaifman condition and a Cournot condition. Demski (2012) defines a prior over first-order theories by drawing length-weighted random sentences and keeping each one consistent with those kept; the resulting random theory is complete. Garrabrant et al. (2016) construct computable logical inductors whose limit is a coherent probability over completions of the given axioms. **Against SAI:** all of these are probabilities of *truth* (or of membership in a random completion). SAI's $\Bel$ is a probability of *provability* by a random theory that is r.e. relative to the tape, so it keeps an independent share and violates the Gaifman condition exactly when the instances do not deductively prove the universal (Prop 5.2; AI-paper Prop B.10 is the version for credences on structures). Sawin and Demski (2013) show that computable distributions which come to believe true $\Pi_1$ sentences must give arbitrarily low limiting probability to some short true $\Pi_2$ sentences. §3 and Cor 2.10 show a parallel divide for the ideal (non-computable) SAI posterior: on $\Pi_1$ data it is derandomisable and its beliefs certify truth; one level up (Boolean combinations of $\Sigma_1$) it can concentrate on false theories. This is a parallel, not a consequence of their result.
* **The modified Demski prior** (Fallenstein, Demski and Garrabrant, forum posts c. 2015; used in Garrabrant, Fallenstein, Demski and Soares, *Inductive Coherence*, 2016; known here only at snippet level, as recorded in `fundamentals` §10 and §14) samples a theory as the output of a universal machine on random bits, which is the sampling stage of $G_U$, and iterates such draws, keeping each one consistent with what was kept, until the theory is complete. So random axiom systems drawn by a universal machine are not new here (passed on by `fundamentals` §16.3; the development round's sentence that the lottery "concerns a phenomenon these frameworks cannot express" is withdrawn, §12). **[proved, for the process as described in `fundamentals` §10]** On the lottery stream of Thm 2.2 the modified prior also gives the stream positive probability: with probability at least $2^{-K(G_{\rm lot})-c}\cdot0.2887$ its first draw is a surviving lottery theory, every later draw is kept only if consistent with it, and the final theory extends it. Its completed theories that contain the stream are not r.e. (Thm 2.2(b)), but completed theories are typically not r.e. anyway (no completion of an extension of $\Q$ is), so the contrast between random and r.e. hypotheses is not visible in that prior. What is specific to the single-draw law $M_A$ is the comparison of the one-tape law with its own coin-free and Kolmogorov versions (Thm 2.2(c)), the identification of the succeeding hypotheses as random theories that are incomplete and r.e. in the tape, and the arity boundary (Props 2.3, 2.7).
* **Random axioms.** Bienvenu, Romashchenko, Shen, Taveneaux and Vermeeren (2014), with Shen (2011) and the discussion around Lipton's 2011 proposal, study adding *randomly chosen true* complexity axioms to $\PA$; Levin (2013) bounds the probability that a randomised algorithm completes $\PA$. §3.4 states what is plausibly new against them and what is not: the threshold device is theirs and de Leeuw et al.'s; the threshold just above the unknown $\Inc_0$, the universal mixture, the single random threshold theory and the coding theorem for an inductive inference rule are what this record adds.
* **Probabilistic enumeration.** de Leeuw, Moore, Shannon and Shapiro (1956) show that what a probabilistic machine produces with positive probability can be produced deterministically; the majority vote at a density point (Prop 2.3; `fundamentals` Lemma 8.0) is that argument, and the threshold $1/(r+1)$ is exact for it (§2.4). The lottery is exactly where it breaks for random theories: majority vote over *consistency* constraints of unbounded arity can be inconsistent.
* **Mass problems and bounded diagonally non-computable functions** (added in the revision; referee A, m16). The closer computability-theoretic ancestor of the lottery is the classical fact that randomness helps for *mass problems*: a class of positive measure can have no computable member. For a computable bound $q$, the $q$-bounded diagonally non-computable functions ($f(n)<q(n)$ and $f(n)$ different from the value of a fixed partial computable diagonal function at $n$) form a $\Pi^0_1$ class with no computable member; under the uniform product measure on $\prod_n\{0,\dots,q(n)-1\}$ it has measure at least $\prod_n(1-1/q(n))$, which is positive exactly when $\sum_n1/q(n)<\infty$. Kučera showed that every Martin-Löf random sequence computes a DNC function, so the class of all DNC functions is not negligible [known: content from secondary sources (arXiv:1411.5874, arXiv:1308.6402, Bienvenu–Patey arXiv:1411.6846); the standard attribution is Kučera (1985), whose bibliographic data were verified, but the theorem's location in it was not], and Bienvenu and Porter (2016) proved the dichotomy for bounded DNC classes: $\mathrm{DNC}_q$ is not tt-negligible when $\sum_n1/q(n)<\infty$, by Kučera's argument and the positivity of $\prod(1-1/q(n))$, and it is deep (a strong form of negligibility) when the sum diverges [known: search snippets of arXiv:1403.0450 and its sequel arXiv:2403.04045; full text not read]. The lottery's survival condition $\sum_k1/(N_k+1)=\sum_k2^{-(k+1)}<\infty$ is this convergence condition, and its diagonal ticket $j^*_k$ plays the role of the diagonal value. The differences: the lottery's diagonal is computed from $\emptyset'$, against r.e. theories, not against partial computable functions; the objects that fail are consistent r.e. *theories proving the stream*, not computable members of a class; and the class of surviving tapes arises as the inducer's compatibility event, so its measure is the inducer's likelihood. What is new here is that consistency constraints of unbounded arity turn the compatibility event into such a mass problem, the arity boundary (Props 2.3, 2.7), and the arithmetic versions with true data (Thm 2.9, Cor 2.10).
* **The lottery paradox.** Kyburg (1961): for a fair lottery each "ticket $j$ loses" is highly probable, "some ticket wins" is certain, and closing highly probable beliefs under conjunction yields inconsistency. Theorem 2.2 turns the paradox into a separation: the majority-vote theory refutes every ticket and is inconsistent (Prop 2.3, Instances), and this is why no deterministic hypothesis can replace the random ones.
* **Chaitin and its critics.** Chaitin (1974): a formal system cannot prove "$K(x)>m$" for $m$ much larger than its own complexity. Raatikainen (1998): the *least* such bound for a given theory is determined by the coding of machines rather than by the theory's complexity; Chaitin's bound is an upper bound. Cor 3.6 uses only the upper bound, in the form "a consistent extension of $\Q$ proving $\mathrm{Kgt}_m(x)$ has complexity $>m-K(m)-c$", and makes no claim about least constants. Prop 3.11 adds that the resulting cost to an inducer is a cost of deciding, visible in the semimeasure and three-valued conventions and absent, for most strings, in the normalised one.
* **Flexible formulas and independent sequences.** Mostowski (1961), Kripke (1962), Woodin (2011), Blanck and Enayat (2017), Hamkins (2018). Lemma 2.8 is the routine weak form needed here, with prescribed truth values.
* **Kreisel's conjecture.** Parikh (1973), Baaz and Pudlák (1993), Hrubeš (2007, 2009), Santos and Kahle (2021); §5.5.
* **Generic-case decidability.** Hamkins and Miasnikov (2006), Myasnikov and Rybalov (2008); Calude and Jürgensen (2005) for the probability of provability; §6.2.
* **Old evidence.** Hacking (1967), Garber (1983); Prop 4.6.
* **Learning in the limit.** Gold (1967), text versus informant, is the learning-theoretic shadow of the two-valued/three-valued distinction (`fundamentals` Prop 8.4). Nothing in this thread changes that picture; the lottery adds that, on text, the hypotheses that succeed need not be r.e. at all.
