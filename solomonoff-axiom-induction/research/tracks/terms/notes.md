## 0. Summary

**The question** (ladder step 3; Hempel's theoretician's dilemma): in which hypothesis classes do theoretical terms pay, and what is it that pays — vocabulary, relations, or entities?

**The answer, in one theorem** (Theorem 8.1, assembled from §2–§7):
1. *With free deduction, theoretical vocabulary costs $O(1)$ bits* — in the one-tape, coin-free and Kolmogorov versions, class by class (posterior odds of observational contents), and under penalties on checking or generating axioms (Thm 2.1). Tape by tape the observational projection of a theory machine has *exactly* the same law on observational data (Thm 2.1(d)).
2. *Craig's construction is useless under charged deduction, for a precise reason*: it writes a run time in unary (Lemma 3.1); every axiomatisation by padded observational literals with polynomial-time membership fits exactly the polynomial-time regularities at polynomial budgets, under every reading of "short derivation" (Thm 3.3); the separations from axiom sets with theoretical symbols are unconditional under (a1), (a2), (b2) and equivalent to $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}\ne\mathsf P$ under (b) (Cor 3.5).
3. *But theoretical vocabulary can be eliminated at polynomial cost under every reading*, by storing the content in objects that no observational term names (Prop 4.1, Prop 4.3, Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7): observational hypotheses have the same fit classes as all hypotheses — $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ under (b), $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ under (a1), every decidable set under (a2), (b2) — and *finite* observational theories already reach all three.
4. *What finite theories cannot do without is a polyadic theoretical relation or theoretical entities*: a finite theory in the observational vocabulary, with any unary theoretical predicates and constants, that does not contradict the first-order theory of the named objects fits exactly the regular regularities (Thm 5.1, Thm 5.3); **one binary theoretical relation** over the named objects restores every decidable regularity under (a2), (b2), $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ under (b) and $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ under (a1) (Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7); so does quantifying over unnamed objects, and an observational theory that does so must *assert* an observational sentence false of the named objects ($\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$; Prop 9.1).
5. *With free deduction, finite theories gain without bound from theoretical functions or sorts*: on "there are at least $n$ things" every monadic finite theory has $K_{\rm fin}=\min_{m\ge n}K(m)\pm c\to\infty$, one unary function symbol gives $O(1)$ (Thm 6.2); in literal symbols the costs are between $n$ and $O(n^2)$ (pure equality), $\Theta(\log n)$ up to a $\log\log n$ term (monadic; new upper bound by a carry counter) and $O(1)$ (with $f$) (Prop 6.3); on PA's theorems, finite $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-theories diverge while a finite theory with theoretical sorts gives $O(1)$ (Thm 6.4); and r.e. axiom induction *is* finite-theory induction in a three-sorted extension, up to $O(1)$, coin-free (Thm 6.5, a Kleene-type theorem with complexity bounds, fully proved) but not one-tape (Rem 6.6).

## 1. Setting

**Definition 1.1 (observational restriction).** $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_O\cup\mathcal L_T$ is a countable first-order language with equality (finite where charged deduction is used); $\mathcal L_O$ is the observational part. $B\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ is decidable and consistent and may contain theoretical symbols. For $A\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ the *observational content* is $T_O(A):=\Cn(A)\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$ ($\Cn$ relative to $B$). A data set $D$ is *observational* if $\dom D\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$.
* *One tape.* For a theory machine $G$ let $A^G_w:=\{\chi:(v,\chi)\in G,\ v\sqsubseteq w\}$ for finite $w$ (so $A^G_\omega=\bigcup_{w\sqsubseteq\omega}A^G_w$). $G_U^O:=\{(w,\chi)\in G_U:\chi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}\}$ is the universal machine with its theoretical emissions dropped, and $M_A^{\mathcal L_O}:=\nu_{G_U^O}$. (It is universal among machines that emit only $\mathcal L_O$-sentences: such a $G$ is run by $G_U$ after a prefix $p_G$, and the filter drops nothing.) $M_A^{\mathcal L}:=M_A$.
* *Coin-free and Kolmogorov.* $W_A^{\mathcal L_O}(D):=\sum_e\mm(e)\,[B\cup(\re e\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O})$ consistent, $\vdash\psi_D]$ and $K_A^{\mathcal L_O}(D)$ the least $K(e)$ over the same $e$.
* *Classes.* For a set $\Theta$ of observational contents, $M_A(\Theta;D):=\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D,\ T_O(A_\omega)\in\Theta\}$; likewise $M_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)$, $W_A(\Theta;D)$, $W_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)$.
* *Penalised versions.* $W_A^{{\rm chk},t}$, $W_A^{{\rm gen},t}$ (TF Def 1.1; `sfi` §7) and their one-tape forms $M_A^{{\rm chk},t}$, $M_A^{{\rm gen},t}$ (`sfi` Def 3.3); the observational restrictions are the sub-sums over hypotheses whose axiom sets consist of $\mathcal L_O$-sentences (on every tape). The machine assumptions (S) (TF §1.2) and (S$_{\rm tape}$) (`sfi` §3.3) are assumed where these appear, as there.

**Definition 1.2 (literal data and charged deduction; SD Defs 1.2, 1.3, 1.6, Fact 1.4).** $\mathcal L_O=\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,R\}$; $t_w:=\mathtt s_{w_1}(\cdots\mathtt s_{w_k}(\mathtt e)\cdots)$, so $\mathtt s_a(t_v)=t_{av}$ and the word $w$ lists the function letters from the outside in; $\varphi_w:=R(t_w)$; $D^X$ lists $(\varphi_w,[w\in X])$ in length-lexicographic order; $f_X$ is the literal labeller of $X$; $\mathfrak T$ is the free term algebra on $\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1$ (its elements are the $t_w$). Derivations are in the AI-paper's Hilbert calculus $\mathsf K$ (SD §1.1). A hypothesis decides an axiom set $A_p$ within time $t$, satisfies **(F1)** ($B\cup A_p$ closed under permutations of the parameters), is consistent, and derives each datum with: (a1) every axiom instance (logical included) of size $\le h$; (a2) every nonlogical instance $\le h$; (b) material (total size of distinct instances) $\le g$; (b2) nonlogical material $\le g$. *Polynomial budgets* are SD Def 1.6 (membership degree charged in the prior). The *fit class* of an inducer is the set of $X$ on whose $D^X$ it has bounded loss; for these mixtures that holds iff a single hypothesis is compatible with every prefix (SD Fact 1.4 = TF Lemma 1.6).

**Definition 1.3 (the named objects).** $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$ is the complete first-order theory of $(\mathfrak T;\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1)$. It contains, for all words $u\ne\varepsilon$, $p,p'$ and letters $a\ne a'$: $\mathtt s_a x=\mathtt s_a y\to x=y$; $\mathtt s_ax\ne\mathtt s_{a'}y$; $\mathtt s_ax\ne\mathtt e$; $x\ne\mathtt e\to\exists y(x=\mathtt s_0y\vee x=\mathtt s_1y)$; $\mathtt s_ux\ne x$; and $t_p\ne t_{p'}$ for $p\ne p'$ (all true in $\mathfrak T$). A theory $T$ in a language $\supseteq\mathcal L_O$ is
* *about the named objects* if it has a model whose $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-reduct is $\mathfrak T$ (scout S's notion: every object is named by an observational closed term);
* *compatible with the named structure* if $T\cup\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$ is consistent (weaker: nonstandard objects are allowed, but they satisfy every first-order law that the named objects satisfy).

In a model of a theory compatible with the named structure, the denotations of the $t_w$ are pairwise distinct and form a copy of $\mathfrak T$ (the *standard part*); if the theory proves every literal datum of $X$, then $R$ is $X$ on the standard part.

## 2. Hempel's horn: vocabulary costs $O(1)$ (target T1)

**Theorem 2.1 (theoretical vocabulary costs $O(1)$ bits, class by class).** There is a constant $c$ (depending on $V$, the coding, $B$ and $\mathcal L_O\subseteq\mathcal L$) such that for every finite observational $D$ and every set $\Theta$ of observational contents:
* (a) *one tape:* $2^{-c}M_A^{\mathcal L}(\Theta;D)\le M_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)\le2^cM_A^{\mathcal L}(\Theta;D)$;
* (b) *coin-free and Kolmogorov:* the same for $W_A$, and $|K_A^{\mathcal L_O}(D)-K_A^{\mathcal L}(D)|\le c$;
* (c) *penalised:* for time-constructible $t(m)\ge m^{a^*}$, the observational restrictions of $W_A^{{\rm chk},t}$ and $W_A^{{\rm gen},t}$ (all forms) agree with $W_A^{\mathcal L}$ within $2^{\pm c}$, class by class, under (S); the observational restrictions of $M_A^{{\rm chk},t}$, $M_A^{{\rm gen},t}$ agree with $M_A^{\mathcal L}$ within $2^{\pm c}$, class by class, under (S$_{\rm tape}$);
* (d) *tape by tape:* for every theory machine $G$ in $\mathcal L$ let $G^O:=\{(w,\varphi):\varphi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_O},\ B\cup A^G_w\vdash\varphi\}$. Then $G^O$ is a theory machine emitting only $\mathcal L_O$-sentences, and for every tape $\omega$: $A^{G^O}_\omega=T_O(A^G_\omega)$; $B\cup A^{G^O}_\omega$ is consistent iff $B\cup A^G_\omega$ is; and for every observational $\varphi$ and $b$, $B\cup A^{G^O}_\omega\vdash\varphi^b$ iff $B\cup A^G_\omega\vdash\varphi^b$. Hence $\nu_{G^O}(\Theta;D)=\nu_G(\Theta;D)$ exactly.

Consequently posterior odds between any two classes of observational contents, and the predictive probabilities $M(D+(\varphi,b))/M(D)$ of observational sentences, agree within $2^{\pm2c}$, and cumulative semimeasure losses on observational reveal sequences within $2c$, in each version. **[proved; (c) given TF Lemma 2.1 / Thm 2.2 under (S) and `sfi` Thm 3.4, Prop 3.5 under (S$_{\rm tape}$); computed (finite analogue of (d)): `checks/t1_hempel_toy.out`]**

*Proof.* (d) $G^O$ is c.e. because $G$ is and $\vdash$ is r.e. By compactness and monotonicity of $A^G_w$ in $w$, $A^{G^O}_\omega=\bigcup_{w\sqsubseteq\omega}\{\varphi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}:B\cup A^G_w\vdash\varphi\}=T_O(A^G_\omega)$. Since $T_O(A)\subseteq\Cn(B\cup A)$, consistency of $B\cup A$ passes to $B\cup T_O(A)$; if $B\cup A$ is inconsistent, $T_O(A)$ contains the observational sentence $\neg\forall x\,x=x$. Again from $T_O(A)\subseteq\Cn(B\cup A)$, $\Cn(B\cup T_O(A))\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}=T_O(A)$, so an observational $\varphi^b$ follows from $B\cup T_O(A)$ iff it lies in $T_O(A)$ iff it follows from $B\cup A$ ($\varphi^b$ is observational when $\varphi$ is). The observational content of $G^O$ on $\omega$ is $T_O(A^G_\omega)$ itself, so class membership is preserved, and the two events coincide tape by tape.

(a) `fundamentals` Thm 4.1 gives the mixture identity tape-wise: the tapes $p\omega'$ with $V(p)$ an index of a machine $G$ produce $A^G_{\omega'}$ on $G_U$, hence $A^G_{\omega'}\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$ on $G_U^O$. *Lower bound.* Apply (d) to $G:=G_U$: $(G_U)^O$ is a fixed machine emitting only $\mathcal L_O$-sentences, so on the tapes $p\omega'$ with $V(p)$ an index of $(G_U)^O$ the filtered universal machine emits exactly $A^{(G_U)^O}_{\omega'}$; hence $M_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)\ge2^{-K((G_U)^O)}\nu_{(G_U)^O}(\Theta;D)=2^{-c}M_A^{\mathcal L}(\Theta;D)$. *Upper bound.* $G_U^O$ is a theory machine of $\mathcal L$, so the same identity in $\mathcal L$ gives $M_A^{\mathcal L}(\Theta;D)\ge2^{-K(G_U^O)}\nu_{G_U^O}(\Theta;D)=2^{-c}M_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)$. The remaining term $(1-\Omega_V)[B\vdash\psi_D]$ of the mixture identity is the same on both sides (program-less tapes emit nothing).

(b) Let $o(e)$ be an index of $\{\varphi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}:B\cup\re e\vdash\varphi\}$ and $f(e)$ one of $\re e\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$; both are computable. By the argument of (d), $o(e)$ (read in $W_A^{\mathcal L_O}$) is compatible with $D$ and has content in $\Theta$ iff $e$ (read in $W_A^{\mathcal L}$) is; and $f(e)$ read in $W_A^{\mathcal L}$ behaves as $e$ read in $W_A^{\mathcal L_O}$. The pushforward of $\mm$ under a computable map is a lower semicomputable semimeasure, hence $\le2^c\mm$; so $W_A^{\mathcal L}(\Theta;D)=\sum_e\mm(e)[P(o(e))]\le2^c\sum_j\mm(j)[P(j)]=2^cW_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)$, $P$ the compatibility-and-class predicate, and symmetrically with $f$. $K(o(e))\le K(e)+c$ and $K(f(e))\le K(e)+c$ give the $K_A$ statement.

(c) *Coin-free.* For an index $e$ let $f^O_e$ be proof search from $B\cup\re e$ restricted to observational inputs (accept $\varphi$ if $B\cup\re e\vdash\varphi$, reject if $\vdash\neg\varphi$, abstain on non-observational inputs); then $\Gamma_{f^O_e}=T_O(\re e)$, consistent with $B$ iff $\re e$ is. TF Thm 2.2 (`sfi` Thm 7.1) maps every consistent labeller $f$, at length cost $c$ and injectively, to the decider (resp. enumerator) of the padded set $\mathrm{Pad}(q_f)$, whose members are conjunction-powers of members of $\Gamma_f$, with $\Cn(B\cup\mathrm{Pad}(q_f))=\Cn(B\cup\Gamma_f)$ and membership (resp. generation) within $t$ (TF Lemma 2.1(a)–(c), under (S)). For $f=f^O_e$ the padded set is observational and has the observational content of $\re e$. Summing over $e$ with the pushforward bound gives the lower bound; the upper bound holds because the observational restriction is a sub-sum of $W_A^{{\rm chk},t}$ (resp. ${\rm gen}$), and a penalised hypothesis is an ordinary one with the same theory (`sfi` Prop 7.7 for the class form). *One tape.* `sfi` Thm 3.4's padded random decider $W_{\rm dec}{}^\frown q$ with $q$ := "yes on $\chi$ iff $\chi$ is observational and $B\cup A^G_\omega\vdash\chi$" (dovetailed proof search over the tape) has, on every tape, the padded copy of $T_O(A^G_\omega)$ as its yes-set; it is observational, has the same observational content as $A^G_\omega$, and meets the time bound uniformly in $\omega$ under (S$_{\rm tape}$). The program-less tapes are matched by the decider of $\emptyset$ (as in `sfi` Prop 3.5). Sum as in (a).

*The consequences.* Divide the class bounds for $\Theta_1,\Theta_2$; divide the bounds for $D+(\varphi,b)$ and $D$; the semimeasure loss is $-\log M(D_n)+\log M(\emptyset)$. ∎

*Computed* (`t1_hempel_toy.out`): a propositional analogue with observational atoms $o_0,o_1,o_2$, theoretical atoms $t_0,t_1$, a theoretical background $(t_0\to o_0)\wedge(t_1\to\neg o_2)$, 40 random theory machines on 16 tapes. The projected machine $G^O$ has the same law on all 24480 (machine, observational data set) pairs checked and the same class-wise law on 21830 checks; the naive alternative that *drops* theoretical axioms instead of projecting changes the law for 37 of the 40 machines.

**Remark 2.2 (relation to `fundamentals` Cor 4.5, and what the theorem says).**
* `fundamentals` Thm 4.4/Cor 4.5 (scout F's F7′) is the inclusion case $B\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$ at the level of laws. Theorem 2.1 adds a theoretical background, the class-level statement (posterior odds of observational contents), the exact tape-wise identity (d) and the penalised versions; (b) and (c) are the coin-free forms of scout S's Thm 3.1, restated with proofs.
* No padding is needed for the one-tape and coin-free versions with free deduction: the r.e. set $T_O$ is enumerated directly. Padding (Craig's device) enters only when axiom membership must be decidable within a time bound, (c).
* *What it says about inductive systematisation.* The posterior predictive probability of every observational sentence, given observational data, is the same up to $2^{\pm2c}$ whether or not hypotheses may use theoretical vocabulary. So in the universal mixture, with deduction free, theoretical terms are not needed for inductive inference among observational sentences. This is a statement about the mixture, not about any single theory, and Hempel's own definition of inductive systematisation was not checked here (scout S); the philosophical assessment is `philosophy`'s.

## 3. Craig's construction in practice (target T2)

Throughout this section: literal data (Def 1.2), and $B$ is $R$-free and has a model in which the closed terms $t_w$ are pairwise distinct (for instance $B=\emptyset$; this is the hypothesis of `sfi` Thm 5.1 / scout S Thm 2.7). Powers: $\psi^{\wedge1}:=\psi$, $\psi^{\wedge(m+1)}:=\psi^{\wedge m}\wedge\psi$, with $\alpha\wedge\beta:=\neg(\alpha\to\neg\beta)$ in $\mathsf K$'s syntax; so $|\psi^{\wedge m}|\ge m|\psi|$.

**Lemma 3.1 (Craig's padding writes a number in unary).** Let $T$ be an axiom set and $f^{\rm can}_T$ *canonical proof search*: on input $\varphi$ it tests the binary strings in length-lexicographic order as codes of $\mathsf K$-derivations from $B\cup T$, in a code in which a derivation of symbol size $s$ has code length $\ge s$, spending at least one step per string, and stops at the first derivation of $\varphi$ or of $\neg\varphi$. Let $d(\varphi^b)$ be the least symbol size of a derivation of whichever of $\varphi,\neg\varphi$ is provable from the consistent set $B\cup T$.
* (a) ${\rm time}_{f^{\rm can}_T}(\varphi)\ge2^{d(\varphi^b)}-1$.
* (b) Let $A^C$ be the Craig set of $f^{\rm can}_T$ restricted to literal inputs, $A^C:=\{(\varphi_w^b)^{\wedge(k+1)}:f^{\rm can}_T$ decides $\varphi_w$ with $b$ in exactly $k$ steps$\}$, or its work-counter padded version $\mathrm{Pad}$ (TF Lemma 2.1). Every $\mathsf K$-derivation of $\varphi_w^b$ from $B\cup A^C$ contains a line of size $\ge2^{d(\varphi_w^b)}|\varphi_w^b|$; so its size, its material and its largest axiom instance are all at least that.
* (c) With Craig's original padding by the Gödel number $g$ of a derivation (the number whose binary expansion is $1$ followed by the code), $A^G:=\{(\varphi_w^b)^{\wedge g}\}$, the same bound holds: $g\ge2^{\ell}\ge2^{d}$, $\ell$ the code length.

**[proved; (b), (c) use Lemma 3.2; computed: scout S's `s2_craig_blowup.out`, rerun identical]**

*Proof.* (a) Every derivation of $\varphi^b$ has size $\ge d$, hence code length $\ge d$; no derivation of $\varphi^{1-b}$ exists. So all $2^0+2^1+\dots+2^{d-1}=2^d-1$ strings of length $<d$ are tested and fail. (b) By Lemma 3.2 the derivation cites a member of $A^C$ equivalent to $\varphi_w^b$; the only such member is $(\varphi_w^b)^{\wedge(k+1)}$ with $k\ge2^d-1$ by (a) (for $\mathrm{Pad}$ the counter is at least the simulated time). (c) Every member of $A^G$ equivalent to $\varphi_w^b$ is a power with $g\ge2^\ell$ factors, $\ell\ge d$; apply Lemma 3.2. ∎

(Scout S wrote $2^{\ell-1}$ in (c); with the leading-1 convention used there the bound is $2^\ell$. Either suffices.)

**Lemma 3.2 (re-check of SD Prop 4.6(i), slightly generalised).** Let $A$ be a set of sentences each logically equivalent to a literal $\pm\varphi_{w'}$, with $B\cup A$ consistent. If $B\cup F\vdash\varphi_w^b$ for a finite $F\subseteq A$, then $F$ contains a member equivalent to $\varphi_w^b$. **[proved]**

*Proof.* Suppose not. The members of $F$ are equivalent to literals; no two of them are complementary, since $B\cup F$ is consistent; those about $w$ (if any) are equivalent to $\varphi_w^{1-b}$. Take a model of $B$ in which the $t_{w'}$ are distinct and reinterpret $R$ (absent from $B$): put $t_{w'}$ into $R$ iff $R(t_{w'})$ is among the literals of $F$, and in addition put $t_w$ into $R$ iff $b=0$. This satisfies $B$, every literal of $F$ and $\neg\varphi_w^b$. ∎

SD's proof of Prop 4.6(i) is this argument for $B=\emptyset$ and the exact Craig set, where $(\varphi_w^b)^{\wedge(k+1)}$ is the *only* member equivalent to $\varphi_w^b$. Re-read and correct.

**Theorem 3.3 (run-padded axiomatisations fit exactly $\mathsf P$).** Call $A\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$ *run-padded* if each member is a power $\psi^{\wedge m}$ ($m\ge1$) of a literal $\psi\in\{\varphi_w,\neg\varphi_w\}$.

### 4.1 Proof padding (reading (b))

**Proposition 4.1 (elimination by proof padding).** Let $\mathcal L$ be finite, $B\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ decidable in polynomial time and closed under permutations of the parameters, and assume (F1). ### 4.2 The pairing gadget and reading (a1)

**The model $\mathfrak U$.** $\mathfrak U$ is the least set containing the words (the elements $t_w$ of $\mathfrak T$) and closed under a formal pairing $(a,b)\mapsto P(a,b)$, where $P$ is injective and its values are new (not words). $\mathtt e$, $\mathtt s_0$, $\mathtt s_1$ act on words as in $\mathfrak T$; on pairs $\mathtt s_0P(a,b):=a$ and $\mathtt s_1P(a,b):=P(b,a)$. $R$ is left free. Define in $\mathcal L_O$:

* $\theta_2(a,b):=\forall z\,(\mathrm{Pair}(z)\to(\mathtt s_0z=a\to(\mathtt s_0\mathtt s_1z=b\to R(z))))$ (scout S's $\theta_2$, written with nested implications); $\theta_k$ by nesting pairs.

*Proof.* (i) On words $\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1t_v=t_{11v}\ne t_v$; on pairs $\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1P(a,b)=\mathtt s_1P(b,a)=P(a,b)$; words are closed under $\mathtt s_a$. (ii) A pair $z=P(c,d)$ has $\mathtt s_0z=c$ and $\mathtt s_0\mathtt s_1z=\mathtt s_0P(d,c)=d$. (iii) By (ii) the premise singles out $z=P(a,b)$. ∎

### 4.3 The machine normal form: two-stack programs, stated backwards

Scout S's Prop 3.10(b) needed a three-tape machine normal form that was marked "standard; not written out", and its relation version used an 8-ary relation. The following normal form is written out in full, needs only a *binary* relation, and gives the observational version by the same gadget.

**Definition 4.4 (two-stack programs).** A *pattern* is $\pi=(r_\pi,\epsilon_r;\,l_\pi,\epsilon_l)$ with words $r_\pi,l_\pi$ and $\epsilon_r,\epsilon_l\in\{\mathrm{var},\mathrm{exact}\}$. A pair of words $(r,l)$ *matches* $\pi$ with values $(Z,Y)$ if $r=r_\pi Z$ (when $\epsilon_r=\mathrm{var}$; when $\epsilon_r=\mathrm{exact}$, $r=r_\pi$ and $Z$ is absent), and likewise $l=l_\pi Y$. A *rule* is a pair $\pi\Rightarrow\pi'$ of patterns with the same $\epsilon$'s (it is *linear*: each variable occurs once on each side); applied to $(r_\pi Z,l_\pi Y)$ it gives $(r_{\pi'}Z,l_{\pi'}Y)$. A *two-stack program* $P$ is a finite set of rules; it is *deterministic* if no pair of words matches the current patterns of two rules. Programs carry a *tag* letter $\eta\in\{0,1\}$ at the front of $l$ (0: acceptance run, 1: rejection run), which every rule preserves, followed by a fixed-length *state code* ${\rm st}(q)$; *halting prefixes* $h_{\rm acc}:=0\,{\rm st}({\rm ACC})$, $h_{\rm rej}:=1\,{\rm st}({\rm REJ})$; *start prefixes* $\eta\,{\rm st}(s)$.

The *backward theory* of $P$ in $\mathcal L_O\cup\{C\}$, $C$ a binary relation symbol, writing $C(uZ,vY)$ for $C(\mathtt s_u(Z),\mathtt s_v(Y))$:
$$T^C_P:=\{\forall Z\forall Y\,(C(\pi')\to C(\pi)):\ (\pi\Rightarrow\pi')\in P\}\cup\{\forall Z\forall Y\,C(Z,h_{\rm acc}Y),\ \forall Z\forall Y\,C(Z,h_{\rm rej}Y)\}$$
$$\cup\{\forall x\forall z\,(C(x,0\,{\rm st}(s)\,z)\to R(x)),\ \forall x\forall z\,(C(x,1\,{\rm st}(s)\,z)\to\neg R(x))\}$$
(quantifiers only over the variables present). Its *observational translation* $T^O_P$: replace each atom $C(a,b)$ by $\theta_2(a,b)$; prefix the matrix of every member by $\mathrm{Std}(v)\to\cdots$ for each of its universally quantified variables $v$; add $\mathrm{Std}(\mathtt e)$ and $\forall y(\mathrm{Std}(y)\to\mathrm{Std}(\mathtt s_ay))$ for $a=0,1$. $T^O_P$ is a finite set of $\mathcal L_O$-sentences.

*Why backwards.* A forward theory (start axiom, transition rules $C(\pi)\to C(\pi')$, output rule reading the input from a component) must keep the input word in the configuration until the end, which is what forced scout S's 8-ary relation (input, state, two stacks for each of three tapes). Stated backwards, $C$ means "this configuration leads to a halting configuration of the right kind", and the input is read only by the output rule, at the start configuration. Alternation is then direct (Prop 4.7).

**Lemma 4.5 (compilation: every verifier is a deterministic two-stack program).** Let $V$ be a verifier: a deterministic multitape Turing machine that halts on every pair $(x,z)$ of binary words with output acc, rej or $\bot$, such that no $x$ has both an accepting and a rejecting certificate ($f_V$ consistent); $X_{\rm acc}:=\{x:\exists z\,V(x,z)={\rm acc}\}$, $X_{\rm rej}$ likewise. There is a deterministic two-stack program $P_V$ such that for all words $x,z$ and both tags:
* (i) every rule is linear, the current patterns are pairwise disjoint, and no rule applies to a pair whose $l$ begins with a halting prefix (or with the code of the state $\bot$);
* (ii) the run from the *start pair* $(x,\,\eta\,{\rm st}(s)\,z)$ reaches a pair with prefix $h_{\rm acc}$ (for $\eta=0$) iff $V(x,z)={\rm acc}$, and one with prefix $h_{\rm rej}$ (for $\eta=1$) iff $V(x,z)={\rm rej}$;
* (iii) the run has $N=O(T_1+|x|+|z|)$ steps, every pair on it has length $O(S_1+|x|+|z|)$, and each step changes $O(1)$ letters, where $T_1,S_1$ are the time and space of a one-tape simulation of $V$ (so $T_1$ is polynomial in $V$'s time and $S_1=O(S_V+|x|+|z|)$).

**[proved, given the one-tape simulation of multitape machines (known; textbook: Hartmanis–Stearns 1965, Hopcroft–Ullman Thm 7.2, Sipser Thm 3.13); computed: `t4_two_stack.out` Part A]**

*Construction.* **(1) The one-tape machine.** By the one-tape simulation theorem there is a deterministic one-tape machine $M_1$ that, started on the tape $\mathtt Z_{z_m}\cdots\mathtt Z_{z_1}\,\#\,x_1\cdots x_n$ (the certificate reversed, in letters $\mathtt Z_0,\mathtt Z_1$, then a separator, then the input) with the head on cell 0, halts in state ACC, REJ or $\bot$ according to $V(x,z)$. (Rearranging the input format costs $O((|x|+|z|)^2)$ steps on one tape; the simulation is polynomial in $V$'s time and linear in its space.) Bring $M_1$ to **normal form**: its tape alphabet $\Gamma$ (without the blank $\square$) contains $\#,0,1,\mathtt Z_0,\mathtt Z_1$ and a symbol $\mathtt B$ that $M_1$ treats exactly as $\square$; wherever $M_1$ would write $\square$ it writes $\mathtt B$. So every transition writes a symbol of $\Gamma$; moving left on cell 0 leaves the head there (the usual convention). Then, on every run from a start tape, the non-blank cells form an initial segment $[0,n)$ and the head position $h$ satisfies $h\le n$: a step at $h<n$ keeps both properties; a step at $h=n$ writes a symbol of $\Gamma$ there, so $n$ grows by one and $h\le n$ still holds.

**(2) Coding.** Fix injective codes ${\rm st}:Q\to\{0,1\}^{k_Q}$ (states of $M_1$ and the phase states $s,s_0,s_1,s_2,s_3$) and ${\rm blk}:\Gamma\to\{0,1\}^{k_B}$. A configuration of $M_1$ with state $q$, tape $a_0\cdots a_{n-1}\square\square\cdots$ and head $h\le n$ is coded, with tag $\eta$, as
$$\llbracket q,a,h\rrbracket_\eta:=\big({\rm blk}(a_h)\cdots{\rm blk}(a_{n-1}),\ \ \eta\,{\rm st}(q)\,{\rm blk}(a_{h-1})\cdots{\rm blk}(a_0)\big):$$
$r$ holds the cells from the head on, $l$ the tag, the state and the cells to the left of the head, nearest first; $r=\varepsilon$ means the head reads $\square$.

**(3) Rules** (for each tag $\eta$; $c(q):=\eta\,{\rm st}(q)$; $Z,Y$ the variables):
* *start:* $(Z;\,c(s)Y)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(\#)Z;\,c(s_0)Y)$ (push the separator onto $r$, above the raw input);
* *phase 0 (certificate):* $(Z;\,c(s_0)aY)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_a)Z;\,c(s_0)Y)$ for $a\in\{0,1\}$ (a raw certificate letter below the state code becomes a block on $r$), and $(Z;\,c(s_0)\,|\,\mathrm{exact})\Rightarrow(Z;\,c(s_1)\,|\,\mathrm{exact})$;
* *phase 1 (to the separator):* $({\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_a)Z;\,c(s_1)Y)\Rightarrow(Z;\,c(s_1){\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_a)Y)$, and $({\rm blk}(\#)Z;\,c(s_1)Y)\Rightarrow(Z;\,c(s_2){\rm blk}(\#)Y)$;
* *phase 2 (input):* $(aZ;\,c(s_2)Y)\Rightarrow(Z;\,c(s_2){\rm blk}(a)Y)$ for $a\in\{0,1\}$, and $(\varepsilon\,|\,\mathrm{exact};\,c(s_2)Y)\Rightarrow(\varepsilon\,|\,\mathrm{exact};\,c(s_3)Y)$;
* *phase 3 (rewind):* $(Z;\,c(s_3){\rm blk}(d)Y)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(d)Z;\,c(s_3)Y)$ for $d\in\{\mathtt Z_0,\mathtt Z_1,\#,0,1\}$, and $(Z;\,c(s_3)\,|\,\mathrm{exact})\Rightarrow(Z;\,c(q_0)\,|\,\mathrm{exact})$;
* *the machine:* for each transition $\delta(q,a)=(q',b,{\rm d})$, with read pattern $\rho:={\rm blk}(a)Z$ if $a\in\Gamma$ and $\rho:=\varepsilon\,|\,\mathrm{exact}$ if $a=\square$ (write $\rho'$ for $Z$, resp. $\varepsilon\,|\,\mathrm{exact}$):
  * ${\rm d}=R$: $(\rho;\,c(q)Y)\Rightarrow(\rho';\,c(q'){\rm blk}(b)Y)$;
  * ${\rm d}=S$: $(\rho;\,c(q)Y)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(b)\rho';\,c(q')Y)$;
  * ${\rm d}=L$: for each $e\in\Gamma$, $(\rho;\,c(q){\rm blk}(e)Y)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(e){\rm blk}(b)\rho';\,c(q')Y)$, and at the left end $(\rho;\,c(q)\,|\,\mathrm{exact})\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(b)\rho';\,c(q')\,|\,\mathrm{exact})$.

*Proof.* (i) Linearity is visible in the list. Disjointness: rules with different tags or state codes differ in a fixed-length prefix of $l$; within one state, the read patterns ${\rm blk}(a)Z$ (fixed length, injective code) and $\varepsilon|\mathrm{exact}$ are pairwise disjoint, as are, within one left-moving transition, the neighbour patterns ${\rm blk}(e)Y$ and the exact left-end pattern; in phase 0 the raw-letter patterns $c(s_0)aY$ and the exact $c(s_0)$ are disjoint, and in phase 2 the raw-letter patterns $aZ$ and the exact $\varepsilon$. There are no rules for ACC, REJ, $\bot$. (ii) *Phases.* From $(x,\,c(s)z)$: the start rule gives $({\rm blk}(\#)x,\,c(s_0)z)$; phase 0 moves the letters of $z$, first letter first, onto $r$, ending in $({\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_{z_m})\cdots{\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_{z_1}){\rm blk}(\#)x,\ c(s_1))$; phase 1 moves these blocks to $l$, the separator last, ending in $(x,\ c(s_2){\rm blk}(\#){\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_{z_1})\cdots{\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_{z_m}))$; phase 2 converts the raw letters of $x$; phase 3 moves all blocks back to $r$ and ends exactly at the left end in $\llbracket q_0,\ \mathtt Z_{z_m}\cdots\mathtt Z_{z_1}\#x,\ 0\rrbracket_\eta$, the code of $M_1$'s start configuration. *Steps.* For a configuration with $h\le n$ and a non-blank initial segment, exactly one rule applies to its code, namely the rule of the transition taken, with the neighbour case given by the cell $h-1$ (or the left end), and its result is the code of the successor: for ${\rm d}=R$ the written $b$ moves to the top of $l$; for $S$ it replaces the top of $r$; for $L$ the left neighbour $e$ moves from $l$ to $r$ above $b$; at $a=\square$ ($r=\varepsilon$) the written $b$ becomes the new last cell. By (1) the invariant is preserved. So the run of $P_V$ from the start pair passes through the codes of $M_1$'s run and stops exactly when $M_1$ halts; the tag is preserved, so a 0-tagged run can reach $h_{\rm acc}$ only through ACC, and a 1-tagged run $h_{\rm rej}$ only through REJ. (iii) Phases take $2|z|+|x|+(|z|+|x|+1)+O(1)$ steps, the machine $T_1$; every rule changes $O(k_Q+k_B)$ letters. ∎

### 4.4 Finite theories from two-stack programs

**Theorem 4.6 (one binary theoretical relation suffices; and its observational translation).** Let $V$ be a verifier as in Lemma 4.5 and $P=P_V$. Let $C^{\mathfrak T}\subseteq\mathfrak T^2$ be the least set containing all pairs with $l$-prefix $h_{\rm acc}$ or $h_{\rm rej}$ and closed under the rules read backwards (if the result of a rule instance is in the set, so is its current pair).
* (i) **Relation version.** $(\mathfrak T,C^{\mathfrak T},R:=X_{\rm acc})\models T^C_P\cup\Gamma_{f_V}$ and $(\mathfrak T,C^{\mathfrak T},R:=\{w:w\notin X_{\rm rej}\})\models T^C_P$. So $T^C_P$ is a finite theory in $\mathcal L_O\cup\{C\}$, $C$ **binary**, about the named objects, consistent with $\Gamma_{f_V}$, and $T^C_P\vdash R(t_w)$ iff $w\in X_{\rm acc}$, $T^C_P\vdash\neg R(t_w)$ iff $w\in X_{\rm rej}$.
* (ii) **Observational version.** In $\mathfrak U$ put $R(t_w):=[w\in X_{\rm acc}]$, $R(P(a,b)):=[(a,b)\in C^{\mathfrak T}]$ for words $a,b$, and $R$ false on all other pairs. Then $\mathfrak U\models T^O_P\cup\Gamma_{f_V}$ (and with $R(t_w):=[w\notin X_{\rm rej}]$, $\mathfrak U\models T^O_P$). So $T^O_P$ is a finite set of $\mathcal L_O$-sentences, consistent with $\Gamma_{f_V}$, proving exactly the literals of $X_{\rm acc}$ and the negated literals of $X_{\rm rej}$.
* (iii) **Derivations.** If $V(w,c)\in\{{\rm acc},{\rm rej}\}$, the corresponding literal has a $\mathsf K$-derivation from $T^C_P$ (resp. $T^O_P$) by A4 and MP only, with $O(N)$ (resp. $O(N+\ell)$) lines, $N$ the number of steps of Lemma 4.5(iii) and $\ell$ the largest length of a pair on the run, each line of size $O(\ell+1)$; its nonlogical instances are members of the theory (of size $\le c_V$, total $\le\Sigma_V$).

Membership is linear-time (finite sets of sentences) and (F1) holds (sentences).

## 5. Vocabulary or ontology (target T4)

Residuals: for $X\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ and a word $v$, $X_v:=\{u:uv\in X\}$. In the term model, $R(t_{uv})=R(\mathtt s_u t_v)$, so $X_v$ is what the subtree below $t_v$ sees. $X$ is regular iff it has finitely many residuals: the $X_v$ are the right quotients of $X$, i.e. the reversals of the left quotients of the reversal of $X$, and Myhill–Nerode applies to the reversal [known; standard].

### 5.1 Finite theories with unary theoretical vocabulary see only finite-state regularities

Relational form. Let $L^{\rm rel}$ have a unary $\mathrm{Root}$, binary $S_0,S_1$, unary $R,P_1,\dots,P_k$ and unary $K_1,\dots,K_j$. An $\mathcal L_O\cup\{P_i\}\cup\{\mathtt c_1,\dots,\mathtt c_j\}$-structure $\mathfrak A$ ($\mathtt c_i$ constants) gives $\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}$: $\mathrm{Root}=\{\mathtt e\}$, $S_a$ the graph of $\mathtt s_a$, $K_i=\{\mathtt c_i\}$. Every sentence $\psi$ has a relational translation $\psi^{\rm rel}$ (unnest the terms) with $\mathfrak A\models\psi$ iff $\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}\models\psi^{\rm rel}$ [known; standard]. $\equiv_q$ is agreement on all sentences of quantifier rank $\le q$; over a finite relational signature there are finitely many $\equiv_q$-classes, and $\equiv_q$ holds iff the duplicator wins the $q$-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game [known: Ehrenfeucht 1961, Fraïssé 1954; textbook form, e.g. Ebbinghaus–Flum, *Finite Model Theory*; not re-checked in this session].

**Lemma 5.2 (composition).** (a) For relational structures in one finite signature, $\mathfrak A_1\equiv_q\mathfrak B_1$ and $\mathfrak A_2\equiv_q\mathfrak B_2$ imply $\mathfrak A_1\sqcup\mathfrak A_2\equiv_q\mathfrak B_1\sqcup\mathfrak B_2$, where $\sqcup$ is the disjoint union with each relation interpreted as the union. (b) If $\mathfrak A,\mathfrak B$ arise from $\mathfrak A',\mathfrak B'$ (same domains) by defining each relation by a quantifier-free formula, then $\mathfrak A'\equiv_q\mathfrak B'$ implies $\mathfrak A\equiv_q\mathfrak B$. **[proved, given the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem]**

*Proof.* (a) The duplicator answers a move in a component with her winning strategy for that component's game. Within a component the chosen tuples form a partial isomorphism; no relation holds across components on either side, and equality across components fails on both sides. (b) Substituting the defining formulas turns a rank-$q$ sentence about $\mathfrak A$ into a rank-$q$ sentence about $\mathfrak A'$. ∎

**Theorem 5.1 (unary theoretical vocabulary: only regular regularities).** Let $T$ be a finite set of sentences in $\mathcal L_O\cup\{P_1,\dots,P_k\}\cup\{\mathtt c_1,\dots,\mathtt c_j\}$ (unary theoretical predicates and constants; $k,j\ge0$) that is **compatible with the named structure** ($T\cup\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$ consistent; in particular if $T$ is about the named objects). If $T\vdash\varphi_w^{[w\in X]}$ for every word $w$, then $X$ is regular. Quantitatively, $X$ has at most $N_q+|E|$ residuals, where $q$ is the quantifier rank of $(\bigwedge T)^{\rm rel}$, $N_q$ the number of $\equiv_q$-classes of $L^{\rm rel}\cup\{\mathrm{Rt},H\}$-structures, and $E$ the set of suffixes of the words $w_i$ with $\mathtt c_i=t_{w_i}$ in the model below, together with $\varepsilon$. **[proved, given the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem; no use of Rabin's theorem]**

*Proof.* Take $\mathfrak A\models T\cup\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$. The closed terms denote pairwise distinct elements, and $R^{\mathfrak A}(t_w)=[w\in X]$ because $T$ proves the literals. For a word $v\ne\varepsilon$, $v=av'$, let $\mathrm{Sub}_v:=\{t_{uv}:u\in\{0,1\}^*\}$ (standard elements) and:
* $\mathfrak S_v$: the restriction of $\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}$ to $\mathrm{Sub}_v$, with a new unary $\mathrm{Rt}:=\{t_v\}$ (and $H:=\emptyset$);
* $\mathfrak C_v$: the restriction to the rest of the domain, with a new unary $H:=\{t_{v'}\}$ (and $\mathrm{Rt}:=\emptyset$).

*One edge between the parts.* $\mathrm{Sub}_v$ is closed under $\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1$; $\mathtt e\notin\mathrm{Sub}_v$ ($\mathtt e$ is not in the range of $\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1$). If $\mathtt s_b y=t_{uv}$ with $y\notin\mathrm{Sub}_v$, then $u=\varepsilon$ (otherwise $u=u'b$ and, by injectivity and disjoint ranges in $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$, $y=t_{u'v}\in\mathrm{Sub}_v$), so $b=a$ and $y=t_{v'}$. Hence $\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}$ is obtained from $\mathfrak C_v\sqcup\mathfrak S_v$ by redefining $S_a(x,y)$ as $S_a(x,y)\vee(H(x)\wedge\mathrm{Rt}(y))$ and forgetting $H,\mathrm{Rt}$: quantifier-free.

*The subtrees are alike up to labels.* For $v,v'\notin E$ the map $t_{uv}\mapsto t_{uv'}$ is an isomorphism of the $\{\mathrm{Root},S_0,S_1,\mathrm{Rt},K_1,\dots,K_j\}$-reducts of $\mathfrak S_v$ and $\mathfrak S_{v'}$ ($\mathrm{Root}$ and the $K_i$ are empty on both: a constant lies in $\mathrm{Sub}_v$ only if it is some $t_{w_i}$ with $v$ a suffix of $w_i$; nonstandard denotations lie in no $\mathrm{Sub}_v$).

*Transplanting.* Suppose $X$ has more than $N_q+|E|$ residuals. Then there are $v\ne v'$ outside $E$ with $X_v\ne X_{v'}$ and $\mathfrak S_v\equiv_q\mathfrak S_{v'}$. Let $\mathfrak A^*$ be $\mathfrak A$ with the labels on $\mathrm{Sub}_v$ replaced by those copied from $\mathrm{Sub}_{v'}$: $R^*(t_{uv}):=R^{\mathfrak A}(t_{uv'})$, $P_i^*(t_{uv}):=P_i^{\mathfrak A}(t_{uv'})$, everything else unchanged (in particular the $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-reduct and the constants). Then $\mathfrak S^*_v\cong\mathfrak S_{v'}\equiv_q\mathfrak S_v$ and $\mathfrak C^*_v=\mathfrak C_v$, so by Lemma 5.2, $(\mathfrak A^*)^{\rm rel}\equiv_q\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}$ and $\mathfrak A^*\models T$. Its reduct still satisfies $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$, so, as $T$ proves the literals, $R^*(t_{uv})=[uv\in X]$ for all $u$. But $R^*(t_{uv})=[uv'\in X]$. Hence $X_v=X_{v'}$, a contradiction. ∎

*Why the hypotheses matter.* Unary predicates and constants are *labels*: they do not connect the subtree to its context, so the composition lemma applies. A binary theoretical relation can connect any two subtrees (Thm 4.6(i)); objects that violate $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$ (pairs, with $\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$) can carry information between subtrees through $\mathtt s_0$, which is not injective on them (Thm 4.6(ii)); and a finite *set* is essential, since infinite sets of padded literals are true of the named objects and fit $\mathsf P$ (Thm 3.3).

*Relation to scout S's proof.* Scout S translated $T$ into monadic second-order logic over the binary tree and used Rabin's tree theorem, plus a reduction from tree automata to word automata proved there. The present proof uses only the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem, needs only compatibility with $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$ (models may have nonstandard elements), and allows constants. Rabin's theorem itself (Rabin 1969, *Trans. AMS* 141, 1–35; bibliographic data checked by scout S) is no longer used.

### 5.2 Every regular regularity has a short observational theory

**Theorem 5.3 (signature theories; scout S Thm 3.12(b)).** For every regular $X$ there is a finite $T\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$, true in $(\mathfrak T,X)$, such that every datum has a $\mathsf K$-derivation from $T$ with $O(|w|)$ lines, each of size $O(|w|)$ (constants depending on $X$). Under (a1), (a2), (b2) the needed budgets are linear, under (b) quadratic. **[proved; computed: `t4_regular.out` Part A (two further languages), scout S's s3 Part C]**

*Proof.* Let $n$ be the number of residuals of $X$; write $A/a:=\{u:ua\in A\}$, so $X_v/a=X_{av}$. Let $E_j$ be the equivalence "agree on all words of length $<j$" on the finite set $\{X_v\}$. Then $E_{j+1}(A,A')$ iff ($\varepsilon\in A\Leftrightarrow\varepsilon\in A'$) and $E_j(A/a,A'/a)$ for $a=0,1$, so $E_{j+1}$ is a fixed function of $E_j$; once $E_j=E_{j+1}$ the sequence is constant; each strict refinement adds a class; so $E_{n-1}=E_\infty$, which is equality. Hence the window $\{0,1\}^{<W}$ with $W:=\max(n-1,1)$ (or any smaller window that separates the residuals and contains $\varepsilon$) determines $X_v$. Put ${\rm sig}(v):=X_v\cap\{0,1\}^{<W}$; ${\rm sig}(av)$ is a function $\sigma\mapsto\sigma_a$ of ${\rm sig}(v)$. With $S_\sigma(x):=\bigwedge_{|u|<W}R(\mathtt s_ux)^{\sigma(u)}$ let
$$T:=\{S_{{\rm sig}(\varepsilon)}(\mathtt e)\}\cup\{\forall x\,(S_\sigma(x)\to S_{\sigma_a}(\mathtt s_ax)):\sigma\text{ realised},\ a\in\{0,1\}\}.$$
$S_\sigma(t_v)$ holds in $(\mathfrak T,X)$ iff ${\rm sig}(v)=\sigma$, so $T$ is true there. Derive $S_{{\rm sig}(v)}(t_v)$ for the suffixes $v$ of $w$ from the inside out (one A4 instance and MPs per letter) and read off the conjunct $u=\varepsilon$, which is $R(t_w)^{[w\in X]}$. (The checker uses the equivalent curried form with one literal per conclusion, so that only A4 and MP occur.) ∎

### 5.3 The table

**Corollary 5.4 (vocabulary or ontology).** On literal sequences, the fit classes of **finite** theories ($B=\emptyset$; budgets as stated; membership is linear-time for finite sets) are:

| finite theories | (a2), (b2): any unbounded budget | (b): polynomial | (a1): polynomial |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathcal L_O$ + unary predicates + constants, compatible with the named structure | REG | REG | REG |
| $\mathcal L_O$ + **one binary** relation, about the named objects | all decidable | $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ | $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ |
| $\mathcal L_O$ only, quantifying over unnamed objects (inconsistent with $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$, Prop 9.1(b)) | all decidable | $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ | $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ |
| all finite theories, any vocabulary | all decidable | $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ | $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ |

**[proved: row 1 Thm 5.1 (upper bound, any budget, also with free deduction) and Thm 5.3; rows 2–3 Thm 4.6 and Prop 4.7; row 4 upper bounds SD Cor 3.2, Cor 4.4 (all hypotheses), lower bounds rows 2–3; CKS and the one-tape simulation as stated there]**

So a finite theory fits a non-regular regularity only if it has a polyadic theoretical relation or quantifies over objects that do not obey the observational laws of the named objects. Either suffices, at the same fit classes, and the translation between them (Thm 4.6(ii)) costs a constant factor in size. Unary theoretical predicates and theoretical constants do not help.

**Corollary 5.5 (with free deduction too).** For a decidable non-regular $X$, let $K^{\rm named}_{\rm fin}(D)$ be the least $K(\psi)$ over single sentences $\psi$ in $\mathcal L_O$ plus any finitely many unary predicates and constants, compatible with the named structure and proving $\psi_D$. Then $K^{\rm named}_{\rm fin}(D^X_n)\to\infty$, while $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_O\cup\{C\}}(D^X_n)\le K(V)+c$ and $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_O}(D^X_n)\le K(V)+c$ for a decider $V$ of $X$. **[proved]**

*Proof.* By Thm 5.1 no admissible sentence proves all of $D^X$; each one fails some datum, and only finitely many sentences have $K\le j$. The upper bounds are the conjunctions of $T^C_{P_V}$ and $T^O_{P_V}$, computable from $V$. ∎

**Remark 5.6 (infinite hypotheses about the named objects).** For *infinite* observational axiom sets true of the named objects (with polynomial membership), the fit classes at polynomial budgets are: exactly $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ under (b) (certificate sets are true of the named objects; the class of all hypotheses bounds it); under (a1), at least $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ and every $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$-hard set in $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ (Prop 9.4; scout S had one specific complete set), at most $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$; equality is open. The dichotomy of Cor 5.4 is a statement about finite theories, the hypotheses whose own axioms must be short.

**Remark 5.7 (what the section says).**
* *Vocabulary is cheap to eliminate; vocabulary and ontology together are not.* Under every reading of bounded deduction, theoretical vocabulary can be eliminated at polynomial cost (§4); the good eliminations store the content in objects no observational term names. A finite theory confined to the observational vocabulary *and* to the laws of the named objects expresses only finite-state regularities.
* *Ramsey and pairing.* Ramsey sentences eliminate theoretical predicates by quantifying over them in second order; the pairing gadget eliminates them in first order by quantifying over objects (Lemma 4.2), and Prop 9.1(b) shows the price: the observational theory must assert that such objects exist.
* *Caveats.* "Named object" is a formal stand-in for "observable thing". The observational language is poor (a free unary structure: observation sequences). With one binary observable relation Thm 5.1 fails (Prop 9.3); with only unary observables it survives (Prop 9.2). That observation terms can apply to unobservable things (the observational $R$ applied to pairs) is Putnam's point against identifying the observational/theoretical vocabulary line with the observable/unobservable line (Putnam 1962, "What theories are not"; content from the SEP entry "Theoretical Terms in Science", as recorded by scout S; known, unverified here).

## 6. Free deduction and finite theories (target T5)

$K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L}(D)$, $W_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L}(D)$: least $K(\psi)$, resp. $\sum\mm(\psi)$, over single sentences $\psi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ with $B\cup\{\psi\}$ consistent and $\vdash\psi_D$ (NOTATION §3.3). In this section $B=\emptyset$.

### 6.1 Infinity

$\mathcal L_{\rm mon}:=\{=\}\cup\{P_i:i\in\mathbb N\}$ (unary predicates), $\mathcal L_f:=\mathcal L_{\rm mon}\cup\{f\}$ ($f$ unary function). $\mathrm{Inf}_n:=\exists x_1\cdots\exists x_n\bigwedge_{i<j}x_i\ne x_j$; $D_n:=\{(\mathrm{Inf}_m,1):m\le n\}$, so $\psi_{D_n}\equiv\mathrm{Inf}_n$. $\iota_f:=\forall x\forall y(fx=fy\to x=y)\wedge\exists y\forall x\,fx\ne y$.

**Lemma 6.1 (quantitative finite model property of monadic logic; scout N1's B1.1).** If $\psi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}$ uses $k$ predicate symbols, has quantifier rank $q$ and has a model, then it has one with at most $2^k\max(q,1)$ elements. For $k=0$ all sets of size $\ge\max(q,1)$ satisfy the same rank-$q$ sentences. **[proved, given the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem; computed: `t5_monadic.out` Part A (three predicates), scout N1's c2 Parts A, B (rerun identical)]**

*Proof* (scout N1's, re-checked). Let $M\models\psi$, $q':=\max(q,1)$; partition $M$ into the $2^k$ cells $C_\tau$ by the values of the $k$ predicates, and let $M'\subseteq M$ keep $\min(|C_\tau|,q')$ elements of each cell ($M'\ne\emptyset$). The duplicator wins the $q$-round game on $(M,M')$ keeping the invariant "chosen pairs lie in the same cell, and $a_i=a_j\iff b_i=b_j$": a repeated element is answered by its partner; a fresh element in cell $\tau$ by a fresh element of $\tau$ on the other side, which exists — if $|C_\tau|\ge q'$ both sides have $\ge q'\ge q$ elements of $\tau$ and fewer than $q$ are chosen; if $|C_\tau|<q'$ both sides have exactly $|C_\tau|$ elements of $\tau$ and the chosen ones are matched bijectively. The invariant gives a partial isomorphism for $=$ and the $P_i$. So $M'\equiv_qM$ and $M'\models\psi$. ∎

**Theorem 6.2 (infinity needs a theoretical function; scout N1's B1).**
* (a) If $\psi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}$ is consistent and $\psi\vdash\mathrm{Inf}_n$, then $n\le N(\psi):=2^{k(\psi)}\max(q(\psi),1)$. No consistent finite monadic theory proves all of $D_\infty$.
* (b) $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}(D_n)=\min_{m\ge n}K(m)\pm c$. So $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}(D_n)\to\infty$, and $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}(D_n)=o(G(n))$ for every computable unbounded nondecreasing $G$.

* (d) Under the prior $\mm$ on $\Sent_{\mathcal L_f}$, the posterior probability, given $D_n$, that the finite hypothesis does not use $f$ is at most $2^c\sum_{j\ge n}\mm(j)/\mm(\iota_f)\to0$.
* (e) $K_A^{\{=\}}(D_n)\le c$ (the r.e. theory $\{\mathrm{Inf}_m:m\ge1\}$).

**[proved]**

*Proof.* (a) Lemma 6.1: a model with $\le N(\psi)$ elements. (b) $N(\psi)\ge n$ is computable from $\psi$, so $K(\psi)\ge K(N(\psi))-c\ge\min_{m\ge n}K(m)-c$; conversely $\mathrm{Inf}_m$ ($m\ge n$) is consistent, proves $\mathrm{Inf}_n$ and has $K\le K(m)+c$. With $g:=\lfloor2^{G/r}\rfloor$ this is $\le2G(n)/r+c''$ for every $r$, hence $o(G)$. *Proof.* (a), (c) as stated (Lemma 6.1 with $k=0$). ### 6.2 Peano arithmetic

**Theorem 6.4 (PA's theorems as data; scout N1's B2).** $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$; $D_\infty$ lists the theorems of $\PA$, labelled true, in the order of an effective enumeration of $\PA$-derivations (every axiom of $\PA$ occurs).
* (a) $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_{\rm ar}}(D_n)\to\infty$ and $W_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_{\rm ar}}(D_n)\to0$; and $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_{\rm ar}}(D_n)\le K(n)+c$.
* (b) $K_A^{\mathcal L_{\rm ar}}(D_n)\le K(\PA)+c=O(1)$.
* (c) In the three-sorted extension $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}^+$ of Theorem 6.5, $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_{\rm ar}^+}(D_n)\le c$, and the posterior over finite $\mathcal L^+_{\rm ar}$-theories puts mass $\to1$ on theories that use the new sorts. The winning theory adds no $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-content: its $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-consequences are exactly $\PA$'s. The classical instance is $\mathrm{ACA}_0$ in the two-sorted language of second-order arithmetic, finitely axiomatisable with the same first-order consequences as $\PA$ [known, unverified here: Simpson, *Subsystems of Second Order Arithmetic*].
* (d) A theoretical *predicate* on the same domain also gives $O(1)$: $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$ plus compositional truth clauses for a predicate $\mathrm{Tr}$ plus $\forall\varphi(\mathrm{PA\text{-}Ax}(\varphi)\to\mathrm{Tr}(\varphi))$ is a finite theory, true in $\mathbb N$, proving every axiom of $\PA$.

**[(a) proved, given (KL) below (known, unverified here) and $\mathrm{Con}(\PA)$; (b) proved; (c) proved via Thm 6.5 (the $\mathrm{ACA}_0$ instance known, unverified); (d) proof sketch]**

*(KL) No consistent $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-sentence proves every axiom of $\PA$.* This is the essential reflexivity of $\PA$ (Kreisel and Lévy 1968: for every sentence $\psi$, $\PA+\psi$ proves the consistency of each finite fragment of itself, in particular of $\{\psi\}$; Mostowski 1952 for reflexivity) combined with Gödel's second theorem: if $\psi\vdash\PA$, the $\PA+\psi$-proof of $\mathrm{Con}(\{\psi\})$ uses finitely many $\PA$-axioms, so $\psi\vdash\mathrm{Con}(\{\psi\})$, impossible for the consistent theory $\{\psi\}\supseteq\Q$. [known, unverified in this session; scout N1 gives a proof sketch via partial satisfaction predicates and cut elimination, not re-checked here]

*Proof.* (a) By (KL) every consistent $\psi$ fails to prove some axiom $\alpha_\psi$ of $\PA$, which occurs in $D_\infty$; so $\psi$ is incompatible with $D_n$ for large $n$. Only finitely many $\psi$ have $K(\psi)\le j$, which gives $K_{\rm fin}\to\infty$; dominated convergence ($\sum\mm\le1$) gives $W_{\rm fin}\to0$. The conjunction of the first $n$ data is consistent (given $\mathrm{Con}(\PA)$) and computable from $n$. (b) $\PA$ is r.e. (c) Theorem 6.5 with $W_e=\PA$ gives a single $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}^+$-sentence $F_e$ with $\Cn(F_e)\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_{\rm ar}}=\Cn(\PA)$; with (a), the posterior mass of $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-sentences tends to 0 while that of $F_e$ is a fixed positive number. (d) *Sketch.* For each standard sentence $\alpha$ the theory proves $\mathrm{Tr}(\ulcorner\alpha\urcorner)\leftrightarrow\alpha$ by external induction on $\alpha$ from the compositional clauses, using that $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$ proves the needed facts about the coding (that numerals evaluate correctly needs $\Sigma_1$-induction); with $\mathrm{Tr}(\ulcorner\alpha\urcorner)$ for every axiom $\alpha$ this proves every axiom. $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$ is finitely axiomatisable [known, unverified]. *Not written out:* the derivation of the Tarski biconditionals from the clauses over $\mathrm I\Sigma_1$. (Scout N1 also wrote that this theory "proves $\mathrm{Con}(\PA)$"; the usual argument needs induction for a formula containing $\mathrm{Tr}$, which the theory does not have, so that remark is withdrawn here. It is not needed.)

*Contrast with infinity.* In arithmetic a unary theoretical predicate suffices, because the domain already carries $+$ and $\cdot$, which code syntax; in pure equality unary predicates never help (Thm 6.2(a)).

### 6.3 r.e. axiom sets are finite theories with theoretical apparatus

**Theorem 6.5 (a Kleene-type theorem with complexity bounds; scout N1's B3, there a proof sketch).** Let $\mathcal L$ be a finite language (relation, function and constant symbols, with equality) and $B\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ decidable. Let $\mathcal L^+$ be the three-sorted extension (many-sorted first-order logic) with:
* sort $\mathsf o$ carrying $\mathcal L$;
* sort $\mathsf n$ with $0,S,+,\cdot,<$;
* sort $\mathsf a$ ("assignments") with $\mathrm{val}:\mathsf a\times\mathsf n\to\mathsf o$, $\mathrm{upd}:\mathsf a\times\mathsf n\times\mathsf o\to\mathsf a$, $\mathrm{den}:\mathsf n\times\mathsf a\to\mathsf o$, and a relation $\mathrm{Sat}\subseteq\mathsf n\times\mathsf a$.

There is a computable injective map $e\mapsto F_e$ from indices to single $\mathcal L^+$-sentences such that for every $e$
$$\Cn(B\cup\{F_e\})\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L}=\Cn(B\cup\re e)\qquad(\text{both sides inconsistent together}).$$
Consequently, for every finite data set $D$ in $\mathcal L$: $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}(D)=K_A^{\mathcal L}(D)\pm c$ and $2^{-c}W_A^{\mathcal L}(D)\le W_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}(D)\le2^cW_A^{\mathcal L}(D)$. **[proved]**

*Construction.* Fix a Gödel numbering of the $\mathcal L$-terms and -formulas (variables $x_0,x_1,\dots$) and $\Sigma_1$ formulas of $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$ that define in $\mathbb N$ the syntactic relations: $\mathrm{Var}(c,v)$ "$c$ codes $x_v$"; for each function symbol $g$ of arity $k$ (constants: $k=0$), $\mathrm{App}_g(c,d_1,\dots,d_k)$ "the $d_i$ code terms $t_i$ and $c$ codes $g(t_1,\dots,t_k)$"; for each relation symbol $Q$, $\mathrm{At}_Q(c,\vec d)$, and $\mathrm{Eq}(c,d,d')$, for atomic formulas over coded terms; $\mathrm{Neg}(c,d)$ "$d$ codes a formula $\chi$ and $c$ codes $\neg\chi$"; $\mathrm{Imp}(c,d,d')$; $\mathrm{All}(c,v,d)$ "$d$ codes $\chi$ and $c$ codes $\forall x_v\chi$"; $\mathrm{Enum}_e(c)$ "$c\in\re e$ and $c$ codes an $\mathcal L$-sentence". $F_e$ is the conjunction of:
* (Q) Robinson's $\Q$ on sort $\mathsf n$;
* (U) $\forall\alpha\forall v\forall x\,\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,v,x),v)=x$ and $\forall\alpha\forall v\forall w\forall x\,(v\ne w\to\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,v,x),w)=\mathrm{val}(\alpha,w))$;
* (D) $\forall c\forall v(\mathrm{Var}(c,v)\to\forall\alpha\,\mathrm{den}(c,\alpha)=\mathrm{val}(\alpha,v))$ and, for each $g$, $\forall c\forall\vec d(\mathrm{App}_g(c,\vec d)\to\forall\alpha\,\mathrm{den}(c,\alpha)=g(\mathrm{den}(d_1,\alpha),\dots,\mathrm{den}(d_k,\alpha)))$;
* (S) $\forall c\forall\vec d(\mathrm{At}_Q(c,\vec d)\to\forall\alpha(\mathrm{Sat}(c,\alpha)\leftrightarrow Q(\mathrm{den}(d_1,\alpha),\dots)))$ for each $Q$; the same for $\mathrm{Eq}$ with $=$; $\forall c\forall d(\mathrm{Neg}(c,d)\to\forall\alpha(\mathrm{Sat}(c,\alpha)\leftrightarrow\neg\mathrm{Sat}(d,\alpha)))$; the same for $\mathrm{Imp}$ with $\to$; $\forall c\forall v\forall d(\mathrm{All}(c,v,d)\to\forall\alpha(\mathrm{Sat}(c,\alpha)\leftrightarrow\forall x\,\mathrm{Sat}(d,\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,v,x))))$, $x$ of sort $\mathsf o$;
* (E) $\forall c\,(\mathrm{Enum}_e(c)\to\forall\alpha\,\mathrm{Sat}(c,\alpha))$, with $e$ written as a numeral (so $e\mapsto F_e$ is injective).

*Proof.* ($\supseteq$) Let $\varphi\in\re e$. $\Q$ proves the true $\Sigma_1$ sentence $\mathrm{Enum}_e(\underline{\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner})$, so $F_e\vdash\forall\alpha\,\mathrm{Sat}(\underline{\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner},\alpha)$. By external induction on terms $t$ and formulas $\chi$ whose variables are among $x_{v_1},\dots,x_{v_j}$: $F_e\vdash\forall\alpha\,\mathrm{den}(\underline{\ulcorner t\urcorner},\alpha)=t^\alpha$ and $F_e\vdash\forall\alpha(\mathrm{Sat}(\underline{\ulcorner\chi\urcorner},\alpha)\leftrightarrow\chi^\alpha)$, where $^\alpha$ replaces each $x_v$ by $\mathrm{val}(\alpha,\underline v)$. Each step instantiates one clause at the numerals of the immediate subterms or subformulas, whose guard is a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence and hence provable in $\Q$; the quantifier step uses (U) and $\Q\vdash\underline v\ne\underline w$ for $v\ne w$, which give $\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,\underline v,x),\underline v)=x$ and $\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,\underline v,x),\underline w)=\mathrm{val}(\alpha,\underline w)$, so the induction hypothesis at $\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,\underline v,x)$ turns $\forall x\,\mathrm{Sat}(\ulcorner\chi\urcorner,\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,\underline v,x))$ into $(\forall x_v\chi)^\alpha$. For the sentence $\varphi$, $\varphi^\alpha=\varphi$; sort $\mathsf a$ is nonempty, so $F_e\vdash\varphi$.
($\subseteq$) Let $\mathfrak M$ be an $\mathcal L$-structure with $\mathfrak M\models B\cup\re e$. Expand it: sort $\mathsf n$ is $\mathbb N$; sort $\mathsf a$ is $M^{\mathbb N}$ with $\mathrm{val}(\alpha,v):=\alpha(v)$, $\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,v,x):=\alpha[v\mapsto x]$; $\mathrm{den}(c,\alpha)$ the value of the term coded by $c$ under $\alpha$ (a fixed element if $c$ codes no term); $\mathrm{Sat}(c,\alpha)$ iff $c$ codes a formula $\chi$ with $\mathfrak M\models\chi[\alpha]$. The guards are evaluated in $\mathbb N$, where the $\Sigma_1$ formulas mean what they say; so each clause holds (the guards of $\mathrm{Neg},\mathrm{Imp},\mathrm{All}$ require the components to code formulas, so the clauses never compare a non-formula with a formula), and (E) holds because $\mathfrak M\models\re e$. Thus every model of $B\cup\re e$ expands to a model of $B\cup\{F_e\}$, and by completeness every $\mathcal L$-consequence of $B\cup\{F_e\}$ is in $\Cn(B\cup\re e)$. If $B\cup\re e$ is inconsistent, so is $B\cup\{F_e\}$ by ($\supseteq$); if it is consistent, a model expands, so $B\cup\{F_e\}$ is consistent.
*Complexities.* $K(F_e)\le K(e)+c$, and $F_e$ is compatible with $D$ iff $e$ is. Conversely the map $F\mapsto g(F)$, an index of $\{\varphi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L}:B\cup\{F\}\vdash\varphi\}$, is computable; $g(F)$ is compatible with $D$ iff $F$ is (the set contains $\neg\forall x\,x=x$ iff $B\cup\{F\}$ is inconsistent); and $K(g(F))\le K(F)+c$. For $W$: $\mm(F_e)\ge2^{-c}\mm(e)$ because $e\mapsto F_e$ is computable and injective, and $\sum_{F:g(F)=j}\mm(F)\le2^c\mm(j)$ because pushforwards of $\mm$ under computable maps are dominated by $\mm$. ∎

*Reading.* The Solomonoff-style prior over r.e. axiom sets in $\mathcal L$ is, up to a constant factor, the prior over *finite* theories in $\mathcal L^+$. The idealised r.e. class already contains theoretical vocabulary ($\mathrm{Sat}$, $\mathrm{val}$, $\mathrm{upd}$, $\mathrm{den}$) and theoretical entities (numbers, assignments) — which is why adding vocabulary costs nothing there (Thm 2.1). The finite-theory prior in $\mathcal L$ alone is the one under which theoretical terms visibly pay (Thm 6.2, 6.4, Cor 5.5). *Many sorts.* With one sort and sort predicates the same holds for the data relativised to the $\mathsf o$-sort, a faithful translation in the sense of `fundamentals` Thm 4.4, at $O(1)$ cost. *Comparison with Kleene (1952) and Craig–Vaught (1958)*: §10.

**Remark 6.6 (the one-tape caveat; scout N1's B4).** Theorem 6.5 is coin-free. For one-tape random *finite* theories, $M^{\mathcal L^+}_{\rm fin}(D):=\Unif\{\omega:$ the machine emits a finite $F_\omega$, consistent with $B$ and proving $\psi_D\}$, it fails: on the lottery stream of `logic` T1 (scout N1's Thm E1) every $F_\omega$ has an r.e. $\mathcal L$-content, which by E1(b) cannot prove all of $D_\infty$, so by continuity from above $M^{\mathcal L^+}_{\rm fin}(D_n)\to0$, while $M_A^{\mathcal L}(D_n)$ stays bounded below. **[proved, given `logic` T1 (scout N1's E1(a), (b))]** Random r.e. theories are strictly stronger than random finite theories with any amount of theoretical apparatus.

## 7. Ramsey and Craig side by side; the hierarchy (target T6)

**Proposition 7.1 (infinity: Ramsey, Craig and a theoretical function).** In pure equality:
* (a) the Ramsey sentence $\exists f\,\iota_f$ of the theory $\{\iota_f\}$ — one existential second-order sentence, with no theoretical constant — has (in standard semantics, in ZFC) exactly the infinite sets as models;
* (b) $\{\mathrm{Inf}_m:m\ge1\}$ has exactly the infinite sets as models, and its consequences form a complete theory;
* (c) so $\exists f\,\iota_f$ and $\{\mathrm{Inf}_m\}$ (Craig's form: a first-order observational axiomatisation, necessarily infinite by Thm 6.2(a)) have the same first-order $\{=\}$-consequences, and no finite first-order theory in $\{=\}$, nor in any monadic extension, has them;
* (d) the first-order theory $\{\iota_f\}$ in the extended language $\{=,f\}$ is finite and has them as its $\{=\}$-consequences.

**[proved; (a) uses the axiom of choice (every infinite set has a countable subset, hence an injective non-surjective self-map)]**

*Proof.* (a) If $f$ is injective and not surjective on $A$, then $A$ is infinite (Thm 6.2(c)); conversely an infinite $A$ has a countable subset $\{a_0,a_1,\dots\}$, and $a_i\mapsto a_{i+1}$, identity elsewhere, is injective and misses $a_0$. (b) The first claim is clear; completeness: by Lemma 6.1 with $k=0$, all sets of size $\ge q$ satisfy the same rank-$q$ sentences, so all infinite sets are elementarily equivalent. (c), (d) Same models, hence the same first-order consequences; Thm 6.2(a) and (c). ∎

So on this example **Ramsey elimination** keeps the theory finite but leaves first-order logic; **Craig elimination** stays first-order but gives up finiteness; **a finite first-order observational theory does not exist**; and a theoretical *function* (not a predicate) is what a finite first-order theory needs. In the inducers: $K_{\rm fin}^{\{=\}}\to\infty$, $K_A^{\{=\}}=O(1)$, $K_{\rm fin}^{\{=,f\}}=O(1)$ (Thm 6.2).

**Proposition 7.2 (factoring an inference graph; scout P's Prop 5.1, re-proved).** Let $\mathcal L$ contain unary $A_1,\dots,A_n$, $B_1,\dots,B_m$; $T_{\rm dir}:=\{\forall x(A_ix\to B_jx)\}_{i,j}$ and, in $\mathcal L\cup\{X\}$, $T_X:=\{\forall x(A_ix\to Xx)\}_i\cup\{\forall x(Xx\to B_jx)\}_j$.
* (a) $\Cn(T_X)\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L}=\Cn(T_{\rm dir})$.
* (b) Every theory consisting of *atom-to-atom implications* $\forall x(Ux\to Vx)$ over $\{A_i,B_j\}$ and equivalent to $T_{\rm dir}$ has at least $nm$ members; $T_X$ has $n+m$.
* (c) In full first-order logic $T_{\rm dir}$ is equivalent to the single sentence $\forall x((A_1x\vee\dots\vee A_nx)\to(B_1x\wedge\dots\wedge B_mx))$ of length $O(n+m)$, so $X$ saves only a constant factor.

**[proved; computed: `t5_monadic.out` Part C, exhaustive for $(n,m)\in\{(1,2),(2,2),(2,3)\}$]**

*Proof.* (a) $T_X\vdash A_i\to X\to B_j$. Every model of $T_{\rm dir}$ expands to one of $T_X$ with $X:=\bigcup_iA_i\subseteq\bigcap_jB_j$. (b) For a set $S$ of atom-to-atom implications, $S\vdash\forall x(Ux\to Vx)$ iff $V$ is reachable from $U$ in the graph $S$: if not, the one-element structure in which exactly the atoms reachable from $U$ hold satisfies $S$, $U$ and $\neg V$. In models of $T_{\rm dir}$ no $A_i\to A_{i'}$, $B_j\to B_{j'}$ ($i\ne i'$, $j\ne j'$) or $B_j\to A_i$ holds (take the one-element structures with only $A_i$, resp. only $B_j$, true, plus all $B$'s when an $A$ is true). So an equivalent $S$ contains only edges $A_i\to B_j$ (and trivial loops), and reachability from $A_i$ to $B_j$ then needs the edge itself: all $nm$. (c) Propositional logic. ∎

So "a new concept factoring a complete bipartite inference graph" (Hänni's working notes) pays in restricted inference formats — atom-to-atom rules, as in a cheap symbolic engine — and only by a constant factor in unrestricted logic, where the "artificial" $X:=\bigvee_iA_i$ is already available.

**Remark 7.3 (reuse) [proof sketch].** If a defined formula of length $\ell$ occurs $k$ times in a theory, the definitional extension (one definition, $k$ occurrences of the new symbol) is shorter by about $(k-1)\ell-O(k)$ symbols. This is an upper bound on the length of the extended theory; that no shorter theory in the base language exists is not claimed.

## 8. The dilemma theorem (target T7)

**Theorem 8.1 (the theoretician's dilemma in axiom induction).** Data are observational sentences; in (ii)–(iv) literal data in $\mathcal L_O=\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,R\}$, with readings and polynomial budgets as in SD.
* (i) *In principle.* With deduction free, restricting hypotheses to the observational vocabulary changes the prior weight of every class of observational contents, the predictive probability of every observational sentence and the cumulative log loss by at most a constant, in the one-tape, coin-free and Kolmogorov versions, and also when the time to check or to generate axioms is penalised. Tape by tape, the observational projection of a random theory has exactly the same law on observational data. (Thm 2.1)
* (ii) *Craig's construction in practice.* When derivations are charged, Craig's axiomatisation of a theory whose observational consequences have derivations of size $d$ needs derivations of size at least $2^d$ (Lemma 3.1). Every axiomatisation by padded observational literals with polynomial-time membership fits, at polynomial budgets, exactly the polynomial-time decidable regularities, under every reading (Thm 3.3). Axiom sets with theoretical symbols fit $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ (total material), $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ (every instance short) and every decidable regularity (only the theory's own axioms short); so Craig's construction loses unconditionally under the last two readings and, under the first, iff $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}\ne\mathsf P$ (Cor 3.5).
* (iii) *Eliminating the vocabulary in practice.* Theoretical vocabulary can be eliminated at polynomial cost under every reading: by storing proofs instead of run times (total material: $O(1)$ bits and a polynomial budget, Prop 4.1), and by storing the eliminated relations in objects that no observational term names (every reading: the same fit classes as all hypotheses, Prop 4.3, Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7), already by *finite* observational theories.
* (iv) *What is indispensable.* A finite theory in the observational vocabulary with any unary theoretical predicates and constants, compatible with the first-order theory of the named objects, fits exactly the regular regularities, under every reading and also with free deduction (Thm 5.1, 5.3). One **binary** theoretical relation over the named objects restores every decidable regularity ((a2), (b2)), $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ ((b)) and $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ ((a1)) (Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7); so does quantifying over unnamed objects, and a finite observational theory that does so must assert an observational sentence false of every named object (Prop 9.1(b)).
* (v) *Free deduction, finite theories.* With deduction free, finite theories gain without bound from a theoretical function (infinity: $K_{\rm fin}=\min_{m\ge n}K(m)\pm c\to\infty$ without it, $O(1)$ with it; unary predicates never help), from theoretical sorts (PA's theorems), and from a binary relation over the named objects (Cor 5.5); and r.e. axiom induction equals finite-theory induction with theoretical sorts up to $O(1)$, coin-free (Thm 6.2, 6.4, 6.5).

**Statuses.** (i) proved (penalised versions under (S), (S$_{\rm tape}$), given TF Lemma 2.1 and `sfi` Thm 3.4); (ii) proved, given SD Cor 3.2, 4.4, Prop 4.12 (under (F1)), the time hierarchy theorems and APSPACE = EXPTIME (known, unverified here), the (b) clause conditional as stated; (iii) proved under (F1), given SD Thm 3.1, Prop 4.3, Cor 4.4, the one-tape simulation and CKS; (iv) proved, given the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem, the one-tape simulation and, for (a1), CKS; (v) proved, the PA clause given (KL) (known, unverified here).

**What this does and does not show** (about the classical reply to Hempel; the philosophical assessment is `philosophy`'s).
* The classical reply — "Craigian axiomatisations are useless in practice" — becomes a theorem here, but only about *Craig's construction* and about every run-padded axiomatisation: they are fast labellers in disguise ((ii)). It is not a theorem about theoretical vocabulary: the vocabulary can be eliminated at polynomial cost under every reading ((iii)). The brief's seed ("the time-bounded separations make that reply a theorem") holds only in this narrow sense.
* What bounded deduction and finiteness make indispensable is theoretical *relations* (polyadic) or theoretical *entities* (objects outside the named structure), and either one will do ((iv)). Theoretical *properties* of observable things (unary predicates) never help a finite theory in this setting.
* In the idealised Solomonoff setting nothing theoretical is indispensable ((i)), because the hypothesis class already contains the apparatus ((v), Thm 6.5): the r.e. prior *is* a finite-theory prior with theoretical sorts. Where theoretical terms pay is a property of the hypothesis class, and the answer differs between classes in exactly the ways tabulated in §7.
* The results concern one poor observational language (observation sequences: a free unary structure) and literal data; with a binary observable relation the boundary moves (Prop 9.3). "Named object" is a formal stand-in for "observable thing". The theorems say nothing about whether scientific theorising *should* use theoretical terms; they locate, inside these inducers, what can and cannot be traded away.

## 9. Extensions (target T8)

### 9.1 Speed-up from conservative theoretical extensions (scout N1's N8)

**Remark 9.0 [proof sketch, given recalled speed-up results (known, unverified)].** For finite theories, conservative extensions with theoretical vocabulary (ACA$_0$ over $\PA$, NBG/GB over ZF) are recalled to shorten some proofs of sentences of the smaller language by more than any elementary function (Gödel 1936, *Über die Länge von Beweisen*, for the phenomenon between systems of different order; Pudlák 1998, *The lengths of proofs*, §§ on speed-up, for the specific pairs; bibliographic data checked by scout L, contents not seen). What this adds to the results above:
* *Finite theories in the base language.* Nothing new on PA's theorems: no finite $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-theory fits them at any budget (Thm 6.4(a)).
* *Schemas.* Under a template (schema) prior, where $\PA$ costs $O(1)$ (the axiom-schemas report), the comparison ACA$_0$ (finite, theoretical sort) against $\PA$ (one schema, no theoretical vocabulary) under a derivation charge is decided by the speed-up: on data whose shortest $\PA$-proofs are much longer than their ACA$_0$-proofs, the derivation-charged inducer prefers the theoretical sort. *Not written out:* a reading of the charged readings for template instance sets, and the verification of a specific speed-up theorem.
* *r.e. hypotheses.* Proof padding (Prop 4.1) neutralises any speed-up: the certificate axioms store the short proofs at $O(1)$ description cost. So speed-up favours theoretical vocabulary only in classes restricted to finitely many axioms or schemas — the same boundary as in §5–§6.

### 9.2 Conservativity and the ontological commitment (scout S's Q3.18)

**Proposition 9.1.** Let $V$ be a verifier and $P=P_V$ (Lemma 4.5).
* (a) $T^C_P$ is conservative over $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}\cup\Gamma_{f_V}$: for every $\mathcal L_O$-sentence $\chi$, $T^C_P\cup\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}\vdash\chi$ iff $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}\cup\Gamma_{f_V}\vdash\chi$.
* (b) $T^O_P\vdash\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$, and $T^O_P\vdash\neg\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1t_w=t_w$ for every word $w$. So $T^O_P\cup\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$ is inconsistent: $T^O_P$ is consistent with all the data but asserts an observational sentence that is false of every named object and refutable from the observational theory of the named structure. In particular $T^O_P$ is not conservative over $\Gamma_{f_V}$ ($\Gamma_{f_V}\cup\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$ is consistent: $(\mathfrak T,X_{\rm acc})$).

**[proved]**

*Proof.* (a) $\Leftarrow$: $T^C_P\vdash\Gamma_{f_V}$ (Thm 4.6). $\Rightarrow$: every model $\mathfrak M$ of $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}\cup\Gamma_{f_V}$ expands to a model of $T^C_P$. Let $C^{\mathfrak M}$ be the least subset of $M^2$ containing the pairs with a halting prefix and closed under the backward rules, interpreted in $\mathfrak M$. The rules and halting axioms hold by construction. In $\mathfrak M$ the current patterns are still pairwise disjoint and the values $(Z,Y)$ of a match are unique (disjointness and injectivity are first-order consequences of $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$, e.g. $\mathtt s_pZ\ne\mathtt s_{p'}Z'$ for incomparable words $p,p'$), so, as in Thm 4.6(i), $C^{\mathfrak M}$ is the set of pairs whose deterministic run in $\mathfrak M$ reaches a halting prefix. Consider a start pair $(x,\eta\,{\rm st}(s)\,z)$. If $z$ is nonstandard, phase 0 never ends: a nonstandard $z\ne\mathtt e$ is $\mathtt s_az'$ for some letter $a$ (a sentence of $\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$) with $z'$ nonstandard, so each step leaves a nonstandard remainder. If $z$ is standard and $x$ nonstandard, phase 2 never ends, for the same reason. If both are standard, the run stays in the standard part, which is a copy of $\mathfrak T$, and is the run of Lemma 4.5. So $(x,0\,{\rm st}(s)z)\in C^{\mathfrak M}$ implies $x=t_w$ with $V(w,c)={\rm acc}$ for some $c$, hence $\mathfrak M\models R(t_w)$ by $\Gamma_{f_V}$; likewise for rejection. Completeness gives the claim. (The "scan first" design of Lemma 4.5 — both raw words are read to their ends before the machine starts — is what makes this work.)
(b) In a model of $T^O_P\cup\{\forall z\,\neg\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z\}$, $\mathrm{Std}$ holds everywhere and every $\theta_2(a,b)$ holds vacuously, so the two output rules give $\forall x\,R(x)$ and $\forall x\,\neg R(x)$: no such model exists. $\mathrm{Std}(t_w)$ follows from $\mathrm{Std}(\mathtt e)$ and the propagation axioms. $\forall z\,\neg\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z\in\mathrm{Th}_{\mathfrak T}$. ∎

*Reading.* Eliminating the binary theoretical relation in favour of unnamed objects is not ontologically free: the observational theory must say, in observational vocabulary, that there are objects unlike every observable one. The theoretical-vocabulary version says nothing new about the observable structure.

### 9.3 Richer observational languages (scout S's Q3.19)

**Proposition 9.2 (unary observational languages).** Let the observational language have constants $\mathtt e_1,\dots,\mathtt e_p$, unary function symbols $\mathtt s_1,\dots,\mathtt s_r$ and unary observable predicates $R_1,\dots,R_j$, the named structure being the free term algebra $\mathfrak T_{p,r}$ and the data all literals $R_i(t)^{\pm}$, $t$ closed. If a finite theory in this language plus unary theoretical predicates and constants is compatible with $\mathrm{Th}(\mathfrak T_{p,r})$ and proves every datum, then each $R_i$ has finitely many residuals ($\{u\in\{1,\dots,r\}^*:R_i(\mathtt s_ut)\}$, $t$ closed), i.e. is regular below each root. **[proved: the proof of Thm 5.1 applies verbatim — subtrees below non-root closed terms are joined to their context by one edge; the roots, like the theoretical constants, go into the finite exceptional set]**

**Proposition 9.3 (one binary observable breaks Theorem 5.1).** Let the observational language be $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,R,J\}$ with $J$ a binary observable relation, the data being all literals of $R$ and $J$ on closed terms, and let the world be $J:=\{(t_u,t_v):\#_0(u)=\#_1(v)\}$, $R:=\mathrm{EQ}=\{w:\#_0w=\#_1w\}$ (not regular). The 15 observational sentences
* $J(\mathtt e,\mathtt e)$; $\forall y\,\neg J(\mathtt e,\mathtt s_1y)$; $\forall x\,\neg J(\mathtt s_0x,\mathtt e)$;
* $\forall y\,(J(\mathtt e,y)\to J(\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0y))$; $\forall x\,(J(x,\mathtt e)\to J(\mathtt s_1x,\mathtt e))$; $\forall x\forall y\,(J(x,y)\to J(\mathtt s_0x,\mathtt s_1y))$; $\forall x\forall y\,(J(\mathtt s_0x,y)\to J(\mathtt s_0x,\mathtt s_0y))$; $\forall x\forall y\,(J(x,y)\to J(\mathtt s_1x,y))$, each also with both $J$-atoms negated;
* $\forall x\,(J(x,x)\to R(x))$, $\forall x\,(\neg J(x,x)\to\neg R(x))$

are true in $(\mathfrak T,R,J)$ — a theory about the named objects with no theoretical vocabulary — and derive every datum with $O(|u|+|v|)$ lines. So with a binary observable, finite observational theories about the named objects fit a non-regular target. **[proved; computed: `t4_regular.out` Part C (all $J$-literals with $|u|,|v|\le5$, all $R$-literals with $|w|\le9$; the windowed residual count of $J$ grows, $5,7,9$ at depths $2,3,4$)]**

*Proof.* Truth: $\#_0$ and $\#_1$ change as the letters say. Determination: every pair $(u,v)$ is $(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, $(\varepsilon,1v')$, $(\varepsilon,0v')$, $(0u',\varepsilon)$, $(1u',\cdot)$, $(0u',1v')$ or $(0u',0v')$, and each case reduces by one axiom (positive or negative form) to a pair of smaller total length or is an axiom. ∎

What the fit class of finite observational theories about the named objects is in the presence of a binary observable — in particular whether it is all decidable targets — is **open**; the answer must depend on the observable (with $J=\emptyset$ it is REG by Prop 9.2-type reasoning).

**Remark 9.3′ (naming composites without observing them) [proved].** If the observational language has a binary function symbol $\mathtt p$ (naming composites) but the data concern only the words $t_w$, then $T^C_P$ with every atom $C(a,b)$ replaced by $R(\mathtt p(a,b))$, with no relativisation, is about the named objects (the term algebra on $\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,\mathtt p$, with $R(\mathtt p(a,b)):=[(a,b)\in C]$ and $R:=X_{\rm acc}$ on words) and fits every decidable $X$ under (a2), (b2). (In the free term algebra matching is syntactic; a start pair with a non-word component gets stuck when phase 0 or 2 reaches the $\mathtt p$-term, so the output rules hold as in Thm 4.6.) This is scout S's caveat made precise: Theorem 5.1 depends on the composites being *unnamed*, or, if named, observed.

### 9.4 Reading (a1) for infinite hypotheses about the named objects (scout S's Q3.16)

**Proposition 9.4.** Let $X\in\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ be $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. There is a parameter-free set $A\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$, true in $(\mathfrak T,X)$, with polynomial-time membership, such that every datum $\varphi_w^{[w\in X]}$ has a derivation from $A$ by MP and $\mathrm A1$ instances whose every line has size polynomial in $|w|$. Hence, at polynomial budgets under (a1), the fit class of observational hypotheses about the named objects contains every $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$-hard set in $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$, besides $\mathsf P$ and $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$. **[proved, given CKS and the standard clocking of alternating machines]**

*Proof.* Let $M$ be an alternating polynomial-space machine for $X$ with all paths halting (CKS, clock), and $\mathrm{Acc}:=\{(w,c):c$ an accepting configuration of $M$ on $w\}\in\mathsf{EXPTIME}$. Let $g$ be a polynomial-time reduction of $\mathrm{Acc}$ (pairs coded as words) to $X$; since every configuration accepts or rejects, $c$ rejects iff $g(w,c)\notin X$. For a word $\kappa$ let $\theta_\kappa:=Z\to(C_\kappa\to Z)$ be SD's valid certificate sentence ($Z=\forall(\#0=\#0)$, size $|\kappa|+14$). $A$ consists of, for all $w$ and configurations $c$ of $M$ on $w$ ($\langle\cdot\rangle$ a pairing of words):
* $\theta_{\langle w,c\rangle}\to R(t_{g(w,c)})$ for halting accepting $c$, and $\theta_{\langle w,c\rangle}\to\neg R(t_{g(w,c)})$ for halting rejecting $c$;
* at an existential $c$ with successors $c_1,c_2$: $\theta_{\langle w,c,c_i\rangle}\to(R(t_{g(w,c_i)})\to R(t_{g(w,c)}))$ ($i=1,2$) and $\theta_{\langle w,c,c_1,c_2\rangle}\to(\neg R(t_{g(w,c_1)})\to(\neg R(t_{g(w,c_2)})\to\neg R(t_{g(w,c)})))$; at a universal $c$ the duals;
* $\theta_{\langle w\rangle}\to(R(t_{g(w,{\rm init}(w))})\to R(t_w))$ and $\theta_{\langle w\rangle}\to(\neg R(t_{g(w,{\rm init}(w))})\to\neg R(t_w))$.

Every member is true in $(\mathfrak T,X)$ ($\theta$ is valid and $R(t_{g(w,c)})$ says that $c$ accepts). Membership: read the coded tuple off the $\theta$-part, recompute the $g$-values and check the transition relation: polynomial time. A datum is derived along an accepting (rejecting) computation tree: each step cites a member, the $\mathrm A1$ instance $\theta_\kappa$, and MPs; every line has polynomial size (configurations and $g$-values are polynomial). ∎

Whether the class is all of $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ is **open**: for an $X$ that is neither easy nor hard, the facts about the named objects that a derivation can use are the values of $X$ itself (and sentences about $(\mathfrak T,X)$), and it is not clear that they can store an exponential computation. If not, theoretical vocabulary or entities would be needed under (a1) even for infinite hypotheses.

## 10. Relation to the literature

*The verification levels are those of scouts L and P unless stated; this session could not search.*
* **Craig (1953, 1956).** Every r.e. theory has a recursive (indeed primitive recursive) axiomatisation by padded conjunctions; Craig (1956) applied this to the replacement of auxiliary expressions [known; bibliographic data checked by scouts P, L; 1956 content not seen]. Thm 2.1 is the Bayesian form inside the prior; that the polynomial-time form of Craig's trick is standard is recorded by scout L (Wikipedia, primary source not identified). Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.3 make "Craigian axiomatisations are useless in practice" precise; scout L found no earlier complexity-theoretic theorem of this kind [new, by scout L's search].
* **Hempel (1958), Putnam (1962, 1965).** The theoretician's dilemma and the replies (Craig's axiomatisations are unmanageable; theoretical terms may be needed for inductive systematisation; observation terms apply to unobservables) [known; contents from secondary sources]. Thm 2.1(c)/Rem 2.2 bear on the inductive-systematisation reply *for the universal mixture*; §5 and Prop 9.1 give a formal version of Putnam's point.
* **Kleene (1952), Craig–Vaught (1958).** Finite axiomatisability of r.e. theories using additional predicates, one-sorted: Kleene's form needs theories with only infinite models when identity is present; Craig–Vaught characterise the theories finitely axiomatisable with additional predicates and (as reported in secondary sources, via Pakhomov–Visser 2019) give a model-conservative strengthening and an example showing that additional *unary* predicates do not suffice in general [known, unverified: all from scout P's notes on secondary sources]. Theorem 6.5 differs in three ways: it uses extra *sorts*, which removes the infinite-models condition; it is effective with an injective map and two-sided complexity bounds, which gives the Bayesian equivalence $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}=K_A^{\mathcal L}\pm c$, $W_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}\asymp W_A^{\mathcal L}$ [new as stated]; and it is proved in full here. Theorem 5.1 (unary predicates never help finite theories about observation sequences) is in the spirit of the reported Craig–Vaught unary example, in a different setting (fixed named structure, fit classes); the exact relation could not be checked.
* **Ramsey (1931), Lewis (1970).** Ramsey sentences; Lewis's definition of theoretical terms through them [known; bibliographic data checked by scout P]. Prop 7.1 places Ramsey and Craig side by side on the simplest example.
* **Inductive logic with theoretical concepts.** Hintikka and Tuomela (1970), Niiniluoto and Tuomela (1973): in monadic (Hintikka-style) inductive logic, theoretical concepts can change inductive probabilities [known; abstract-level, scout L]. Niiniluoto (1972) surveys inductive systematisation [bibliographic data and abstract checked]. Contrast: in the universal prior they cannot, beyond a constant factor (Thm 2.1); in finite-theory priors they can, unboundedly (§5–§6), but unary ones only where the domain codes syntax (arithmetic, Thm 6.4(d)), not over observation sequences (Thm 5.1) or pure equality (Thm 6.2(a)).
* **Predicate invention in ILP.** Muggleton and Buntine (1988) justify invented predicates by compression [known; bibliographic data checked by scout L]. Prop 7.2 is the elementary instance; Theorem 5.1 shows that invented *unary* predicates cannot make a finite theory of observation sequences fit a non-regular target, and Theorem 4.6 that one invented binary relation can [new].
* **Rabin (1969).** The MSO/tree-automata route to Theorem 5.1(a) (scout S) [known; bibliographic data checked by scout S; the theorem from secondary sources]; no longer needed.
* **Ehrenfeucht (1961), Fraïssé (1954); Löwenheim (1915).** The EF theorem (used in Lemma 5.2, Thm 5.1, Lemma 6.1) [known; textbook; references not re-checked]; Löwenheim's finite model property for monadic logic, of which Lemma 6.1 is a quantitative form [known, unverified].
* **Ryll-Nardzewski (1952), Montague (1961), Mostowski (1952), Kreisel–Lévy (1968), Simpson (2009).** Non-finite axiomatisability of PA and ZF; reflexivity and essential reflexivity of PA; ACA$_0$ [known, unverified here]. Used only in Thm 6.4(a), (c) (the ACA$_0$ instance) and Remark 9.0.
