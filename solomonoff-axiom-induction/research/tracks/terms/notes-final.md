## 0. Summary

**The question** (ladder step 3 of the project brief; Hempel's theoretician's dilemma): in which hypothesis classes do theoretical terms pay, and what is it that pays — vocabulary, relations, or entities?

**The answer, in one theorem** (Theorem 8.1, assembled from §2–§7):
1. *With free deduction, theoretical vocabulary costs $O(1)$ bits* — in the one-tape, coin-free and Kolmogorov versions, class by class (posterior odds of observational contents), and under penalties on checking or generating axioms (Thm 2.1). Tape by tape, the observational projection of a theory machine has *exactly* the same law on observational data (Thm 2.1(d)).
2. *Craig's construction is useless under charged deduction, for a precise reason*: it writes a run time in unary (Lemma 3.1). Every axiomatisation by padded observational literals with polynomial-time membership fits exactly the polynomial-time regularities at polynomial budgets, under every reading of "short derivation" (Thm 3.3). The separations from axiom sets with theoretical symbols are unconditional under (a1), (a2), (b2), and under (b) they hold iff $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}\ne\mathsf P$ (Cor 3.5).
3. *Theoretical vocabulary can nevertheless be eliminated at polynomial cost*, by storing the content in objects that no observational term names. Under reading (b) this works hypothesis by hypothesis, at $O(1)$ bits and a polynomial change of budget (Prop 4.1). Under (a1), (a2), (b2) it works at the level of fit classes: observational hypotheses fit what all hypotheses fit — $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ under (a1), every decidable set under (a2), (b2) — and *finite* observational theories already do (Prop 4.3, Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7).
4. *What finite theories cannot do without is a polyadic theoretical relation or theoretical entities.* A finite theory in the observational vocabulary, with any unary theoretical predicates and constants, that does not contradict the first-order theory of the named objects fits exactly the regular regularities (Thm 5.1, Thm 5.3). One **binary** theoretical relation over the named objects restores every decidable regularity under (a2), (b2), $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ under (b) and $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ under (a1) (Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7). Quantifying over unnamed objects does the same, but then the theory must prove some $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-sentence that is false in the named structure (Thm 5.1); for the theory built here that sentence is $\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$, true of no named object (Prop 9.1).
5. *With free deduction, finite theories gain without bound from theoretical functions or sorts.* On "there are at least $n$ things", every consistent monadic finite theory has $K_{\rm fin}=\Kpad(n)\pm c\to\infty$, while one unary function symbol gives $O(1)$ (Thm 6.2). In literal symbols the costs are $\ge n$ (pure equality), $\Theta(\log n)$ (monadic) and $O(1)$ (with $f$); in the bits of a normalisable symbol-wise code the monadic cost is $\Theta(\log n\log\log n)$ (Prop 6.3). On PA's theorems, finite $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-theories diverge while a finite theory with theoretical sorts gives $O(1)$ (Thm 6.4). And r.e. axiom induction *is* finite-theory induction in a three-sorted extension, up to $O(1)$, coin-free (Thm 6.5, an effective many-sorted form of the Kleene–Craig–Vaught theorem) but not one-tape (Rem 6.6).

**What is new** (relative to the literature found by scouts L, P and this round's searches; §10 gives the comparisons):
* **Derivation-bounded finite theories with one binary theoretical relation** (Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7), via a fully written-out machine normal form, *two-stack programs stated backwards* (Def 4.4, Lemma 4.5): the fit classes of such theories under every reading of charged deduction, and their observational translation through the pairing gadget. *With free deduction*, the existence of a finite conservative extension with one binary relation symbol is the theorem of Kleene (1952) and Craig–Vaught (1958) (known at the level of secondary summaries, §10); what is new is the explicit construction with derivation bounds and the fit classes (A-M2).
* **The regularity theorem** (Thm 5.1): unary theoretical predicates and constants never let a finite theory compatible with the named structure fit a non-regular regularity. The proof uses only the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem and needs only compatibility with $\Th_{\mathfrak T}$. It is in the spirit of Craig–Vaught's example that unary additional predicates do not suffice in general, in a different setting; novelty plausible, not established.
* **The forced ontological commitment** (Prop 9.1): the relation version is conservative over the data plus the theory of the named objects; the observational version is consistent with all data but refutes a sentence of that theory.
* **The Bayesian form of the Kleene–Craig–Vaught theorem** (Thm 6.5): an effective, injective, many-sorted construction, written out in full, giving $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}=K_A^{\mathcal L}\pm c$ and $W_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}\asymp W_A^{\mathcal L}$. Only the Bayesian corollary is claimed as new (A-m4).
* **Monadic costs of "at least $n$ things"**: $\Theta(\log n)$ symbols by a carry counter (new relative to scout N1, whose bound was $O(\log^2n)$; the counter is very likely folklore), $\Theta(\log n\log\log n)$ bits under a normalisable code (Prop 6.3).
* **Exact factoring gain** (Prop 7.2(d)): over atom-to-atom implications, a new intermediate predicate shortens the complete bipartite theory from $n_An_B$ to $\min(n_An_B,n_A+n_B)$ members, so it pays iff $(n_A-1)(n_B-1)>1$.
* **Reading (a1) for infinite hypotheses about the named objects** (Prop 9.4): every $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$-hard set in $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ is fitted (scout S had one specific complete set); whether all of $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ is fitted is open.
* **Richer observational languages** (Prop 9.2, 9.3, Rem 9.3′): Thm 5.1 holds for every observational language with only unary function symbols, constants and unary observables; one binary observable breaks it already for $\mathrm{EQ}$, with a 13-sentence observational theory.
* **The tape-wise identity behind Hempel's horn** (Thm 2.1(d)) is made explicit and given its class form; the projection machine is the pull-back machine of `fundamentals` Thm 4.4 for the inclusion (A-m10).

## 1. Setting

### 1.1 Observational restriction

**Definition 1.1 (observational restriction).** $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_O\cup\mathcal L_T$ is a countable first-order language with equality and computable syntax (finite where charged deduction is used); $\mathcal L_O$ is the observational part. The background $B\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ is decidable and consistent and may contain theoretical symbols. For $A\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ the *observational content* is $T_O(A):=\Cn(A)\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$ ($\Cn$ relative to $B$, NOTATION §3.1). A data set $D$ is *observational* if $\dom D\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$.
* *One tape.* Theory machines $G$, $A^G_\omega$, $\nu_G$, $G_U$, $A_\omega$ and $M_A=\nu_{G_U}$ are as in `fundamentals` Defs 1.2, 1.4. $G_U^O:=\{(w,\chi)\in G_U:\chi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}\}$ is the universal machine with its theoretical emissions dropped, and $M_A^{\mathcal L_O}:=\nu_{G_U^O}$. It is universal among machines that emit only $\mathcal L_O$-sentences: such a $G$ is run by $G_U$ after a prefix $p_G$, and the filter drops nothing. $M_A^{\mathcal L}:=M_A$.
* *Coin-free and Kolmogorov.* $W_A^{\mathcal L_O}(D):=\sum_e\mm(e)\,\ind[B\cup(\re e\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O})$ consistent and $\vdash\psi_D]$, and $K_A^{\mathcal L_O}(D)$ is the least $K(e)$ over the same $e$.
* *Classes.* For a Borel set $\Theta$ of observational contents (subsets of $\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$, with the product topology on $2^{\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}}$), $M_A(\Theta;D):=\Unif\{\omega:B\cup A_\omega\text{ consistent},\ \vdash\psi_D,\ T_O(A_\omega)\in\Theta\}$; likewise $M_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)$, $W_A(\Theta;D)$, $W_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)$. (The map $\omega\mapsto T_O(A_\omega)$ is Borel: each coordinate "$\varphi\in T_O(A_\omega)$" is the effectively open event $O_\varphi$, `fundamentals` Lemma 1.3(a). So the event is measurable. This is `sfi` Prop 3.5's $M_A(\mathcal C,D)$ with $\mathcal C=\{T:T\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}\in\Theta\}$.)
* *Penalised versions.* $W_A^{{\rm chk},t}$, $W_A^{{\rm gen},t}$ (TF Def 1.1; `sfi` §7) and their one-tape forms $M_A^{{\rm chk},t}$, $M_A^{{\rm gen},t}$ (`sfi` Def 3.3). The observational restrictions are the sub-sums over hypotheses whose axiom sets consist of $\mathcal L_O$-sentences (on every tape). The machine assumptions (S) (TF §1.2) and (S$_{\rm tape}$) (`sfi` §3.3) are assumed wherever these versions appear, as there.

### 1.2 Literal data and charged deduction

**Definition 1.2 (literal data and charged deduction; SD Defs 1.2, 1.3, 1.6, Fact 1.4; NOTATION §3.4, §5).** $\mathcal L_O=\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,R\}$; $t_w:=\mathtt s_{w_1}(\cdots\mathtt s_{w_k}(\mathtt e)\cdots)$, so $\mathtt s_a(t_v)=t_{av}$ and the word $w$ lists the function letters from the outside in; $\varphi_w:=R(t_w)$, $|\varphi_w|=|w|+2$; $D^X$ lists $(\varphi_w,[w\in X])$ in length-lexicographic order; $f_X$ is the literal labeller of $X$; $\mathfrak T$ is the free term algebra on $\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1$ (its elements are the $t_w$). Derivations are in the AI-paper's Hilbert calculus $\mathsf K$ (SD §1.1), sizes counting nodes. A hypothesis decides an axiom set $A_p$ within time $t$, satisfies **(F1)** ($B\cup A_p$ closed under permutations of the parameters), is consistent, and derives each datum with: (a1) every axiom instance, logical ones included, of size $\le h$; (a2) every nonlogical instance $\le h$; (b) material (total size of the distinct instances) $\le g$; (b2) nonlogical material $\le g$. These are $W_A^{a1}[t,h]$, $W_A^{a2}[t,h]$, $W_A^{b}[t,g]$, $W_A^{b2}[t,g]$ (NOTATION §5). *Polynomial budgets* are SD Def 1.6 (membership degree charged in the prior). The *fit class* of an inducer is the set of $X$ on whose $D^X$ it has bounded loss; for these mixtures that holds iff a single hypothesis is compatible with every prefix (SD Fact 1.4 = TF Lemma 1.6).

When this record says "literal data" with a background $B$, it assumes the *fresh-atom* setting of `fundamentals` Def 1.1′: $R$ does not occur in $B$, and some model of $B$ interprets the $t_w$ as pairwise distinct elements (for instance $B=\emptyset$). This is the hypothesis of `sfi` Thm 5.1.

### 1.3 The named objects

**Definition 1.3 (the named objects).** $\Th_{\mathfrak T}$ is the complete first-order theory of $(\mathfrak T;\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1)$. It contains, for all words $u\ne\varepsilon$, $p\ne p'$ and letters $a\ne a'$: $\mathtt s_ax=\mathtt s_ay\to x=y$; $\mathtt s_ax\ne\mathtt s_{a'}y$; $\mathtt s_ax\ne\mathtt e$; $x\ne\mathtt e\to\exists y\,(x=\mathtt s_0y\vee x=\mathtt s_1y)$; $\mathtt s_ux\ne x$; and $t_p\ne t_{p'}$ (all true in $\mathfrak T$). A theory $T$ in a language $\supseteq\mathcal L_O$ is
* *about the named objects* if it has a model whose $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-reduct is $\mathfrak T$ (scout S's notion: every object is named by an observational closed term);
* *compatible with the named structure* if $T\cup\Th_{\mathfrak T}$ is consistent (weaker: nonstandard objects are allowed, but they satisfy every first-order law that the named objects satisfy).

In a model of a theory compatible with the named structure, the denotations of the $t_w$ are pairwise distinct and form a copy of $\mathfrak T$ (the *standard part*); if the theory proves every literal datum of $X$, then $R$ is $X$ on the standard part.

## 2. Hempel's horn: vocabulary costs $O(1)$ (target T1)

**Theorem 2.1 (theoretical vocabulary costs $O(1)$ bits, class by class).** There is a constant $c$ (depending on $V$, the coding, $B$ and $\mathcal L_O\subseteq\mathcal L$) such that for every finite observational $D$ and every Borel set $\Theta$ of observational contents:
* (a) *one tape:* $2^{-c}M_A^{\mathcal L}(\Theta;D)\le M_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)\le2^cM_A^{\mathcal L}(\Theta;D)$;
* (b) *coin-free and Kolmogorov:* the same for $W_A$, and $|K_A^{\mathcal L_O}(D)-K_A^{\mathcal L}(D)|\le c$;
* (c) *penalised:* for each time-constructible $t(m)\ge m^{a^*}$ there is $c_t$ (it depends on $t$ through the padded decider's constants and the clock) such that the observational restrictions of $W_A^{{\rm chk},t}$ and $W_A^{{\rm gen},t}$ (all forms) agree with $W_A^{\mathcal L}$ within $2^{\pm c_t}$, class by class, under (S); and the observational restrictions of $M_A^{{\rm chk},t}$, $M_A^{{\rm gen},t}$ agree with $M_A^{\mathcal L}$ within $2^{\pm c_t}$, class by class, under (S$_{\rm tape}$);
* (d) *tape by tape:* for every theory machine $G$ in $\mathcal L$ let $G^O:=\{(w,\varphi):\varphi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_O},\ B\cup A^G_w\vdash\varphi\}$. Then $G^O$ is a theory machine emitting only $\mathcal L_O$-sentences, and for every tape $\omega$: $A^{G^O}_\omega=T_O(A^G_\omega)$; $B\cup A^{G^O}_\omega$ is consistent iff $B\cup A^G_\omega$ is; and for every observational $\varphi$ and $b$, $B\cup A^{G^O}_\omega\vdash\varphi^b$ iff $B\cup A^G_\omega\vdash\varphi^b$. Hence the two events are equal tape by tape, and $\nu_{G^O}(\Theta;D)=\nu_G(\Theta;D)$ exactly. Part (d) holds for every set $\Theta$, as an identity of events.

Consequently posterior odds between any two classes of observational contents, and the predictive probabilities $M(D+(\varphi,b))/M(D)$ of observational sentences, agree within $2^{\pm2c}$, and cumulative semimeasure losses on observational reveal sequences within $2c$, in each version. **[proved; (c) given TF Lemma 2.1 / Thm 2.2 under (S) and `sfi` Thm 3.4, Prop 3.5 under (S$_{\rm tape}$); computed (finite analogue of (d)): `checks/t1_hempel_toy.out`, and independently `referee_code/rb7_hempel_projection.out`]**

*Proof.* (d) $G^O$ is c.e. because $G$ is and $\vdash$ is r.e. By compactness and monotonicity of $A^G_w$ in $w$ (`fundamentals` Lemma 1.3(a)), $A^{G^O}_\omega=\bigcup_{w\sqsubseteq\omega}\{\varphi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}:B\cup A^G_w\vdash\varphi\}=T_O(A^G_\omega)$. Since $T_O(A)\subseteq\Cn(B\cup A)$, consistency of $B\cup A$ passes to $B\cup T_O(A)$; if $B\cup A$ is inconsistent, $T_O(A)$ contains the observational sentence $\neg\forall x\,x=x$. Again from $T_O(A)\subseteq\Cn(B\cup A)$, $\Cn(B\cup T_O(A))\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}=T_O(A)$, so an observational $\varphi^b$ follows from $B\cup T_O(A)$ iff it lies in $T_O(A)$ iff it follows from $B\cup A$ ($\varphi^b$ is observational when $\varphi$ is). The observational content of $G^O$ on $\omega$ is $T_O(A^G_\omega)$ itself, so class membership is preserved, and the two events coincide tape by tape.

(a) `fundamentals` Thm 4.1 gives the mixture identity tape by tape: the tapes $p\omega'$ with $V(p)$ an index of a machine $G$ produce $A^G_{\omega'}$ on $G_U$, hence $A^G_{\omega'}\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$ on $G_U^O$, and $\Unif(\cdot\cap[p])=2^{-|p|}\,\Unif(\cdot)$ on the shifted event (this is where $\Theta$ must be measurable). *Lower bound.* Apply (d) to $G:=G_U$: $(G_U)^O$ is a fixed machine emitting only $\mathcal L_O$-sentences, so on the tapes $p\omega'$ with $V(p)$ an index of $(G_U)^O$ the filtered universal machine emits exactly $A^{(G_U)^O}_{\omega'}$; hence $M_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)\ge2^{-K((G_U)^O)}\nu_{(G_U)^O}(\Theta;D)=2^{-c}M_A^{\mathcal L}(\Theta;D)$. *Upper bound.* $G_U^O$ is a theory machine of $\mathcal L$, so the same identity in $\mathcal L$ gives $M_A^{\mathcal L}(\Theta;D)\ge2^{-K(G_U^O)}\nu_{G_U^O}(\Theta;D)=2^{-c}M_A^{\mathcal L_O}(\Theta;D)$. The remaining term $(1-\Omega_V)\ind[B\vdash\psi_D,\ T_O(\emptyset)\in\Theta]$ of the mixture identity is the same on both sides (program-less tapes emit nothing).

(b) Let $o(e)$ be an index of $\{\varphi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}:B\cup\re e\vdash\varphi\}$ and $f(e)$ one of $\re e\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$; both maps are computable. By the argument of (d), $o(e)$ (read in $W_A^{\mathcal L_O}$) is compatible with $D$ and has content in $\Theta$ iff $e$ (read in $W_A^{\mathcal L}$) is; and $f(e)$ read in $W_A^{\mathcal L}$ behaves as $e$ read in $W_A^{\mathcal L_O}$. ### 4.3 The machine normal form: two-stack programs, stated backwards

Scout S's Prop 3.10(b) needed a three-tape machine normal form that was marked "standard; not written out", and its relation version used an 8-ary relation. The following normal form is written out in full, needs only a *binary* relation, and gives the observational version by the same gadget.

**Definition 4.4 (two-stack programs).** A *pattern* is $\pi=(r_\pi,\epsilon_r;\,l_\pi,\epsilon_l)$ with words $r_\pi,l_\pi$ and $\epsilon_r,\epsilon_l\in\{\mathrm{var},\mathrm{exact}\}$. A pair of words $(r,l)$ *matches* $\pi$ with values $(Z_r,Z_l)$ if $r=r_\pi Z_r$ (when $\epsilon_r=\mathrm{var}$; when $\epsilon_r=\mathrm{exact}$, $r=r_\pi$ and $Z_r$ is absent), and likewise $l=l_\pi Z_l$. A *rule* $\pi\Rightarrow\pi'$ is a pair of patterns with the same $\epsilon$'s (it is *linear*: each variable occurs once on each side); applied to $(r_\pi Z_r,l_\pi Z_l)$ it gives $(r_{\pi'}Z_r,l_{\pi'}Z_l)$. A *two-stack program* $\mathcal P$ is a finite set of rules; it is *deterministic* if no pair of words matches the current patterns of two rules. Programs carry a *tag* letter $\eta\in\{0,1\}$ at the front of $l$ (0: acceptance run; 1: rejection run), which every rule preserves, followed by a fixed-length *state code* ${\rm st}(q)$; the *halting prefixes* are $H_{\rm acc}:=0\,{\rm st}({\rm ACC})$ and $H_{\rm rej}:=1\,{\rm st}({\rm REJ})$; the *start prefixes* are $\eta\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start})$.

The *backward theory* of $\mathcal P$ in $\mathcal L_O\cup\{C\}$, $C$ a binary relation symbol, writing $C(uZ_r,vZ_l)$ for $C(\mathtt s_u(Z_r),\mathtt s_v(Z_l))$:
$$T^C_{\mathcal P}:=\{\forall Z_r\forall Z_l\,(C(\pi')\to C(\pi)):\ (\pi\Rightarrow\pi')\in\mathcal P\}\cup\{\forall Z_r\forall Z_l\,C(Z_r,H_{\rm acc}Z_l),\ \forall Z_r\forall Z_l\,C(Z_r,H_{\rm rej}Z_l)\}$$
$$\cup\{\forall x\forall z\,(C(x,0\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start})\,z)\to R(x)),\ \forall x\forall z\,(C(x,1\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start})\,z)\to\neg R(x))\}$$
(quantifiers only over the variables present). The output rules carry a free certificate variable $z$; they are used **only** for programs with a certificate phase that consumes $z$ (Lemma 4.5), never for the alternating programs of Prop 4.7, which use exact output rules (§4.5).

Its *observational translation* $T^O_{\mathcal P}$: replace each atom $C(a,b)$ by $\theta_2(a,b)$ (with its fresh bound variable); prefix the matrix of every member by $\mathrm{Std}(v)\to\cdots$ for each of its universally quantified variables $v$; add $\mathrm{Std}(\mathtt e)$ and $\forall y\,(\mathrm{Std}(y)\to\mathrm{Std}(\mathtt s_ay))$ for $a=0,1$. $T^O_{\mathcal P}$ is a finite set of $\mathcal L_O$-sentences.

*Why backwards.* A forward theory (start axiom, transition rules $C(\pi)\to C(\pi')$, output rule reading the input from a component) must keep the input word in the configuration until the end, which is what forced scout S's 8-ary relation (input, state, two stacks for each of three tapes). Stated backwards, $C$ means "this configuration leads to a halting configuration of the right kind", and the input is read only by the output rule, at the start configuration. Alternation is then direct (Prop 4.7).

**Lemma 4.5 (compilation: every verifier is a deterministic two-stack program).** Let $\mathcal V$ be a verifier: a deterministic multitape Turing machine that halts on every pair $(x,z)$ of binary words with output acc, rej or $\bot$, such that no $x$ has both an accepting and a rejecting certificate ($f_{\mathcal V}$ consistent); $X_{\rm acc}:=\{x:\exists z\,\mathcal V(x,z)={\rm acc}\}$, and $X_{\rm rej}$ likewise. There is a deterministic two-stack program $\mathcal P_{\mathcal V}$ such that for all words $x,z$ and both tags:
* (i) every rule is linear, the current patterns are pairwise disjoint, and no rule applies to a pair whose $l$ begins with a halting prefix (or with the code of the state $\bot$);
* (ii) the run from the *start pair* $(x,\,\eta\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start})\,z)$ reaches a pair with prefix $H_{\rm acc}$ (for $\eta=0$) iff $\mathcal V(x,z)={\rm acc}$, and one with prefix $H_{\rm rej}$ (for $\eta=1$) iff $\mathcal V(x,z)={\rm rej}$;
* (iii) the run has $N_{\rm run}=O(T_1+|x|+|z|)$ steps, every pair on it has length $O(S_1+|x|+|z|)$, and each step changes $O(1)$ letters, where $T_1,S_1$ are the time and space of a one-tape simulation of $\mathcal V$ (so $T_1$ is polynomial in $\mathcal V$'s time and $S_1=O(S_{\mathcal V}+|x|+|z|)$).

**[proved, given the one-tape simulation of multitape machines (known; textbook: Hartmanis–Stearns 1965, Hopcroft–Ullman Thm 7.2, Sipser Thm 3.13); computed: `t4_two_stack.out` Part A; `rb1_two_stack.out` Part A (referee B's own compiler and machines, including a verifier that reads $\mathtt B$); `t6_revision.out` Part B]**

*Construction.* **(1) The one-tape machine.** By the one-tape simulation theorem there is a deterministic one-tape machine $M_1$ that, started on the tape $\mathtt Z_{z_m}\cdots\mathtt Z_{z_1}\,\#\,x_1\cdots x_{|x|}$ (the certificate reversed, in letters $\mathtt Z_0,\mathtt Z_1$, then a separator, then the input) with the head on cell 0, halts in state ACC, REJ or $\bot$ according to $\mathcal V(x,z)$. (Rearranging the input format costs $O((|x|+|z|)^2)$ steps on one tape; the simulation is polynomial in $\mathcal V$'s time and linear in its space.) Bring $M_1$ to **normal form**: its tape alphabet $\Sigma_M$ (without the blank $\square$) contains $\#,0,1,\mathtt Z_0,\mathtt Z_1$ and a symbol $\mathtt B$ on which $M_1$ has exactly the transitions it has on $\square$; wherever $M_1$ would write $\square$ it writes $\mathtt B$. So every transition writes a symbol of $\Sigma_M$; moving left on cell 0 leaves the head there (the usual convention). Then, on every run from a start tape, the non-blank cells form an initial segment $[0,\mathrm{nb})$ and the head position $\mathrm{hp}$ satisfies $\mathrm{hp}\le\mathrm{nb}$: a step at $\mathrm{hp}<\mathrm{nb}$ keeps both properties; a step at $\mathrm{hp}=\mathrm{nb}$ writes a symbol of $\Sigma_M$ there, so $\mathrm{nb}$ grows by one and $\mathrm{hp}\le\mathrm{nb}$ still holds.

**(2) Coding.** Fix injective codes ${\rm st}:Q\to\{0,1\}^{k_Q}$ (states of $M_1$ and the phase states $\mathrm{start},\mathrm{cert},\mathrm{sep},\mathrm{inp},\mathrm{rew}$) and ${\rm blk}:\Sigma_M\to\{0,1\}^{k_B}$. A configuration of $M_1$ with state $q$, tape $a_0\cdots a_{\mathrm{nb}-1}\square\square\cdots$ and head $\mathrm{hp}\le\mathrm{nb}$ is coded, with tag $\eta$, as
$$\llbracket q,a,\mathrm{hp}\rrbracket_\eta:=\big({\rm blk}(a_{\mathrm{hp}})\cdots{\rm blk}(a_{\mathrm{nb}-1}),\ \ \eta\,{\rm st}(q)\,{\rm blk}(a_{\mathrm{hp}-1})\cdots{\rm blk}(a_0)\big):$$
$r$ holds the cells from the head on, $l$ the tag, the state and the cells to the left of the head, nearest first; $r=\varepsilon$ means the head reads $\square$.

**(3) Rules** (for each tag $\eta$; $c(q):=\eta\,{\rm st}(q)$):
* *start:* $(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{start})Z_l)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(\#)Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{cert})Z_l)$ (push the separator onto $r$, above the raw input);
* *phase cert (certificate):* $(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{cert})aZ_l)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_a)Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{cert})Z_l)$ for $a\in\{0,1\}$ (a raw certificate letter below the state code becomes a block on $r$), and $(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{cert})\,|\,\mathrm{exact})\Rightarrow(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{sep})\,|\,\mathrm{exact})$;
* *phase sep (to the separator):* $({\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_a)Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{sep})Z_l)\Rightarrow(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{sep}){\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_a)Z_l)$, and $({\rm blk}(\#)Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{sep})Z_l)\Rightarrow(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{inp}){\rm blk}(\#)Z_l)$;
* *phase inp (input):* $(aZ_r;\,c(\mathrm{inp})Z_l)\Rightarrow(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{inp}){\rm blk}(a)Z_l)$ for $a\in\{0,1\}$, and $(\varepsilon\,|\,\mathrm{exact};\,c(\mathrm{inp})Z_l)\Rightarrow(\varepsilon\,|\,\mathrm{exact};\,c(\mathrm{rew})Z_l)$;
* *phase rew (rewind):* $(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{rew}){\rm blk}(e)Z_l)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(e)Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{rew})Z_l)$ for $e\in\{\mathtt Z_0,\mathtt Z_1,\#,0,1\}$, and $(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{rew})\,|\,\mathrm{exact})\Rightarrow(Z_r;\,c(q_0)\,|\,\mathrm{exact})$;
* *the machine:* for each transition $\delta(q,a)=(q',b,\mathrm{dir})$, with read pattern $\rho:={\rm blk}(a)Z_r$ if $a\in\Sigma_M$ and $\rho:=\varepsilon\,|\,\mathrm{exact}$ if $a=\square$ (write $\rho'$ for $Z_r$, resp. $\varepsilon\,|\,\mathrm{exact}$):
  * $\mathrm{dir}=R$: $(\rho;\,c(q)Z_l)\Rightarrow(\rho';\,c(q'){\rm blk}(b)Z_l)$;
  * $\mathrm{dir}=S$: $(\rho;\,c(q)Z_l)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(b)\rho';\,c(q')Z_l)$;
  * $\mathrm{dir}=L$: for each $e\in\Sigma_M$, $(\rho;\,c(q){\rm blk}(e)Z_l)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(e){\rm blk}(b)\rho';\,c(q')Z_l)$, and at the left end $(\rho;\,c(q)\,|\,\mathrm{exact})\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(b)\rho';\,c(q')\,|\,\mathrm{exact})$.

*Proof.* (i) Linearity is visible in the list. Disjointness: rules with different tags or state codes differ in a fixed-length prefix of $l$; within one state, the read patterns ${\rm blk}(a)Z_r$ (fixed length, injective code) and $\varepsilon|\mathrm{exact}$ are pairwise disjoint, as are, within one left-moving transition, the neighbour patterns ${\rm blk}(e)Z_l$ and the exact left-end pattern; in phase cert the raw-letter patterns $c(\mathrm{cert})aZ_l$ and the exact $c(\mathrm{cert})$ are disjoint, and in phase inp the raw-letter patterns $aZ_r$ and the exact $\varepsilon$. There are no rules for ACC, REJ, $\bot$. Fixed-length state codes prevent a spurious halting prefix. (ii) *Phases.* From $(x,\,c(\mathrm{start})z)$: the start rule gives $({\rm blk}(\#)x,\,c(\mathrm{cert})z)$; phase cert moves the letters of $z$, first letter first, onto $r$, ending in $({\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_{z_m})\cdots{\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_{z_1}){\rm blk}(\#)x,\ c(\mathrm{sep}))$; phase sep moves these blocks to $l$, the separator last, ending in $(x,\ c(\mathrm{inp}){\rm blk}(\#){\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_{z_1})\cdots{\rm blk}(\mathtt Z_{z_m}))$; phase inp converts the raw letters of $x$; phase rew moves all blocks back to $r$ and ends exactly at the left end in $\llbracket q_0,\ \mathtt Z_{z_m}\cdots\mathtt Z_{z_1}\#x,\ 0\rrbracket_\eta$, the code of $M_1$'s start configuration. *Steps.* For a configuration with $\mathrm{hp}\le\mathrm{nb}$ and a non-blank initial segment, exactly one rule applies to its code, namely the rule of the transition taken, with the neighbour case given by the cell $\mathrm{hp}-1$ (or the left end), and its result is the code of the successor: for $\mathrm{dir}=R$ the written $b$ moves to the top of $l$; for $S$ it replaces the top of $r$; for $L$ the left neighbour $e$ moves from $l$ to $r$ above $b$; at $a=\square$ ($r=\varepsilon$) the written $b$ becomes the new last cell. Reading $\mathtt B$ is reading the block ${\rm blk}(\mathtt B)$, with the transitions of $\square$. By (1) the invariant is preserved. So the run of $\mathcal P_{\mathcal V}$ from the start pair passes through the codes of $M_1$'s run and stops exactly when $M_1$ halts; the tag is preserved, so a 0-tagged run can reach $H_{\rm acc}$ only through ACC, and a 1-tagged run $H_{\rm rej}$ only through REJ. (iii) Phases take $2|z|+|x|+(|z|+|x|+1)+O(1)$ steps, the machine $T_1$; every rule changes $O(k_Q+k_B)$ letters. ∎

### 4.4 Finite theories from two-stack programs

**Theorem 4.6 (one binary theoretical relation suffices under charged deduction; and its observational translation).** Let $\mathcal V$ be a verifier as in Lemma 4.5 and $\mathcal P=\mathcal P_{\mathcal V}$. Membership is linear-time (finite sets of sentences) and (F1) holds (sentences).

### 4.5 Alternation and reading (a1)

**Proposition 4.7 (finite theories under (a1): $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$).** For every $X\in\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ there are a finite theory in $\mathcal L_O\cup\{C\}$ ($C$ binary) about the named objects and a finite set of $\mathcal L_O$-sentences, each consistent with $\Gamma_{f_X}$, from which every datum $\varphi_w^{[w\in X]}$ has a $\mathsf K$-derivation whose every line (logical axiom instances included) has size $O(s(|w|)+|w|)$, $s$ a polynomial. Hence at polynomial budgets the fit class under (a1) of finite theories with one binary theoretical relation about the named objects, and of finite observational theories, is $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$, the class of all hypotheses (SD Cor 4.4). This answers scout S's Question 3.17 (a proof sketch there). **[proved, given $\mathsf{APSPACE}=\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ (CKS 1981; known, abstract level) and the one-tape simulation (textbook); computed: `t3_alternating.out` Part B, `t6_revision.out` Part A; independently `rb1_two_stack.out` Part C]**

The proof is written out in four steps. (In `notes.md` the proof said "compile as in Lemma 4.5 with phases 2 and 3 only … the rest of the proof of Thm 4.6 applies"; read literally, that takes Lemma 4.5's start rule and Def 4.4's free-$z$ output rules, and both fail. That construction is kept, refuted, in §11 item 1.)

*Step 1 (the machine).* An *alternating one-tape machine* has states partitioned into existential, universal and the halting states ACC, REJ, and a transition relation giving, for each non-halting state $q$ and each symbol $a\in\Sigma_M\cup\{\square\}$, a list $\delta(q,a)$ of one or two moves $(q_i,b_i,\mathrm{dir}_i)$. A configuration *accepts* if its state is ACC, or it is existential and some successor accepts, or it is universal and all successors accept (least fixed point); when every computation path halts, the computation tree from a configuration is finite (König), acceptance is decided by induction on it, and a configuration that does not accept *rejects*. *Normal form:* (1) as in Lemma 4.5(1) (writes only symbols of $\Sigma_M$; $\mathtt B$ has the transitions of $\square$; a left move at cell 0 stays), so $\mathrm{hp}\le\mathrm{nb}$ along every path from a start tape; (2) $\delta(q,a)$ is nonempty for every non-halting $q$ and every $a$ (add a move to REJ at an existential state and to ACC at a universal state where none exists; this does not change acceptance); (3) every computation path from a start configuration (tape $\#x$, head on cell 0, state $q_0$) halts.

Such a machine exists for every $X\in\mathsf{EXPTIME}$. By CKS, $X$ is decided by a multitape alternating machine $M$ in space $S(m)$, a polynomial. *Clock.* $M$ has at most $C(m)=2^{c(S(m)+\log m)}$ configurations on inputs of length $m$. Let $A_j$ be the set of configurations with an accepting computation tree of height $\le j$; $A_j\subseteq A_{j+1}$, the accepting configurations are $\bigcup_jA_j$, and the sequence stabilises after at most $C(m)$ strict increases, so every accepting configuration has an accepting tree of height $\le C(m)$. Add a binary step counter of $c(S(m)+\log m)+1$ bits on an extra tape, and go to REJ when it overflows. If $M$ accepts $x$, the clocked machine follows an accepting tree of height $\le C(m)$ and accepts; if the clocked machine accepts, its accepting tree contains no overflow (overflow leads to REJ, which an accepting tree does not contain) and is an accepting tree of $M$. Every path of the clocked machine halts. *One tape.* Simulate the tapes on tracks of one tape, each step of the clocked machine by a deterministic sequence of steps (existential states with one successor) that ends with the branching step; the computation trees correspond, so acceptance is preserved and all paths halt, in space $O(S+\log m)$. Normal form (1)–(2) is a local change. Call the result $M_1$; split moves so that $|\delta(q,a)|\le2$.

*Step 2 (the compile).* Fix codes ${\rm st}$ (states of $M_1$ and the phase states $\mathrm{start},\mathrm{inp},\mathrm{rew}$) and ${\rm blk}$ as in Lemma 4.5(2). For each tag $\eta$, with $c(q):=\eta\,{\rm st}(q)$:
* *start:* $(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{start})Z_l)\Rightarrow(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{inp})\,{\rm blk}(\#)Z_l)$ — the separator goes onto $l$ (there is no certificate phase);
* *phase inp:* $(aZ_r;\,c(\mathrm{inp})Z_l)\Rightarrow(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{inp}){\rm blk}(a)Z_l)$ for $a\in\{0,1\}$, and $(\varepsilon\,|\,\mathrm{exact};\,c(\mathrm{inp})Z_l)\Rightarrow(\varepsilon\,|\,\mathrm{exact};\,c(\mathrm{rew})Z_l)$;
* *phase rew:* $(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{rew}){\rm blk}(e)Z_l)\Rightarrow({\rm blk}(e)Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{rew})Z_l)$ for $e\in\{\#,0,1\}$, and $(Z_r;\,c(\mathrm{rew})\,|\,\mathrm{exact})\Rightarrow(Z_r;\,c(q_0)\,|\,\mathrm{exact})$;
* *the machine:* for each non-halting $q$ and $a\in\Sigma_M\cup\{\square\}$, with read pattern $\rho$ and $\rho'$ as in Lemma 4.5(3): the *neighbour cases* are $\nu\in\{{\rm blk}(e)Z_l:e\in\Sigma_M\}\cup\{|\,\mathrm{exact}\}$ if some move in $\delta(q,a)$ goes left, and the single case $\nu=Z_l$ otherwise. For each case the current pattern is $\pi:=(\rho;\,c(q)\nu)$, and each move $(q_i,b_i,\mathrm{dir}_i)$ gives a next pattern $\pi'_i$: $(\rho';\,c(q_i){\rm blk}(b_i)\nu)$ for $R$; $({\rm blk}(b_i)\rho';\,c(q_i)\nu)$ for $S$; $({\rm blk}(e){\rm blk}(b_i)\rho';\,c(q_i)Z_l)$ for $L$ when $\nu={\rm blk}(e)Z_l$, and $({\rm blk}(b_i)\rho';\,c(q_i)\,|\,\mathrm{exact})$ for $L$ at the left end. The rule is *conjunctive*, $\pi\Rightarrow\{\pi'_1,\pi'_2\}$ with backward form $\forall Z_r\forall Z_l\,(C(\pi'_1)\to(C(\pi'_2)\to C(\pi)))$, when all successors are needed: at universal states for tag 0 and at existential states for tag 1. Otherwise there is one rule $\pi\Rightarrow\pi'_i$ per move, with backward form $\forall Z_r\forall Z_l\,(C(\pi'_i)\to C(\pi))$. A single move gives a single rule.
* *halting axioms:* $\forall Z_r\forall Z_l\,C(Z_r,H_{\rm acc}Z_l)$ and $\forall Z_r\forall Z_l\,C(Z_r,H_{\rm rej}Z_l)$;
* *output rules, exact:* $\forall x\,(C(x,\,0\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start}))\to R(x))$ and $\forall x\,(C(x,\,1\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start}))\to\neg R(x))$, where $0\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start})$ stands for the closed term $t_{0\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start})}$. In the observational version: $\forall x\,(\mathrm{Std}(x)\to(\theta_2(x,t_{0\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start})})\to R(x)))$ and its rejecting twin.

Call the result $\mathcal A$, $T^C_{\mathcal A}$ its backward theory (rules, halting axioms, exact output rules) and $T^O_{\mathcal A}$ its observational translation (Def 4.4). The current patterns of distinct cases (state, read symbol, neighbour case, tag) are pairwise disjoint, by the argument of Lemma 4.5(i); the two rules of a non-conjunctive branching share one current pattern.

*Step 3 (the meaning of $C$ at reachable pairs).* Let $C^{\mathfrak T}$ be the least set of pairs of words containing the pairs with a halting prefix and closed under the backward rules (a conjunctive rule adds $\pi$ when all its next pairs are in; the others when their one next pair is in). Let $\mathrm{Reach}$ be the set of pairs reachable by the rules from the **exact** start pairs $(x,\,\eta\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start}))$, $x$ a word. Every pair in $\mathrm{Reach}$ is a phase pair of the run from a start pair or the code $\llbracket q,a,\mathrm{hp}\rrbracket_\eta$ of a configuration of $M_1$ reachable from $\#x$: the start rule gives $(x,\,c(\mathrm{inp}){\rm blk}(\#))$, phase inp gives $(\varepsilon,\,c(\mathrm{rew}){\rm blk}(x_{|x|})\cdots{\rm blk}(x_1){\rm blk}(\#))$, phase rew gives $({\rm blk}(\#){\rm blk}(x_1)\cdots{\rm blk}(x_{|x|}),\,c(q_0))=\llbracket q_0,\#x,0\rrbracket_\eta$, and from a configuration code the patterns that match are exactly those of its case, whose next pairs are the codes of its successors (the step argument of Lemma 4.5). For $\sigma\in\mathrm{Reach}$ let ${\rm acc}(\sigma)$ mean that the configuration of $\sigma$ accepts (for a phase pair: the start configuration it leads to accepts).

*Claim.* For $\sigma\in\mathrm{Reach}$ with tag 0: $\sigma\in C^{\mathfrak T}$ iff ${\rm acc}(\sigma)$. For $\sigma\in\mathrm{Reach}$ with tag 1: $\sigma\in C^{\mathfrak T}$ iff not ${\rm acc}(\sigma)$.

($\Leftarrow$, tag 0) By induction on the height of the finite computation tree of $\sigma$ (phase steps count as deterministic steps). A halting accepting configuration has prefix $H_{\rm acc}$, so it is in the base. At an existential configuration some successor accepts, and the rule for that move puts $\sigma$ in; at a universal one all successors accept and the conjunctive rule puts $\sigma$ in; a deterministic step or a phase step uses its single rule.
($\Rightarrow$, tag 0) The set $S_0:=\{\sigma\in\mathrm{Reach}:\text{tag }0,\ {\rm acc}(\sigma)\}\cup\{\text{0-tagged pairs outside }\mathrm{Reach}\}\cup\{\text{all 1-tagged pairs}\}$ contains the base (a reachable pair with prefix $H_{\rm acc}$ is a configuration in state ACC) and is closed under the backward rules: take a rule instance with current pair $\sigma$ whose next pairs (all of them, for a conjunctive rule; its one next pair, otherwise) lie in $S_0$. If $\sigma$ is 1-tagged or outside $\mathrm{Reach}$, $\sigma\in S_0$. Otherwise $\sigma\in\mathrm{Reach}$ is 0-tagged, the rule belongs to $\sigma$'s case, and its next pairs are codes of successors of $\sigma$, hence in $\mathrm{Reach}$ and 0-tagged, hence accepting. A conjunctive rule at tag 0 sits at a universal (or deterministic) configuration, all of whose successors are then accepting; a non-conjunctive one sits at an existential configuration with an accepting successor. Either way ${\rm acc}(\sigma)$. So $C^{\mathfrak T}\subseteq S_0$.
(tag 1) The same with acceptance and rejection, existential and universal, exchanged: at tag 1 the conjunctive rules sit at existential states.

So for the exact start pairs: $(x,0\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start}))\in C^{\mathfrak T}$ iff $M_1$ accepts $\#x$ iff $x\in X$, and $(x,1\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start}))\in C^{\mathfrak T}$ iff $x\notin X$.

*Step 4 (theories, truth, derivations).* By Step 3, $(\mathfrak T,C^{\mathfrak T},R:=X)\models T^C_{\mathcal A}\cup\Gamma_{f_X}$: the rules by closure, the halting axioms by the base, the exact output rules by the Claim. So $T^C_{\mathcal A}$ is about the named objects, and $T^C_{\mathcal A}\vdash\varphi_w^b$ only if $b=[w\in X]$. In $\mathfrak U$ put $R(t_w):=[w\in X]$, $R(\mathrm{pr}(a,b)):=[(a,b)\in C^{\mathfrak T}]$ for words $a,b$, and $R$ false on other pairs; as in Thm 4.6(ii), $\mathfrak U\models T^O_{\mathcal A}\cup\Gamma_{f_X}$ (the exact output rule's ground term $t_{\eta\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start})}$ is a word, so $\theta_2(x,t_{\eta\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start})})$ holds iff $(x,\eta\,{\rm st}(\mathrm{start}))\in C^{\mathfrak T}$). *Derivations.* For $w\in X$ fix an accepting computation tree of $M_1$ on $\#x$ (all successors at universal nodes, one accepting successor at existential nodes) and the phase pairs before it; derive $C(\sigma)$ for its nodes bottom-up: a leaf by the halting axiom (two A4 instances, two MPs), an inner node by the instance of its rule at its values and one MP per premise; then the exact output rule at $x:=t_w$ and MP. Rejection likewise with a rejecting tree and tag 1. In $T^O_{\mathcal A}$, $\mathrm{Std}$ premises are discharged as in Thm 4.6(iii). The number of lines may be exponential, but every line is a member, an instance of a member at the values of one reachable pair, or an atom about one, so every line, logical instances included, has size $O(S_1+|w|)$, polynomial. That is reading (a1) at a polynomial budget; the upper bound $\subseteq\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ is SD Thm 4.2. ∎

*Why the output rules must be exact.* Without a certificate phase nothing consumes the $z$ of Def 4.4's output rules. Phase rew rewinds every block-aligned $z$ onto the tape, so $M_1$ runs on $z'\#x$, a tape the normal form says nothing about. If $M_1$ has, at $q_0$, transitions on the letters $0$ and $1$ (legal: cell 0 of a start tape always holds $\#$), the free-$z$ theory proves $R(t_x)$ and $\neg R(t_x)$ for every $x$ (§11 item 1). In Lemma 4.5 and Thm 4.6 phase cert consumes $z$, so the free-$z$ output rules are correct there (Prop 9.1(a) uses this too).

## 5. Vocabulary or ontology (target T4)

Residuals: for $X\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ and a word $v$, $X_v:=\{u:uv\in X\}$. In the term model, $R(t_{uv})=R(\mathtt s_ut_v)$, so $X_v$ is what the subtree below $t_v$ sees. $X$ is regular iff it has finitely many residuals: the $X_v$ are the right quotients of $X$, i.e. the reversals of the left quotients of the reversal of $X$, and the Myhill–Nerode theorem applies to the reversal; regular languages are closed under reversal [known; textbook].

### 5.1 Finite theories with unary theoretical vocabulary see only finite-state regularities

*Relational form.* Let $L^{\rm rel}$ have a unary $\mathrm{Root}$, binary $S_0,S_1$, unary $R,P_1,\dots,P_k$ and unary $\mathrm{Cst}_1,\dots,\mathrm{Cst}_j$. An $\mathcal L_O\cup\{P_i\}\cup\{\mathtt c_1,\dots,\mathtt c_j\}$-structure $\mathfrak A$ ($\mathtt c_i$ constants) gives $\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}$: $\mathrm{Root}=\{\mathtt e\}$, $S_a$ the graph of $\mathtt s_a$ ($S_a(x,y)$ iff $\mathtt s_ax=y$), $\mathrm{Cst}_i=\{\mathtt c_i\}$. Every sentence $\psi$ has a relational translation $\psi^{\rm rel}$ (unnest the terms) with $\mathfrak A\models\psi$ iff $\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}\models\psi^{\rm rel}$ [known; standard]. $\equiv_q$ is agreement on all sentences of quantifier rank $\le q$; over a finite relational signature there are finitely many $\equiv_q$-classes, and $\equiv_q$ holds iff the duplicator wins the $q$-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game [known: Ehrenfeucht, *Fund. Math.* 49 (1961) 129–141 (bibliographic data checked, §14); Fraïssé 1954; textbook form, e.g. Ebbinghaus–Flum, *Finite Model Theory*].

**Lemma 5.2 (composition).** (a) For relational structures in one finite signature, $\mathfrak A_1\equiv_q\mathfrak B_1$ and $\mathfrak A_2\equiv_q\mathfrak B_2$ imply $\mathfrak A_1\sqcup\mathfrak A_2\equiv_q\mathfrak B_1\sqcup\mathfrak B_2$, where $\sqcup$ is the disjoint union with each relation interpreted as the union. (b) If $\mathfrak A,\mathfrak B$ arise from $\mathfrak A',\mathfrak B'$ (same domains) by defining each relation by a quantifier-free formula, then $\mathfrak A'\equiv_q\mathfrak B'$ implies $\mathfrak A\equiv_q\mathfrak B$. **[proved, given the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem; computed: `rb3_ef_games.out` Parts A, B (referee B's exact game solver: 0 violations of (a) on 581 unions of informative $\equiv_q$ pairs, $q\le3$; the one-edge gluing of Thm 5.1, 101 cases, 0 violations, with a control that does fail)]**

*Proof.* (a) The duplicator answers a move in a component with her winning strategy for that component's game. Within a component the chosen tuples form a partial isomorphism; no relation holds across components on either side, and equality across components fails on both sides. (b) Substituting the defining formulas turns a rank-$q$ sentence about $\mathfrak A$ into a rank-$q$ sentence about $\mathfrak A'$. ∎

**Theorem 5.1 (unary theoretical vocabulary: only regular regularities).** Let $T$ be a finite set of sentences in $\mathcal L_O\cup\{P_1,\dots,P_k\}\cup\{\mathtt c_1,\dots,\mathtt c_j\}$ (unary theoretical predicates and constants; $k,j\ge0$) that is **compatible with the named structure** ($T\cup\Th_{\mathfrak T}$ consistent; in particular if $T$ is about the named objects). If $T\vdash\varphi_w^{[w\in X]}$ for every word $w$, then $X$ is regular. Quantitatively, $X$ has at most $N_q+|\mathrm{Suf}_{\rm c}|$ residuals, where $q$ is the quantifier rank of $(\bigwedge T)^{\rm rel}$, $N_q$ the number of $\equiv_q$-classes of $L^{\rm rel}\cup\{\mathrm{Rt},\mathrm{Att}\}$-structures, and $\mathrm{Suf}_{\rm c}$ the set of suffixes of the words $w_i$ with $\mathtt c_i=t_{w_i}$ in the model below, together with $\varepsilon$. **[proved, given the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem; no use of Rabin's theorem]**

*Proof.* Take $\mathfrak A\models T\cup\Th_{\mathfrak T}$. The closed terms denote pairwise distinct elements, and $R^{\mathfrak A}(t_w)=[w\in X]$ because $T$ proves the literals. For a word $v\ne\varepsilon$, $v=a\hat v$ ($a$ a letter), let $\mathrm{Sub}_v:=\{t_{uv}:u\in\{0,1\}^*\}$ (standard elements) and:
* $\mathfrak S_v$: the restriction of $\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}$ to $\mathrm{Sub}_v$, with a new unary $\mathrm{Rt}:=\{t_v\}$ (and $\mathrm{Att}:=\emptyset$);
* $\mathfrak C_v$: the restriction to the rest of the domain, with a new unary $\mathrm{Att}:=\{t_{\hat v}\}$ (and $\mathrm{Rt}:=\emptyset$).

*One edge between the parts.* $\mathrm{Sub}_v$ is closed under $\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1$; $\mathtt e\notin\mathrm{Sub}_v$ ($\mathtt e$ is not in the range of $\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1$). If $\mathtt s_b y=t_{uv}$ with $y\notin\mathrm{Sub}_v$, then $u=\varepsilon$: otherwise $u=b'u'$ and $t_{uv}=\mathtt s_{b'}t_{u'v}$ (outside-in convention), so $b=b'$ by disjoint ranges and $y=t_{u'v}\in\mathrm{Sub}_v$ by injectivity, both in $\Th_{\mathfrak T}$. So $\mathtt s_by=t_v=\mathtt s_at_{\hat v}$, hence $b=a$ and $y=t_{\hat v}$. Hence $\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}$ is obtained from $\mathfrak C_v\sqcup\mathfrak S_v$ by redefining $S_a(x,y)$ as $S_a(x,y)\vee(\mathrm{Att}(x)\wedge\mathrm{Rt}(y))$ and forgetting $\mathrm{Att},\mathrm{Rt}$: quantifier-free.

*The subtrees are alike up to labels.* For $v,v'\notin\mathrm{Suf}_{\rm c}$ the map $t_{uv}\mapsto t_{uv'}$ is an isomorphism of the $\{\mathrm{Root},S_0,S_1,\mathrm{Rt},\mathrm{Cst}_1,\dots,\mathrm{Cst}_j\}$-reducts of $\mathfrak S_v$ and $\mathfrak S_{v'}$: $\mathrm{Root}$ and the $\mathrm{Cst}_i$ are empty on both, because a constant lies in $\mathrm{Sub}_v$ only if it is some $t_{w_i}$ with $v$ a suffix of $w_i$, and nonstandard denotations lie in no $\mathrm{Sub}_v$.

*Transplanting.* Suppose $X$ has more than $N_q+|\mathrm{Suf}_{\rm c}|$ residuals. Choose one word per residual; more than $N_q$ of them lie outside $\mathrm{Suf}_{\rm c}$, so two of them, $v\ne v'$, have $X_v\ne X_{v'}$ and $\mathfrak S_v\equiv_q\mathfrak S_{v'}$. Let $\mathfrak A^*$ be $\mathfrak A$ with the labels on $\mathrm{Sub}_v$ replaced by those copied from $\mathrm{Sub}_{v'}$: $R^*(t_{uv}):=R^{\mathfrak A}(t_{uv'})$, $P_i^*(t_{uv}):=P_i^{\mathfrak A}(t_{uv'})$, everything else unchanged (in particular the $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-reduct and the constants). Then $\mathfrak S^*_v\cong\mathfrak S_{v'}\equiv_q\mathfrak S_v$ and $\mathfrak C^*_v=\mathfrak C_v$, so by Lemma 5.2, $(\mathfrak A^*)^{\rm rel}\equiv_q\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}$ and $\mathfrak A^*\models T$. Its reduct still satisfies $\Th_{\mathfrak T}$, so, as $T$ proves the literals, $R^*(t_{uv})=[uv\in X]$ for all $u$. But $R^*(t_{uv})=[uv'\in X]$. Hence $X_v=X_{v'}$, a contradiction. (Overlapping subtrees, $\mathrm{Sub}_v\supset\mathrm{Sub}_{v'}$, are harmless: the labels are copied from the original structure.) ∎

*Contrapositive form* (A-m5). A finite theory in $\mathcal L_O$ plus unary predicates and constants that proves every literal datum of a non-regular $X$ is inconsistent with $\Th_{\mathfrak T}$; by compactness it proves the negation of some $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-sentence true in $\mathfrak T$. This covers the finite observational theories of Thm 4.6(ii) and Prop 4.7, which have no theoretical vocabulary at all; for $T^O_{\mathcal P}$ the sentence is $\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$ (Prop 9.1(b)).

*Why the hypotheses matter.* Unary predicates and constants are *labels*: they do not connect the subtree to its context, so the composition lemma applies. A binary theoretical relation can connect any two subtrees (Thm 4.6(i)); objects that violate $\Th_{\mathfrak T}$ (pairs, with $\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$) can carry information between subtrees through $\mathtt s_0$, which is not injective on them (Thm 4.6(ii)); and a finite *set* is essential, since infinite sets of padded literals are true of the named objects and fit $\mathsf P$ (Thm 3.3).

*Relation to scout S's proof.* Scout S translated $T$ into monadic second-order logic over the binary tree and used Rabin's tree theorem (Rabin 1969, *Trans. AMS* 141, 1–35; bibliographic data checked by scout S), plus a reduction from tree automata to word automata proved there. The present proof uses only the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem, needs only compatibility with $\Th_{\mathfrak T}$ (models may have nonstandard elements), and allows constants. Rabin's theorem is no longer used.

### 5.2 Every regular regularity has a short observational theory

**Theorem 5.3 (signature theories; scout S Thm 3.12(b)).** For every regular $X$ there is a finite $T\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$, true in $(\mathfrak T,X)$, such that every datum has a $\mathsf K$-derivation from $T$ with $O(|w|)$ lines, each of size $O(|w|)$ (constants depending on $X$). So the budgets needed are: under (a1), linear; under (a2) and (b2), a constant ($\ge$ the largest member, resp. the total size of $T$; with smaller budgets, a finite table of short data as in Thm 4.6(iv)); under (b), quadratic. **[proved; computed: `t4_regular.out` Part A (two languages not used by scout S), scout S's s3 Part C; `rb4_regular.out` Part B (three further languages); `t6_revision.out` Part F (the window)]**

*Proof.* Let $k_X$ be the number of residuals of $X$; write $A/a:=\{u:ua\in A\}$, so $X_v/a=X_{av}$. Let $\approx_j$ be the equivalence "agree on all words of length $<j$" on the finite set $\{X_v\}$. Then $A\approx_{j+1}A'$ iff ($\varepsilon\in A\Leftrightarrow\varepsilon\in A'$) and $A/a\approx_jA'/a$ for $a=0,1$, so $\approx_{j+1}$ is a fixed function of $\approx_j$; once $\approx_j=\approx_{j+1}$ the sequence is constant; $\approx_0$ has one class and each strict refinement adds a class; so $\approx_{k_X-1}$ is equality. Hence the window $\{0,1\}^{<W}$ with $W:=\max(k_X-1,1)$, or any smaller window that separates the residuals and contains $\varepsilon$, determines $X_v$. Put ${\rm sig}(v):=X_v\cap\{0,1\}^{<W}$; ${\rm sig}(av)$ is a function $\sigma\mapsto\sigma_a$ of ${\rm sig}(v)$. With $S_\sigma(x):=\bigwedge_{|u|<W}R(\mathtt s_ux)^{\sigma(u)}$ let
$$T:=\{S_{{\rm sig}(\varepsilon)}(\mathtt e)\}\cup\{\forall x\,(S_\sigma(x)\to S_{\sigma_a}(\mathtt s_ax)):\sigma\text{ realised},\ a\in\{0,1\}\}.$$
$S_\sigma(t_v)$ holds in $(\mathfrak T,X)$ iff ${\rm sig}(v)=\sigma$, so $T$ is true there. Derive $S_{{\rm sig}(v)}(t_v)$ for the suffixes $v$ of $w$ from the inside out (one A4 instance and MPs per letter) and read off the conjunct $u=\varepsilon$, which is $R(t_w)^{[w\in X]}$. (The checker uses the equivalent curried form with one literal per conclusion, so that only A4 and MP occur.) ∎

The bound $W=\max(k_X-1,1)$ is attained (B6): on random automata the least separating window equals $k_X-1$ in many cases (computed: `rb4_regular.out` Part A, 249 of 3000 DFAs; `t6_revision.out` Part F, 90 of 1500 DFAs with $k_X\ge3$; it never exceeds $\max(k_X-1,1)$ in either).

*Computed* (`t4_regular.out`, Part A; languages not used by scout S): $X_A$ = "contains $010$" ($k_X=4$) and $X_B$ = "the third letter is 1" ($k_X=8$), both separated by the window $|u|<3$; theories of 63 and 119 sentences in curried form, true in $(\mathfrak T,X)$ for all $|x|\le9$; all 1023 data with $|w|\le9$ derived and checked; at most 637 lines, largest line 155 symbols. Part B: windowed residual counts at depths $2,4,6,8$ stabilise for $X_A$, $X_B$ ($4$; $8$) and grow for $\{0^m1^m\}$ ($5,9,13,17$), $\mathrm{EQ}$ ($5,9,13,17$) and the palindromes ($7,31,127,511$), the "colliding residuals" count that rules out a finite signature theory. The script uses the least separating window, which the proof allows; the guaranteed window $W$ would have given $2^7$ literals per signature for $X_B$ and exhausted memory in a first version. Referee B (`rb4` Part B): MOD3, END01 and a random 10-residual language, windows 2, 3, 3, theories of 21, 63, 147 sentences, all true at $|v|\le9$, all 511 data $|w|\le8$ derived, lines linear in $|w|$; residual counts (`rb4` Part C) identical to `t4_regular` Part B for all five languages.

### 5.3 The table

**Corollary 5.4 (vocabulary or ontology).** On literal sequences, the fit classes of **finite** theories ($B=\emptyset$; membership is linear-time for finite sets) are:

| finite theories | (a2), (b2): every nondecreasing unbounded budget with $h(m)\ge m+1$ | (b): polynomial | (a1): polynomial |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathcal L_O$ + unary predicates + constants, compatible with the named structure | REG | REG | REG |
| $\mathcal L_O$ + **one binary** relation, about the named objects | all decidable | $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ | $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ |
| $\mathcal L_O$ only, quantifying over unnamed objects (inconsistent with $\Th_{\mathfrak T}$, Thm 5.1) | all decidable | $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ | $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ |
| all finite theories, any vocabulary | all decidable | $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ | $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ |

**[proved: row 1 Thm 5.1 (upper bound, any budget, also with free deduction) and Thm 5.3; rows 2–3 Thm 4.6 and Prop 4.7; row 4 upper bounds SD Cor 3.2, Cor 4.4 (all hypotheses), lower bounds rows 2–3; CKS and the one-tape simulation as stated there]**

So a finite theory fits a non-regular regularity only if it has a polyadic theoretical relation or quantifies over objects that do not obey the observational laws of the named objects. Either suffices, at the same fit classes, and the translation between them (Thm 4.6(ii)) costs a constant factor in size. Unary theoretical predicates and theoretical constants do not help.

**Corollary 5.5 (with free deduction too).** For a decidable non-regular $X$, let $K^{\rm named}_{\rm fin}(D)$ be the least $K(\psi)$ over single sentences $\psi$ in $\mathcal L_O$ plus any finitely many unary predicates and constants, compatible with the named structure and proving $\psi_D$. Then $K^{\rm named}_{\rm fin}(D^X_n)\to\infty$, while $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_O\cup\{C\}}(D^X_n)\le K(\mathcal V)+c$ and $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_O}(D^X_n)\le K(\mathcal V)+c$ for a decider $\mathcal V$ of $X$. **[proved]**

*Proof.* By Thm 5.1 no admissible sentence proves all of $D^X$; each one fails some datum, and only finitely many sentences have $K\le j$. The upper bounds are the conjunctions of $T^C_{\mathcal P_{\mathcal V}}$ and $T^O_{\mathcal P_{\mathcal V}}$, computable from $\mathcal V$. ∎

The upper bound for $C$ is an effective instance of the Kleene–Craig–Vaught theorem (§10): $\{$the axioms of Def 1.3$\}\cup\Gamma_{f_X}$ is r.e. without finite models, so (as reported) it has a finitely axiomatised, model-conservative extension with one additional binary relation symbol, into which $(\mathfrak T,X)$ expands.

**Remark 5.6 (infinite hypotheses about the named objects).** For *infinite* observational axiom sets true of the named objects (with polynomial membership), the fit classes at polynomial budgets are: exactly $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ under (b) (certificate sets are true of the named objects, since $\theta_c$ is valid; the class of all hypotheses bounds it); under (a1), at least $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ and every $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$-hard set in $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ (Prop 9.4; scout S had one specific complete set), at most $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$; equality is open. The dichotomy of Cor 5.4 is a statement about finite theories, the hypotheses whose own axioms must be short. **[proved (bounds); open (equality)]**

**Remark 5.7 (what the section says).**
* *Vocabulary is cheap to eliminate; vocabulary and ontology together are not.* Theoretical vocabulary can be eliminated at polynomial cost (§4: hypothesis by hypothesis under (b), at the level of fit classes under (a1), (a2), (b2)); the good eliminations store the content in objects no observational term names. A finite theory confined to the observational vocabulary *and* to the laws of the named objects expresses only finite-state regularities.
* *Ramsey and pairing.* Ramsey sentences eliminate theoretical predicates by quantifying over them in second order; the pairing gadget eliminates them in first order by quantifying over objects (Lemma 4.2). The price: the observational theory must prove the negation of some sentence true of the named structure (Thm 5.1, contrapositive form); the theory built here proves that there are objects $z$ with $\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$, which no named object is (Prop 9.1(b)).
* *Caveats.* "Named object" is a formal stand-in for "observable thing". The observational language is poor (a free unary structure: observation sequences). With one binary observable relation Thm 5.1 fails (Prop 9.3); with only unary observables it survives (Prop 9.2). That observation terms can apply to unobservable things (the observational $R$ applied to pairs) is Putnam's point against identifying the observational/theoretical vocabulary line with the observable/unobservable line (Putnam 1962, "What theories are not"; content from the SEP entry "Theoretical Terms in Science", as recorded by scout S; known, secondary).

## 6. Free deduction and finite theories (target T5)

$K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L}(D)$, $W_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L}(D)$: least $K(\psi)$, resp. $\sum\mm(\psi)$, over single sentences $\psi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ with $B\cup\{\psi\}$ consistent and $\vdash\psi_D$ (NOTATION §3.3). $\Kpad(n):=\min_{m\ge n}K(m)$ (SOI-N; `fundamentals` Prop 1.8(d)), nondecreasing and unbounded. ### 6.1 Infinity

$\mathcal L_{\rm mon}:=\{=\}\cup\{P_i:i\in\mathbb N\}$ (unary predicates), $\mathcal L_f:=\mathcal L_{\rm mon}\cup\{f\}$ ($f$ unary function). $\mathrm{Inf}_n:=\exists x_1\cdots\exists x_n\bigwedge_{i<j}x_i\ne x_j$; $D_n:=\{(\mathrm{Inf}_m,1):m\le n\}$, so $\psi_{D_n}\equiv\mathrm{Inf}_n$. $\iota_f:=\forall x\forall y\,(fx=fy\to x=y)\wedge\exists y\forall x\,fx\ne y$ (NOTATION §6.3).

**Lemma 6.1 (quantitative finite model property of monadic logic; scout N1's B1.1).** If $\psi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}$ uses $k$ predicate symbols, has quantifier rank $q$ and has a model, then it has one with at most $2^k\max(q,1)$ elements. For $k=0$ all sets of size $\ge\max(q,1)$ satisfy the same rank-$q$ sentences. **[proved, given the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem; computed: `t5_monadic.out` Part A (three predicates), scout N1's c2 Parts A, B (rerun identical); `rb3_ef_games.out` Part C (by games: truncation at $\max(q,1)$ preserves $\equiv_q$ in all 185 structures; truncation at $q-1$ breaks it in 40/40; the bound $2^kq$ is attained by "every cell has $\ge q$ elements")]**

*Proof* (scout N1's, re-checked). Let $M\models\psi$, $q':=\max(q,1)$; partition $M$ into the $2^k$ cells $C_\tau$ by the values of the $k$ predicates, and let $M'\subseteq M$ keep $\min(|C_\tau|,q')$ elements of each cell ($M'\ne\emptyset$). The duplicator wins the $q$-round game on $(M,M')$ keeping the invariant "chosen pairs lie in the same cell, and $a_i=a_j\iff b_i=b_j$": a repeated element is answered by its partner; a fresh element in cell $\tau$ by a fresh element of $\tau$ on the other side, which exists — if $|C_\tau|\ge q'$ both sides have $\ge q'\ge q$ elements of $\tau$ and fewer than $q$ are chosen; if $|C_\tau|<q'$ both sides have exactly $|C_\tau|$ elements of $\tau$ and the chosen ones are matched bijectively. The invariant gives a partial isomorphism for $=$ and the $P_i$. So $M'\equiv_qM$ and $M'\models\psi$. ∎

**Theorem 6.2 (infinity needs a theoretical function; scout N1's B1).** Let $\mathrm{mb}(\psi):=2^{k(\psi)}\max(q(\psi),1)$ for $\psi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}$.
* (a) If $\psi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}$ is consistent and $\psi\vdash\mathrm{Inf}_n$, then $n\le\mathrm{mb}(\psi)$. No consistent finite monadic theory proves all of $D_\infty$.
* (b) $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}(D_n)=\Kpad(n)\pm c$. So $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}(D_n)\to\infty$, and $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_{\rm mon}}(D_n)=o(G(n))$ for every computable unbounded nondecreasing $G$.
* (c) $\iota_f$ is consistent and proves every $\mathrm{Inf}_n$, so $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L_f}(D_n)\le K(\iota_f)+c$.
* (d) Under the prior $\mm$ on $\Sent_{\mathcal L_f}$, the posterior probability, given $D_n$, that the finite hypothesis does not use $f$ is at most $2^c\sum_{m\ge n}\mm(m)/\mm(\iota_f)\to0$.
* (e) $K_A^{\{=\}}(D_n)\le c$ (the r.e. theory $\{\mathrm{Inf}_m:m\ge1\}$).

**[proved]**

*Proof.* (a) Lemma 6.1: a model with $\le\mathrm{mb}(\psi)$ elements. (b) $\mathrm{mb}(\psi)\ge n$ is computable from $\psi$, so $K(\psi)\ge K(\mathrm{mb}(\psi))-c\ge\Kpad(n)-c$; conversely $\mathrm{Inf}_m$ ($m\ge n$) is consistent, proves $\mathrm{Inf}_n$ and has $K\le K(m)+c$. **Proposition 6.3 (the cost of "at least $n$ things" in symbols and in bits; sharpening scout N1's B1′).** Let $|\psi|$ be the number of symbols (each $P_i$ one symbol), and let $\psi$ range over **consistent** sentences with $\psi\vdash\mathrm{Inf}_n$ (an inconsistent sentence proves everything; A-m2).
* (a) *Pure equality:* $q(\psi)\ge n$ for $n\ge2$, so $|\psi|\ge n$; $\mathrm{Inf}_n$ itself has $\Theta(n^2)$ symbols ($1+5\binom n2+2n$ in scout N1's convention).
* (b) *Monadic, symbols:* $|\psi|\ge\log n-\log\log n-O(1)$, from $n\le2^kq\le2^{|\psi|}|\psi|$; and $|\psi|=O(\log n)$ is attained, by the **carry counter**
$$\mathrm{Cnt}'_k:=\forall x\,Q_0x\ \wedge\ \bigwedge_{i<k}\forall x\,(Q_{i+1}x\leftrightarrow(Q_ix\wedge P_ix))\ \wedge\ \exists x\bigwedge_{i<k}\neg P_ix\ \wedge\ \forall x\big(\neg Q_kx\to\exists y\bigwedge_{i<k}(P_iy\leftrightarrow(P_ix\oplus Q_ix))\big),$$
which has $64k+13$ symbols in scout N1's convention (scout N1's $\mathrm{Cnt}_k$, without carry predicates, is quadratic) and holds exactly in the monadic structures in which the $Q_i$ are the carries of the $P$-vector and all $2^k$ $P$-cells are nonempty. So $\mathrm{Cnt}'_{\lceil\log n\rceil}\vdash\mathrm{Inf}_n$, and the monadic cost in symbols is $\Theta(\log n)$ up to the $\log\log n$ term of the lower bound.
* (c) *With $f$:* $O(1)$ ($\iota_f$).
* (d) *Monadic, bits.* Code sentences in Polish notation, symbol by symbol, with a prefix-free code on the symbols in which each of the finitely many logical symbols and variables used costs between 1 and $c_0$ bits and the predicate symbol $P_i$ costs $\ell(i)$ bits, $\ell$ the length function of a prefix-free code on $\mathbb N$ with $\ell(i)\le2\log(i+1)+O(1)$ (e.g. Elias gamma). Then the set of codes of sentences is prefix-free, so $\sum_\psi2^{-{\rm bits}(\psi)}\le1$ and $2^{-\alpha\,{\rm bits}(\psi)}$, $\alpha\ge1$, is a genuine sub-probability on sentences. In this code: pure equality costs $\ge n$ bits; with $f$, $O(1)$ bits; monadic, $\Theta(\log n\cdot\log\log n)$ bits.

Hence, for the weight $2^{-\alpha|\psi|}$ of the *single best* theory in each class (a comparison of single theories; with each $P_i$ one symbol this weight is not normalisable and is not a prior, A-m2), the best theory with $f$ outweighs the best monadic theory by $n^{\Theta(\alpha)}$ and the best pure-equality theory by $2^{\Omega(\alpha n)}$. Under the normalisable bit weight of (d) the factors are $2^{\Theta(\alpha\log n\log\log n)}=n^{\Theta(\alpha\log\log n)}$ and $2^{\Omega(\alpha n)}$. **[proved; computed: `t5_monadic.out` Part B (the counter), `rb6_monadic_factoring.out` Parts A, B (the counter as written, and its symbol count in three conventions: $64k+13$, $62k+13$, $24k+14$, linear in each), `t6_revision.out` Part G (the bit bounds)]**

*Proof.* (a) Let $M\models\psi$; then $|M|\ge n$. If $|M|\ge\max(q,1)$, Lemma 6.1 with $k=0$ makes every set of size $\max(q,1)$ a model of $\psi$, so $\max(q,1)\ge n$; otherwise $\max(q,1)>|M|\ge n$. Either way $q\ge n$ when $n\ge2$, and each quantifier is a symbol. (b) *Lower bound:* Thm 6.2(a) gives $n\le2^k\max(q,1)$ with $k,q\le|\psi|$. *Correctness of $\mathrm{Cnt}'_k$:* the definitional conjuncts force $Q_ix\leftrightarrow\bigwedge_{j<i}P_jx$, the carry into bit $i$ of the binary increment. If all $P$-vectors are realised, the remaining conjuncts hold (every non-maximal vector has its increment). Conversely, the vector $0$ is realised, and if the vector of value $v<2^k-1$ is realised then so is that of $v+1$ ($\neg Q_kx$ says that $x$'s vector is not all ones; bit $i$ of $v+1$ is $P_i\oplus Q_i$); by induction all $2^k$ vectors are realised, so there are $\ge2^k$ elements. Each conjunct indexed by $i$ has constant size. (c) Thm 6.2(c). (d) *Prefix-freeness:* a Polish-notation sentence is determined, symbol by symbol, by its own beginning, so no sentence code is a proper prefix of another. *Lower bound, monadic:* a consistent $\psi\vdash\mathrm{Inf}_n$ with $k$ distinct predicate symbols and rank $q$ contains at least $q$ quantifier symbols ($\ge1$ bit each) and $k$ distinct predicate codewords. Sort their lengths $\ell_{(1)}\le\dots\le\ell_{(k)}$; by Kraft, $j\,2^{-\ell_{(j)}}\le\sum_{i\le j}2^{-\ell_{(i)}}\le1$, so $\ell_{(j)}\ge\log j$ and the predicate codewords cost $\ge\log k!\ge k\log k-k\log e$ bits. With $n\le2^k\max(q,1)$: if $k\ge\frac12\log n$, the cost is $\ge\frac12\log n\,(\log\log n-1)-\frac{\log e}2\log n$; if $k<\frac12\log n$, then $q\ge n/2^k>\sqrt n$. Either way ${\rm bits}(\psi)=\Omega(\log n\log\log n)$. *Upper bound:* $\mathrm{Cnt}'_k$ with $k=\lceil\log n\rceil$, its $2k+1$ predicates numbered $1,\dots,2k+1$, has $64k+13$ symbols of at most $\max(c_0,2\log(2k+2)+O(1))$ bits each: $O(\log n\log\log n)$. *Pure equality:* $\ge q\ge n$ symbols of $\ge1$ bit. *With $f$:* $\iota_f$ is a fixed sentence. ∎

### 6.3 r.e. axiom sets are finite theories with theoretical apparatus

**Theorem 6.5 (an effective, many-sorted form of the Kleene–Craig–Vaught theorem, with the induced Bayesian equivalence; scout N1's B3, there a proof sketch).** Let $\mathcal L$ be a finite language (relation, function and constant symbols, with equality) and $B\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L}$ decidable. Let $\mathcal L^+$ be the three-sorted extension (many-sorted first-order logic; every sort has a nonempty domain) with:
* sort $\mathsf o$ carrying $\mathcal L$;
* sort $\mathsf n$ with $0,S,+,\cdot,<$;
* sort $\mathsf a$ ("assignments") with $\mathrm{val}:\mathsf a\times\mathsf n\to\mathsf o$, $\mathrm{upd}:\mathsf a\times\mathsf n\times\mathsf o\to\mathsf a$, $\mathrm{den}:\mathsf n\times\mathsf a\to\mathsf o$, and a relation $\mathrm{Sat}\subseteq\mathsf n\times\mathsf a$.

There is a computable injective map $e\mapsto F_e$ from indices to single $\mathcal L^+$-sentences such that for every $e$
$$\Cn(B\cup\{F_e\})\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L}=\Cn(B\cup\re e)\qquad(\text{both sides inconsistent together}).$$
Consequently, for every finite data set $D$ in $\mathcal L$: $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}(D)=K_A^{\mathcal L}(D)\pm c$ and $2^{-c}W_A^{\mathcal L}(D)\le W_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}(D)\le2^cW_A^{\mathcal L}(D)$. **[proved, given the $\Sigma_1$-completeness of $\Q$ (known; textbook, §14)]**

*Construction.* Fix a Gödel numbering of the $\mathcal L$-terms and -formulas (variables $x_0,x_1,\dots$) and $\Sigma_1$ formulas of $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$ that define in $\mathbb N$ the syntactic relations: $\mathrm{Var}(c,v)$ "$c$ codes $x_v$"; for each function symbol $g$ of arity $k$ (constants: $k=0$), $\mathrm{App}_g(c,d_1,\dots,d_k)$ "the $d_i$ code terms $t_i$ and $c$ codes $g(t_1,\dots,t_k)$"; for each relation symbol $Q$, $\mathrm{At}_Q(c,\vec d)$, and $\mathrm{Eq}(c,d,d')$, for atomic formulas over coded terms; $\mathrm{Neg}(c,d)$ "$d$ codes a formula $\chi$ and $c$ codes $\neg\chi$"; $\mathrm{Imp}(c,d,d')$; $\mathrm{All}(c,v,d)$ "$d$ codes $\chi$ and $c$ codes $\forall x_v\chi$"; $\mathrm{Enum}_e(c)$ "$c\in\re e$ and $c$ codes an $\mathcal L$-sentence". The bounded quantifiers in these formulas use $<$. $F_e$ is the conjunction of:
* (Q) Robinson's $\Q$ on sort $\mathsf n$ **including the defining axiom $\forall x\forall y\,(x<y\leftrightarrow\exists z\,(Sz+x=y))$** (axiom Q8 in the Open Logic Project's formulation of $\Q$), so that $\Q$ is $\Sigma_1$-complete for formulas whose bounded quantifiers use $<$ (A-m3);
* (U) $\forall\alpha\forall v\forall x\,\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,v,x),v)=x$ and $\forall\alpha\forall v\forall w\forall x\,(v\ne w\to\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,v,x),w)=\mathrm{val}(\alpha,w))$;
* (D) $\forall c\forall v\,(\mathrm{Var}(c,v)\to\forall\alpha\,\mathrm{den}(c,\alpha)=\mathrm{val}(\alpha,v))$ and, for each $g$, $\forall c\forall\vec d\,(\mathrm{App}_g(c,\vec d)\to\forall\alpha\,\mathrm{den}(c,\alpha)=g(\mathrm{den}(d_1,\alpha),\dots,\mathrm{den}(d_k,\alpha)))$;
* (S) $\forall c\forall\vec d\,(\mathrm{At}_Q(c,\vec d)\to\forall\alpha\,(\mathrm{Sat}(c,\alpha)\leftrightarrow Q(\mathrm{den}(d_1,\alpha),\dots)))$ for each $Q$; the same for $\mathrm{Eq}$ with $=$; $\forall c\forall d\,(\mathrm{Neg}(c,d)\to\forall\alpha\,(\mathrm{Sat}(c,\alpha)\leftrightarrow\neg\mathrm{Sat}(d,\alpha)))$; the same for $\mathrm{Imp}$ with $\to$; $\forall c\forall v\forall d\,(\mathrm{All}(c,v,d)\to\forall\alpha\,(\mathrm{Sat}(c,\alpha)\leftrightarrow\forall x\,\mathrm{Sat}(d,\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,v,x))))$, $x$ of sort $\mathsf o$;
* (E) $\forall c\,(\mathrm{Enum}_e(c)\to\forall\alpha\,\mathrm{Sat}(c,\alpha))$, with $e$ written as a numeral (so $e\mapsto F_e$ is injective).

*Proof.* ($\supseteq$) Let $\varphi\in\re e$. $\Q$ proves the true $\Sigma_1$ sentence $\mathrm{Enum}_e(\underline{\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner})$, so $F_e\vdash\forall\alpha\,\mathrm{Sat}(\underline{\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner},\alpha)$. By external induction on terms $t$ and formulas $\chi$ whose variables are among $x_{v_1},\dots,x_{v_j}$: $F_e\vdash\forall\alpha\,\mathrm{den}(\underline{\ulcorner t\urcorner},\alpha)=t^\alpha$ and $F_e\vdash\forall\alpha\,(\mathrm{Sat}(\underline{\ulcorner\chi\urcorner},\alpha)\leftrightarrow\chi^\alpha)$, where $^\alpha$ replaces each $x_v$ by $\mathrm{val}(\alpha,\underline v)$. Each step instantiates one clause at the numerals of the immediate subterms or subformulas, whose guard is a true $\Sigma_1$ sentence and hence provable in $\Q$; the quantifier step uses (U) and $\Q\vdash\underline v\ne\underline w$ for $v\ne w$, which give $\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,\underline v,x),\underline v)=x$ and $\mathrm{val}(\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,\underline v,x),\underline w)=\mathrm{val}(\alpha,\underline w)$, so the induction hypothesis at $\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,\underline v,x)$ turns $\forall x\,\mathrm{Sat}(\ulcorner\chi\urcorner,\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,\underline v,x))$ into $(\forall x_v\chi)^\alpha$. Extra (nonstandard) witnesses of the guards in other models only add constraints; they cannot block these derivations. For the sentence $\varphi$, $\varphi^\alpha=\varphi$; sort $\mathsf a$ is nonempty (many-sorted logic), so from $\forall\alpha\,\varphi$ we get $F_e\vdash\varphi$.
($\subseteq$) Let $\mathfrak M$ be an $\mathcal L$-structure with $\mathfrak M\models B\cup\re e$. Expand it: sort $\mathsf n$ is $\mathbb N$ (with the true $<$); sort $\mathsf a$ is $M^{\mathbb N}$ with $\mathrm{val}(\alpha,v):=\alpha(v)$, $\mathrm{upd}(\alpha,v,x):=\alpha[v\mapsto x]$; $\mathrm{den}(c,\alpha)$ the value of the term coded by $c$ under $\alpha$ (a fixed element if $c$ codes no term); $\mathrm{Sat}(c,\alpha)$ iff $c$ codes a formula $\chi$ with $\mathfrak M\models\chi[\alpha]$. The guards are evaluated in $\mathbb N$, where the $\Sigma_1$ formulas mean what they say; so each clause holds (the guards of $\mathrm{Neg},\mathrm{Imp},\mathrm{All}$ require the components to code formulas, so the clauses never compare a non-formula with a formula), and (E) holds because $\mathfrak M\models\re e$. Thus every model of $B\cup\re e$ expands to a model of $B\cup\{F_e\}$, and by completeness every $\mathcal L$-consequence of $B\cup\{F_e\}$ is in $\Cn(B\cup\re e)$. If $B\cup\re e$ is inconsistent, so is $B\cup\{F_e\}$ by ($\supseteq$); if it is consistent, a model expands, so $B\cup\{F_e\}$ is consistent.
*Complexities.* $K(F_e)\le K(e)+c$, and $F_e$ is compatible with $D$ iff $e$ is. Conversely the map $F\mapsto g(F)$, an index of $\{\varphi\in\Sent_{\mathcal L}:B\cup\{F\}\vdash\varphi\}$, is computable; $g(F)$ is compatible with $D$ iff $F$ is (the set contains $\neg\forall x\,x=x$ iff $B\cup\{F\}$ is inconsistent); and $K(g(F))\le K(F)+c$. For $W$: $\mm(F_e)\ge2^{-c}\mm(e)$ because $e\mapsto F_e$ is computable and injective, and $\sum_{F:g(F)=j}\mm(F)\le2^c\mm(j)$ because pushforwards of $\mm$ under computable maps are dominated by $\mm$. ∎

*Reading.* The Solomonoff-style prior over r.e. axiom sets in $\mathcal L$ is, up to a constant factor, the prior over *finite* theories in $\mathcal L^+$. The idealised r.e. class already contains theoretical vocabulary ($\mathrm{Sat}$, $\mathrm{val}$, $\mathrm{upd}$, $\mathrm{den}$) and theoretical entities (numbers, assignments) — which is why adding vocabulary costs nothing there (Thm 2.1). The finite-theory prior in $\mathcal L$ alone is the one under which theoretical terms visibly pay (Thm 6.2, 6.4, Cor 5.5).

*One sort* **[proof sketch]**. With one sort and unary sort predicates $O,N,A$ in place of the sorts, the same construction, relativised, gives $\Cn(B^O\cup\{F^1_e\})\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L}^O=\Cn(B\cup\re e)^O$ for the relativisations $\chi^O$ of $\mathcal L$-sentences to $O$ (plus the axiom $\exists x\,O(x)$), so the equivalence holds for data relativised to $O$, a faithful translation in the sense of `fundamentals` Thm 4.4, at $O(1)$ cost. ## 7. Ramsey and Craig side by side; the hierarchy (target T6)

**Proposition 7.1 (infinity: Ramsey, Craig and a theoretical function).** In pure equality:
* (a) the Ramsey sentence $\exists f\,\iota_f$ of the theory $\{\iota_f\}$ — one existential second-order sentence, with no theoretical constant — has (in standard semantics) exactly the infinite sets as models;
* (b) $\{\mathrm{Inf}_m:m\ge1\}$ has exactly the infinite sets as models, and its consequences form a complete theory;
* (c) so $\exists f\,\iota_f$ and $\{\mathrm{Inf}_m\}$ (Craig's form: a first-order observational axiomatisation, necessarily infinite by Thm 6.2(a)) have the same first-order $\{=\}$-consequences, and no finite first-order theory in $\{=\}$, nor in any monadic extension, has them;
* (d) the first-order theory $\{\iota_f\}$ in the extended language $\{=,f\}$ is finite and has them as its $\{=\}$-consequences.

**[proved; (a) uses countable choice (every infinite set has a countable subset); computed: `rb3_ef_games.out` Part D (1200 random pure-equality sentences of rank 1–4: truth constant on sizes $q$ to $q+2$), `rb6` Part C]**

*Proof.* (a) If $f$ is injective and not surjective on $A$, then $A$ is infinite (Thm 6.2(c)); conversely an infinite $A$ has a countable subset $\{a_0,a_1,\dots\}$, and $a_i\mapsto a_{i+1}$, identity elsewhere, is injective and misses $a_0$. (b) The first claim is clear; completeness: by Lemma 6.1 with $k=0$, all sets of size $\ge q$ satisfy the same rank-$q$ sentences, so all infinite sets are elementarily equivalent. (c), (d) Same models, hence the same first-order consequences; Thm 6.2(a) and (c). ∎

So on this example **Ramsey elimination** keeps the theory finite but leaves first-order logic; **Craig elimination** stays first-order but gives up finiteness; **a finite first-order observational theory does not exist**; and a theoretical *function* (not a predicate) is what a finite first-order theory needs. In the inducers: $K_{\rm fin}^{\{=\}}\to\infty$, $K_A^{\{=\}}=O(1)$, $K_{\rm fin}^{\{=,f\}}=O(1)$ (Thm 6.2).

**Proposition 7.2 (factoring an inference graph; scout P's Prop 5.1, re-proved and completed).** Let $\mathcal L$ contain unary $A_1,\dots,A_{n_A}$, $B_1,\dots,B_{n_B}$; $T_{\rm dir}:=\{\forall x\,(A_ix\to B_jx)\}_{i,j}$ and, in $\mathcal L\cup\{\mathrm{Mid}\}$, $T_{\rm Mid}:=\{\forall x\,(A_ix\to\mathrm{Mid}\,x)\}_i\cup\{\forall x\,(\mathrm{Mid}\,x\to B_jx)\}_j$. An *atom-to-atom implication* is a sentence $\forall x\,(Ux\to Vx)$ with $U,V$ distinct unary predicate symbols.
* (a) $\Cn(T_{\rm Mid})\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L}=\Cn(T_{\rm dir})$.
* (b) Every theory of atom-to-atom implications over $\{A_i,B_j\}$ equivalent to $T_{\rm dir}$ has at least $n_An_B$ members.
* (c) In full first-order logic $T_{\rm dir}$ is equivalent to the single sentence $\forall x\,((A_1x\vee\dots\vee A_{n_A}x)\to(B_1x\wedge\dots\wedge B_{n_B}x))$ of length $O(n_A+n_B)$, so $\mathrm{Mid}$ saves only a constant factor there.
* (d) Every theory $S$ of atom-to-atom implications over $\{A_i,B_j,\mathrm{Mid}\}$ with $\Cn(S)\cap\Sent_{\mathcal L}=\Cn(T_{\rm dir})$ has at least $\min(n_An_B,\ n_A+n_B)$ members, and $T_{\rm dir}$ or $T_{\rm Mid}$ attains the bound. So the new predicate shortens the theory iff $(n_A-1)(n_B-1)>1$, by $\max(0,n_An_B-n_A-n_B)$ members.

**[proved; computed: `t5_monadic.out` Part C ((b), exhaustive for $(n_A,n_B)\in\{(1,2),(2,2),(2,3)\}$); `t6_revision.out` Part C ((b) for 9 pairs with $n_An_B\le6$, (d) for 10 pairs up to $(3,3)$, exact decision of the consequence relation, exhaustive in increasing size); `rb6_monadic_factoring.out` Part D (independently, (b) for 9 pairs and (d) for 4)]**

*Proof.* (a) $T_{\rm Mid}\vdash A_i\to\mathrm{Mid}\to B_j$. Every model of $T_{\rm dir}$ expands to one of $T_{\rm Mid}$ with $\mathrm{Mid}:=\bigcup_iA_i\subseteq\bigcap_jB_j$. (b) For a set $S$ of atom-to-atom implications over any set of unary predicates, $S\vdash\forall x\,(Ux\to Vx)$ iff $V$ is reachable from $U$ in the graph $S$: if not, the one-element structure in which exactly the atoms reachable from $U$ hold satisfies $S$, $U$ and $\neg V$. In models of $T_{\rm dir}$ no $A_i\to A_{i'}$, $B_j\to B_{j'}$ ($i\ne i'$, $j\ne j'$) or $B_j\to A_i$ holds (take the one-element structures with only $A_i$ true together with all $B$'s, resp. only $B_j$ true). (d) (B3.) $S$ proves every $A_i\to B_j$ and, by the equality of $\mathcal L$-consequences, none of the forbidden implications of (b). Take a shortest path from $A_i$ to $B_j$ in the graph $S$ (it has no repeated vertex). An interior vertex $A_{i'}$ would give $S\vdash A_i\to A_{i'}$ ($i'\ne i$); an interior $B_{j'}$ would give $S\vdash B_{j'}\to B_j$ ($j'\ne j$). So the path is the edge $A_i\to B_j$ or the path $A_i\to\mathrm{Mid}\to B_j$. With $I:=\{i:(A_i\to\mathrm{Mid})\in S\}$ and $J:=\{j:(\mathrm{Mid}\to B_j)\in S\}$, every pair outside $I\times J$ needs its own edge, so $|S|\ge|I|+|J|+n_An_B-|I||J|$. This function is affine in each of $|I|$ and $|J|$, so its minimum over $[0,n_A]\times[0,n_B]$ is at a corner: $n_An_B$, $n_An_B+n_A$, $n_An_B+n_B$ or $n_A+n_B$. Finally $n_An_B-(n_A+n_B)=(n_A-1)(n_B-1)-1$. ∎

**Remark 7.3 (reuse) [proof sketch].** If a defined formula of length $\ell$ occurs $k$ times in a theory, the definitional extension (one definition, $k$ occurrences of the new symbol) is shorter by about $(k-1)\ell-O(k)$ symbols. This is an upper bound on the length of the extended theory; that no shorter theory in the base language exists is not claimed.

## 8. The dilemma theorem (target T7)

**Theorem 8.1 (the theoretician's dilemma in axiom induction).** Data are observational sentences; in (ii)–(iv) literal data in $\mathcal L_O=\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,R\}$, with readings and polynomial budgets as in SD.
* (i) *In principle.* With deduction free, restricting hypotheses to the observational vocabulary changes the prior weight of every (Borel) class of observational contents, the predictive probability of every observational sentence and the cumulative log loss by at most a constant, in the one-tape, coin-free and Kolmogorov versions, and also when the time to check or to generate axioms is penalised. Tape by tape, the observational projection of a random theory has exactly the same law on observational data. (Thm 2.1)
* (ii) *Craig's construction in practice.* When derivations are charged, Craig's axiomatisation of a theory whose observational consequences have derivations of size $\mathrm{ds}$ needs derivations of size at least $2^{\mathrm{ds}}$ (Lemma 3.1). Every axiomatisation by padded observational literals with polynomial-time membership fits, at polynomial budgets, exactly the polynomial-time decidable regularities, under every reading (Thm 3.3). Axiom sets with theoretical symbols fit $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ (total material), $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ (every instance short) and every decidable regularity (only the theory's own axioms short); so Craig's construction loses unconditionally under the last two readings and, under the first, iff $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}\ne\mathsf P$ (Cor 3.5).
* (iii) *Eliminating the vocabulary in practice.* Theoretical vocabulary can be eliminated at polynomial cost: per hypothesis, with $O(1)$ bits and a polynomial change of budget, under total material (b), by storing proofs instead of run times (Prop 4.1); and at the level of fit classes under (a1), (a2), (b2), by storing the eliminated relations in objects that no observational term names — observational hypotheses, and already *finite* observational theories, fit what all hypotheses fit (Prop 4.3, Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7).
* (iv) *What is indispensable.* A finite theory in the observational vocabulary with any unary theoretical predicates and constants, compatible with the first-order theory of the named objects, fits exactly the regular regularities, under every reading and also with free deduction (Thm 5.1, 5.3). One **binary** theoretical relation over the named objects restores every decidable regularity ((a2), (b2)), $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ ((b)) and $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ ((a1)) (Thm 4.6, Prop 4.7). So does quantifying over unnamed objects; a finite theory that does so without theoretical vocabulary, and fits a non-regular regularity, must prove some $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1\}$-sentence false in the named structure (Thm 5.1); for the theory built here it is $\exists z\,\mathtt s_1\mathtt s_1z=z$, true of no named object (Prop 9.1(b)).
* (v) *Free deduction, finite theories.* With deduction free, finite theories gain without bound from a theoretical function (infinity: $K_{\rm fin}=\Kpad(n)\pm c\to\infty$ without it, $O(1)$ with it; unary predicates never help), from theoretical sorts (PA's theorems), and from a binary relation over the named objects (Cor 5.5); and r.e. axiom induction equals finite-theory induction with theoretical sorts up to $O(1)$, coin-free (Thm 6.2, 6.4, 6.5).

**Statuses.** (i) proved (penalised versions under (S), (S$_{\rm tape}$), given TF Lemma 2.1 and `sfi` Thm 3.4, Prop 3.5); (ii) proved, given SD Cor 3.2, 4.4, Prop 4.12 (under (F1)), the time hierarchy theorem and $\mathsf{APSPACE}=\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ (known, abstract level), the (b) clause conditional as stated; (iii) proved under (F1), given SD Thm 3.1, Prop 4.3, Cor 4.4, the one-tape simulation and CKS; (iv) proved, given the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem, the one-tape simulation and, for (a1), CKS; (v) proved, the PA clause given (KL) (Ryll-Nardzewski 1952; secondary level) and $\Con(\PA)$.

**What this does and does not show** (about the classical reply to Hempel; the philosophical assessment is `philosophy`'s).
* The classical reply — "Craigian axiomatisations are useless in practice" — becomes a theorem here, but only about *Craig's construction* and every run-padded axiomatisation: they are fast labellers in disguise ((ii)). It is not a theorem about theoretical vocabulary, which can be eliminated at polynomial cost ((iii): per hypothesis under total material; at the level of fit classes under the other readings, which is all the argument needs). The brief's seed ("the time-bounded separations make that reply a theorem") holds only in this narrow sense.
* What bounded deduction and finiteness make indispensable is theoretical *relations* (polyadic) or theoretical *entities* (objects outside the named structure), and either one will do ((iv)). Theoretical *properties* of observable things (unary predicates) never help a finite theory in this setting.
* In the idealised Solomonoff setting nothing theoretical is indispensable ((i)), because the hypothesis class already contains the apparatus ((v), Thm 6.5): the r.e. prior *is* a finite-theory prior with theoretical sorts. Where theoretical terms pay is a property of the hypothesis class, and the answer differs between classes in exactly the ways tabulated in §7.
* With free deduction, the existence of a finite theory with one binary theoretical relation fitting any r.e. observational content without finite models is the Kleene–Craig–Vaught theorem (§10); the theorems here add the derivation bounds, the fit classes and the comparison with unary vocabulary.
* The results concern one poor observational language (observation sequences: a free unary structure) and literal data; with a binary observable relation the boundary moves (Prop 9.3). "Named object" is a formal stand-in for "observable thing". The theorems say nothing about whether scientific theorising *should* use theoretical terms; they locate, inside these inducers, what can and cannot be traded away.

## 9. Extensions (target T8)

### 9.1 Speed-up from conservative theoretical extensions (scout N1's N8)

**Remark 9.0 [proof sketch, given the speed-up results (known, secondary level)].** For finite theories, conservative extensions with theoretical vocabulary shorten some proofs of sentences of the smaller language by more than any elementary function: Pudlák's chapter "The lengths of proofs" (*Handbook of Proof Theory*, ed. Buss, Elsevier 1998, ch. VIII) gives speed-ups of hyper-exponential (tower) magnitude for NGB over ZF and for ACA$_0$ over PA, by finite consistency statements and cut-shortening [known: attributed so in recent papers found by WebSearch (e.g. "Truth and feasible reducibility", JSL / arXiv 1902.00392); the chapter itself not read]; Gödel (1936) observed the phenomenon between systems of different order [bibliographic data checked by scout L; content recalled]. What this adds to the results above:
* *Finite theories in the base language.* Nothing new on PA's theorems: no finite $\mathcal L_{\rm ar}$-theory fits them at any budget (Thm 6.4(a)).
* *Schemas.* Under a template (schema) prior, where $\PA$ costs $O(1)$ (the axiom-schemas report), the comparison ACA$_0$ (finite, theoretical sort) against $\PA$ (one schema, no theoretical vocabulary) under a derivation charge is decided by the speed-up: on data whose shortest $\PA$-proofs are much longer than their ACA$_0$-proofs, the derivation-charged inducer prefers the theoretical sort. *Not written out:* a definition of the charged readings for template instance sets, and the check of a specific speed-up theorem against its source.
* *r.e. hypotheses.* Proof padding (Prop 4.1) neutralises any speed-up: the certificate axioms store the short proofs at $O(1)$ description cost. So speed-up favours theoretical vocabulary only in classes restricted to finitely many axioms or schemas — the same boundary as in §5–§6.

### 9.2 Conservativity and the ontological commitment (scout S's Q3.18)

**Proposition 9.1.** Let $\mathcal V$ be a verifier and $\mathcal P=\mathcal P_{\mathcal V}$ (Lemma 4.5).

### 9.3 Richer observational languages (scout S's Q3.19)

**Proposition 9.2 (unary observational languages).** Let the observational language have constants $\mathtt e_1,\dots,\mathtt e_p$, unary function symbols $\mathtt s_1,\dots,\mathtt s_r$ and unary observable predicates $R_1,\dots,R_j$, the named structure being the free term algebra $\mathfrak T_{p,r}$ and the data all literals $R_i(t)^{\pm}$, $t$ closed. *Proof* (A-m13: the changes to the proof of Thm 5.1, written out). Use, from $\Th(\mathfrak T_{p,r})$: each $\mathtt s_i$ is injective, the ranges of distinct $\mathtt s_i$ are disjoint, no root is in any range, and distinct closed terms are distinct. For a non-root closed term $t=\mathtt s_at'$, put $\mathrm{Sub}_t:=\{\mathtt s_ut:u\in\{1,\dots,r\}^*\}$; by these facts, an element $y\notin\mathrm{Sub}_t$ with $\mathtt s_by\in\mathrm{Sub}_t$ must be $t'$ with $b=a$ (the argument of Thm 5.1's "one edge", with $\{0,1\}$ replaced by $\{1,\dots,r\}$), so $\mathfrak A^{\rm rel}$ is $\mathfrak C_t\sqcup\mathfrak S_t$ with one quantifier-free redefined edge. The exceptional set is the set of closed subterms of the constants' denotations together with the roots (roots are never the top of a $\mathrm{Sub}_t$); for $t,t'$ outside it, $\mathfrak S_t$ and $\mathfrak S_{t'}$ are isomorphic up to labels, possibly under different roots. The transplant then bounds the number of distinct residuals, over all closed terms, by $N_q$ plus the size of the exceptional set, for each $R_i$ (the transplant copies all labels $R_1,\dots,R_j,P_1,\dots$ at once). ∎

**Proposition 9.3 (one binary observable breaks Theorem 5.1).** Let the observational language be $\{\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,R,J\}$ with $J$ a binary observable relation, the data being all literals of $R$ and $J$ on closed terms, and let the world be $J:=\{(t_u,t_v):\#_0(u)=\#_1(v)\}$, $R:=\mathrm{EQ}=\{w:\#_0w=\#_1w\}$ (not regular). The 13 observational sentences
* $J(\mathtt e,\mathtt e)$; $\forall y\,\neg J(\mathtt e,\mathtt s_1y)$; $\forall x\,\neg J(\mathtt s_0x,\mathtt e)$;
* $\forall y\,(J(\mathtt e,y)\to J(\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0y))$; $\forall x\forall y\,(J(x,y)\to J(\mathtt s_0x,\mathtt s_1y))$; $\forall x\forall y\,(J(\mathtt s_0x,y)\to J(\mathtt s_0x,\mathtt s_0y))$; $\forall x\forall y\,(J(x,y)\to J(\mathtt s_1x,y))$, each also with both $J$-atoms negated;
* $\forall x\,(J(x,x)\to R(x))$, $\forall x\,(\neg J(x,x)\to\neg R(x))$

are true in $(\mathfrak T,R,J)$ — a theory about the named objects with no theoretical vocabulary — and derive every datum with $O(|u|+|v|)$ lines. So with a binary observable, finite observational theories about the named objects fit a non-regular target. **[proved; computed: `t6_revision.out` Part E (the 13 sentences: 0 false at $|x|,|y|\le6$; all 3969 $J$-literals with $|u|,|v|\le5$ and all 1023 $R$-literals with $|w|\le9$ derived and checked); `t4_regular.out` Part C and `rb5_binary_observable.out` (the 15-sentence version of `notes.md`, which adds $\forall x\,(J(x,\mathtt e)\to J(\mathtt s_1x,\mathtt e))$ and its negated form; these are the instances $y:=\mathtt e$ of the fifth sentence pair and redundant (B4); the windowed residual count of $J$ grows, $5,7,9$ at depths $2,3,4$)]**

*Proof.* Truth: $\#_0$ and $\#_1$ change as the letters say. Determination: every pair $(u,v)$ is $(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, $(\varepsilon,1v')$, $(\varepsilon,0v')$, $(0u',\varepsilon)$, $(1u',v)$ ($v$ arbitrary, including $\varepsilon$), $(0u',1v')$ or $(0u',0v')$, and each case reduces by one sentence (positive or negative form; for $(1u',v)$ the sentence $\forall x\forall y\,(J(x,y)\to J(\mathtt s_1x,y))$ instantiated at $y:=t_v$) to a pair of smaller total length, or is a sentence instance. The two $R$ sentences finish. ∎

What the fit class of finite observational theories about the named objects is in the presence of a binary observable — in particular whether it is all decidable targets — is **open**; the answer must depend on the observable (with $J=\emptyset$ it is REG, by the proof of Thm 5.1).

**Remark 9.3′ (naming composites without observing them) [proved].** If the observational language has a binary function symbol $\mathtt p$ (naming composites) but the data concern only the words $t_w$, then $T^C_{\mathcal P}$ with every atom $C(a,b)$ replaced by $R(\mathtt p(a,b))$, with no relativisation, is about the named objects (the term algebra on $\mathtt e,\mathtt s_0,\mathtt s_1,\mathtt p$, with $R(\mathtt p(a,b)):=[(a,b)\in C^{\mathfrak T}]$ for words and false for other composites, and $R:=X_{\rm acc}$ on words) and fits every decidable $X$ under (a2), (b2). In the free term algebra matching is syntactic; a start pair with a non-word component gets stuck when phase cert or phase inp reaches the $\mathtt p$-term (no rule matches a term whose head is $\mathtt p$), so the output rules hold as in Thm 4.6. This is scout S's caveat made precise: Theorem 5.1 depends on the composites being *unnamed*, or, if named, observed.

### 9.4 Reading (a1) for infinite hypotheses about the named objects (scout S's Q3.16)

**Proposition 9.4.** Let $X\in\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ be $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. There is a parameter-free set $A\subseteq\Sent_{\mathcal L_O}$, true in $(\mathfrak T,X)$, with polynomial-time membership, such that every datum $\varphi_w^{[w\in X]}$ has a derivation from $A$ by MP and A1 instances whose every line has size polynomial in $|w|$. Hence, at polynomial budgets under (a1), the fit class of observational hypotheses about the named objects contains every $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$-hard set in $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$, besides $\mathsf P$ and $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$. **[proved, given CKS and the clocking of Prop 4.7, Step 1]**

*Proof.* Let $M$ be an alternating polynomial-space machine for $X$ with all paths halting (Prop 4.7, Step 1), and $\mathrm{Acc}:=\{(w,c):c$ an accepting configuration of $M$ on $w\}\in\mathsf{EXPTIME}$. Let $\mathrm{red}$ be a polynomial-time reduction of $\mathrm{Acc}$ (pairs coded as words) to $X$; since every configuration accepts or rejects, $c$ rejects iff $\mathrm{red}(w,c)\notin X$. For a word $u$ let $\theta_u:=Z\to(C_u\to Z)$ be SD's valid certificate sentence ($Z=\forall(\#0=\#0)$, size $|u|+14$). $A$ consists of, for all $w$ and configurations $c$ of $M$ on $w$ ($\langle\cdot\rangle$ a pairing of words):
* $\theta_{\langle w,c\rangle}\to R(t_{\mathrm{red}(w,c)})$ for halting accepting $c$, and $\theta_{\langle w,c\rangle}\to\neg R(t_{\mathrm{red}(w,c)})$ for halting rejecting $c$;
* at an existential $c$ with successors $c_1,c_2$: $\theta_{\langle w,c,c_i\rangle}\to(R(t_{\mathrm{red}(w,c_i)})\to R(t_{\mathrm{red}(w,c)}))$ ($i=1,2$) and $\theta_{\langle w,c,c_1,c_2\rangle}\to(\neg R(t_{\mathrm{red}(w,c_1)})\to(\neg R(t_{\mathrm{red}(w,c_2)})\to\neg R(t_{\mathrm{red}(w,c)})))$; at a universal $c$ the duals;
* $\theta_{\langle w\rangle}\to(R(t_{\mathrm{red}(w,{\rm init}(w))})\to R(t_w))$ and $\theta_{\langle w\rangle}\to(\neg R(t_{\mathrm{red}(w,{\rm init}(w))})\to\neg R(t_w))$.

Every member is true in $(\mathfrak T,X)$ ($\theta$ is valid and $R(t_{\mathrm{red}(w,c)})$ says that $c$ accepts). Membership: read the coded tuple off the $\theta$-part, recompute the $\mathrm{red}$-values and check the transition relation: polynomial time. A datum is derived along an accepting (rejecting) computation tree: each step cites a member, the A1 instance $\theta_u$, and MPs; every line has polynomial size (configurations and $\mathrm{red}$-values are polynomial). ∎

Whether the class is all of $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$ is **open**: for an $X$ that is neither easy nor hard, the facts about the named objects that a derivation can use are the values of $X$ itself (and sentences about $(\mathfrak T,X)$), and it is not clear that they can store an exponential computation. If not, theoretical vocabulary or entities would be needed under (a1) even for infinite hypotheses.

## 10. Relation to the literature

*Verification levels are given in §14; "secondary" means confirmed through search-result summaries of other works, not the primary text.*
* **Craig (1953, 1956).** Every r.e. theory has a recursive (indeed primitive recursive) axiomatisation by padded conjunctions; Craig (1956) applied this to the replacement of auxiliary expressions [known; bibliographic data checked by scouts P, L; 1956 content not seen]. Thm 2.1 is the Bayesian form inside the prior; that the polynomial-time form of Craig's trick is standard is recorded by scout L (Wikipedia; primary source not identified). Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.3 make "Craigian axiomatisations are useless in practice" precise; scout L found no earlier complexity-theoretic theorem of this kind [new, by scout L's search].
* **Hempel (1958), Putnam (1962, 1965).** The theoretician's dilemma and the replies (Craig's axiomatisations are unmanageable; theoretical terms may be needed for inductive systematisation; observation terms apply to unobservables) [known; contents from secondary sources]. Thm 2.1/Rem 2.2 bear on the inductive-systematisation reply *for the universal mixture*; §5 and Prop 9.1 give a formal version of Putnam's point.
* **Kleene (1952), Craig–Vaught (1958)** (A-M2). As reported in the abstract and in search summaries of Pakhomov and Visser, "On a question of Krajewski's" (*J. Symbolic Logic* 84(1) (2019) 343–358; arXiv 1712.01713): every r.e. theory $U$ without finite models has a finitely axiomatised conservative extension in the language of $U$ expanded by a binary relation symbol (Kleene), and Craig and Vaught strengthened this so that the extension is *model-conservative* (every model of $U$ expands to a model of it); additional *unary* predicates do not suffice in general [known, secondary level; the primary texts were not seen, and the arity attribution rests on the summaries]. Consequences for this record:
  * *Thm 4.6(i) with free deduction, Cor 5.5's upper bound and Prop 9.1(a)* are instances: $U:=\{$the axioms of Def 1.3$\}\cup\Gamma_{f_X}$, $X$ decidable, is r.e. without finite models, so it has a finitely axiomatised model-conservative extension with one binary relation symbol, into which $(\mathfrak T,X)$ expands; it proves exactly the literals of $X$. What is new here is the explicit backward two-stack construction with derivation bounds (Thm 4.6(iii)), hence the fit classes under (a2), (b2), (b), (a1); the observational pairing translation and the forced ontological commitment (Thm 4.6(ii), Prop 9.1(b)); and the regularity theorem for unary vocabulary over the named structure (Thm 5.1), which is in the spirit of the reported Craig–Vaught unary example but in a different setting (fixed named structure, fit classes; novelty plausible, not established).
  * *Theorem 6.5* is an effective many-sorted form of the same theorem. Using sorts to remove the infinite-models condition is routine (a two-sorted class-theory version via satisfaction classes appears in the literature, e.g. arXiv 1201.5509, found by referee A and not checked). What is new is only the Bayesian corollary $K_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}=K_A^{\mathcal L}\pm c$, $W_{\rm fin}^{\mathcal L^+}\asymp W_A^{\mathcal L}$, which needs the construction to be effective with an injective map and two-sided complexity bounds; the construction is written out in full here.
* **Ramsey (1931), Lewis (1970).** Ramsey sentences; Lewis's definition of theoretical terms through them [known; bibliographic data checked by scout P]. Prop 7.1 places Ramsey and Craig side by side on the simplest example.
* **Inductive logic with theoretical concepts.** Hintikka and Tuomela (1970), Niiniluoto and Tuomela (1973): in monadic (Hintikka-style) inductive logic, theoretical concepts can change inductive probabilities [known; abstract level, scout L]. Niiniluoto (1972) surveys inductive systematisation [bibliographic data and abstract checked]. Contrast: in the universal prior theoretical concepts cannot change inductive probabilities beyond a constant factor (Thm 2.1); in finite-theory priors they can, unboundedly (§5–§6); unary ones do not help over observation sequences (Thm 5.1) or in pure equality (Thm 6.2(a)), and in arithmetic a unary truth predicate appears to (Thm 6.4(d), proof sketch).
* **Predicate invention in ILP.** Muggleton and Buntine (1988) justify invented predicates by compression [known; bibliographic data checked by scout L]. Prop 7.2 is the elementary instance, with the exact gain (d); Theorem 5.1 shows that invented *unary* predicates cannot make a finite theory of observation sequences fit a non-regular target, and Theorem 4.6 that one invented binary relation can, with short derivations [new under charged deduction; with free deduction, Kleene–Craig–Vaught].
* **Rabin (1969).** The MSO/tree-automata route to Theorem 5.1 (scout S) [known; bibliographic data checked by scout S]; no longer needed.
* **Ehrenfeucht (1961), Fraïssé (1954); Löwenheim (1915).** The EF theorem (used in Lemma 5.2, Thm 5.1, Lemma 6.1) [known; textbook; Ehrenfeucht's bibliographic data checked]; Löwenheim's finite model property for monadic logic, of which Lemma 6.1 is a quantitative form [known, unverified].
* **Ryll-Nardzewski (1952), Mostowski (1952), Kreisel–Lévy (1968), Montague (1961), Simpson (2009).** Non-finite axiomatisability of PA (Ryll-Nardzewski, used as (KL)); reflexivity and essential reflexivity of PA (the proof idea of (KL)); non-finite axiomatisability of ZF (Montague; not used); ACA$_0$ finitely axiomatisable and conservative over PA (Simpson; used only for the classical instance in Thm 6.4(c)). Levels in §14.
* **Chandra–Kozen–Stockmeyer (1981)** ($\mathsf{APSPACE}=\mathsf{EXPTIME}$), **Hartmanis–Stearns (1965)** and the textbook one-tape simulation, **Cook–Reckhow (1979)**, **Fischer–Rabin (1974)**, **Gödel (1936)**, **Pudlák (1998)**: as in SD and `sfi`, levels in §14.
