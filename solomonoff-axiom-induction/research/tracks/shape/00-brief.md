## Goal

Answer, for natural classes of laws and with exact numbers, the brief's open question: **in what precise sense is a theory better shaped like a logical theory than like a program?** Craig rules out any answer based only on what can be represented, so the comparison must be relative to a reasoner. Scout N2's first pass gives the shape of the answer, and the thread must make it complete and exact:
* for a linear law over GF(2) and all conditional questions in every direction, the declarative core ($k$ equations) suffices for a Gaussian-elimination reasoner, while a unit-propagation reasoner needs an explicit theory exponentially larger without intermediate concepts;
* intermediate concepts (auxiliary variables) make one law linear-size and usable, but not, in general, a system of laws;
* "procedural" directed rules cost exactly a factor of the number of directions over clauses without intermediate concepts, and a factor $\le3$ with them, so **the large separations are between reasoners and between vocabularies, not between declarative and procedural shapes**;
* the know-how needed to answer with bounded effort is paid for in the reasoner, in explicit lemmas, or in a search the cost model does not charge, which locates the earlier report's charged-deduction results (they leave the search free).
This is also the textbook point made exact: "the written theory is insufficient for a simple reasoner" becomes a property of the pair (theory, reasoner).

## Targets

**T1 (core) Setting.** Define laws (affine subspaces $V=\{x:Ax=b\}$ over GF(2), and, for T7, implication chains), conditional questions $\mathcal Q(V)$, explicit theories over base and auxiliary variables (size = number of clauses; also a bit-length measure), the reasoners $\mathrm{UP}$, $\mathrm{UP}_k$, $\mathrm{GE}$, usability, and the reasoner-relative inducers $K_A^{\mathcal R}$, $W_A^{\mathcal R}$ (NOTATION-draft §6.1). Prove the bridge to the main object: with $\mathcal R$ = unbounded proof search, $K_A^{\mathcal R}$ and $W_A^{\mathcal R}$ on question data agree with $K_A$ and $W_A$ up to $O(1)$ (or state precisely the encoding of question data as labelled sentences $\alpha\to\ell$ under which they do).

**T2 (core) No intermediate concepts.** N2's Lemma 2.2 (determination ⇔ a dual codeword, ⇔ a circuit), Prop 2.3 (prime implicates of $V$ are the clauses on circuit supports; $\mathrm{PI}(V)$ is UP-usable in one step), Prop 2.4 ((a) every CNF over the base variables equivalent to $V$ has $\ge(1-2^{-k})2^d$ clauses; (b) for $d\ge3$ every UP-usable CNF over the base variables contains all $2^{d-1}$ clauses on the support of every minimum-weight codeword, so $\ge A_d2^{d-1}$ clauses), the simplex family ($n=2^m-1$: $\log(n+1)$ equations against $n\,2^{(n-1)/2}$ clauses, and $\mathrm{PI}(V)$ itself is the smallest usable theory).

**T3 (core) Intermediate concepts.** N2's Prop 2.5 (the chain encoding of one parity law with $n-2$ auxiliary variables is propagation complete: re-check the forest argument), Prop 2.6 (a usable library: the union of chain encodings of all circuits, size $O(n\cdot\#\text{circuits})$), the computed fact that the core does not suffice for systems (UP answers 74–81% of sampled questions at $n=12$–$20$) and that auxiliary variables in the core do not help. **Verify** Gwynne–Kullmann (LATA 2014 or its journal version) on the absence of polynomial-size arc-consistent (propagation-complete) representations of XOR systems even with auxiliary variables: exact statement, hypotheses, and whether it applies to UP-usability as defined here. If it cannot be verified, state the claim as "known, unverified" and say what in-house result would replace it. **Extension:** an in-house lower bound for one explicit family of XOR systems with auxiliary variables (likely hard: monotone circuit lower bounds).

**T4 (core) Directionality: the separation the brief asked for, and its limit.** N2's Prop 2.7: (a) for forward chaining a clause of width $w$ is $w$ directed rules, so directed-rule and clausal theories differ by at most the maximum width; (b) for one parity law on $n$ variables without auxiliary variables, every direction-complete set of sound directed rules has exactly $n2^{n-1}$ rules against $2^{n-1}$ clauses: a factor $n$, the number of query directions; (c) with the chain's auxiliary variables the factor is $\le3$. Then state precisely, as a theorem plus a short reading: the brief's sought family exists (b), and it does not show that logical shape is what matters (c); the exponential gaps (T2–T3) are between reasoners and between vocabularies. Include scout P's caveat (a program may contain a generic solver at $O(1)$ length, so "programs lose" cannot be the statement; the right statement is about what a fixed weak reasoner needs) and say whether a time bound on the reasoner changes this (a query-time bound below that of elimination).

**T5 (core) Three homes of know-how.** N2's Prop 2.8: (a) reasoner (GE on $k$ equations); (b) explicit lemmas (T2–T3 bounds for UP); (c) membership procedure plus free search: $\mathrm{PI}(V)$ has a polynomial-time decider of length $K(A,b)+O(1)$, and each question has a derivation from one axiom instance, so under the AI-paper's charged-deduction readings (which bound derivation size and membership time but not the search) $V$ is fit by a short theory with short derivations. State the consequence exactly: those readings model a reasoner that is handed the derivation (their fit class under total material is NP ∩ coNP, `sfi` T8), so they cannot express the textbook point; a deterministic bounded reasoner can.

**T6 (core) Learning consequence.** Make N2's proof sketch (end of §2.6) a theorem about the reasoner-relative inducer: for one parity law on $d$ variables and questions with $|S|=d-1$ drawn from a stated distribution (or in any order), an inducer restricted to explicit theories without auxiliary variables, with a simplicity prior and reasoner UP, must see each of the $2^{d-1}$ value patterns before it answers them all correctly, and errs or abstains on (a stated number of) the others, while the GE inducer or the UP inducer with auxiliary variables pays $O(d\log d)$ bits in total. Fix the prior on explicit theories, the loss convention (semimeasure; abstention = "independent"), and prove a coverage bound (coupon collector for random question sequences). Say what this means: a weak reasoner learns its know-how pattern by pattern; with intermediate concepts it learns the law at once.

**T7 (extension, high priority) Depth–size trade-off: lemma libraries against reasoning depth.** For the implication-chain law $x_1\to x_2\to\dots\to x_n$ and reasoner $\mathrm{UP}_k$ ($k$ propagation rounds), the smallest usable 2-CNF theory over the base variables is a $k$-hop transitive-closure spanner of the directed path; the known tight bounds are $\Theta(n\log n)$ for $k=2$, $\Theta(n\log\log n)$ for $k=3$, and $\Theta(n\lambda_k(n))$ in general with inverse-Ackermann-type $\lambda_k$ (Alon–Schieber 1987; Chandra–Fortune–Lipton 1985; Yao 1982; Bhattacharyya, Grigorescu, Jung, Raskhodnikova, Woodruff, "Transitive-closure spanners"). Verify these references and statements; prove the reduction (including whether wider clauses or auxiliary variables can beat the 2-CNF bound, which corresponds to Steiner points); compute the exact minima for small $n$ and $k$ by exhaustive or ILP search. This gives the brief's "trade-off between the compression of axioms and their usability" as an exact curve: the theory size a reasoner needs as a function of its depth budget.

**T8 (extension) Lemmas for bounded proof search in general.** State what is known for full first-order proof search: non-elementary lower bounds for eliminating cuts (Statman 1979; Orevkov), so lemma-free theories can be non-elementarily worse for a cut-free reasoner; relate to the AI-paper's finding that frequently used lemmas become axioms under a derivation cost. Verify references; claim no more than a proof sketch for anything not proved here. The physics illustration ("Newton plus a perturbation lemma") is for `philosophy`; do not develop it.

**T9 (core) Literature.** Verify and place: knowledge compilation (Darwiche–Marquis 2002; Selman–Kautz 1996; propagation completeness, Bordeaux–Marques-Silva 2012), learning to reason (Khardon–Roth 1997; Juba 2013), algorithm = logic + control (Kowalski 1979), the declarative/procedural controversy (Winograd 1975; McCarthy 1959), resolution on Tseitin formulas (Urquhart 1987; Ben-Sasson–Wigderson 2001), and TC-spanners (T7). Say precisely which bounds are folklore or known (scout N2 suspected T2's bounds are folklore in knowledge compilation) and what is new: the reading inside a universal inducer, the directionality result, the three homes of know-how, the learning consequence.
