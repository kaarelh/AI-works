## 0. Summary

The thread answers the brief's open question ("in what precise sense is a theory better shaped like a logical theory than like a program?") for linear laws over GF(2) and for the implication chain, relative to a reasoner. Statuses are in §12.

1. **Setting and bridge (§1).** Explicit theories are finite sets of clauses over base and auxiliary variables; data are conditional questions; reasoners are $\mathrm{UP}_k$ ($k$ rounds of parallel unit propagation), $\mathrm{UP}$, $\mathrm{GE}$ and $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$; there are three inducers: program length ($K_A^{\mathcal R}$, NOTATION §6.1), listing ($K_{\rm lst}^{\mathcal R}$, the theory charged as written) and time-charged ($Kt_A^{\mathcal R}$), each with the hypothesis class as a superscript when it matters. With unbounded proof search, $K_A^{\mathcal R}$ is $K_A$ on question data **provided** a conditional datum requires the theory to be consistent with its antecedent (Prop 1.9); the two-valued encoding "$\alpha\to\ell$ is true" is degenerate, since a single world fits all question data (Prop 1.8) [proved].
2. **Program length sees neither the reasoner nor the vocabulary (Thm 1.11, Thm 1.12(c)) [proved].** On the question data of an affine law, $K_A^{\mathcal R}=K(\mathcal S)\pm c$ for every named reasoner, with or without intermediate concepts, because a fixed program writes out the prime-implicate library. The textbook point is visible only when the written theory is charged ($K_{\rm lst}$) or the time to print it ($Kt$) (Thm 1.12(a), (b)).
3. **Exact minimum without intermediate concepts (§2) [proved; computed].** For every affine law the clauses common to all unit-propagation-usable theories over the base variables are exactly those of the *indecomposable* circuits, the derived equations that cannot be split as two derived equations chained through one intermediate base quantity (Props 2.5, 2.5a). When $d_*\ge3$ these clauses are themselves usable, so they form the unique minimum (Thm 2.6). For the simplex family the minimum is $m2^{(m-1)/2}$ clauses against $\log_2(m+1)$ equations (Cor 2.8). For $d_*=2$ the minimum is different: an equivalence class of $c$ variables needs exactly $c$ clauses, a directed cycle (Prop 2.9a).
4. **Intermediate concepts (§3), and the depth they need.**
   * *One round* of propagation cannot use them: every $\mathrm{UP}_1$-usable theory, with any auxiliary variables, contains all prime implicates of the law (Prop 3.1a) [proved].
   * *Fixed depth $k$:* for one parity law on $m$ variables every $\mathrm{UP}_k$-usable theory, with any auxiliary variables, has written length $2^{\Omega(m^{1/(2k-1)})}$ (Prop 3.8(c)) [proved, given Håstad].
   * *Enough depth:* the balanced-tree encoding ($4m-6$ clauses) is usable with $2\lceil\log_2m\rceil-1$ rounds; the chain encoding needs exactly $m-1$ (Prop 3.8(a), (b)) [proved; computed]. For the simplex family, unbounded propagation with intermediate concepts needs only $m(2m-4)$ clauses (Rem 3.2a) [proved; computed].
   * *Systems:* for the hidden-state law every unit-propagation-usable theory, with any intermediate concepts and any depth, has written length $2^{\Omega(m^{\varepsilon/3})}$, while Gaussian elimination uses a linear-size theory (Thm 3.6) [proved, given Göös–Kamath–Robere–Sokolov 2019]. Intermediate concepts private to one law add nothing to a system (Prop 3.3) [proved].
   * So the exponential separations are between *pairs* (reasoner, vocabulary): vocabulary helps a reasoner only if the reasoner can chain through it (Cor 3.9).
5. **Directionality (§4) [proved].** For one parity law, a rule base (sound directed rules over the base variables, forward chaining) is exactly $m$ times the clausal law, $m$ being the number of query directions; the same factor holds for one round of reasoning with any vocabulary. With intermediate concepts and unbounded rounds the factor is at most 9 in written length, and for every round budget it is at most the clause width; a straight-line program answers all directions of parity with $O(m)$ operations. Directionality is real but cheap; it is not where the large separations are.
6. **Three homes of know-how (§5) [proved; one derivation count is a proof sketch].** The know-how to answer questions lives in the reasoner, in written lemmas, or in a computation the cost model does not charge. Under the AI-paper's charged-deduction readings, extended to conditional data, the full lemma library has a polynomial-time decider whose length is a time-bounded description of the law, and no compatible hypothesis is shorter than a description of the law: these readings do not charge for writing the library out, so they cannot express the textbook point (Thm 5.2).
7. **Learning (§6) [proved; computed].** With the listing prior and no intermediate concepts, a unit-propagation inducer learns pattern by pattern: every minimal compatible theory fails on at least one question of every unseen value pattern; it is fully correct only after all $2^{m-1}$ patterns have been seen (coupon collector: about $2^{m-1}(m-1)\ln2$ random questions); on block-complete data its Bayes loss is at least $\mathrm{len}_m\approx m\log_2m$ bits per pattern. The Gaussian-elimination inducer and the inducer with intermediate concepts pay $O(m\log m)$ bits in total. Under program length there is no gap; under $Kt$ the gap is at least $m-1$ bits without intermediate concepts and $O(\log m)$ with them.
8. **Depth against size for the implication chain (§7).** For $\mathrm{UP}_k$ and theories over the base variables the least usable theory has exactly $s_k(m)$ clauses, the size of a minimum $k$-hop TC-spanner of the path [proved], $\Theta(m\,\mathrm{ia}_k(m))$ [known]; a 2-CNF is usable iff it is a $k$-hop TC-spanner, and a usable theory with wide clauses becomes one under every replacement of its clauses by prime implicates, but not conversely [proved; refuted converse kept]. Exact values for small $m$ [computed], with $s_2(m)=\sum_{i\le m}\lfloor\log_2i\rfloor$ for $m\le18$ and conjecturally always. At depth 2, intermediate concepts and wide clauses save at most a factor about 2 [proved]; superlinear size at every fixed depth for bounded width [proved, given Chandra–Fortune–Lipton].
9. **First-order lemmas (§8).** Lemma-free theories can be non-elementarily worse for a cut-free reasoner [known: Statman, Orevkov]; adding one valid lemma sentence per cut restores cut-free proofs of polynomial size [proof sketch]; a lemma enters a derivation-charged objective iff it saves more than its length [proved].

**Reading for the paper [assessment].** Whether a written theory suffices depends on the reader (whether it can do algebra, and how many steps it can chain) and on the vocabulary the theory is written in. The idealised inducer sees none of this, because it charges a theory by the length of a program that may compute the missing lemmas. Logical shape buys many-directional use only up to a constant factor once intermediate concepts are available and the reasoner can chain through them. No "Possible error in the author's view" arises (§10).

## 1. Setting (target T1)

### 1.1 Objects

**Definition 1.1 (explicit theories and hypothesis classes).** An *explicit theory* is a finite set $\Phi$ of non-tautological clauses over $X\cup Y$, $X=\{x_1,\dots,x_m\}$. A *linear theory* is a finite set $\Phi$ of equations over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ in variables from $X\cup Y$. The *projection* is $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi:=\{x\in\{0,1\}^m:\exists y\ (x,y)\models\Phi\}$. Auxiliary variables are the *intermediate concepts*. Theories that differ only by a renaming of auxiliary variables are identified; each class has a fixed canonical representative, whose auxiliary variables are $y_1,\dots,y_{m_Y}$ (for instance the lexicographically least listing, Def 1.6). The hypothesis classes are $\mathcal C^X$ (explicit theories over $X$ only) and $\mathcal C^{XY}$ (any auxiliary variables), and likewise for linear theories. (Logically, $X$ and $Y$ are 0-ary relation symbols of a first-order language $\mathcal L_m$ with equality, or ground atoms; an explicit theory is a finite set of quantifier-free sentences.)

**Definition 1.2 (laws and questions).** A *law* is a nonempty $\mathcal S\subseteq\{0,1\}^m$. A *conditional question* (NOTATION §3.2) is a pair $(\alpha,\ell)$: $\alpha$ a partial assignment to $X$ with domain $I$, $\ell$ a base literal on a variable $x_j$, $j\notin I$. The *question set* of $\mathcal S$ is
$$\mathcal Q(\mathcal S):=\{(\alpha,\ell):\ \alpha\text{ consistent with }\mathcal S,\ \mathcal S\wedge\alpha\models\ell\}.$$
A *conditional data set* $D$ is a finite set of such pairs, read "given $\alpha$, $\ell$ holds".

**Definition 1.3 (reasoners).** A *reasoner* $\mathcal R$ maps a theory and an antecedent $\alpha$ to a set of base literals or to $\bot$ (a conflict). It is *sound* if $\ell\in\mathcal R(\Phi,\alpha)$ implies $\Phi\cup\alpha\models\ell$, and $\bot$ occurs only when $\Phi\cup\alpha$ is unsatisfiable. The named reasoners:
* $\mathrm{UP}_k$, $k$ rounds of **parallel unit propagation**: start from $\alpha$; in each round, every clause all of whose literals but one are falsified by the current assignment, the remaining one unassigned, assigns that literal (all such clauses fire at once; two opposite derivations, or a clause with all literals falsified, give $\bot$). $\mathrm{UP}:=\mathrm{UP}_\infty$ (to the fixpoint). Directed rules and forward chaining are in §4.
* $\mathrm{GE}$ on linear theories: Gaussian elimination of $\Phi\cup\alpha$, which returns exactly the entailed base literals (it is complete for linear systems).
* $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$: unbounded proof search; returns exactly the base literals entailed by $\Phi\cup\alpha$. $\mathrm{PS}_t$: proof search with time budget $t$ (named for T8; not used in theorems).

$\mathcal R$ is *complete for $\Phi$* if $\mathcal R(\Phi,\alpha)$ contains every base literal outside $\mathrm{dom}\,\alpha$ entailed by $\Phi\cup\alpha$, for every $\alpha$ with $\Phi\cup\alpha$ satisfiable.

**Definition 1.4 (compatibility and usability).** $\Phi$ is *$\mathcal R$-compatible* with $D$ if, for every $(\alpha,\ell)\in D$, $\Phi\cup\alpha$ is satisfiable and $\ell\in\mathcal R(\Phi,\alpha)$. $\Phi$ is *$\mathcal R$-usable* for $\mathcal S$ if $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi=\mathcal S$ and $\Phi$ is $\mathcal R$-compatible with $\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)$. For sound $\mathcal R$ and $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi=\mathcal S$, usability is completeness of $\mathcal R$ for $\Phi$ on base literals outside $\mathrm{dom}\,\alpha$ (referee A m5).

**Lemma 1.5 (compatibility with all questions forces the projection) [proved; computed: c7 A].** Let $\mathcal R$ be sound and $\Phi$ $\mathcal R$-compatible with $\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)$. Then $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi\subseteq\mathcal S$; and $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi=\mathcal S$ if every $x\in\mathcal S$ has a coordinate determined by the others on $\mathcal S$. This holds for every affine law with $r\ge1$ (every coordinate in the support of a nonzero derived equation is determined; if some $x\in\mathcal S$ had none, no nonzero derived equation would exist and $\mathcal S$ would be the whole cube) and for the chain law $\mathrm{Ch}_m=\{x:x_1\le\dots\le x_m\}$ with $m\ge3$ (each point $0^a1^{m-a}$ has $a\ge2$, and then $x_2=0$ forces $x_1=0$, or $m-a\ge2$, and then $x_{m-1}=1$ forces $x_m=1$). So for these laws "$\Phi$ is compatible with $\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)$" and "$\Phi$ is usable" coincide.

**[refuted] for the chain law with $m=2$** (`notes.md` stated $m\ge2$; referee A m1, referee B Issue 4): $\Phi=\{\neg x_1\vee x_2,\ x_1\vee\neg x_2\}$ is $\mathrm{UP}$- and $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$-compatible with $\mathcal Q(\mathrm{Ch}_2)$ (its only questions are $x_1{=}1\Rightarrow x_2{=}1$ and $x_2{=}0\Rightarrow x_1{=}0$), but $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi=\{00,11\}\ne\mathrm{Ch}_2=\{00,01,11\}$; the point $01$ has no determined coordinate. Of the 256 theories over two variables, 2 are compatible and 1 has the wrong projection (c7 A; `refA_checks` A, `refB_t1_setting` A). Nothing downstream uses $m=2$: §7 uses usability, and Theorem 7.2 needs $m\ge4$.

*Proof.* Let $x\notin\mathcal S$. Choose $I\subsetneq[m]$ maximal such that $\alpha:=x|_I$ is consistent with $\mathcal S$ ($I=\emptyset$ qualifies since $\mathcal S\ne\emptyset$; $I\ne[m]$ since $x\notin\mathcal S$). For $j\notin I$, $\alpha\cup\{x_j{=}x_j\}$ is inconsistent, so $\mathcal S\wedge\alpha$ entails the literal $\ell$ "$x_j\ne x_j$-value", and $(\alpha,\ell)\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)$. Compatibility and soundness give $\Phi\cup\alpha\models\ell$; $x$ extends $\alpha$ and falsifies $\ell$, so $x\notin\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi$. Conversely let $x\in\mathcal S$ with $x_j$ determined by $\alpha:=x|_{[m]\setminus\{j\}}$. Then $(\alpha,x_j\text{-value})\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)$, so $\Phi\cup\alpha$ is satisfiable, by a point of $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi\subseteq\mathcal S$ extending $\alpha$; the only such point of $\mathcal S$ is $x$. ∎

### 1.2 The inducers

Data are conditional data sets; $\mathcal C$ is a hypothesis class of Def 1.1. Explicit theories are the hypotheses of $\mathrm{UP}_k$, $\mathrm{UP}$, $\mathrm{PS}$; linear theories those of $\mathrm{GE}$.

**Definition 1.6 (inducers).**
* **Program length** (NOTATION §6.1): $K_A^{\mathcal R,\mathcal C}(D):=\min\{|p|:V(p)=\langle\Phi\rangle,\ \Phi\in\mathcal C\ \mathcal R\text{-compatible with }D\}$ and $W_A^{\mathcal R,\mathcal C}(D):=\sum2^{-|p|}$ over the same $p$ ($V$ the universal prefix machine, $\langle\Phi\rangle$ a fixed encoding). For r.e. hypotheses (Prop 1.9(a)) $V(p)$ is an index $e$ of the r.e. set $\mathsf W_e$, as in `fundamentals` Def 1.7.
* **Listing** (the theory is charged as written). Let $\ell_{\mathbb N}(n):=|\delta(n+1)|$ for $n\ge0$, with $\delta$ the Elias delta code of the positive integers; $\ell_{\mathbb N}$ is a nondecreasing prefix code of $\{0,1,2,\dots\}$ (`notes.md` used $\delta(n)$ itself, which does not code $0$; referee A m4, referee B Issue 8). For a canonical explicit theory with $\bar m=m+m_Y$ variables,
  $$\ell_{\rm lst}(\Phi):=\ell_{\mathbb N}(m_Y)+\ell_{\mathbb N}(|\Phi|)+\sum_{C\in\Phi}\big(\ell_{\mathbb N}(|C|)+|C|(\lceil\log_2\bar m\rceil+1)\big),$$
  clauses listed in a canonical order (each literal: variable index and sign). Linear theories likewise, each equation listed by its support and its constant ($\ell_{\mathbb N}(|u|)+|u|\lceil\log_2\bar m\rceil+1$ bits). Then $K_{\rm lst}^{\mathcal R,\mathcal C}(D):=\min\ell_{\rm lst}(\Phi)$ and $W_{\rm lst}^{\mathcal R,\mathcal C}(D):=\sum2^{-\ell_{\rm lst}(\Phi)}$ over canonical $\mathcal R$-compatible $\Phi\in\mathcal C$.
* **Time-charged** (Levin): $Kt_A^{\mathcal R,\mathcal C}(D):=\min\{|p|+\log_2t_p\}$ over the same $p$ as in $K_A^{\mathcal R,\mathcal C}$, $t_p$ the running time of $V$ on $p$. For strings, $Kt(x):=\min\{|p|+\log_2t:V(p)=x\text{ in }t\text{ steps}\}$, and for a polynomial $q$, $K^{q}(x):=\min\{|p|:V(p)=x\text{ within }q(|x|)\text{ steps}\}$.

**Convention.** When the class is omitted, $\mathcal C=\mathcal C^{XY}$ (intermediate concepts allowed); the superscript $X$ marks the class $\mathcal C^X$: $K_{\rm lst}^{\mathcal R,X}$, $W_{\rm lst}^{\mathcal R,X}$, $Kt_A^{\mathcal R,X}$, $K_A^{\mathcal R,X}$ (referee A M1, m11).

**Lemma 1.7 (the listing code is a code) [proved; computed: c7 J].** $\sum2^{-\ell_{\rm lst}(\Phi)}\le1$ over canonical explicit theories, and $\ell_{\rm lst}(\Phi\setminus\{C\})<\ell_{\rm lst}(\Phi)$ for every $C\in\Phi$. In particular an $\ell_{\rm lst}$-shortest compatible theory is inclusion-minimal among compatible theories.

*Proof.* Fix $m_Y$. The per-clause code (a length by $\ell_{\mathbb N}$, then that many literals of $\lceil\log_2\bar m\rceil+1$ bits each) is a prefix code on finite sequences of literals, so $\sum_C2^{-\ell(C)}\le1$ over clauses written in canonical order. The sets of $s$ distinct clauses, listed in canonical order, have total weight at most $(\sum_C2^{-\ell(C)})^s/s!\le1$. The codes $\ell_{\mathbb N}(m_Y)$ and $\ell_{\mathbb N}(s)$ contribute factors whose sums over $m_Y$ and $s$ are at most 1; canonical theories are a subset of all clause sets. Removing a clause removes $\ell(C)>0$ bits and replaces $\ell_{\mathbb N}(s)$ by $\ell_{\mathbb N}(s-1)\le\ell_{\mathbb N}(s)$; if it removes the last occurrence of an auxiliary variable, $m_Y$ and $\lceil\log_2\bar m\rceil$ do not increase either. ∎

The check (c7 J): over all 256 theories over two base variables, $\sum2^{-\ell_{\rm lst}}=0.2525$; referee B's `refB_t1_setting` B gives the same value and $0.2519$ for $m=3$ with at most 6 clauses.

### 1.3 The bridge to $K_A$, $W_A$

Take SAI (NOTATION §4.2; `fundamentals` Def 1.7) over the language $\mathcal L_m$ of Definition 1.1 with $B=\emptyset$, hypotheses r.e. sets of $\mathcal L_m$-sentences. Two encodings of a conditional datum $(\alpha,\ell)$ as SAI data:

* **(E2) two-valued:** the sentence $\alpha\to\ell$ labelled true. Compatibility: $T$ consistent and $T\vdash\alpha\to\ell$.
* **(E3) conditional:** $T\cup\{\alpha\}$ consistent and $T\cup\{\alpha\}\vdash\ell$; equivalently $T\vdash\alpha\to\ell$ and $T\nvdash\neg\alpha$. Write $K_A^{\rm cond}$, $W_A^{\rm cond}$ for $K_A$, $W_A$ with this compatibility. (E3) is not a single three-valued datum of $M_A^3$: it is "label$(\alpha\to\ell)=1$" together with "label$(\neg\alpha)\ne1$", and the latter is the union of the two $M_A^3$-events label $0$ and label $\iota$. $W_A^{\rm cond}$ is defined directly, not as a value of $M_A^3$ (referee A m17).

**Proposition 1.8 (the two-valued encoding is degenerate) [proved; computed: c4 C, `refB_t1_setting` C].** Under (E2), every consistent strengthening of a compatible theory is compatible (proof-requiring compatibility is upward closed). In particular, for any law $\mathcal S$ and any $x^0\in\mathcal S$, the complete theory $T_{x^0}:=\{x_i\leftrightarrow x^0_i:i\le m\}$ proves $\alpha\to\ell$ for every $(\alpha,\ell)\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)$, so $K_A(\{(\alpha\to\ell,1):(\alpha,\ell)\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)\})\le K(x^0)+c$; for a homogeneous affine law ($b=0$, $x^0=0$) this is $\le K(m)+c$, below the description length of almost every such law.

*Proof.* If $\alpha$ agrees with $x^0$, then $x^0\in\mathcal S\wedge\alpha$, so $x^0\models\ell$ and $T_{x^0}\vdash\ell$; otherwise $T_{x^0}\vdash\neg\alpha$. Either way $T_{x^0}\vdash\alpha\to\ell$. ∎

*Reading.* Under (E2) the data cannot distinguish a law from a single world that satisfies it: the posterior is free to learn "initial conditions" instead of the law. The conditional encoding (E3) makes "given $\alpha$" hypothetical: a compatible theory must leave every antecedent open. Two-valued proof-requiring labels cannot express (E3), since (E3) is not upward closed.

**Proposition 1.9 (bridge) [proved].**
(a) With $\mathcal R=\mathrm{PS}_\infty$ and hypotheses the r.e. sets of $\mathcal L_m$-sentences, or the r.e. sets of clauses over $X$, $K_A^{\mathrm{PS}_\infty}(D)=K_A^{\rm cond}(D)\pm c$ and $W_A^{\mathrm{PS}_\infty}\asymp W_A^{\rm cond}$ on every conditional data set $D$.
(b) With finite explicit theories, of either class,
$$K_A^{\rm cond}(D)-c\ \le\ K_A^{\mathrm{PS}_\infty,\mathcal C}(D)\ \le\ K_A^{\rm cond}(D)+\ell_{\mathbb N}(m)+\ell_{\mathbb N}(2^m)+c,$$
i.e. up to $m+O(\log m)$ bits.

*Proof.* (a) For sentence sets, $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$-compatibility of an r.e. set $T$ is (E3) by the completeness theorem: $T\cup\alpha$ is satisfiable iff consistent, and $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$ returns $\ell$ iff $T\cup\alpha\vdash\ell$. For clause sets: a fixed program turns an enumeration of $T$ into an enumeration of $\mathrm{Cl}(T)$, the set of clauses over $X$ derivable from $T$. For every question, $T\cup\alpha$ is inconsistent iff $T\vdash\neg\alpha$ iff the clause $\neg\alpha$ is in $\mathrm{Cl}(T)$ iff $\mathrm{Cl}(T)\cup\alpha$ is inconsistent (as $\mathrm{Cl}(T)\subseteq\Cn(T)$); and $T\cup\alpha\vdash\ell$ iff the clause $\neg\alpha\vee\ell$ (non-tautological, as $\ell$ is on a variable outside $\mathrm{dom}\,\alpha$) is in $\mathrm{Cl}(T)$ iff $\mathrm{Cl}(T)\cup\alpha\vdash\ell$. So $T$ and $\mathrm{Cl}(T)$ are compatible with the same conditional data. (A theory need not consist of Boolean combinations of the atoms: $\mathcal L_m$ has equality and quantifiers; `notes.md`'s "CNF of each sentence" was not available, and $\mathrm{Cl}(T)$ replaces it; referee A m2.) The converse direction is trivial (clauses are sentences).
(b) *Left.* A fixed prefix replaces a finite explicit theory $\Phi$ (possibly with auxiliary variables, which are not symbols of $\mathcal L_m$) by $\mathrm{PI}(\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi)$, a finite set of $\mathcal L_m$-sentences; it is $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$-compatible with the same data, since $\Phi\cup\alpha$ is satisfiable iff $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi$ meets $\alpha$, and for base $\ell$, $\Phi\cup\alpha\models\ell$ iff every point of $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi$ extending $\alpha$ satisfies $\ell$ (referee A m3). *Right.* Let $p$ enumerate a consistent $T$ compatible with $D$ under (E3); let $\mathcal S_T:=\{x:\ T\cup\{\hat x\}\text{ consistent}\}$ ($\hat x$ the conjunction of the base literals of $x$) and $s:=|\mathcal S_T|$; $\mathcal S_T\ne\emptyset$, since otherwise $T$ proves $\bigwedge_x\neg\hat x$, which is refutable. Since $x\notin\mathcal S_T$ iff $T\vdash\neg\hat x$, a program given $p$, $m$ and $s$ (self-delimiting, $\ell_{\mathbb N}(m)+\ell_{\mathbb N}(s)+c$ extra bits) enumerates refutations until $2^m-s$ points are refuted and outputs $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S_T)$. $T\cup\alpha$ is consistent iff $\mathcal S_T$ meets $\alpha$, and $T\cup\alpha\vdash\ell$ iff every point of $\mathcal S_T$ extending $\alpha$ satisfies $\ell$ (completeness, and $\ell$ is a base literal); so $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S_T)$ is $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$-compatible with $D$ exactly when $T$ is. And $\ell_{\mathbb N}(s)\le\ell_{\mathbb N}(2^m)$. ∎

So the reasoner-relative inducers extend SAI: with an ideal deducer and the conditional encoding they *are* SAI on question data, up to the finite/r.e. gap of (b), which we did not try to show is attained. This is the precise sense of NOTATION §6.1's "with $\mathcal R$ unbounded proof search these reduce to $K_A$, $W_A$ on the question data": it holds for (E3), not for (E2).

### 1.4 The reasoner is invisible to program length

**Lemma 1.10 (prime implicates and one round of propagation) [proved; known in substance: del Val 1994; computed: c4 A, c1 B, c7 C].** Let $\mathcal S$ be any law.
(a) $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$, the set of prime implicates of $\mathcal S$ over $X$, is $\mathrm{UP}_1$-complete: if $\alpha$ is consistent with $\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal S\wedge\alpha\models\ell$, one round of unit propagation on $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)\cup\alpha$ derives $\ell$.
(b) Conversely, every $\mathrm{UP}_1$-usable $\Phi\in\mathcal C^X$ contains $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$. (Prop 3.1a extends this to $\mathcal C^{XY}$.)

*Proof.* (a) The clause $\neg\alpha\vee\ell$ is an implicate of $\mathcal S$, so some prime implicate $C\subseteq\neg\alpha\vee\ell$. If $\ell\notin C$, then $C\subseteq\neg\alpha$ is falsified by $\alpha$, contradicting the consistency of $\alpha$ with $\mathcal S$. So $\ell\in C$ and all other literals of $C$ are falsified by $\alpha$: $C$ fires in round 1. No conflict arises, since $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)\cup\alpha$ is satisfiable. (b) Let $C=\neg\alpha'\vee\ell\in\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$. Since $C$ is prime, $\neg\alpha'$ is not an implicate, so $\alpha'$ is consistent with $\mathcal S$, and $(\alpha',\ell)\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)$. In one round from $\alpha'$, the clause of $\Phi$ that derives $\ell$ has all its other literals falsified by $\alpha'$, so it is some $C'\subseteq\neg\alpha'\vee\ell=C$ with $\ell\in C'$; $C'$ is an implicate of $\mathcal S$ (as $\Phi\equiv\mathcal S$ over $X$), so $C'=C$ by primality. ∎

**Theorem 1.11 (reasoner invariance under program-length priors) [proved; computed: c4 B, `refB_t1_setting` D].** Let $\mathcal R'$ be any sound reasoner whose hypotheses are explicit or linear theories (of either class). Then for every conditional data set $D$,
$$K_A^{\mathrm{UP}_1,X}(D)\le K_A^{\mathcal R'}(D)+c,\qquad W_A^{\mathrm{UP}_1,X}(D)\ge2^{-c}W_A^{\mathcal R'}(D),$$
and $K_A^{\mathrm{UP}_1}=K_A^{\mathrm{UP}}=K_A^{\mathrm{PS}_\infty}\pm c$ (finite explicit theories, either class). In particular, on the question data of any law, a one-round unit-propagation reasoner over the base variables is as good as Gaussian elimination or unbounded proof search with any intermediate concepts, up to a machine constant.

*Proof.* Let $\mathrm{comp}(\Phi'):=\mathrm{PI}(\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi')$, computable from $\Phi'$ (a linear theory is first written as clauses). If $\Phi'$ is $\mathcal R'$-compatible with $D$ then, by soundness, $\Phi'\cup\alpha$ is satisfiable and entails $\ell$; so $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi'$ meets $\alpha$ and entails $\ell$ given $\alpha$ ($\ell$ is a base literal), and by Lemma 1.10(a) $\mathrm{comp}(\Phi')\in\mathcal C^X$ is $\mathrm{UP}_1$-compatible with $D$. A program for $\mathrm{comp}(\Phi')$ is a fixed prefix followed by a program for $\Phi'$. For the equalities: $\mathrm{UP}_1$, $\mathrm{UP}$ and $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$ are sound, so each is covered by the inequality; conversely a $\mathrm{UP}_1$-compatible theory is $\mathrm{UP}$- and $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$-compatible, since those reasoners derive at least what $\mathrm{UP}_1$ derives. ∎

**Reading [assessment].** This is Craig's collapse again, now for reasoners: under a universal prior the know-how can live in a *generator* of the explicit theory (here the compiler $\mathrm{comp}$, which writes out the lemma library) at $O(1)$ cost, as scout P's caveat says a program can contain a generic solver at $O(1)$. So "the written theory is insufficient for a simple reasoner" is not visible to program length. It becomes visible exactly when the theory is charged as written, or when writing it takes time that is charged:

**Theorem 1.12 (where the reasoner and the vocabulary become visible) [proved].** Let $\mathcal S$ be an affine law with $r\ge1$, $\mathcal R$ a sound reasoner and $\mathcal C$ a class.
(a) *Listing:* $K_{\rm lst}^{\mathcal R,\mathcal C}(\mathcal Q(\mathcal S))=\min\{\ell_{\rm lst}(\Phi):\Phi\in\mathcal C\ \mathcal R\text{-usable for }\mathcal S\}$ (Lemma 1.5). So the size bounds of §2 are bounds on $K_{\rm lst}^{\mathrm{UP},X}$ (and, by Prop 3.1a, on $K_{\rm lst}^{\mathrm{UP}_1}$ for either class), and those of §3 on $K_{\rm lst}^{\mathrm{UP}}$, up to the per-literal code length ($\ell_{\rm lst}(\Phi)\ge\|\Phi\|$).
(b) *Time-charged:* $Kt_A^{\mathcal R,\mathcal C}(\mathcal Q(\mathcal S))\ge\log_2\min\{\|\Phi\|:\Phi\in\mathcal C\ \mathcal R\text{-usable}\}$, because $V$ needs at least $\|\Phi\|$ steps to print $\langle\Phi\rangle$. Upper bounds:
$$Kt_A^{\mathrm{UP}_1}(\mathcal Q(\mathcal S))\le Kt(A,b)+\log_2|\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)|+O(r+\log m),\quad Kt_A^{\mathrm{UP}}(\mathcal Q(\mathcal S))\le Kt(A,b)+O(r+\log m),\quad Kt_A^{\mathrm{GE}}(\mathcal Q(\mathcal S))\le Kt(A,b)+O(\log m).$$
(c) *Program length:* $K_A^{\mathcal R,\mathcal C}(\mathcal Q(\mathcal S))=K(\mathcal S)\pm c$ for $\mathcal R\in\{\mathrm{UP}_k\ (k\ge1),\mathrm{UP},\mathrm{PS}_\infty,\mathrm{GE}\}$ and either class.

*Proof.* (a) Lemma 1.5. (b) The lower bound is the printing time. For the upper bounds, a program runs a program $p$ for $(A,b)$ (time $t_p$), enumerates $\mathrm{Row}(A)$ ($2^r$ vectors), keeps the circuits (time $\mathrm{poly}(m)4^r$) and prints: their clauses ($\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$, Prop 2.2; time $\mathrm{poly}(m)|\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)|$), or the library of Prop 3.2 with intermediate concepts (at most $2^r$ circuits, each with at most $4m$ clauses), or, for $\mathrm{GE}$, just the $r$ equations, without enumerating $\mathrm{Row}(A)$. Then $\log_2$ of the total time is at most $\log_2t_p$ plus $\log_2|\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)|+O(r+\log m)$, resp. $O(r+\log m)$, resp. $O(\log m)$, and $|p|+\log_2t_p=Kt(A,b)$ for an optimal $p$. (`notes.md` wrote $K(A,b)$ here; a program of length $K(A,b)$ for $(A,b)$ need not run in any computable time bound, so $Kt$ is needed; referee A M2.) (c) Upper bounds: from $\mathcal S$ a fixed program computes $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)\in\mathcal C^X$, which is usable for every $\mathrm{UP}_k$, $\mathrm{UP}$ and $\mathrm{PS}_\infty$ (Lemma 1.10(a)), and a canonical system of equations, usable for $\mathrm{GE}$. Lower bound: from a program for any compatible $\Phi$ a fixed program computes $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi=\mathcal S$ (Lemma 1.5). (This also gives the converse $K_A^{\rm GE}\le K_A^{\mathrm{UP}_1}+c$ that `notes.md` left implicit; referee A m6.) ∎

**What this shows and does not show [assessment].** The textbook point ("the written theory is insufficient for a simple reasoner") is a statement about the pair (theory, reasoner), about the vocabulary the theory may use, *and* about a cost measure that charges the theory as written (or the work of writing it). Under the idealised measure of SAI (program length) every sound reasoner and every vocabulary is equivalent to the weakest one at $O(1)$ cost, so the idealised inducer cannot see the textbook point any more than it can see theory shape. The remaining sections state their separations for $K_{\rm lst}$ (written size) and say what survives under $Kt$. `notes.md`'s reading of the simplex family as an exponential gap "between reasoners" holds only for theories over the base variables or for one round of propagation; with intermediate concepts and unbounded propagation it is polynomial (referee A M1, referee B Issue 1).

## 2. Linear laws without intermediate concepts (target T2)

Throughout, $\mathcal S=\{x:Ax=b\}\ne\emptyset$, $\mathrm{rank}\,A=r\ge1$. A *circuit* is a nonzero $u\in\mathrm{Row}(A)$ whose support contains the support of no other nonzero derived equation. Over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ a vector is determined by its support, so a circuit is the unique nonzero derived equation supported in its support.

**Lemma 2.1 (determination; scout N2 Lemma 2.2) [proved; computed: c1 A, `refB_t2_linear` A].** For $I\subseteq[m]$ and $j\notin I$ the following are equivalent: (i) $x_j$ is a function of $x_I$ on $\mathcal S$; (ii) some $u\in\mathrm{Row}(A)$ has $j\in\mathrm{supp}(u)\subseteq I\cup\{j\}$; (iii) some circuit has this property.

*Proof.* (ii)⇒(i): $x_j=b_u+\sum_{i\in\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\{j\}}x_i$ on $\mathcal S$. (i)⇒(ii): if no such $u$ exists, $e_j\notin\mathrm{Row}(A)+\mathrm{span}\{e_i:i\in I\}$, so some $z$ is orthogonal to that space with $z_j=1$; then $z\in\ker A$, $z_i=0$ on $I$, and $x,x+z\in\mathcal S$ agree on $I$ and differ at $j$. (ii)⇒(iii): take $u$ of least support among those in (ii); if a nonzero $u'$ had $\mathrm{supp}(u')\subsetneq\mathrm{supp}(u)$, either $j\in\mathrm{supp}(u')$ (contradicting minimality) or $u+u'$ is a smaller witness. ∎

The checks: 2,944 pairs $(I,j)$ over four random systems ($m=7,8$), 0 mismatches (c1 A); 6,640 pairs over 24 laws with $d_*=1,2,3,4$, 0 mismatches (`refB_t2_linear` A).

**Proposition 2.2 (prime implicates; scout N2 Prop 2.3) [proved; computed: c1 B, `refB_t2_linear` B].** For $Z\subseteq[m]$, $\mathrm{proj}_Z\mathcal S$ is the affine subspace cut out by the derived equations supported in $Z$; so $C_{Z,\beta}$ is an implicate of $\mathcal S$ iff $\beta$ violates some derived equation supported in $Z$. The prime implicates of $\mathcal S$ are exactly the clauses $C_{\mathrm{supp}(u),\beta}$ with $u$ a circuit and $u\cdot\beta\ne b_u$; there are $\sum_{\text{circuits}}2^{|u|-1}$ of them, and $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$ is $\mathrm{UP}_1$-usable.

*Proof.* The functionals on $\{0,1\}^Z$ constant on $\mathrm{proj}_Z\mathcal S$ are the restrictions of functionals constant on $\mathcal S$, i.e. of $\mathrm{Row}(A)$ (since $\mathcal S=x^0+\ker A$ and $(\ker A)^\perp=\mathrm{Row}(A)$), that vanish off $Z$. So $C_{Z,\beta}$ is an implicate iff $\beta$ violates some $u$ with $\mathrm{supp}(u)\subseteq Z$. Every such $u$ is a sum of circuits supported in $\mathrm{supp}(u)$ (repeat Lemma 2.1 (ii)⇒(iii)); parities add, so $\beta$ violates one of them, $u_0$, and $C_{\mathrm{supp}(u_0),\beta|}$ subsumes $C_{Z,\beta}$. A clause $C_{\mathrm{supp}(u),\beta}$ with $u$ a circuit is prime: a smaller implicate would be violated on a smaller support, contradicting minimality. Usability: Lemma 1.10(a). ∎

The check: prime implicates by brute force (all $3^m$ clauses) equal the circuit clauses for six random systems ($m=6$–$8$); minimal width $=d_*$ in all six; $\mathrm{PI}$ answers all entailed literals in one round (c1 B); 16 further laws, 0 failures (`refB_t2_linear` B).

**Proposition 2.3 (any CNF; scout N2 Prop 2.4(a)) [proved; computed: c1 C, `refB_misc2` B].** Every implicate of $\mathcal S$ has width $\ge d_*$, so every CNF over $X$ equivalent to $\mathcal S$ has at least $(1-2^{-r})2^{d_*}$ clauses.

*Proof.* For $|Z|<d_*$ no nonzero derived equation is supported in $Z$, so $\mathrm{proj}_Z\mathcal S=\{0,1\}^Z$ (Prop 2.2). A clause of width $w\ge d_*$ excludes $2^{m-w}\le2^{m-d_*}$ points; the $2^m-2^{m-r}$ non-solutions must be excluded. ∎

Referee B computed exact minimum CNF sizes (set cover by prime implicates) for 14 laws: the bound holds everywhere and is tight for the 7 single-equation laws.

**Lemma 2.4 (subsumption) [proved; computed: c1 G, `refB_misc2` A].** Let $\mathcal S$ be any law, $\Phi$ a set of implicates of $\mathcal S$ over $X$, and $\Phi'$ obtained by replacing each clause $C$ by any prime implicate $C'\subseteq C$. For every $\alpha$ consistent with $\mathcal S$ and every $k$, every literal derived by $\mathrm{UP}_k$ on $\Phi\cup\alpha$ is derived by $\mathrm{UP}_k$ on $\Phi'\cup\alpha$, and neither run reaches a conflict. So minimum usable theories over $X$ may be sought among subsets of $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$, round for round.

*Proof.* Induction on rounds. If $C$ fires in round $t$ deriving $L$, all other literals of $C$ are falsified after round $t-1$, hence (induction) in the $\Phi'$-run. $C'\subseteq C$; if $L\notin C'$, every literal of $C'$ is falsified by the state after round $t-1$ of the $\Phi$-run. That state consists of literals entailed by $\Phi\cup\alpha$, so it holds at every point of $\mathcal S\wedge\alpha\neq\emptyset$ (all clauses of $\Phi$ are implicates of $\mathcal S$); such a point would falsify the implicate $C'$, which is impossible. So $L\in C'$ and $C'$ fires by round $t$. The same argument shows that neither run conflicts. ∎

Only one direction is claimed: $\Phi'$ derives at least as much as $\Phi$. The converse fails (Thm 7.1's counterexamples).

**Definition.** A circuit $u$ is *decomposable* if $u=u'+u''$ with $u',u''\in\mathrm{Row}(A)$ nonzero and $\mathrm{supp}(u')\cap\mathrm{supp}(u'')=\{i\}$ for a single $i\notin\mathrm{supp}(u)$: two derived equations chained through one intermediate base quantity $x_i$. Otherwise it is *indecomposable*. *Equivalent form* [proved; computed: c1 H, 0 violations]: $u$ is decomposable iff for some, equivalently every, $j\in\mathrm{supp}(u)$ there are a derived equation $u'$ and $i\notin\mathrm{supp}(u)$ with $i\in\mathrm{supp}(u')\subseteq(\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\{j\})\cup\{i\}$. (A decomposition through $i$ gives such a $u'$ for every $j$, taking $u'$ or $u''$, whichever does not contain $j$; conversely such a $u'$ gives the decomposition $u=u'+(u+u')$, whose supports meet in $\{i\}$.)

**Proposition 2.5 (indecomposable circuits must be written) [proved; computed: c7 H, `refB_t2_linear` C].** Every $\mathrm{UP}$-usable explicit theory over $X$ contains all $2^{|u|-1}$ clauses $C_{\mathrm{supp}(u),\beta}$, $u\cdot\beta\ne b_u$, of every indecomposable circuit $u$. This holds for every $d_*$.

*Proof.* Let $\Phi\equiv\mathcal S$ be usable, $u$ indecomposable, $j\in\mathrm{supp}(u)$, $I:=\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\{j\}$, and $\alpha$ any assignment to $I$. No nonzero derived equation is supported in $I$ ($u$ is a circuit), so $\alpha$ is consistent and determines $x_j$ (Lemma 2.1). Unit propagation is sound and $\Phi\cup\alpha$ satisfiable, so it never conflicts, and by usability it derives a literal outside $I$; so round 1 derives one. A literal derived in round 1 on $x_i$, $i\notin I$, comes from a clause $C\in\Phi$ whose other literals are on $I$ and falsified by $\alpha$, so $\mathrm{supp}(C)\subseteq I\cup\{i\}$, and $C$ is an implicate. By Prop 2.2 some derived equation $u'$ with $\mathrm{supp}(u')\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(C)$ is violated by $C$'s falsifying assignment, and $i\in\mathrm{supp}(u')$ (otherwise $u'$ is supported in $I$). If $i\ne j$, then $i\notin\mathrm{supp}(u)$ and $u'$ contradicts indecomposability (equivalent form). So $i=j$, $\mathrm{supp}(u')\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(u)$, hence $u'=u$ (minimality of circuits), and $C=C_{\mathrm{supp}(u),(\alpha,\neg\ell)}$. The $2^{|u|-1}$ choices of $\alpha$ give all the clauses of $u$. ∎

**Proposition 2.5a (the converse: nothing else is forced) [proved; computed: c7 H, `refB_t2_linear` C].** For every affine law with $r\ge1$ and every clause $C\notin F_{\rm ind}(\mathcal S)$ (defined in Thm 2.6) there is a $\mathrm{UP}$-usable theory over $X$ that does not contain $C$. Hence, for every $d_*$, *the clauses contained in every $\mathrm{UP}$-usable theory over $X$ are exactly $F_{\rm ind}(\mathcal S)$.* (Referee B Issue 11 suggested and sketched this.)

*Proof.* Let $\mathrm{Imp}(\mathcal S)$ be the set of all non-tautological implicates of $\mathcal S$ over $X$; we show that $\mathrm{Imp}(\mathcal S)\setminus\{C\}$ is usable. It consists of implicates, so unit propagation on it from a consistent antecedent never conflicts, and once it is compatible with $\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)$ its projection is $\mathcal S$ (Lemma 1.5). If $C$ is not a prime implicate, $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)\subseteq\mathrm{Imp}(\mathcal S)\setminus\{C\}$, and this set is usable by Lemma 1.10(a) and monotonicity of unit propagation in the clause set. Otherwise $C=C_{\mathrm{supp}(u),\beta}$ for a circuit $u$ that is decomposable. Let $(\alpha,\ell)\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)$. In the one-round run on $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)\cup\alpha$ some prime implicate $C'\subseteq\neg\alpha\vee\ell$ fires (Lemma 1.10(a)). If $C'\ne C$, it is in our set. If $C'=C$, then $\ell$ is the literal of $C$ on some $j\in\mathrm{supp}(u)$ and $\alpha$ contains $\beta|_{\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\{j\}}$. By the equivalent form there are $u'$ and $i\notin\mathrm{supp}(u)$ with $i\in\mathrm{supp}(u')\subseteq(\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\{j\})\cup\{i\}$; put $u'':=u+u'$, so $j\in\mathrm{supp}(u'')\subseteq(\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\mathrm{supp}(u'))\cup\{i\}$. Let $v_i$ be the value of $x_i$ that $u'$ forces given $\alpha$. The clause on $\mathrm{supp}(u')$ falsified by $\alpha|_{\mathrm{supp}(u')\setminus\{i\}}\cup\{x_i{=}1-v_i\}$ is an implicate (that assignment violates $u'$; Prop 2.2), differs from $C$ (its support contains $i\notin\mathrm{supp}(u)$) and fires in round 1, deriving $x_i{=}v_i$. The clause on $\mathrm{supp}(u'')$ falsified by the restriction of $\alpha\cup\{x_i{=}v_i\}$ to $\mathrm{supp}(u'')\setminus\{j\}$, extended by the wrong value of $x_j$, is likewise an implicate different from $C$, and fires in round 2, deriving $\ell$ (soundness: $\alpha\cup\{x_i{=}v_i\}$ is consistent, and the value is the one entailed). So every question is answered. With Prop 2.5 the intersection statement follows. ∎

The checks: on 36 random laws (12 each with $d_*=1$, $d_*=2$, $d_*\ge3$; $m=5$–$7$), the clauses whose deletion from the set of all derived-equation clauses breaks usability (tested for every prime implicate and 10 random non-prime ones) are exactly $F_{\rm ind}$ in 36 of 36 (c7 H); referee B found the same on 40 laws, 33 of them with $d_*\le2$ (`refB_t2_linear` C).

**Theorem 2.6 (the unique minimum when $d_*\ge3$) [proved; computed: c1 D, H; `refA_checks` D; `refB_t2_linear` C2, D].** If $d_*\ge3$, the clauses of the indecomposable circuits,
$$F_{\rm ind}(\mathcal S):=\{C_{\mathrm{supp}(u),\beta}:\ u\text{ an indecomposable circuit},\ u\cdot\beta\ne b_u\},$$
form a $\mathrm{UP}$-usable theory. Hence an explicit theory over $X$ equivalent to $\mathcal S$ is $\mathrm{UP}$-usable iff it contains $F_{\rm ind}(\mathcal S)$, and $F_{\rm ind}(\mathcal S)$ is the unique minimum, of size $\sum_{u\text{ indecomposable}}2^{|u|-1}$.

*Proof.* We show by induction on $|w|$: for every nonzero $w\in\mathrm{Row}(A)$, every $j\in\mathrm{supp}(w)$ and every assignment $\alpha$ to $\mathrm{supp}(w)\setminus\{j\}$ consistent with $\mathcal S$, $\mathrm{UP}$ on $F_{\rm ind}\cup\alpha$ derives the value of $x_j$ (and never conflicts, as $F_{\rm ind}\cup\alpha$ is satisfiable). By Lemma 2.1 and monotonicity of unit propagation (a larger consistent antecedent derives at least as much), this is usability.
* *$w$ not a circuit.* By Lemma 2.1(iii) with $I=\mathrm{supp}(w)\setminus\{j\}$ there is a circuit $c'\ne w$ with $j\in\mathrm{supp}(c')\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(w)$, $|c'|<|w|$; apply the induction hypothesis to $c'$ and $\alpha|_{\mathrm{supp}(c')\setminus\{j\}}$.
* *$w=u$ an indecomposable circuit.* Its clauses are in $F_{\rm ind}$; one fires.
* *$w=u$ a decomposable circuit.* Take $u=u'+u''$ through $i\notin\mathrm{supp}(u)$ with $j\in\mathrm{supp}(u'')$ (equivalent form). If $\mathrm{supp}(u')=(\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\{j\})\cup\{i\}$, then $u''=e_i+e_j$ has weight $2<d_*$, impossible; so $|u'|\le|u|-1$. Also $|\mathrm{supp}(u')\cap\mathrm{supp}(u)|=|u'|-1\ge d_*-1\ge2$, so $|u''|=|\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\mathrm{supp}(u')|+1\le|u|-1$. By induction on $u'$ (target $i$, antecedent $\alpha$ restricted to $\mathrm{supp}(u')\setminus\{i\}\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\{j\}$), unit propagation derives the value $v_i$ of $x_i$ determined by $\alpha$. The assignment $\alpha\cup\{x_i=v_i\}$ is consistent, and its restriction to $\mathrm{supp}(u'')\setminus\{j\}\subseteq(\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\{j\})\cup\{i\}$ is an antecedent for $u''$ and target $j$; by induction unit propagation derives $x_j$.

The base case is contained in the induction: a circuit of weight $d_*\ge3$ is indecomposable (if $u=u'+u''$ through $i$ with $j\in\mathrm{supp}(u'')$, the two bounds above give $|u'|,|u''|\le d_*-1$, contradicting minimality of $d_*$). Necessity: every usable theory over $X$ contains $F_{\rm ind}$ itself (Prop 2.5). Sufficiency: an explicit theory over $X$ equivalent to $\mathcal S$ consists of implicates, so adding its clauses to the usable $F_{\rm ind}$ keeps every derivation and causes no conflict on consistent antecedents. ∎

*Checks.* Exhaustive minimum over subsets of $\mathrm{PI}$ for 30 random laws ($m=5$–$8$, $d_*\ge2$): for all 10 laws with $d_*\ge3$ it equals $|F_{\rm ind}|$, sometimes far above $a_*2^{d_*-1}$ (40 against 8) (c1 D). 600 random laws with $d_*\ge3$ ($m=7$–$10$, $r=2$–$5$): $F_{\rm ind}$ usable in all 600; in 60 of them deleting any single clause of $F_{\rm ind}$ broke usability (2,208/2,208) (c1 H). Independently: 100 of 100 random laws, 93 of them with a decomposable circuit (`refA_checks` D); 30 targeted laws with decomposable circuits, where usability of $F_{\rm ind}$ and necessity of each of its clauses together prove uniqueness law by law, and 13 laws by exhaustive search over subsets of $\mathrm{PI}$ assuming nothing about $F_{\rm ind}$ (`refB_t2_linear` C2, D).

**Corollary 2.7 [proved; computed: `refB_t2_linear` E, E2].** Let $d_*\ge3$.
(a) (Scout N2 Prop 2.4(b).) Every circuit of weight $d_*$ is indecomposable, so every $\mathrm{UP}$-usable theory over $X$ has at least $a_*2^{d_*-1}$ clauses.
(b) *When the core suffices.* The core (the $r$ given equations, each written as all of its $2^{|{\rm row}|-1}$ clauses) is $\mathrm{UP}$-usable iff every indecomposable circuit is one of the given rows. In words: **a circuit must be written down iff it cannot be obtained by chaining two derived equations through one intermediate base quantity**; the written core suffices for the propagation reasoner exactly when it already contains every such circuit. (A derived equation that is not a circuit, for example the sum of two circuits with disjoint supports, never needs to be written; `notes.md` said "a derived law"; referee A m7.)
(c) A single parity law on $m\ge3$ variables needs exactly its $2^{m-1}$ clauses.

*Proof.* (a) As in the base case of Theorem 2.6. (b) The core is equivalent to $\mathcal S$, so by Theorem 2.6 it is usable iff it contains $F_{\rm ind}$. A clause on the support of a circuit $u$ belongs to the core iff some row has that support, i.e. (a vector is its support) iff $u$ is a row. (c) $\mathrm{Row}(A)=\{0,1^m\}$ and $1^m$ is indecomposable. ∎

Referee B checked (b) on 120 systems (60 random, 60 built with a non-circuit row): the prediction agrees with exhaustive usability in 120 of 120, and the core is usable in 40 and 0 of the two groups.

**Corollary 2.8 (the simplex family) [proved; computed: c1 E, `refB_t2_linear` F].** Let $r\ge3$, $m=2^r-1$, and $A$ the $r\times m$ matrix whose columns are all nonzero vectors of $\mathrm{GF}(2)^r$ ($\mathcal S$ is a coset of the Hamming code). Every nonzero derived equation has weight $(m+1)/2=2^{r-1}$, so all $m$ of them are circuits, all indecomposable, and the unique minimum $\mathrm{UP}$-usable theory over $X$ is $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$, with $m\,2^{(m-1)/2}$ clauses of width $(m+1)/2$; against $r=\log_2(m+1)$ equations for $\mathrm{GE}$. By Prop 3.1a the same holds for $\mathrm{UP}_1$ with any intermediate concepts; with intermediate concepts and unbounded propagation it does not (Rem 3.2a).

**Proposition 2.9a (equivalence laws) [proved; computed: c7 G].** Call $\mathcal S$ an *equivalence law* if $\mathrm{Row}(A)$ is spanned by derived equations of weight 2. Then $i\sim j:\iff e_i+e_j\in\mathrm{Row}(A)$ partitions the variables that occur in derived equations into classes $K_1,\dots,K_s$ of sizes $c_1,\dots,c_s\ge2$, and the least $\mathrm{UP}$-usable theory over $X$ has exactly $c_1+\dots+c_s$ clauses: a directed cycle of implications on each class.

*Proof.* Every derived equation is a sum of $e_i+e_j$ with $i\sim j$, so its restriction to each class has even weight; hence every derived equation of weight $\ge3$ contains in its support two variables of one class, and the circuits are exactly the weight-2 equations, which are within classes; there is no derived equation of weight 1. *Lower bound.* Fix a class $K$, $i\in K$ and $v\in\{0,1\}$. The antecedent $\{x_i{=}v\}$ is consistent and entails a literal on each $x_{i'}$, $i'\in K\setminus\{i\}$, so unit propagation derives something in round 1, by a clause all of whose other literals are falsified by $\{x_i{=}v\}$: a unit clause, which is not an implicate (no variable is fixed), or a 2-clause containing the literal $x_i{\ne}v$. A 2-clause implicate has as support a derived equation's support (Prop 2.2), i.e. a pair within one class. So each of the $2c_K$ literals on $K$ has its negation in some 2-clause within $K$; a 2-clause contains two literals, so at least $c_K$ clauses lie within $K$, and classes do not share clauses. *Upper bound.* Fix $x^0\in\mathcal S$ and put $\lambda_i:=[x_i=x^0_i]$; on $\mathcal S$, $\lambda_i\leftrightarrow\lambda_{i'}$ for $i\sim i'$. For $K=\{i_1,\dots,i_c\}$ take the $c$ implicates $\neg\lambda_{i_t}\vee\lambda_{i_{t+1}}$ (indices mod $c$). A question $(\alpha,\ell)$ with $\ell$ on $x_j$, $j\in K$, has $\alpha$ assigning some $x_i$, $i\in K$ (Lemma 2.1(iii): the circuits are the pairs); if $\alpha$ makes $\lambda_i$ true, propagation runs forward around the cycle to $\lambda_j$; if false, backward. No conflict arises (implicates, consistent antecedent). ∎

The check (c7 G, exhaustive over subsets of $\mathrm{PI}$): one class of $c=2,3,4,5$ variables: minimum $c$; an anti-equivalence class $x_1=x_2\oplus1=x_3$: 3; two classes of sizes $(2,3)$ and $(3,3)$: 5 and 6.

**Reading [assessment].** Props 2.5, 2.5a and Theorem 2.6 sharpen scout N2's lower bound into an exact description, and it is itself about intermediate concepts: base variables already act as intermediate quantities. A circuit that factors through one base quantity ($u=u'+u''$ through $x_i$) need not be written, because the reasoner can compute $x_i$ and continue; a circuit that does not factor so must be written in full, in all $2^{|u|-1}$ patterns. Exponential size arises when many indecomposable circuits are wide, as for the simplex family, where no base quantity can serve as an intermediate. This is the textbook point for a reader who only applies rules and has only the base vocabulary: the explicit theory must contain every circuit the reader cannot assemble from intermediate quantities it already has.

## 3. Intermediate concepts (target T3)

### 3.1 One law

**Proposition 3.1 (the chain encoding; scout N2 Prop 2.5) [proved; computed: c2 A, `refA_checks` J, `refB_t3_aux` A].** Encode $x_1\oplus\dots\oplus x_m=b$ ($m\ge4$) by the constraints $y_1=x_1\oplus x_2$, $y_i=y_{i-1}\oplus x_{i+1}$ ($2\le i\le m-2$), $y_{m-2}\oplus x_m=b$, each three-variable constraint written as its 4 clauses and the last as 2: $4m-6$ clauses, $m-2$ auxiliary variables. The encoding is *propagation complete on all variables*: for every partial assignment $\alpha$ to base and auxiliary variables, unit propagation derives every literal entailed by the encoding and $\alpha$, and reaches a conflict iff $\alpha$ is inconsistent. Without auxiliary variables $2^{m-1}$ clauses are necessary (Corollary 2.7(c)) and sufficient.

*Proof.* Each constraint's clause set is all prime implicates of that one constraint, so (Lemma 1.10(a)) it derives its third variable when two are assigned and gives a conflict when all are assigned inconsistently. Consider the fixpoint of unit propagation without conflict. Every constraint is then either fully assigned, and satisfied, or has at least two unassigned variables. Call the latter *active*. Each variable occurs in at most two constraints, and consecutive constraints share exactly one auxiliary variable, so the graph on active constraints, joined when they share an unassigned variable, is a disjoint union of paths. Fix an unassigned variable $z$ and a value for it. Process each component breadth-first from a root (in the component of $z$, a constraint containing $z$, with $z$ set first). When a constraint is processed, at most one of its variables is already set: the one it shares with its parent, or $z$ at the root. It has at least two unassigned variables, so at least one is still free: give the other free ones arbitrary values and solve for that one. This yields a solution extending the fixpoint with either value of $z$, so no unassigned variable is entailed; and if $\alpha$ were inconsistent, the same construction (without $z$) would give a solution, so unit propagation must have reached a conflict. ∎

The checks: exhaustive over all $3^{m+(m-2)}$ partial assignments to base and auxiliary variables, $m=3,\dots,7$, both constants (for $m=7$: 150,147 consistent and 381,294 inconsistent assignments): 0 failures (c2 A; the referees' independent code agrees for $m\le6$ and $m\le7$).

*Known context.* Emdin, Kulikov, Mihajlin and Slezkin (MFCS 2022) show [known: abstract level] that every CNF encoding of parity on $m$ variables with auxiliary variables has at least $3m$ clauses, and $\Omega(2^{m/(s+1)}/m)$ clauses with at most $s$ auxiliary variables. So the chain is within a factor $4/3$ of the least possible clause count even among encodings that need not be usable; the chain encoding itself is folklore.

**Proposition 3.1a (one round of propagation cannot use intermediate concepts) [proved; computed: c7 C].** Let $\mathcal S$ be any law and $\Phi\in\mathcal C^{XY}$ a $\mathrm{UP}_1$-usable theory for $\mathcal S$. Then $\Phi\supseteq\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$. So the least $\mathrm{UP}_1$-usable theory, with any intermediate concepts, is $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$: $2^{m-1}$ clauses for one parity law and $m2^{(m-1)/2}$ for the simplex family. (Referee B Issue 1 observed this for $\mathrm{UP}_1$.)

*Proof.* Let $\alpha$ be an antecedent of a question; it assigns base variables only. In round 1 a clause fires only if all its literals but one are falsified; auxiliary literals are unassigned, so a clause containing an auxiliary literal fires only if that literal is its only unfalsified one, and then it derives that auxiliary literal. Hence the base literals derived in round 1 are derived by the clauses of $\Phi$ over $X$, $\Phi_X$; no conflict occurs, as $\Phi\cup\alpha$ is satisfiable. Each $C\in\Phi_X$ is an implicate of $\mathcal S$ ($\Phi\models C$ and $\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi=\mathcal S$). The argument of Lemma 1.10(b), which uses only that the firing clause is an implicate of $\mathcal S$, gives $\Phi_X\supseteq\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$. ∎

The check (c7 C): for 12 random laws ($m=6$–$8$, $r=3$, $d_*=1,2,3$), $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$ together with a chain-encoded circuit library with auxiliary variables loses $\mathrm{UP}_1$-usability when any one clause of $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$ is deleted (all copies), in every case (350 deletions); the chain-encoded simplex libraries for $r=3,4$ are $\mathrm{UP}$-usable but not $\mathrm{UP}_1$-usable. (A first version of the test deleted only one copy of clauses that the library also contains, and reported spurious survivals; Verification log.)

### 3.2 Systems of laws

**Proposition 3.2 (lemma libraries with intermediate concepts; scout N2 Prop 2.6) [proved; computed: c2 B, c7 I, `refB_t3_aux` B].** Let $\mathrm{Circ}_{\rm aux}(\mathcal S)$ write each circuit $u$ of $\mathrm{Row}(A)$ by the cheaper of its full clause set and its chain encoding with its own auxiliary variables: directly if $|u|\le4$, by the chain if $|u|\ge5$. It is $\mathrm{UP}$-usable, of size $\sum_{\text{circuits}}s(|u|)$ with $s(w):=2^{w-1}$ for $w\le4$ and $s(w):=4w-6$ for $w\ge5$. The same holds for the library over all $2^r-1$ nonzero derived equations, of size at most $(2^r-1)(4m-6)$ for $m\ge4$. (`notes.md` described the construction as "weight $\le3$ written directly" and gave the size $\sum\min(2^{|u|-1},4|u|-6)$, which is $-2$ at $|u|=1$ and does not match that construction at $|u|=4$; referee B Issue 7. The clause counts reported from c2 below were computed with the chain already at weight 4, a usable variant with $10$ instead of $8$ clauses per weight-4 circuit.)

*Proof.* A question $(\alpha,\ell)$ has a circuit $u$ with $j\in\mathrm{supp}(u)\subseteq I\cup\{j\}$ (Lemma 2.1); the encoding of $u$ is propagation complete (Prop 3.1, or Lemma 1.10(a) for the direct clauses), and $\alpha$ fixes all of $\mathrm{supp}(u)$ but $j$, so unit propagation on that encoding alone derives $\ell$, and unit propagation is monotone in the clause set. Each auxiliary variable is a partial sum of base variables, so the union projects onto $\mathcal S$ (the circuits span $\mathrm{Row}(A)$), and a consistent $\alpha$ gives no conflict. ∎

The second form has the size $O(m\,2^r)$ of Gwynne and Kullmann's fixed-parameter construction [known: GK 2014, abstract level: an arc-consistent representation with $O(n\cdot2^m)$ clauses for $m$ equations in $n$ variables]. On six random systems ($m=8$–$11$) both libraries were usable (exhaustive over all partial assignments; up to 219,180 entailed literals) (c2 B); the revised construction matches the formula $\sum_us(|u|)$ on eight random systems with circuits of weights 1 to 6, and is usable (c7 I).

**Remark 3.2a (the simplex family with intermediate concepts) [proved; computed: c7 E, `refA_checks` C, `refA_extra` I, `refB_t3_aux` S].** For the simplex family with $r\ge4$ every circuit has weight $w=2^{r-1}\ge8$, so $\mathrm{Circ}_{\rm aux}$ has $m(4w-6)=m(2m-4)$ clauses of width $\le3$ and $m(w-2)$ auxiliary variables, and is $\mathrm{UP}$-usable: a polynomial-size theory. Listing lengths (bits; the GE core and the library with the code of Def 1.6):

| $r$ | $m$ | $\mathrm{PI}$ over $X$ | $\mathrm{Circ}_{\rm aux}$ | GE core | ratio library / core |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 15 | $9.2\times10^4$ | 11,066 (390 clauses) | 170 | 65 |
| 5 | 31 | $1.1\times10^8$ | 62,280 (1,798 clauses) | 456 | 137 |

| 7 | 127 | $6.1\times10^{23}$ | 1,488,480 (31,750 clauses) | 3,229 | 461 |

The library is usable: exhaustively at $r=3$ (chain-encoded, 70 clauses; all $3^7$ base partial assignments), on all 15,360 minimal questions and 4,000 random partial assignments at $r=4$ (referee B), and on 3,000 sampled minimal questions at $r=4$ (c7 C). The ratio grows polynomially, not like $2^{(m-1)/2}$. So the simplex family separates $\mathrm{GE}$ from $\mathrm{UP}$ exponentially only over the base variables (Cor 2.8), or for one round (Prop 3.1a), or for a fixed number of rounds (Prop 3.8(c)); with intermediate concepts and enough rounds the gap is polynomial in written length and $O(\log m)$ bits under $Kt$ (Thm 1.12(b)). Writing each circuit by the balanced-tree encoding of Prop 3.8(b) instead of the chain gives the same size and makes the library $\mathrm{UP}_{2\lceil\log_2w\rceil-1}$-usable, since each question is answered inside one circuit's encoding (as in Prop 3.2's proof); at $r=4$ the chain library needs $w-1=7$ rounds and the balanced-tree library 5 (c7 E).

**Proposition 3.3 (private intermediate concepts add nothing to a system) [proved; computed: c2 C, `refB_t3_aux` C].** Let $\Phi=\bigcup_t\Phi_t$, where $\Phi_t$ encodes the constraint $c_t$ (over base variables $X_t$) with auxiliary variables $Y_t$ private to it ($Y_t\cap Y_s=\emptyset$, $Y_t$ occurs only in $\Phi_t$, $\exists Y_t\,\Phi_t\equiv c_t$), and each $\Phi_t$ is propagation complete on $X_t\cup Y_t$. For a partial assignment $\alpha$ to the base variables, its *arc-consistency closure* $\mathrm{AC}(\alpha)$ is the least set of base literals containing $\alpha$ and closed under adding every base literal on $X_t$ entailed by $c_t$ and the current literals on $X_t$, for every $t$; it is *contradictory* if it contains two opposite literals or its literals on some $X_t$ are inconsistent with $c_t$. Then unit propagation on $\Phi\cup\alpha$ reaches a conflict iff $\mathrm{AC}(\alpha)$ is contradictory; otherwise the base literals at its fixpoint are exactly $\mathrm{AC}(\alpha)$. In particular the core written directly and the core chain-encoded derive exactly the same base literals and conflict on the same antecedents.

*Proof.* *Invariant.* At every point of the run, the literals on $Y_t$ are entailed by $\Phi_t$ and the literals on $X_t$ (they are derived only by clauses of $\Phi_t$, and each derivation step preserves this). Hence a literal derived by a clause of $\Phi_t$ is entailed by $\Phi_t$ and the current literals on $X_t$; if it is a base literal, it is entailed by $c_t$ and the current literals on $X_t$ ($\exists Y_t\Phi_t\equiv c_t$). By induction on rounds, every base literal derived before a conflict lies in $\mathrm{AC}(\alpha)$. A conflict arises either from a clause of some $\Phi_t$ with all literals falsified, so that the current literals on $X_t\cup Y_t$ are inconsistent with $\Phi_t$ and (invariant) those on $X_t$ are inconsistent with $c_t$, or from two opposite derivations, both from one $\Phi_t$ if the variable is auxiliary (same conclusion) or both base literals of $\mathrm{AC}(\alpha)$; either way $\mathrm{AC}(\alpha)$ is contradictory. *Conversely,* suppose the run reaches a conflict-free fixpoint $\gamma$ with base part $\beta$. Since no clause of $\Phi_t$ fires at $\gamma$ and $\Phi_t$ is propagation complete, $\gamma|_{X_t\cup Y_t}$ is consistent with $\Phi_t$ and contains every literal it entails with $\Phi_t$; so $\beta|_{X_t}$ is consistent with $c_t$, and if $c_t\wedge\beta|_{X_t}\models\ell$ then $\Phi_t\wedge\gamma|_{X_t\cup Y_t}\models\ell$ and $\ell\in\gamma$. So $\beta\supseteq\alpha$ is closed and not contradictory, hence $\beta\supseteq\mathrm{AC}(\alpha)$ and $\mathrm{AC}(\alpha)$ is not contradictory; with the first part, $\beta=\mathrm{AC}(\alpha)$. The direct encoding of one equation (all its clauses) is propagation complete on its own variables by Lemma 1.10(a). ∎

(`notes.md` stated the equality without separating conflicting runs and argued the inclusion $\supseteq$ step by step rather than at the fixpoint; referee A m12.)

So intermediate concepts that belong to one law each cannot help a weak reasoner combine laws; the useful intermediate concepts are shared across laws (derived equations and their partial sums).

**Computed: the core is not enough [computed: c2 C, c7 K; `refB_misc_core`; scout N2 E5 agrees].** On sparse random systems (row weights 3–4, $r=m/2$), unit propagation on the core answers fully only part of the sampled questions, and the fraction varies from system to system: c2 (one system per $m$, 3,000 questions) 0.803 ($m=12$), 0.772 ($m=16$), 0.826 ($m=20$); c7 K (five systems per $m$, 1,000 questions each) per-system ranges 0.693–0.885 ($m=12$), 0.623–0.826 ($m=16$), 0.796–0.816 ($m=20$); referee B (three systems per $m$) 0.805–0.857, 0.701–0.789, 0.736–0.876. The chain-encoded core answers exactly the same fractions (Prop 3.3); the circuit library answers all of them (c2: 334, 2,004 and 6,208 clauses). Exhaustively, on four systems with $m=8$–$10$, direct and chain-encoded cores gave the same base literals on all $3^m$ partial assignments (c2 C). When $d_*\ge3$, Corollary 2.7(b) says exactly when the core suffices. (`notes.md` reported the three single-system fractions as if they were characteristic of $m$; the spread is as large as the differences; referee B Issue 9.)

### 3.3 No polynomial library for systems, even with intermediate concepts

**Lemma 3.4 (usability implies conflict detection) [proved; computed: `refB_t3_aux` D].** If $\Phi$ is $\mathrm{UP}$-usable for $\mathcal S$, then for every partial assignment $\alpha$ to the base variables that is inconsistent with $\mathcal S$, unit propagation on $\Phi\cup\alpha$ reaches a conflict. So $\mathrm{UP}$-usability as defined here is the same as *arc consistency* (GAC) of a representation in the sense of Bessiere et al. and Gwynne–Kullmann (unit propagation detects every forced base literal and every inconsistency).

*Proof.* Take $\alpha'\subseteq\alpha$ minimal inconsistent and a literal $x_j{=}v$ of $\alpha'$. Then $\alpha'':=\alpha'\setminus\{x_j{=}v\}$ is consistent and entails $x_j{=}1-v$, so unit propagation on $\Phi\cup\alpha''$ derives it. Unit propagation is monotone up to conflict: run on the larger $\Phi\cup\alpha$, every clause that fired in the smaller run either fires again or meets a conflict. So the larger run derives $x_j{=}1-v$ against $x_j{=}v\in\alpha$, or conflicts earlier. ∎

**Lemma 3.5 (unit propagation as a circuit) [proved; known in substance: Bessiere, Katsirelos, Narodytska, Walsh 2009; computed: c2 D, c7 D].** Let $\Phi$ be an explicit theory with $\bar m$ variables, $\|\Phi\|$ literal occurrences and clause width at most $w$, and $\mathcal I$ a set of literals ("inputs").
(a) There is a monotone circuit of $O(\bar m(\|\Phi\|+\bar m))$ fan-in-2 AND/OR gates with one input per literal of $\mathcal I$ that outputs, for every literal $L$, whether unit propagation on $\Phi\cup\alpha$ derives $L$, on every input $\alpha\subseteq\mathcal I$ for which $\Phi\cup\alpha$ is satisfiable.
(b) For every $k$ there is a monotone circuit of unbounded fan-in AND/OR gates, of depth $2k$, with at most $k(\|\Phi\|+2\bar m)$ gates and at most $k(w\|\Phi\|+2\bar m)$ wires, with the same inputs, that outputs whether $\mathrm{UP}_k$ derives $L$, on the same inputs.

*Proof.* Gates $g_t(L)$ for rounds $t=0,1,\dots$: $g_0(L)$ is the input bit if $L\in\mathcal I$, else the constant 0; $g_{t+1}(L):=g_t(L)\vee\bigvee_{C\ni L}\bigwedge_{L'\in C,L'\ne L}g_t(\neg L')$ (the empty conjunction, for a unit clause $C=(L)$, is the constant 1). On a satisfiable $\Phi\cup\alpha$ unit propagation never conflicts, so a clause whose other literals are all falsified fires (its last literal is unassigned or already true), exactly as in the formula. (a) Prefix and suffix products give the $|C|$ conjunctions of a clause with $3|C|$ fan-in-2 gates, so a round costs $O(\|\Phi\|+\bar m)$ gates; each round before the fixpoint assigns at least one new variable and no variable is assigned twice, so $\bar m$ rounds suffice (`notes.md` said $2\bar m$; referee A m18). (b) Per round, one AND gate per pair (clause, literal of it), of fan-in $|C|-1\le w-1$, and one OR gate per literal, of fan-in one plus the number of clauses containing it: at most $\|\Phi\|+2\bar m$ gates and $w\|\Phi\|+2\bar m$ wires per round. AND layers and OR layers alternate (an OR gate feeding an OR gate of the next round is merged into it), so the depth is $2k$. Constants are removed by simplification without increasing size or depth. ∎

The checks: on the hidden-state law with four hidden variables (weight-3 version of `notes.md`, 9 base variables), the circuit of (a) built from a usable theory (2,442 gates) agrees with unit propagation and with XOR-unsatisfiability on all 256 inputs; on random laws with circuit libraries it agrees with unit propagation on every literal (c2 D; a first version mishandled unit clauses and was fixed, development-round Verification log). The circuit of (b) built from the balanced-tree encoding of parity ($m=4,\dots,8$, $k$ the rounds that encoding needs) has exactly $k(\|\Phi\|+2\bar m)$ gates and depth $2k$, and computes $x_1$ as the parity of $x_2,\dots,x_m$ on all inputs (c7 D).

**Theorem 3.6 (systems of laws need exponential written length, with any intermediate concepts) [proved, given GKRS 2019; computed: c7 M].** For $q\ge1$ let $E_q:=\{(u,c):u\in\mathrm{GF}(2)^q\text{ of weight 1 or 3},\ c\in\{0,1\}\}$, viewed as vectors of $\mathrm{GF}(2)^{q+1}$, so $|E_q|=2\binom q3+2q$, and $g_\star:=(0^q,1)$. The *hidden-state law* $\mathcal H_q$ on the $m=|E_q|+1$ base variables $(x_e)_{e\in E_q}$, $x_\star$ is
$$\mathcal H_q:=\{((z\cdot e)_{e\in E_q},\ z\cdot g_\star):\ z\in\mathrm{GF}(2)^{q+1}\},$$
a linear law: the observed quantities are fixed linear functionals of an unobserved state $z=(y,z_0)$. The vectors of $E_q$ span $\mathrm{GF}(2)^{q+1}$ (already $(e_v,0)$ and $(e_v,1)$, $v\le q$, do), so $\dim\mathcal H_q=q+1$ and the rank is $r=m-q-1$. With $z$ as $q+1$ auxiliary variables it is written as the $m$ sparse equations $x_{(u,c)}=u\cdot y+c\,z_0$, $x_\star=z_0$ (each of weight $\le5$), which $\mathrm{GE}$ uses directly. Then there are $\varepsilon>0$ and $q_0$ such that for $q\ge q_0$ every $\mathrm{UP}$-usable explicit theory $\Phi$ for $\mathcal H_q$, with any number of auxiliary variables, clauses of any width and any number of rounds, has
$$\|\Phi\|\ \ge\ 2^{q^{\varepsilon}/2-O(1)}-m\ =\ 2^{\Omega(m^{\varepsilon/3})}.$$

*Proof.* Antecedents $\alpha_S:=\{x_e=0:e\in S\}$, $S\subseteq E_q$, are consistent ($0\in\mathcal H_q$). On $\mathcal H_q$, $x_\star$ is determined by $x_S$ iff $g_\star\in\mathrm{span}(S)$ (if not, some $z$ has $z\cdot e=0$ on $S$ and $z\cdot g_\star=1$, and $z$, $0$ give two points that agree on $S$), iff the XOR system $\{u\cdot y=c:(u,c)\in S\}$ over $y\in\mathrm{GF}(2)^q$ is unsatisfiable (a subset of the equations sums to $0=1$, i.e. a subset of $S$ sums to $g_\star$). If determined, the value is that of the point 0, so usability gives "unit propagation derives $\neg x_\star$"; if not, soundness ($\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi=\mathcal H_q$) excludes any literal on $x_\star$. So the output for $\neg x_\star$ of the circuit of Lemma 3.5(a) with inputs $\mathcal I=\{\neg x_e:e\in E_q\}$ computes the monotone function $f_q(S)=[\text{the system }S\text{ is unsatisfiable}]$, with $O((\|\Phi\|+m)^2)$ gates (auxiliary variables that do not occur in $\Phi$ are dropped, so $\bar m\le\|\Phi\|+m$).

GKRS's function $3\mathrm{Xor\text{-}Sat}_q$ has one input for each ordered triple $(v_1,v_2,v_3)\in[q]^3$ and constant $c\in\{0,1\}$ (by the search excerpts of their ECCC report, $2q^3$ inputs), and accepts iff the selected constraints $y_{v_1}\oplus y_{v_2}\oplus y_{v_3}=c$ are unsatisfiable. Each input $(v_1,v_2,v_3,c)$ is the constraint $u\cdot y=c$ with $u=e_{v_1}+e_{v_2}+e_{v_3}$, of weight 1 (if two indices coincide) or 3, so $(u,c)\in E_q$; hence $3\mathrm{Xor\text{-}Sat}_q(Z)=f_q(\pi(Z))$, where $\pi(Z)_e$ is the OR of the inputs that map to $e$. A monotone circuit for $f_q$ plus at most $2q^3$ OR gates is a monotone circuit for $3\mathrm{Xor\text{-}Sat}_q$. (If instead GKRS's inputs are only triples of distinct variables, $3\mathrm{Xor\text{-}Sat}_q$ is $f_q$ with the weight-1 inputs set to 0, and a circuit for $f_q$ again gives one for it. Either way a lower bound for $3\mathrm{Xor\text{-}Sat}_q$ is one for $f_q$, up to $2q^3$ gates.) Göös, Kamath, Robere and Sokolov prove that $3\mathrm{Xor\text{-}Sat}_q$ needs monotone circuits of size $2^{q^{\varepsilon}}$ for some $\varepsilon>0$. Hence $O((\|\Phi\|+m)^2)+2q^3\ge2^{q^\varepsilon}$, and $m=\Theta(q^3)$. ∎

*Why the weight-1 functionals.* `notes.md` used weight-3 functionals only. Then its function is the *restriction* of $3\mathrm{Xor\text{-}Sat}_q$ that sets the inputs with repeated indices to 0, and a monotone lower bound does not pass to a restriction (referee A M7). With weight 1 and 3, GKRS's function is a projection of $f_q$ (or a restriction of it), which is the direction that transfers lower bounds. The check (c7 M): every one of the $2q^3$ ordered-triple inputs maps to a coordinate of $\mathcal H_q$ and every coordinate is hit ($q=3,\dots,6$); $\dim\mathcal H_q=q+1$ ($q=3,4$); "$x_\star$ determined", "system unsatisfiable" and "$g_\star\in\mathrm{span}(S)$" agree on all $2^{|E_q|}$ subsets ($q=3$: 256; $q=4$: 65,536); and unit propagation on $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal H_3)$ (256 clauses) derives $\neg x_\star$ from $\alpha_S$ exactly when the system is unsatisfiable, on all 256 inputs. Referee B checked the weight-3 version's reduction at $q=4,5$ (`refB_t3_aux` E).

**Remark 3.7 (Gwynne–Kullmann) [known: GK 2014, abstract level].** Gwynne and Kullmann ("On SAT representations of XOR constraints", LATA 2014, LNCS 8370, pp. 409–420; arXiv 1309.3060) show that XOR systems have no polynomial-size arc-consistent representation in general, even with auxiliary variables, by combining the monotone-circuit method of Bessiere et al. with Babai, Gál and Wigderson's function that has linear-size monotone span programs but needs superpolynomial monotone circuits. **Reading [assessment].**
* For one law, intermediate concepts (partial sums) turn an exponentially long law into a linear one and make it usable by a reasoner that can chain enough steps (Prop 3.1, Prop 3.8).
* For systems of laws, intermediate concepts do not rescue unit propagation: the hidden-state law is short *with* its natural intermediate concept (the hidden state $z$: $m$ equations of weight $\le5$), and Gaussian elimination uses that short theory directly; but no choice of intermediate concepts gives unit propagation a usable theory of polynomial size, at any depth. Private intermediate concepts add exactly nothing (Prop 3.3).
* So the brief's summary "intermediate concepts make one law linear-size and usable, but not, in general, a system of laws" is a theorem, modulo the cited monotone lower bound and with the depth qualification of §3.4. The simplex family is *not* an example of the "in general" clause: it is a system for which intermediate concepts do help, polynomially (Rem 3.2a).

### 3.4 Depth: intermediate concepts help only a reasoner that can chain through them

**Proposition 3.8 (rounds and size for one parity law) [(a), (b) proved and computed: c7 D, `refB_misc3_depth`; (c) proved, given Håstad 1986].** Let $\mathrm{Par}^b_m:=\{x:x_1\oplus\dots\oplus x_m=b\}$. For this law every question has $|I|=m-1$ (the only circuit is $1^m$).
(a) *Chain.* For $m\ge4$ the chain encoding (Prop 3.1) is $\mathrm{UP}_{m-1}$-usable and not $\mathrm{UP}_{m-2}$-usable.
(b) *Balanced tree.* Let $\mathcal T$ be a rooted binary tree of height $h:=\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ with the base variables as leaves; give every internal node other than the root an auxiliary variable $y_v$ with the constraint $y_v=y_{v'}\oplus y_{v''}$ for its children $v',v''$ (a leaf's "$y$" is its base variable), written as 4 clauses, and give the root the constraint $y_{v'}\oplus y_{v''}=b$, written as 2 clauses. This encoding has $4m-6$ clauses ($m\ge2$) and $m-2$ auxiliary variables and is $\mathrm{UP}_{2h-1}$-usable.
(c) *Fixed depth.* There is an absolute constant $c_H>0$ such that for every $k\ge1$ and every $m$ large enough given $k$, every $\mathrm{UP}_k$-usable theory $\Phi\in\mathcal C^{XY}$ for $\mathrm{Par}^b_m$ has
$$3\|\Phi\|+2m\ \ge\ 2^{c_H(m-1)^{1/(2k-1)}}/k .$$
More generally the same holds, with $m-1$ replaced by $w-1$, for every affine law with a circuit of weight $w$ (for the simplex family $w=(m+1)/2$).
(d) *Consequence.* If $\mathrm{UP}_k$-usable theories of size polynomial in $m$ exist for one parity law, then $k=\Omega(\log m/\log\log m)$; and $k=2\lceil\log_2m\rceil-1$ rounds suffice for $4m-6$ clauses.

*Proof.* (a) Write $C_1=(x_1,x_2,y_1)$, $C_i=(y_{i-1},x_{i+1},y_i)$ for $2\le i\le m-2$, and $C_{m-1}=(y_{m-2},x_m)$. Each constraint's clauses mention only its own variables and fire only when all but one of them are assigned. *Upper bound:* let $x_j$ be the unknown base variable and $C_{j'}$ its constraint ($j'=\max(1,j-1)$). The constraints to the left of $C_{j'}$ resolve from the left end ($C_1$ derives $y_1$ in round 1, $C_2$ derives $y_2$ in round 2, …) and those to the right from the right end ($C_{m-1}$ derives $y_{m-2}$ in round 1, …); $C_{j'}$ then has all but $x_j$ assigned by round $\max(j'-1,m-1-j')$ and derives $x_j$ one round later, by round $m-1$. *Lower bound:* for the question with $x_1$ unknown, after round $t\le m-2$ exactly the auxiliary variables $y_{m-2},\dots,y_{m-1-t}$ are assigned (by induction on $t$: $C_1$ has the two unknowns $x_1,y_1$ and each $C_i$, $2\le i\le m-2$, the two unknowns $y_{i-1},y_i$, until the boundary reaches it, so in each round exactly one constraint has a single unknown), so $x_1$ is derived in round $m-1$ and not before.
(b) Each constraint's clause set is its prime implicates, so it derives its third variable in the round after the other two are known (Lemma 1.10(a)). Let $x_j$ be the unknown leaf and $v_0,\dots,v_s=x_j$ the path from the root, $s\le h$; for $1\le i\le s$ let $w_i$ be the sibling of $v_i$. Every node $v$ not on the path has only known leaves below it, and by induction on its height $\mathrm{ht}(v)$ its value is derived by round $\mathrm{ht}(v)$; $w_i$ is at depth $i$, so $\mathrm{ht}(w_i)\le h-i$. The root constraint gives $y_{v_1}$ by round $\mathrm{ht}(w_1)+1\le h$. If $y_{v_i}$ is known by round $h+i-1$, the constraint at $v_i$ gives $y_{v_{i+1}}$ by round $\max(h+i-1,h-i-1)+1=h+i$. So $x_j$ is derived by round $h+s-1\le2h-1$. No conflict arises (the antecedent is consistent). Counting: $m-1$ internal nodes, of which $m-2$ non-root, each with 4 clauses, and the root with 2.
(c) Let $\Phi$ be $\mathrm{UP}_k$-usable; drop auxiliary variables that do not occur, so $\bar m\le m+\|\Phi\|$. Apply Lemma 3.5(b) with inputs the literals on $x_2,\dots,x_m$ (for a general affine law: on $\mathrm{supp}(u)\setminus\{j\}$ for a circuit $u$ of weight $w$ and some $j\in\mathrm{supp}(u)$). Every assignment to these variables is consistent and determines $x_1$ as $b$ plus their parity (Lemma 2.1), so by usability and soundness the output gate for the literal $x_1{=}1$ computes, on inputs that give each variable exactly one true literal, the parity of $m-1$ bits or its negation. This is a depth-$2k$ circuit with unbounded fan-in AND/OR gates over the literals of $m-1$ variables, with at most $k(\|\Phi\|+2\bar m)\le k(3\|\Phi\|+2m)$ gates. Håstad's theorem: every such circuit of depth $d\ge2$ computing parity of $n$ variables has at least $2^{c_Hn^{1/(d-1)}}$ gates, for $n$ large enough given $d$. With $n=m-1$, $d=2k$ the claim follows. (For $k=1$, Prop 3.1a gives the exact value $2^{m-1}$.)
(d) From (b) and (c): polynomial size $2^{O(\log m)}\ge2^{c_H(m-1)^{1/(2k-1)}}/k$ forces $(m-1)^{1/(2k-1)}=O(\log m)$, i.e. $2k-1\ge\log(m-1)/\log O(\log m)$. ∎

The checks (c7 D; all $m2^{m-1}$ questions, $m=3,\dots,10$): the chain needs exactly $m-1$ rounds for $m\ge4$ (for $m=3$ the encoding is the 4 direct clauses, 1 round); the balanced tree needs 1, 3, 4, 5, 5, 5, 6, 7 rounds for $m=3,\dots,10$, within $2\lceil\log_2m\rceil-1$ = 3, 3, 5, 5, 5, 5, 7, 7; both have $4m-6$ clauses for $m\ge4$ (for $m=3$ the script writes the 4 direct clauses instead of the 6-clause tree). Referee B's tree (a different split) needs at most 5 rounds for $m\le10$ (`refB_misc3_depth`). **Status of Håstad's theorem:** [known: Håstad, "Almost optimal lower bounds for small depth circuits", STOC 1986, pp. 6–20; bibliographic data confirmed by referee B's search; the depth-$d$ form $2^{\Omega(n^{1/(d-1)})}$ is the standard statement, checked by referee B at the level of secondary sources]. (c) is a short application of it, very probably folklore; referee B sketched it (Issue 2).

## 4. Directionality: the separation the brief asked for, and its limit (target T4)

**Definition 4.1.** A *directed rule* is $P\Rightarrow\ell$, $P$ a set of literals (the premise) and $\ell$ a literal on a variable that does not occur in $P$. (A "rule" whose conclusion's variable occurs in its premise is *trivial*: it corresponds to a tautological clause and is excluded, as tautological clauses are excluded from explicit theories; referee A m8, referee B Issue 5.) *Forward chaining* $\mathrm{FC}_k$ applies, in each of $k$ parallel rounds, every rule whose premise is satisfied by the current assignment (a conclusion whose opposite is assigned, or two opposite conclusions, give a conflict). A rule is sound for $\mathcal S$ if $P$ is consistent with $\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal S\wedge P\models\ell$; with auxiliary variables, the projection of a rule set is that of its clauses $\neg P\vee\ell$, required to be $\mathcal S$. A set of rules is *direction-complete* (usable) if forward chaining derives every entailed base literal from every consistent antecedent. A rule has written length $|P|+1$. A *rule base* is a set of sound directed rules over the base variables.

**Proposition 4.2 (directed rules against clauses) [proved; computed: c3 A–C, c7 F, `refB_t4_rules`, `refA_checks` G].**
(a) For a clause $C$ let $\mathrm{rules}(C):=\{\neg(C\setminus\{L\})\Rightarrow L:L\in C\}$. For every theory $\Phi$, $k$ and $\alpha$, $\mathrm{FC}_k$ with $\mathrm{rules}(\Phi)$ and $\mathrm{UP}_k$ with $\Phi$ derive the same literals (and conflict together). Conversely a rule $P\Rightarrow\ell$ is the clause $\neg P\vee\ell$, and unit propagation with the clause derives at least what forward chaining with the rule derives. Hence, for every law and every round budget $k$, with or without auxiliary variables: the smallest usable rule set has at least as many rules as the smallest usable clause set has clauses, and at most $w$ times as many, $w$ the largest clause width of a smallest usable clause set; in written length the factor is likewise at most $w$.
(b) *One parity law on $m\ge2$ variables, no auxiliary variables.* A sound rule concluding a literal on $x_j$ has all other $m-1$ variables in its premise. So there are exactly $m2^{m-1}$ sound rules, each question with $|I|=m-1$ needs its own rule, and the only usable rule base is the set of all of them: $m2^{m-1}$ rules of length $m$, against the unique minimum of $2^{m-1}$ clauses of length $m$ (Corollary 2.7(c) for $m\ge3$; Prop 2.9a with one class of two variables for $m=2$): **a factor $m$, the number of query directions, in count and in written length.** (If trivial rules were admitted they would add nothing, and the set of all non-trivial sound rules would be the unique *minimal* usable set.)
(c) *With auxiliary variables, for every law.* Splitting each clause $(l_1\vee\dots\vee l_w)$, $w\ge4$, into $(l_1\vee l_2\vee z_1),(\neg z_1\vee l_3\vee z_2),\dots,(\neg z_{w-3}\vee l_{w-1}\vee l_w)$ with fresh $z$'s preserves the projection and $\mathrm{UP}$-usability. So every law has a usable rule set (with unbounded rounds) of written length at most 9 times the least written length of a usable clausal theory with auxiliary variables (each clause of length $w$ becomes clauses of total length $\le3w$, each 3-clause becomes 3 rules of length 3). For the chain encoding of parity: $4m-6$ clauses against $3(4m-6)-2$ rules.
(d) *One round, any vocabulary.* For one parity law on $m\ge2$ variables, every $\mathrm{FC}_1$-usable set of sound rules, with any auxiliary variables, contains all $m2^{m-1}$ sound rules over the base variables, against the $2^{m-1}$ clauses that every $\mathrm{UP}_1$-usable theory with any auxiliary variables must contain (Prop 3.1a): again a factor $m$.

*Proof.* (a) Induction on rounds: a clause fires in round $t$ deriving $L$ exactly when the rule $\neg(C\setminus\{L\})\Rightarrow L$ has its premise satisfied after round $t-1$ and $L$ is not yet assigned (forward chaining signals a conflict when the conclusion's opposite is assigned, as unit propagation does when a clause is falsified). The bounds follow by translating a smallest usable theory in either direction. (b) If a sound rule $P\Rightarrow(x_j{=}v)$ had a premise missing some $x_i$, $i\ne j$, then by Lemma 2.1 $x_j$ would not be determined by the consistent premise $P$, so the rule would be unsound. So each sound rule is $\beta|_{[m]\setminus\{j\}}\Rightarrow(x_j{=}\text{its forced value})$, one for each $j$ and each of the $2^{m-1}$ assignments to the other variables, and the question with that antecedent is answered only by that rule. (c) Splitting: $\exists z$ of the pieces is the clause. If all literals but $l_t$ are falsified, propagation from the left derives $z_1,\dots,z_{t-2}$ and from the right $\neg z_{w-3},\dots,\neg z_{t-1}$, and the piece containing $l_t$ fires (the end cases are the same with one side empty). Every derivation of the original run is reproduced, with more rounds; soundness is unchanged. The length count is direct. (d) From an antecedent $\alpha$ on base variables, a rule fires in round 1 only if its premise is satisfied by $\alpha$, so its premise consists of base literals of $\alpha$; a rule answering the question with antecedent $\beta|_{[m]\setminus\{j\}}$ concludes a literal on $x_j$, and by (b) its premise is that whole antecedent. ∎

The checks: for $m=2,\dots,7$ the non-trivial sound rules number exactly $m2^{m-1}$, all with premises of size $m-1$ (c3 A, c7 F); counting trivial ones as well gives 12, 54, 216, 810, 2,916 for $m=2,\dots,6$ (c7 F, `refA_extra` H, `refB_t4_rules` A); for $m=3$ the only usable subset of the 12 non-trivial rules is the full set, and for $m\le5$ deleting any one rule breaks usability (c3 B, c7 F, `refB_t4_rules` A); forward chaining with $\mathrm{rules}(\Phi)$ equals $\mathrm{UP}_k$ with $\Phi$ in 16,000 random runs (c3) and in 20,000 runs, 8,378 with conflicts (`refB_t4_rules` B); splitting preserves usability in 20 of 20 random laws (`refA_checks` G), and the written-length ratios rules/PI are 5.4 and 6.0 for parity with $m=5,6$ (`refB_t4_rules` C).

**Theorem 4.3 (the brief's separation, and its limit) [proved].**
1. *A separation of the kind the brief asked for holds for rule bases without intermediate concepts, and for one round of reasoning with any vocabulary:* for one parity law with conditional questions in all $m$ directions, every direction-complete rule base needs exactly $m$ times the clauses of the declarative law, and the factor is the number of query directions (Prop 4.2(b), (d)).
2. *Not beyond that.* With intermediate concepts and unbounded rounds the factor is at most 9 in written length for every law (3 for the chain encoding of parity), and for every round budget it is at most the largest clause width of a smallest usable clausal theory (Prop 4.2(a), (c)). A program in the ordinary sense, with internal variables, answers all $m$ directions of parity with $O(m)$ operations (output $b$ plus the sum of the given bits), so for programs the number of directions costs nothing.
3. *It vanishes under program length* (Thm 1.11; for rule bases see Rem 4.4).
4. *The large separations are elsewhere:* between pairs (reasoner, vocabulary) (Cor 3.9): between reasoners with any vocabulary ($\mathrm{GE}$ against $\mathrm{UP}$ at any depth for the hidden-state law, Thm 3.6; $\mathrm{UP}_1$ or fixed-depth $\mathrm{UP}_k$ for one parity law, Props 3.1a, 3.8(c)), and between vocabularies for a reasoner deep enough to use them (Cor 2.7(c) against Prop 3.8(b): $2^{m-1}$ against $4m-6$ with $2\lceil\log_2m\rceil-1$ rounds).

(`notes.md` stated item 1 for "every hypothesis shaped like a program"; what is proved is the statement for rule bases over the base variables, and item 2's sentence on programs is new; referee A M6. Referee A proposed "for rule bases without intermediate concepts, and only there"; Prop 4.2(d) shows that the factor $m$ also survives intermediate concepts when the reasoner has a single round. Its item 4 cited the simplex family as a gap between reasoners; with intermediate concepts that gap is polynomial for unbounded propagation, Rem 3.2a; referee A M1, referee B Issues 1–2.)

**What this shows and does not show [assessment].** A family of the kind the brief looked for exists, and the dependence on the number of directions is exactly linear; but it is an artefact of forbidding intermediate concepts, and of comparing two tabular representations. A logical (clausal) theory buys multi-directional use only up to a constant factor over a rule base once intermediate concepts are allowed, and a program with internal variables pays nothing for directions. So "theories are better shaped like logical theories than like programs" does not get its support from directionality; what the formal setting supports is that a theory's usefulness depends on the reasoner it is paired with (its algebra and its depth) and on its vocabulary. It does not show that logic, rather than (say) probabilistic programs with conditioning, is the right formal home (the brief's caveat).

**Remark 4.4 (scout P's caveat; rule bases under program length; time bounds) [assessment, with the proved parts cited].**
* *Programs can contain solvers.* A program may consist of a generic solver applied to a declarative law at $O(1)$ extra length, so "programs lose" cannot be the statement; Theorem 1.11 is the precise form for reasoners: under program length, the weakest reasoner plus a compiler is as good as any. The separations above are about what a *fixed* weak reasoner needs *written*.
* *Rule bases under program length [proved].* Theorem 1.11 extends to rule bases: a fixed program maps a compatible rule set $R$ to its clauses $\{\neg P\vee\ell\}$, then to $\mathrm{PI}(\mathrm{proj})$, which is $\mathrm{UP}_1$-compatible (Lemma 1.10(a)), and conversely maps a $\mathrm{UP}_1$-compatible $\Phi$ to $\mathrm{rules}(\Phi)$, which is $\mathrm{FC}_1$-compatible (Prop 4.2(a)). So $K_A^{\mathrm{FC}_k}$ and $K_A^{\mathrm{UP}_1}$ agree within $c$ on every conditional data set (referee A m9).
* *Time bounds.* The translation of Prop 4.2(a) is round-for-round, so the directionality factor is the same under every round budget. A per-question time bound below that of elimination ($O(r^2m)$ bit operations for $\mathrm{GE}$ on $r$ equations) does not separate directed rules from clauses either: the clausal library $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$ and its rule translation both answer every question of the simplex family in one round. What a time bound does change is the reasoner–reasoner comparison: a reasoner that may not run elimination must be handed a lemma library, which over the base variables is exponentially larger (§2); if its time is charged at least linearly in the size of the theory it reads, it cannot even read that library. The cell-probe-type question (is there a polynomial-size theory answering every question of the simplex family in time below elimination, for some reasoner model?) is not addressed here [open].

## 5. Three homes of know-how (target T5)

**Proposition 5.1 (scout N2 Prop 2.8, extended) [proved, except the derivation size in (c), proof sketch; computed: c3 D, `refB_misc2` C].** For an affine law $\mathcal S=\{Ax=b\}$, the know-how to answer every question in $\mathcal Q(\mathcal S)$ can be paid for in any of the following places:
(a) **in the reasoner:** the $r$ equations with $\mathrm{GE}$ ($\ell_{\rm lst}=O(rm\log m)$ bits);
(b) **in explicit lemmas:** for $\mathrm{UP}$ over explicit theories, exactly $F_{\rm ind}(\mathcal S)$ without intermediate concepts when $d_*\ge3$ (Theorem 2.6; exponential for the simplex family), and a library such as $\mathrm{Circ}_{\rm aux}$ with them (Prop 3.2; polynomial for the simplex family, Rem 3.2a; exponential for some systems whatever the intermediate concepts, Theorem 3.6, and for one parity law at any fixed depth, Prop 3.8(c));
(c) **in uncharged computation:** either a *membership procedure plus free search for derivations*: for a suitable polynomial $q$, the axiom set $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$ has a decider of length $K^{q}(A,b)+c$ running in time polynomial in $m$, and every question has a derivation from a single axiom instance, of size polynomial in $m$; or a *generator* of the explicit theory under a program-length prior (Theorem 1.11: the compiler $\Phi'\mapsto\mathrm{PI}(\mathrm{proj}\,\Phi')$ costs $O(1)$ bits). Here $K^{q}(A,b)\le rm+r+O(\log m)$ (print the literal matrix and vector), and $K^q(A,b)=O(\log m)$ for one parity law and for the simplex family.

*Proof of (c), first form.* A clause $C=C_{Z,\beta}$ is in $\mathrm{PI}(\mathcal S)$ iff the derived equations supported in $Z$ are exactly $\{0,1_Z\}$ and $\beta$ violates $1_Z$ (Prop 2.2; a circuit is the unique nonzero derived equation supported in its support). The derived equations supported in $Z$ are $yA$ for $y$ in the kernel of the columns of $A$ outside $Z$, computable by Gaussian elimination in time polynomial in $m$, once $(A,b)$ is available; a program of length $K^q(A,b)$ produces $(A,b)$ within $q(rm+m)$ steps. (`notes.md` wrote "length $K(A,b)+c$", which does not give a time bound; referee A M2.) **Reading [assessment].** The know-how to answer with bounded effort is paid for in the reasoner, in written lemmas, or in a computation that the cost model does not charge. Charged deduction measures the length of a membership test, not the length of what a reader must be handed; the membership test for the full library runs Gaussian elimination, so the know-how lives in the decider (home (c)). This locates the AI-paper's short-derivation results: on literal sequences, under (F1), at polynomial budgets with degrees charged, charged-deduction SAI corresponds to certificate SOI, whose fit class under total material is $\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP}$ (`sfi` Thm 8.1, Cor 8.2); the nondeterminism *is* the free search (`sfi` Rem 8.5). Those theorems model a reasoner that is handed the derivation, so they cannot express the textbook point, which is about a reasoner that must find the answer. A deterministic bounded reasoner with a cost on the written theory can express it (§1.4, §2–§3).

## 6. Learning know-how pattern by pattern (target T6)

**Setting.** $m\ge3$, the parity law $\mathrm{Par}_m:=\mathrm{Par}^0_m=\{x:x_1\oplus\dots\oplus x_m=0\}$ (the constant 1 is symmetric). The questions with $|I|=m-1$ form $\mathcal Q_{m-1}$, which is all of $\mathcal Q(\mathrm{Par}_m)$; write a question as $q=(j,\gamma)$ with $\gamma\in\mathrm{Par}_m$ (its *positive point*), antecedent $\alpha(q)=\gamma|_{[m]\setminus\{j\}}$ and answer $x_j=\gamma_j$. Its *pattern* is the violating point $\pi(q):=\gamma\oplus e_j\in\mathcal P_m:=\{0,1\}^m\setminus\mathrm{Par}_m$. So $|\mathcal Q_{m-1}|=m2^{m-1}$, $|\mathcal P_m|=2^{m-1}$, and every pattern and every positive point carries exactly $m$ questions. For data $D\subseteq\mathcal Q_{m-1}$, $\pi(D)$ is the set of *seen* patterns. Hypotheses without auxiliary variables are explicit theories over $X$; with the listing prior (Def 1.6) each full-width clause $C_\beta$ costs $\mathrm{len}_m:=\ell_{\mathbb N}(m)+m(\lceil\log_2m\rceil+1)$ bits. "Fails on $q$" means: errs, abstains, or is inconsistent with $\alpha(q)$.

**Lemma 6.1 (answers on $\mathcal Q_{m-1}$) [proved; computed: `refA_checks` F, `refB_t6_learning` A].** For an explicit theory $\Phi$ over $X$ and $q=(j,\gamma)$, the following are equivalent: (i) $\Phi$ is $\mathrm{UP}$-compatible with $q$; (ii) $\Phi$ answers $q$ correctly (unit propagation derives exactly $x_j=\gamma_j$, without conflict); (iii) $\gamma$ satisfies $\Phi$, and some clause of $\Phi$ containing $x_j$ is falsified by the pattern $\pi(q)$.

*Proof.* Only $x_j$ is unassigned, so unit propagation either finds a clause falsified by $\alpha$ (conflict), or derives values of $x_j$ from clauses whose other literals are falsified by $\alpha$. A clause containing $x_j$ with all other literals falsified by $\alpha$ derives $x_j=\gamma_j$ iff it is falsified by $\pi(q)$, and derives $x_j=1-\gamma_j$ iff it is falsified by $\gamma$; a clause without $x_j$ falsified by $\alpha$ is falsified by $\gamma$. So (iii) gives a derivation of the answer and no conflict, i.e. (ii), and $\gamma$ witnesses satisfiability, i.e. (i). If (i), the satisfying completion of $\alpha$ is $\gamma$ (the clause that derives $x_j=\gamma_j$ excludes $\pi(q)$), which gives (iii). (ii)⇒(iii): a correct derivation needs a clause falsified by $\pi(q)$; a clause falsified by $\gamma$ would cause a conflict or a wrong derivation. ∎

**Theorem 6.2 (a weak reasoner learns its know-how pattern by pattern) [proved; computed: c5, c7 L, `refB_t6_learning`].**
(a) *Full correctness.* An explicit theory over $X$ answers every question of $\mathcal Q_{m-1}$ correctly iff it is exactly $\mathrm{PI}(\mathrm{Par}_m)=\{C_\beta:\beta\in\mathcal P_m\}$.
(b) *Minimal theories, any order.* Let $D\subseteq\mathcal Q_{m-1}$ and let $\Phi$ be an inclusion-minimal explicit theory over $X$ that is $\mathrm{UP}$-compatible with $D$ (for instance any $\ell_{\rm lst}$-shortest one, Lemma 1.7). Then for every unseen pattern $\beta\notin\pi(D)$, $\Phi$ fails on some question of pattern $\beta$. Hence $\Phi$ fails on at least $|\mathcal P_m\setminus\pi(D)|$ questions, and a shortest compatible theory is fully correct only after all $2^{m-1}$ patterns have been seen.
(c) *Random order (coupon collector).* If the questions are drawn independently and uniformly from $\mathcal Q_{m-1}$, the patterns are i.i.d. uniform on $\mathcal P_m$; after $T$ questions the expected number of unseen patterns is $2^{m-1}(1-2^{1-m})^T$, and the expected time until all have been seen is $2^{m-1}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{m-1}}1/i=2^{m-1}\big((m-1)\ln2+0.5772\ldots\big)+\tfrac12+O(2^{-m})$. (`notes.md` omitted the $+\tfrac12$; referee B Issue 10.)
(d) *Bayes, block-complete data (semimeasure convention).* If $D$ contains all $m$ questions of each seen pattern, every $\mathrm{UP}$-compatible explicit theory over $X$ contains $\{C_\beta:\beta\in\pi(D)\}$, and $-\log_2W_{\rm lst}^{\mathrm{UP},X}(D)\ge\sigma\,\mathrm{len}_m$ with $\sigma:=|\pi(D)|$. So the cumulative loss is at least $\sigma\,\mathrm{len}_m-c$ bits: about $m\log_2m$ bits per pattern, $2^{m-1}\mathrm{len}_m$ in all.
(e) *With the reasoner or with intermediate concepts.* For every $D\subseteq\mathcal Q(\mathrm{Par}_m)$: $-\log_2W_{\rm lst}^{\mathrm{GE}}(D)\le\ell_{\rm lst}(\text{the one equation})=m\lceil\log_2m\rceil+O(\log m)$, and $-\log_2W_{\rm lst}^{\mathrm{UP}}(D)\le\ell_{\rm lst}(\text{chain encoding})=O(m\log m)$ (Prop 3.1). So these inducers pay $O(m\log m)$ bits in total on every question sequence.
(f) *Other measures.* Under program length there is no gap: $K_A^{\mathrm{UP},X}(\mathcal Q(\mathrm{Par}_m))=K(m)\pm c$ (Thm 1.12(c)). Under time charging, $Kt_A^{\mathrm{UP},X}(\mathcal Q(\mathrm{Par}_m))\ge\log_2(m2^{m-1})=m-1+\log_2m$ (Thm 1.12(b); the unique usable theory over $X$ has $2^{m-1}$ clauses of width $m$), while $Kt_A^{\mathrm{UP}}(\mathcal Q(\mathrm{Par}_m))$ and $Kt_A^{\mathrm{GE}}(\mathcal Q(\mathrm{Par}_m))$ are $O(\log m)$ (the chain encoding and the equation are printed from $m$ in polynomial time). (`notes.md` stated the $Kt$ gap as $m+O(\log m)$ without the class; with intermediate concepts it is $O(\log m)$; referee A M1, m11.)

*Proof.* (a) By Lemma 6.1(iii) for all questions: every point of $\mathrm{Par}_m$ satisfies $\Phi$, so every clause is an implicate, of width $m$ (Prop 2.3, $d_*=m$), i.e. some $C_\beta$; and every pattern $\beta$ needs a clause falsified by it, which among width-$m$ clauses is $C_\beta$ itself.
(b) $C_\beta$ is falsified only by $\beta$, so by Lemma 6.1 it serves only questions of pattern $\beta$; for unseen $\beta$, removing it keeps compatibility, so by minimality $C_\beta\notin\Phi$. If for some $j$ no clause of $\Phi$ containing $x_j$ is falsified by $\beta$, $\Phi$ fails on $(j,\beta\oplus e_j)$. Otherwise take a clause $C\in\Phi$ falsified by $\beta$; $C\ne C_\beta$, so $|C|<m$; take $i\notin\mathrm{supp}(C)$. The question $(i,\beta\oplus e_i)$ has pattern $\beta$, and its positive point $\beta\oplus e_i$ agrees with $\beta$ on $\mathrm{supp}(C)$, so it falsifies $C$, and $\Phi$ fails on it (Lemma 6.1). Distinct patterns give distinct questions.
(c) Each pattern carries $m$ of the $m2^{m-1}$ questions; the rest is the coupon collector, with $\sum_{i\le n}1/i=\ln n+0.5772\ldots+\frac1{2n}+O(n^{-2})$.
(d) For a seen pattern $\beta$, every $\beta\oplus e_i$ is a seen positive point, so every clause of a compatible $\Phi$ is satisfied by all of them (Lemma 6.1); a clause falsified by $\beta$ of width $<m$ is falsified by $\beta\oplus e_i$ for $i$ outside its support; so the only admissible clause falsified by $\beta$ is $C_\beta$, and Lemma 6.1 requires one. With $R:=\{C_\beta:\beta\in\pi(D)\}$: summing the listing weights of all theories over $X$ containing $R$ (here $m_Y=0$), $\sum_s2^{-\ell_{\mathbb N}(s)}\,2^{-\sigma\,\mathrm{len}_m}\sum_{|\Psi|=s-\sigma}\prod_{C\in\Psi}2^{-\ell(C)}\le2^{-\sigma\,\mathrm{len}_m}\sum_s2^{-\ell_{\mathbb N}(s)}/(s-\sigma)!\le2^{-\sigma\,\mathrm{len}_m}$ (as in Lemma 1.7). The cumulative loss in the semimeasure convention is $-\log W(D)+\log W(\emptyset)$, and $W(\emptyset)\ge2^{-c}$.
(e) The single equation (resp. the chain encoding) is compatible with every $D\subseteq\mathcal Q(\mathrm{Par}_m)$, so $W\ge2^{-\ell_{\rm lst}}$; the listing lengths are direct.
(f) Theorems 1.11 and 1.12. ∎

**What this shows and does not show [assessment].** With a cost on the written theory, a unit-propagation reasoner without intermediate concepts must acquire its know-how pattern by pattern: each of the $2^{m-1}$ value patterns has to be met (and, in the Bayes form on block-complete data, paid for with about $m\log_2m$ bits) before the inducer answers it, while an inducer with an algebraic reasoner, or with intermediate concepts and unbounded propagation, learns the whole law after $O(m\log m)$ bits. This is specific to the listing (or time-charged, without intermediate concepts) prior: under program length the same inducer learns the generator of the lemma library at once (Theorem 6.2(f)). We have not stated a learning theorem for a fixed number of rounds with intermediate concepts, where Prop 3.8(c) makes every usable theory superpolynomial, nor for systems of laws, where Theorem 3.6 does so for every depth (§13).

## 7. Depth against size: lemma libraries for a bounded-depth reasoner (target T7)

**Setting.** The *chain law* $\mathrm{Ch}_m:=\{x:x_1\le x_2\le\dots\le x_m\}$ ($x_i\to x_{i+1}$), $m\ge2$. The reasoner $\mathrm{UP}_k$ has $k$ rounds. A *$k$-hop TC-spanner* of the directed path $P_m$ is a set $E$ of pairs $(i,j)$, $i<j$, such that for all $i<j$ there is a path from $i$ to $j$ in $E$ with at most $k$ edges; $s_k(m)$ is the least $|E|$.

**Theorem 7.1 (the reduction) [proved; computed: c6 A, c7 B, `refB_t7_spanners` A; the converse of (iii) refuted, counterexamples kept].**
(i) The prime implicates of $\mathrm{Ch}_m$ are exactly the clauses $\neg x_i\vee x_j$, $i<j$.
(ii) A 2-CNF of prime implicates, with edge set $E$, is $\mathrm{UP}_k$-usable iff $E$ is a $k$-hop TC-spanner of $P_m$.
(iii) If an explicit theory $\Phi$ over $X$, with clauses of any width, is $\mathrm{UP}_k$-usable, then for *every* choice of a prime implicate contained in each clause, the chosen edges form a $k$-hop TC-spanner of $P_m$.
(iv) Hence the least number of clauses of a $\mathrm{UP}_k$-usable theory over $X$ is exactly $s_k(m)$, for every $k$.

**[refuted] the converse of (iii).** `notes.md` stated "usable iff, after replacing each clause by a prime implicate it contains, its edges form a $k$-hop TC-spanner". This is false for wide clauses, under the existential reading ("some replacement") and under the universal reading ("every replacement"), and also when the replacement is forced (referee A M5, referee B Issue 3). Counterexamples (each clause is an implicate; $F$ is not usable; c7 B re-checks all four):
* $m=3$, $k=1$: $\{\neg x_1\vee x_2,\ \neg x_2\vee x_3,\ \neg x_1\vee\neg x_2\vee x_3\}$: choosing $\neg x_1\vee x_3$ inside the wide clause gives the 1-hop spanner $\{12,23,13\}$, but from $x_1=1$ one round does not derive $x_3$ (referee A).
* $m=4$, $k=2$: $\{\neg x_1\vee x_2,\ \neg x_2\vee x_3,\ \neg x_3\vee x_4,\ x_1\vee\neg x_2\vee x_4\}$: the wide clause contains only $\neg x_2\vee x_4$, the replaced edges $\{12,23,34,24\}$ form a 2-hop spanner, but from $x_4=0$ two rounds do not derive $x_1=0$ (referee A).
* $m=3$, $k=1$: $\{\neg x_1\vee x_2,\ \neg x_1\vee x_3,\ \neg x_2\vee x_1\vee x_3\}$: forced replacement $\neg x_2\vee x_3$ gives $\{12,13,23\}$, but from $x_3=0$ one round does not derive $x_2=0$ (referee B).
* $m=4$, $k=2$: $\{\neg x_1\vee x_2,\ \neg x_1\vee x_3,\ \neg x_3\vee x_4,\ \neg x_2\vee x_1\vee x_3\}$: forced replacement gives the 2-hop spanner $\{12,13,23,34\}$, but from $x_4=0$ two rounds do not derive $x_2=0$ (referee B).
The mechanism: a wide clause propagates in fewer situations than the edge it contains (it needs its extra literals falsified too).

*Proof.* (i) A partial assignment is consistent with $\mathrm{Ch}_m$ iff it sets no $x_i=1$, $x_j=0$ with $i<j$; so a clause is an implicate iff it contains some $\neg x_i\vee x_j$, $i<j$, and these 2-clauses are the prime implicates. (ii) For a set $E$ of edges, $\mathrm{UP}_k$ from a consistent $\alpha$ derives $x_j=1$ iff some $x_i=1$ in $\alpha$ has a path to $j$ of at most $k$ edges, and $x_i=0$ iff some $x_j=0$ in $\alpha$ is reached from $i$ within $k$ edges (the same clauses read contrapositively); an entailed literal is entailed by a single literal of $\alpha$. So usability is the spanner property (take $\alpha=\{x_i=1\}$ for necessity). (iii) Lemma 2.4 holds for every law (its proof uses only that the clauses are implicates and the antecedent is consistent): the replaced theory derives, round for round, at least what $\Phi$ derives, so it is $\mathrm{UP}_k$-usable, and it is a 2-CNF of prime implicates, so (ii) applies (two clauses may map to the same edge). (iv) By (iii) a usable $\Phi$ has $|\Phi|\ge|E|\ge s_k(m)$; a minimum spanner written as a 2-CNF is usable by (ii). ∎

The checks: 600 random 2-CNFs of prime implicates ($m=4$–$6$, $k=1$–$3$): usable iff spanner in all 600; 60 random usable theories with wide clauses, 10 random replacements each: every replacement a spanner (c7 B). Referee A: of 400 random implicate theories at $m=4$, 46 are not usable although every replacement is a spanner, and none is usable with a non-spanner replacement (`refA_checks` B); referee B: 19 usable wide theories, 5 replacements each, all spanners (`refB_t7_spanners` A). (The development-round check c6 A tested only the direction (iii), which is why it did not see the failure of the converse.)

**Proposition 7.1a (the midpoint construction, exactly) [proved; computed: c6 C, `refB_t7_spanners` C].** The midpoint recursion ($S(1)=0$, $S(n)=n-1+S(\lceil\frac{n-1}2\rceil)+S(\lfloor\frac{n-1}2\rfloor)$) gives $s_2(m)\le S(m)=\sum_{i=1}^m\lfloor\log_2i\rfloor=(m+1)L-2^{L+1}+2$, $L:=\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor$.
*Proof.* $\Sigma(m):=\sum_{i\le m}\lfloor\log_2i\rfloor$ satisfies the same recursion: splitting $i=2j$ and $i=2j+1$ ($i\ge2$), $\lfloor\log_2 i\rfloor=1+\lfloor\log_2j\rfloor$, so $\Sigma(m)=(m-1)+\Sigma(\lfloor m/2\rfloor)+\Sigma(\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor)$, and $\lfloor m/2\rfloor=\lceil(m-1)/2\rceil$; the closed form is standard. ∎ (Recursion, sum and closed form agree for all $m\le3000$: `refB_t7_spanners` C.)

**Conjecture 7.1b.** $s_2(m)=\sum_{i=1}^m\lfloor\log_2i\rfloor$ for every $m$ (true for $m\le18$: c6 B for $m\le16$, referee B for $m=17,18$). The best lower bound proved here is Theorem 7.2's $\approx\frac12m\log_2m$, which holds even with auxiliary variables.

**Theorem 7.2 (intermediate concepts and wide clauses gain at most a factor about 2 at depth 2) [proved; ingredients computed: c4 E; `refB_t7_spanners` D].** Let $m\ge4$. Every $\mathrm{UP}_2$-usable explicit theory $\Phi$ for $\mathrm{Ch}_m$, with any auxiliary variables and clauses of any width, has
$$|\Phi|\ \ge\ \tfrac12\big((m-2)\log_2\tfrac{m-2}{2}-m\big),$$
(an asymptotic bound: it is positive only from $m=8$ on, 11.7 at $m=16$ against $s_2(16)=38$), while $s_2(m)\le m\log_2m$ (midpoint recursion: a hub vertex joined to all others, then recursion on both sides, $S(n)=n-1+S(a)+S(b)$ with $a+b=n-1$, $a,b\le n/2$, so by induction $S(n)\le n-1+(n-1)\log_2(n/2)\le n\log_2n$). So at depth 2 the least usable theory has $\Theta(m\log m)$ clauses with or without intermediate concepts, and they save at most a factor about 2.

*Proof.* *Simplification.* Let $U$ be the literals derived by unit propagation from $\Phi$ alone; they are auxiliary ($\mathrm{Ch}_m$ fixes no base variable). Simplifying $\Phi$ by $U$ (delete satisfied clauses and falsified literals) gives $\Phi'$ with $|\Phi'|\le|\Phi|$, the same projection, no unit clauses, and every $\mathrm{UP}_2$ derivation of $\Phi$ outside $U$ made by $\Phi'$ no later (induction on rounds: a clause of $\Phi$ that fires contains no literal of $U$, as that literal would be falsified, i.e. its negation derived, contradicting soundness; its simplification fires in $\Phi'$). So assume no unit clauses.

*Single sources.* For a base index $v$ let $N(v):=\{x_v\}\cup\{L:(\neg x_v\vee L)\in\Phi\}$, the literals known after one round from $\alpha=\{x_v=1\}$. For a clause $C$ containing $x_j$ let $P(C,j):=\{\neg L':L'\in C\setminus\{x_j\}\}$, its premises for deriving $x_j$. From $\{x_u=1\}$, $\mathrm{UP}_2$ derives $x_j$ iff some $C\ni x_j$ has $P(C,j)\subseteq N(u)$ (round 1 is the case $C=\neg x_u\vee x_j$). Say $C$ *serves* $u$ for $j$. Soundness: if $C$ serves $u$ for $j$ then $u\le j$. A clause serving any source for $j$ contains no other positive base literal $x_w$ (its premise $\neg x_w$ would have to follow from a single $x_u=1$, which is unsound). Let $c(j)$ be the number of clauses containing $x_j$ that serve some $u<j$; then $\sum_jc(j)\le|\Phi|$, and $\sum_v|N(v)|\le m+|\Phi|$ (a 2-clause $\neg x_v\vee L$ is counted only for $v$; a 2-clause with two negative base literals is unsound).

*Random experiment.* Give every literal $M$ an independent fair coin, *left* or *right*. Say $v$ is *source-alive* if all of $N(v)$ is right, and $j$ is *target-alive* if every clause containing $x_j$ that serves some $u<j$ has a left premise. Then no $u<j$ has $u$ source-alive and $j$ target-alive: by usability some $C$ serves $u$ for $j$, all its premises lie in $N(u)$ and are right.

*Pairing.* For $a=1,\dots,A':=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ let $\mathcal E_a$: $2a$ source-alive and $2a-1$ target-alive. For $a<a'$ we have $2a<2a'-1$, so at most one $\mathcal E_a$ occurs and $\sum_a\Pr[\mathcal E_a]\le1$. Given that $N(2a)$ is all right, a clause serving some $u<2a-1$ for $2a-1$ cannot have all its premises in $N(2a)$ (it would serve $2a$ for $2a-1$, unsound), so it is blocked by a left coin outside $N(2a)$ with probability at least $1/2$, independently of $N(2a)$'s coins; these blocking events are increasing in the set of left coins, so by Harris's inequality they occur together with probability at least $2^{-c(2a-1)}$. Hence $\Pr[\mathcal E_a]\ge2^{-|N(2a)|-c(2a-1)}$, and by convexity of $2^{-x}$, $\sum_a(|N(2a)|+c(2a-1))\ge A'\log_2A'$. The shifted pairing ($2a+1$ source-alive, $2a$ target-alive, $a\le A'':=\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor$; $a<a'$ gives $2a+1<2a'$) gives $\sum_a(|N(2a+1)|+c(2a))\ge A''\log_2A''$. Adding, $m+2|\Phi|\ge\sum_v|N(v)|+\sum_jc(j)\ge(m-2)\log_2\frac{m-2}2$. ∎

This is Hansel's lemma (covering $K_n$ by bicliques needs total size $\ge n\log_2n$; known: Hansel 1964, Katona–Szemerédi 1967, via secondary sources) adapted to ordered pairs and to conjunctive premises; the pairing trick handles the diagonal, and Harris's inequality handles clauses with several premises.

*Relation to known Steiner TC-spanner bounds [known: abstract level, from referee A's search; the dimension-1 specialisation and its constant not checked].* For **2-CNFs** with auxiliary variables the order of Theorem 7.2 is implied by known results: after the simplification step, a $\mathrm{UP}_2$-usable 2-CNF over $X\cup Y$ gives, through its implication graph (edges $\neg a\to b$, $\neg b\to a$ per clause $a\vee b$), a Steiner 2-TC-spanner of the path with the positive base literals as original vertices, all other literals as Steiner vertices and at most $2|\Phi|$ edges (soundness excludes false reachability between original vertices; usability gives the 2-hop paths). Berman, Bhattacharyya, Grigorescu, Raskhodnikova, Woodruff and Yaroslavtsev (Combinatorica 34(3) (2014) 255–277; ICALP 2011; arXiv 1011.6100) prove, by their abstract, a nearly tight lower bound on the size of Steiner 2-TC-spanners of $d$-dimensional directed hypergrids; for $d=1$ (the path) this gives $\Omega(m\log m)$. What Theorem 7.2 adds is the case of wide clauses (conjunctive premises, which are AND-gates, not graph edges), handled by the Harris step, and the explicit constant $\frac12$ (referee A M8). Referee B's recursive Steiner (auxiliary-hub) constructions at depth 2 are usable and never smaller than $s_2(m)$ for $m=4,\dots,14$ (e.g. 48 against 31 at $m=14$), consistent with the bound.

**Proposition 7.3 (every fixed depth: superlinear with intermediate concepts) [proved, given Chandra–Fortune–Lipton 1983 (known: abstract level; the depth correspondence not checked)].** For every fixed $k$ and every bound $w$ on clause width, a $\mathrm{UP}_k$-usable theory $\Phi$ for $\mathrm{Ch}_m$ with auxiliary variables has $\|\Phi\|\ge m\cdot\omega(1)$.

*Proof.* On multi-source antecedents $\alpha_{A}=\{x_i=1:i\in A\}$ (all consistent), soundness and usability give: $\mathrm{UP}_k$ derives $x_j$ iff $A\cap[1,j]\ne\emptyset$. The circuit of Lemma 3.5(b) (inputs the literals $x_i{=}1$) is a monotone circuit of depth $2k$ with at most $k(w\|\Phi\|+2\bar m)$ **wires** computing all prefix-ORs, and $\bar m\le m+\|\Phi\|$. Chandra, Fortune and Lipton ("Lower bounds for constant depth circuits for prefix problems", ICALP 1983, LNCS 154, doi 10.1007/BFb0036901) prove that monotone unbounded fan-in circuits of depth $O(d)$ computing prefix-OR need $\Omega(nf_d^{-1}(n))$ wires for an increasing hierarchy $f_d$ of primitive recursive functions, superlinear for every fixed depth. (Size must be counted in wires: with gates counted, depth-1 prefix-OR needs only $n$ gates; referee A m13.) Hence $(w+2)k\|\Phi\|+2km\ge m\cdot\omega(1)$. ∎

For 2-CNFs, an alternative route avoids the unverified depth correspondence: the implication graph is a Steiner $k$-TC-spanner of the path, and Berman et al. also state lower bounds for Steiner $k$-TC-spanners of low-dimensional posets (abstract level; not checked for $d=1$).

**Reading [assessment].** This is the brief's "trade-off between the compression of axioms and their usability" as an exact curve for one natural law: the written theory a reasoner with depth budget $k$ needs has $s_k(m)=\Theta(m\,\mathrm{ia}_k(m))$ clauses, from $\binom m2$ at $k=1$ (every consequence written down) through $\Theta(m\log m)$ at $k=2$ and $\Theta(m\log\log m)$ at $k=3$ down to the $m-1$ axioms of the law itself at $k\ge m-1$. The lemmas the shallow reasoner needs are "shortcut" implications $x_i\to x_j$ through hub variables; intermediate concepts (auxiliary hubs) and wider clauses do not change the order at depth 2 (Theorem 7.2), and do not make the size linear at any fixed depth with bounded width (Prop 7.3). Together with Prop 3.8 this gives one picture: depth and written size trade off, and new vocabulary moves the trade-off only for a reasoner that can chain through it.

## 8. Lemmas for bounded proof search in general (target T8)

**Known [known: Statman 1979, PAMS 75(1):104–107 (bibliographic data verified by scout L); Orevkov, J. Soviet Math. 20 (1982) 2337–2350, translation of Zap. Nauchn. Sem. LOMI 88 (1979) 137–161 (verified by search in the development round; content from the abstract)].** There are sequences of first-order sentences $C_k$ with $\mathsf{LK}$ proofs of size linear in $k$, using cuts, whose cut-free proofs have size not bounded by any Kalmár-elementary function of $k$ (towers of exponentials of height growing with $k$). Cut-free proofs correspond to analytic, lemma-free reasoning; cut formulas are lemmas.

**Speed-up by added axioms and stronger systems [known: bibliographic data from scout L's verified `refs.bib`; content at the level of scout L's summaries].** Gödel, "Über die Länge von Beweisen" (Ergebnisse eines mathematischen Kolloquiums 7 (1936) 23–24): passing to a stronger system shortens some proofs by more than any function computable in the weaker one. Parikh, "Some results on the length of proofs" (Trans. AMS 177 (1973) 29–36). Pudlák, "The lengths of proofs" (Handbook of Proof Theory, ed. Buss, 1998, ch. VIII). Bienvenu, Romashchenko, Shen, Taveneaux, Vermeeren, "The axiomatic power of Kolmogorov complexity" (Ann. Pure Appl. Logic 165(9) (2014) 1380–1402): adding random true axioms "$C(x)>n$" to PA proves no new interesting theorems in the probabilistic sense but can shorten proofs unless NP = PSPACE. (Added at referee A's request, m15; scout L §5.1 named these for this seed.)

**Proposition 8.1 (valid lemma sentences restore short analytic proofs) [proof sketch].** Let $\pi$ be an $\mathsf{LK}$ proof of $T_0\vdash\varphi$, $T_0\subseteq T$ finite, with cuts numbered $1,\dots,n_{\rm cut}$, the $i$-th with left premise $\Gamma_i\vdash\Delta_i,A_i$. Let $L_i:=\forall\vec v\,(\bigwedge\Gamma_i\to\bigvee\Delta_i\vee A_i)$, the universal closure over the free variables. Then (a) each $L_i$ is valid (the sequent $\Gamma_i\vdash\Delta_i,A_i$ is derivable and $\mathsf{LK}$ is sound), so $T\cup\{L_1,\dots,L_{n_{\rm cut}}\}$ is deductively equivalent to $T$; and (b) $L_1,\dots,L_{n_{\rm cut}},T_0\vdash\varphi$ has a cut-free proof of size polynomial in $|\pi|$.
*Sketch.* Replace the $i$-th cut, with premises $\Gamma\vdash\Delta,A$ and $A,\Gamma'\vdash\Delta'$, as follows: from the right premise and identity sequents $\delta\vdash\delta$ ($\delta\in\Delta$), $\vee$-left gives $\bigvee\Delta\vee A,\Gamma'\vdash\Delta,\Delta'$; from identity sequents $\gamma\vdash\gamma$, $\wedge$-right gives $\Gamma\vdash\bigwedge\Gamma$; $\to$-left gives $\bigwedge\Gamma\to\bigvee\Delta\vee A,\Gamma,\Gamma'\vdash\Delta,\Delta'$; $\forall$-left steps (instantiating the bound variables by the free variables of the cut's premises) give $L_i,\Gamma,\Gamma'\vdash\Delta,\Delta'$. The left premise's subproof is dropped. Carry $L_i$ down to the end sequent (weakening where branches meet, contraction for duplicates). $L_i$ is closed, so no eigenvariable condition below the former cut is affected. Identity sequents for composite formulas have cut-free proofs of size polynomial in the formula. *Not written out:* the bookkeeping of contexts in the chosen sequent calculus.

(`notes.md` used the closures $\forall\vec xA_i$ of the cut formulas themselves. A cut formula can be arbitrary, for instance $\bot$ in a proof of $P\to P$ that cuts on $\bot$ with both premises obtained by weakening, so those closures need not follow from $T$ and can make it inconsistent; the reading "the same theory with its know-how written out" was then wrong. Referee A M4. Alternatively, if every cut's left premise has the form $T_i\vdash A_i$ with $T_i\subseteq T$, the closures $\forall\vec xA_i$ are theorems of $T$ and serve directly.)

**Proposition 8.2 (when a lemma pays) [proved, elementary].** Under any objective of the form $J(T)=\ell(T)+\sum_{\varphi\in D}\rho_T(\varphi)$, with $\ell$ additive over axioms and $\rho_T(\varphi)$ the cost of the cheapest derivation of the datum $\varphi$ from $T$ (non-increasing when axioms are added), adding a theorem $L$ of $T$ as an axiom lowers $J$ iff $\sum_{\varphi}(\rho_T(\varphi)-\rho_{T\cup\{L\}}(\varphi))>\ell(L)$: a lemma enters iff the derivation cost it saves over the data exceeds its own length. (Immediate from additivity.) This is the general form of the AI-paper's observation that a time-bounded likelihood favours making theorems with long proofs axioms (AI-paper §6.6, `rem:time:links`).

**Reading [assessment].** In first-order logic the cost of a lemma-free theory for an analytic reasoner can be non-elementary, far beyond the exponential gaps of §2–§3. Prop 8.1 shows that the gap is closed by adding sentences that are *valid*: the theory's deductive closure, and so everything the idealised inducer sees, is unchanged, and only its written form changes. This is the textbook point in first-order form: the know-how is in the written lemmas, not in the content. The physics illustration ("Newton plus a perturbation lemma") belongs to `philosophy` and is not developed here.

## 10. What the thread shows about "what a theory is", and what it does not

**The brief's question:** in what precise sense is a theory better shaped like a logical theory than like a program? Our answer, for the classes studied (linear laws over GF(2), the implication chain), with every step a theorem above:

1. **Under the idealised measure, in no sense.** Craig's collapse makes representation shape invisible (`sfi`, `terms`); Theorems 1.11 and 1.12(c) add that *reasoners and vocabularies* are invisible too: under program length every sound reasoner, with or without intermediate concepts, is equivalent, up to a machine constant, to one round of unit propagation over the base variables, because a program can write out the lemma library. The textbook point cannot be stated for the idealised inducer.
2. **With a cost on the written theory, shape relative to a reasoner matters, and exactly so** (Theorem 1.12, Cor 3.9). The theory must then contain the know-how its reader lacks, and the written size it needs depends
   * *exponentially on the reasoner's algebra, for any vocabulary:* Gaussian elimination needs a linear-size theory for the hidden-state law, unit propagation at any depth and with any intermediate concepts $2^{\Omega(m^{\varepsilon/3})}$ (Theorem 3.6, given GKRS);
   * *on the reasoner's depth, for any vocabulary:* one round of propagation needs all prime implicates whatever the vocabulary ($2^{m-1}$ for one parity law; Prop 3.1a); a fixed number $k$ of rounds needs $2^{\Omega(m^{1/(2k-1)})}$ (Prop 3.8(c), given Håstad); for the implication chain, $\Theta(m\,\mathrm{ia}_k(m))$ clauses at depth $k$ (§7);
   * *exponentially on the vocabulary, for a reasoner deep enough to use it:* one parity law needs $2^{m-1}$ clauses over the base variables and $4m-6$ with intermediate quantities, for unit propagation with $2\lceil\log_2m\rceil-1$ rounds (Cor 2.7(c), Prop 3.8(b)); the simplex family, $m2^{(m-1)/2}$ against $m(2m-4)$ (Cor 2.8, Rem 3.2a);
   * *and only by a constant factor on declarative against procedural shape* once intermediate concepts are allowed and the reasoner can chain (rule bases against clauses: exactly the number of directions $m$ without intermediate concepts or with one round, at most 9 in written length with intermediate concepts and unbounded rounds; Theorem 4.3).
3. **Which candidate properties of logical theories this bears on.** Multi-directional use is real but cheap: a clause is $w$ directed rules, and a program with internal variables pays nothing for directions. Closure under inference is what the reasoner supplies, and its price is the subject of §2–§3 and §7. Partiality and modularity are not addressed here.
4. **Where the know-how lives** (Prop 5.1): in the reasoner, in written lemmas, or in a computation the cost model does not charge (free proof search, as in the AI-paper's charged deduction; or a generator, as under program length). The AI-paper's charged-deduction readings, extended to conditional data, charge the full lemma library no more than a time-bounded description of the law (Theorem 5.2) and so cannot express the textbook point.
5. **Learning** (Theorem 6.2): a weak reasoner without intermediate concepts acquires its know-how pattern by pattern; with an algebraic reasoner, or with intermediate concepts and enough depth, the law is learned at once.
6. **First-order logic** (§8): the gap between a lemma-free theory and the same theory with its lemmas can be non-elementary for an analytic reasoner, and it is closed by adding valid sentences, which change the written theory but not its content.

**What it does not show.** It is not a theorem about textbooks: the laws are linear or implicational, the reasoners are unit propagation, forward chaining and elimination, and the cost of a theory is its written length. It does not show that logic, rather than, say, probabilistic programs with conditioning or classes of models, is the right formal home for theories (the brief's caveat): the large separations are between pairs (reasoner, vocabulary), and a rule base with intermediate concepts is within a constant of a clausal theory. It does not model a community of reasoners that communicate, check and extend theories (`select.md` §4 rejected that as a thread).

**For `philosophy` [assessment].** Recommended sentence for the paper (revised): *"Whether a written theory suffices depends on the reader. Paired with a reasoner that can do algebra, a linear law's few equations suffice; paired with one that only applies rules, the theory must spell out exponentially many derived laws, unless it is written with new intermediate quantities and the reader can chain many steps through them. **Possible error in the author's view.** None arises in this thread. The constant at depth 2 with intermediate concepts: between $\frac12m\log_2m$ (Theorem 7.2) and $\sum_{i\le m}\lfloor\log_2i\rfloor\approx m\log_2m$; Conjecture 7.1b (the latter is exact without auxiliary variables for $m\le18$); the exact effect of auxiliary variables at depth $k\ge3$ (only "superlinear" is shown, given CFL).
