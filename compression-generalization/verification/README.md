# Referee round 2: skeptical paper review, new code, claims against results

Each candidate defect then went to an independent adjudicator, who checked it against the current files. Full record: `referee_round2.json` (including what each referee checked and found correct).

**Outcome.** No referee found an error in a theorem, a proof, or a reported bound; every tabulated bound was recomputed independently from the stored counts. # Final numbers check

After round 2, four AI checkers each took a slice of the text (abstract, introduction, headline table and README; the Boolean sections and appendix; the image sections and methods; the initialization sections, discussion and theory note) and checked every quantitative claim against the raw result files, recomputing bounds with `accounting.py`. They checked 381 claims. Each claimed discrepancy went to an independent verifier: 10 were confirmed and fixed, 3 were rejected. The confirmed ones were scope and wording: "within 0.005 at m = 16000" holds for the noise-free targets only; the 64-unit MNIST bound (0.87) is just below chance, not vacuous; the worst-case probe needs 8–9 bits per coefficient, not 6–8; two missing m qualifiers; the measured design-charge cost on the training-data-only Boolean bounds; η/δ' for the 256-unit networks; the Boolean summary headers (their part columns exclude the design charge). One was a conclusion: the claim that feature learning is what makes trained networks short is not supported by these runs, since lazily trained networks distil into the same short juntas as richly trained ones. It was replaced, in the paper, the theory note and the README, by what the runs show: the training regime matters for the weight-space codec, not for function-space codecs. Record: `numbers_check.json`.
