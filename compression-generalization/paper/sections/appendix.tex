\section{Experimental details}\label{app:details}

\paragraph{Boolean tasks.} Relevant coordinates are a fixed random subset (seed 1000). Training inputs, labels and label noise use a seed determined by the run's seed and $m$. The network has PyTorch's default initialization (uniform on $\pm1/\sqrt{\text{fan-in}}$ for weights and biases), drawn from an explicit generator seeded by the run's seed. Training stops at zero training error and mean training loss at most 0.01, or after $4\times10^4$ steps. The influence estimates use $2000$ inputs per coordinate. Students are distilled for 2000 Adam steps (batch 256, learning rate $3\times10^{-3}$, cosine schedule) on a pool of $10^5$ uniform inputs labelled by $f$. Quantization-aware fine-tuning runs 500 steps at learning rate $10^{-3}$ for each of three steps $\{0.5,0.25,0.125\}\times$RMS per layer. Candidate selection uses $3\times10^4$ further uniform inputs. For $n=50$, the certified disagreement uses $2\times10^5$ fresh inputs. In the runs that record a \texttt{cert\_seed} (36 of 147), these come from a generator seeded with OS entropy after $g$ is fixed. The other 111 runs, made before that change, used a fixed seed ($4242+1000\cdot\text{seed}+m$) that was independent of $g$'s construction but had also been used in development runs. They are covered by the design charge below, which takes the disagreement term at $\delta_2/16$. For $n=20$ it is computed on all $2^{20}$ inputs. The predictor $f$ whose error is bounded is the float64 evaluation of the trained weights.

\paragraph{Images.} Pixels are scaled to $[0,1]$. One-hidden-layer networks are trained with Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 128) for 20 epochs. Weight-noise training adds, at every step, Gaussian noise of the stated multiple of each tensor's initial RMS before the forward pass, and removes it before the update. Sparse-update networks are trained densely for 10 epochs in the parameterization $W=W_0+AB^\top$. The largest coefficients of $A$ are then kept and the network is fine-tuned for 5 epochs at learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$ with the support fixed. The dither step grid has 162 points: a global scale $\gamma\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2,4,8\}$ (first-layer steps $\gamma\cdot10^{-2}$ times the band and unit factors), band exponents $p_b\in\{0.5,0.75,1\}$, unit exponents $p_u\in\{0,0.5,1\}$, and the hidden-bias and output-weight step $0.002\,\gamma\,v_m$ with $v_m\in\{1,4,16\}$ (the output bias has the fixed step $2^{-10}$). The point is chosen on 6000 training points with a selection dither. (A first pass used a fixed $v_m=1$; its results are kept in \texttt{results/mnist/*search\_v1.jsonl} and are not reported.)

\paragraph{Design charge.} The codec families, step grids, band layout and certificate parameters were developed with exploratory runs on the same datasets. We therefore charge every description 4 bits, and take every disagreement term at confidence $\delta_2/16$. This covers a menu of up to 16 designs (\repo{code/experiments/accounting.py}). It costs about $4\ln2/m$ in the Occam term, plus a slightly wider disagreement interval. On the reported means this is at most 0.025 on the Boolean grids (at $m=100$) and 0.014 at $m=250$, under 0.003 for the MNIST bounds that use unlabeled inputs, under $4\times10^{-4}$ for the training-data-only Boolean bounds and under $10^{-4}$ for the training-data-only MNIST bounds.

\section{Provenance and verification}\label{app:provenance}

This work was produced by an AI system (Claude) in one session. The literature review (\repo{literature.md}) was assembled by AI agents from web-search results and from the public repositories of \citet{bazinet2026bound} and \citet{lotfi2022pac}. Partway through, the session's web-search budget was exhausted, so some citations are marked there as unconfirmed. The theory and its implementation were checked by four independent AI referees, each with a different lens: the proof of \cref{thm:dither,lem:cert}; code against proof, including an independent reimplementation of the certificate; the remaining theorems and the experimental protocols; and attempts to break the bounds numerically. Each claimed defect was then checked by an independent adjudicator. A second round used three further referees (a skeptical reading of the paper, the code added since the first round, and the claims checked against the raw result files), again with one adjudicator per claimed defect. Neither round found an error invalidating a theorem or a bound. The defects found (statement scope, notation, test coverage, bookkeeping of selection effects in the first round; overclaims, descriptions of prior work and missing baselines in the second) were fixed and are listed in \repo{verification/README.md}. No human has checked the proofs or the code.
