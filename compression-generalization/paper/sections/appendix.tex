\section{Experimental details}\label{app:details}

\paragraph{Boolean tasks.} Relevant coordinates are a fixed random subset (seed 1000). Training inputs, labels and label noise use a seed determined by the run's seed and $m$. The network has PyTorch's default initialization (uniform on $\pm1/\sqrt{\text{fan-in}}$ for weights and biases), drawn from an explicit generator seeded by the run's seed. Training stops at zero training error and mean training loss at most 0.01, or after $4\times10^4$ steps. The influence estimates use $2000$ inputs per coordinate. Students are distilled for 2000 Adam steps (batch 256, learning rate $3\times10^{-3}$, cosine schedule) on a pool of $10^5$ uniform inputs labelled by $f$. Quantization-aware fine-tuning runs 500 steps at learning rate $10^{-3}$ for each of three steps $\{0.5,0.25,0.125\}\times$RMS per layer. Candidate selection uses $3\times10^4$ further uniform inputs. For $n=50$, the certified disagreement uses $2\times10^5$ fresh inputs from a generator seeded with OS entropy after $g$ is fixed. For $n=20$ it is computed on all $2^{20}$ inputs. The predictor $f$ whose error is bounded is the float64 evaluation of the trained weights.

\paragraph{Images.} Pixels are scaled to $[0,1]$. One-hidden-layer networks are trained with Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 128) for 20 epochs. Weight-noise training adds, at every step, Gaussian noise of the stated multiple of each tensor's initial RMS before the forward pass, and removes it before the update. Sparse-update networks are trained densely for 10 epochs in the parameterization $W=W_0+AB^\top$. The largest coefficients of $A$ are then kept and the network is fine-tuned for 5 epochs at learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$ with the support fixed. The dither step grid has 162 points: a global scale $\gamma\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2,4,8\}$ (first-layer steps $\gamma\cdot10^{-2}$ times the band and unit factors), band exponents $p_b\in\{0.5,0.75,1\}$, unit exponents $p_u\in\{0,0.5,1\}$, and the bias and output-layer step $0.002\,\gamma\,v_m$ with $v_m\in\{1,4,16\}$. The point is chosen on 6000 training points with a selection dither. (A first pass used a fixed $v_m=1$; its results are kept in \texttt{results/mnist/*search\_v1.jsonl} and are not reported.)

\paragraph{Design charge.} The codec families, step grids, band layout and certificate parameters were developed with exploratory runs on the same datasets. We therefore charge every description 4 bits, and take every disagreement term at confidence $\delta_2/16$. This covers a menu of up to 16 designs (\repo{code/experiments/accounting.py}) and changes no reported number by more than a few units in the third decimal.

\section{Provenance and verification}\label{app:provenance}

This work was produced by an AI system (Claude) in one session. The literature review (\repo{literature.md}) was assembled by AI agents from web-search results and from the public repositories of \citet{bazinet2026bound} and \citet{lotfi2022pac}. Partway through, the session's web-search budget was exhausted, so some citations are marked there as unconfirmed. The theory and its implementation were checked by four independent AI referees, each with a different lens: the proof of \cref{thm:dither,lem:cert}; code against proof, including an independent reimplementation of the certificate; the remaining theorems and the experimental protocols; and attempts to break the bounds numerically. Each claimed defect was then checked by an independent adjudicator. No error invalidating a theorem or a bound was found. The defects found (statement scope, notation, test coverage, bookkeeping of selection effects) were fixed and are listed in \repo{verification/README.md}. No human has checked the proofs or the code.
