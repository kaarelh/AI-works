With $n=20$ the whole input space has $2^{20}\approx10^6$ points, so the disagreement between $f$ and $g$ can be computed exactly, and \cref{cor:anyD} turns compression into a bound that holds for \emph{every} input distribution. \Cref{tab:boolean_n20} uses the same architecture and training protocol as before, with widths $(20,512,512,1)$. Once a network has learned its target (test error below $10^{-5}$), its compressed description agrees with it on all $2^{20}$ inputs, or on all but at most 8 of them. Appending the exception set $E$ then costs at most about 150 bits, and the distribution-free bound is close to the uniform-distribution bound: 0.0065 against 0.0061 for \texttt{parity3} at $m=4000$, and 0.018 against 0.017 for \texttt{junta6}. When $f$ still errs on a small fraction of inputs (e.g.\ \texttt{maj5} at $m=250$, with 500--3700 exceptions), the uniform-distribution bound is non-vacuous (0.12) but the distribution-free one is not. That is correct: an input distribution concentrated on the exceptions would make $f$ wrong almost always.
