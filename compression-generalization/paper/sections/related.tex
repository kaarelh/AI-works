\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Occam, MDL and PAC-Bayes.} Bounding the error of a hypothesis by its description length is the Occam bound \cite{blumer1987occam,langford2005tutorial}. Applied to network weights it is the minimum-description-length programme of \citet{hinton1993keeping} and the flat-minima argument of \citet{hochreiter1997flat}. PAC-Bayes \cite{mcallester1999pac} lets the hypothesis be a distribution over weights; \citet{langford2001not} and \citet{dziugaite2017computing} obtained non-vacuous PAC-Bayes bounds for stochastic networks, the latter with a prior centred at the random initialization, and \citet{perezortiz2021tighter} and \citet{dziugaite2021role} tightened them with data-dependent priors.

\paragraph{Bounds for compressed networks.} \citet{arora2018stronger} bound a compressed network $g$ obtained by random projections from a trained $f$. \citet{zhou2019nonvacuous} combine pruning, quantization and coding with a noisy posterior and obtain non-vacuous bounds at ImageNet scale for the noisy compressed network. \citet{lotfi2022pac} train in a random subspace \cite{li2018intrinsic}, quantize and arithmetic-code, and obtain the tightest data-independent bounds we know of (11.6\% on MNIST and 32.8\% on Fashion-MNIST, values from their repository); \citet{lotfi2024nonvacuous} extend this to language models. In all of these the certified predictor is the compressed (or noisy, or subspace-trained) network, not an independently trained $f$.

\paragraph{Bounds for the original network through a surrogate.} The reduction $\err(f)\le\err(g)+\Pr[f\ne g]$ with the disagreement estimated on unlabeled data goes back at least to \citet{kaariainen2005generalization}. \citet{hsu2021generalization} use a distilled network and control the distance with samples from an augmentation distribution. \citet{garg2021ratt} certify the original network using randomly labelled unlabeled data. Most directly, \citet{bazinet2026bound} implement the reduction for modern networks with surrogates certified by sample compression, PAC-Bayes or Lotfi-style compression, and disagreement on a held-out unlabeled set. Their repository reports bounds on the unmodified network of 1.78--3.45\% for an MNIST CNN, depending on the surrogate, and 17--35\% for a CIFAR-10 ResNet-18. Our unlabeled-data experiments are an instance of their method. Theoretical treatments of the same step without extra data include \citet{suzuki2020compression} (local Rademacher complexity of the difference class), \citet{daniely2019generalization} (approximation at every input), \citet{sefidgaran2022rate} (rate-distortion) and the surrogate framework of \citet{negrea2020defense}; none yields numerical bounds.

\paragraph{Deterministic bounds from noise resilience.} \citet{neyshabur2018pacbayes} and \citet{nagarajan2019deterministic} derandomize PAC-Bayes for the original network using margins and robustness to weight perturbations, checked on the training data; \citet{wei2020improved} use an all-layer margin. These bounds are vacuous in practice. \citet{biggs2022nonvacuous} obtain non-vacuous bounds from the training data for deterministic one-hidden-layer networks with erf or GELU activations (about 0.7--0.8 on MNIST without a data-dependent prior, by our reading of their slides; unverified). Their bounds rest on the exact identity $\E[\mathrm{sign}(w\cdot x+\text{noise})]\propto\mathrm{erf}(\cdot)$, which makes such a network a PAC-Bayes majority vote; see also \citet{biggs2022margins}. Our dithered transfer theorem shares their structure (a perturbation set around $f$ plus a per-point condition transferred from training to test), but it uses a single public random draw, an Occam code of the perturbed network, and a per-input concentration bound in place of worst-case or norm-based perturbation bounds. Sound bound propagation under weight perturbations \cite{xu2020automatic} and subtractive dither \cite{schuchman1964dither} are the tools; shared-randomness model coding also appears in \citet{havasi2019minimal}, and single-draw arguments in \citet{blanchard2007occam}.

\paragraph{Boolean tasks and simplicity.} \citet{valleperez2019deep} bound the Bayesian posterior of networks on 7-bit Boolean functions with a prior induced by random initialization. Our Boolean experiments instead bound the network produced by gradient descent, via distillation into a junta, a linear threshold function or a small quantized network, with the disagreement computed exactly or on free samples from the known input distribution. A companion project in the same repository \cite{gdcircuit2026} reports similar certificates for rich-regime networks on Boolean targets, using unlabelled inputs. When the labelling function is also known, a network's error can be computed outright; \citet{gross2024compact} study proofs of such statements and the trade-off between proof length and tightness.

\paragraph{Initialization.} A data-independent initialization costs nothing in PAC-Bayes \cite{dziugaite2017computing} and in Occam codes \cite{arora2018stronger,lotfi2022pac}. Supermasks \cite{zhou2019deconstructing} show that a random initialization can carry most of the information of a good network at zero cost. Lazy and rich training \cite{chizat2019lazy,jacot2018ntk,mei2018mean} predict how the update $\theta-\theta_0$ is structured, and deterministic initializations exist \cite{zhao2022zero}. We are not aware of a study measuring the description length of the update as a function of the initialization scale, or comparing coding relative to the initialization and relative to zero.
