# Referee report: separations (proof lens)

**Verdict.** The mathematics in sec:separations is essentially sound: every construction checks out, and so do its probabilities and reductions. One headline claim goes beyond what is proved (major): "the prover helps exactly when P is not weakly automatable", repeated in the abstract and intro. The corollary is true, but the chain of citations behind it skips a needed step (major for exposition). Everything else needs only minor fixes. No critical errors.

**What was checked.** Line-by-line re-derivation of every step in sec-part2.tex lines 265-344, plus the definitions they use (def:sound, def:argument, thm:ma, def:pcis).
(1) Universal verifier V_T. If the T-proved sentence is false, its negation is a true Delta_0 (hence Sigma_1) sentence. A Sigma_1-complete T proves it, so T is inconsistent. Hence V_T is sound if T is consistent and Sigma_1-complete. For p-simulation, I checked that instantiating a T-proof of the soundness of a clocked V' gives an argument of polynomial size that has the same output law.
(2) Valiant-Vazirani. A sound procedure that gives L>0 on uniquely satisfiable circuits, run on O(n) VV instances with small delta, decides SAT with two-sided error, so NP is in BPP.
(3) One-way-permutation instance. Brute force on a random permutation with k=3 confirms p(C_y) is in {0, 2^-2k}, and equals 2^-2k exactly when b=<x0,r0>. The Goldreich-Levin predictor argument (threshold on L>0) gives success at least 1-delta.
(4) Canonical-pair equivalence, both directions. Separator to procedure: amplify the separator, output [0,0] or [0,1]. Procedure to separator: needs D uniform or of full support.
(5) pad_a. Brute force over all truth tables for n<=4, a<=3 confirms p = 2^-(n+a+1) + #C * 2^-(n+1). Threshold U <= 2^-(n+1) separates; this uses the strict SAT-side bound.
(6) Corollary. Rebuilt the chain: the hard disjoint NP pair from BPR/KP98, with poly-size P-proofs that it is disjoint; closure of P under restrictions; the reduction u -> A_1(u,.) into (Z_P^m, SAT); Tseitin versus formula refutations in Frege-type systems; Adleman's BPP-in-P/poly. The corollary holds.
(7) UP cap coNP. Brute force for k<=5 over every witness position confirms p(C_u) = 2^-(k+1) * (1+[u in L]), including when 0^k is the witness. Deciding L needs eps<1.
(8) NP-hardness barrier. Orientation 1: an AMA protocol, which is in AM (Babai), then BHZ. Orientation 2: Goldwasser-Sipser on the large side. Non-monotone many-one reductions are also covered by combining the two.
(9) Universality. Both directions of "lower-bound complete within O(1) for all circuits iff prSBP is in prMA" hold. Forward: amplify the gap with an AND of k independent copies. Backward: Merlin sends j = ceil(log2(1/p)) and a prMA proof for the gap at threshold 2^-j, which gives factor 2*gamma.

## Findings

### 1. [major] sec-part2.tex, paragraph header 'Upper bounds: the prover helps exactly when P is not weakly automatable'; repeated in main.tex abstract ('the prover's advantage for upper bounds is exactly the non-weak-automatability of its proof system') and sec-intro.tex l.106-107

**Problem.** The paragraph proves something narrower. For the specific task of certifying p=0 on Z_P^m (and, by padding, positive exactly-certified p), a sound procedure without a prover exists iff (Z_P^m, SAT) can be separated in randomized polynomial time. The headline 'exactly when' goes further in two ways. (a) It claims that if P is weakly automatable, the prover gives no advantage for upper bounds at all. That is never shown, and there is no reason to expect it. A PCIS argument has non-deductive content: the envelopes E_j, the DAG, the split variables and the sample sizes. Weak automatability only lets a procedure without a prover test candidate implications that have short proofs; it does not find those objects. (b) Pudlak's equivalence is between weak automatability and deterministic polynomial-time separability of the canonical pair. With a randomized separator, the construction of the Cook-Reckhow system Q fails, because Q's proofs must be checked deterministically. 'P not weakly automatable' does not imply 'canonical pair not BPP-separable' unless P=BPP-type derandomization holds. The text proves the BPP-separability version.

**Proposed fix.** Restate it, for example: 'Upper bounds: refutation-certified instances. For the task of certifying p=0 on Z_P^m, a sound procedure without a prover exists iff (Z_P^m,SAT) is separable in randomized polynomial time. In particular, the prover helps whenever P is not weakly automatable by randomized algorithms (in the separation sense).' Drop 'exactly' from the header, the abstract and the intro. Or explicitly restrict the claim to the family {pad_a(C): C in Z_P^m}. Add a sentence saying that weak automatability of P does not by itself remove the prover's advantage for general PCIS arguments, because that advantage includes choosing the envelope and structure.

### 2. [major] sec-part2.tex, 'separability of the canonical pair is weak automatability of P [pudlak2003pairs]. Padding turns this into ... Known results then give:' and Corollary cor:automatability

**Problem.** The corollary is true, but the cited chain does not show it as written. The corollary needs this: (Z_P^m, SAT) is not separable by randomized polynomial-time procedures, for some polynomial m. BPR2000 is stated as non-automatizability or no feasible interpolation, and KP1998 as no feasible interpolation. Plain non-automatizability does not imply non-weak-automatizability. The step from 'no feasible interpolation' to 'canonical pair not separable' needs three things the text never states. First, what BPR/KP98 actually prove: a specific disjoint NP pair (A_0,A_1) (Diffie-Hellman-based or RSA-based) has poly-size P-proofs that it is disjoint, and no poly-size circuits separate it under the assumption. The weaker property 'interpolants cannot be computed from proofs' would not be enough. Second, P is closed under restrictions. Then for u in A_0 with witness y, substituting (u,y) into the disjointness proof gives a short P-refutation of A_1(u,.), so u -> A_1(u,.) reduces (A_0,A_1) to (Z_P^m,SAT) for a suitable m (Razborov 1994; Pudlak 2003). Third, a BPP separator gives poly-size circuits (Adleman). One also needs to note that Frege-type systems pass from formula refutations to refutations of Tseitin(C) and [C=1] with polynomial overhead. Two smaller points. EF is already covered by BPR under the factoring assumption, since EF p-simulates TC^0-Frege, so the RSA hypothesis for EF is redundant. AC^0-Frege could be added under subexponential Diffie-Hellman hardness (Bonet-Domingo-Gavalda-Maciel-Pitassi 2004). ACG2024 show that no quantum algorithm weakly automates TC^0-Frege under quantum-hard LWE. Their proof goes through the same pair-reduction pattern, so the quantum clause also follows, but this too should be said.

**Proposed fix.** Insert this argument before the corollary: 'BPR (KP98, ACG) exhibit a disjoint NP pair (A_0,A_1) with poly-size P-proofs that it is disjoint, and no poly-size (resp. quantum poly-time) separator unless factoring Blum integers (RSA, LWE) is easy. Since P is closed under restrictions, u -> A_1(u,.) is a polynomial-time reduction of (A_0,A_1) to (Z_P^m,SAT) for m the polynomial bounding the restricted proofs (cf. Razborov 1994, Pudlak 2003). A BPP separator of the latter would give poly-size circuits separating the former (Adleman).' Cite Razborov 1994 for the canonical pair. Note that EF also follows from the factoring assumption.

### 3. [minor] sec-part2.tex, 'A universal verifier' paragraph (V_T)

**Problem.** (i) For V_T to run in polynomial time, the time bound t must be at most poly(|pi|), for example written in unary; otherwise 'runs A' can take exponential time. (ii) 'Consistent' is not enough. The soundness argument needs T to be Sigma_1-complete (for example, T contains Q, or IDelta_0+exp for the counting formalisation). Also, 'at most delta_A 2^t coin strings ...' is a counting statement: Delta_0 in the language with exponentiation (or Delta_1), not literally a 'bounded arithmetic' sentence. 'Sound whenever T is' should say 'whenever T is consistent'. (iii) For p-simulation, T must prove soundness of V' clocked at its polynomial time bound, uniformly. Otherwise the instance 'V' halts within t on these inputs' is a Delta_0 fact whose Sigma_1-completeness proof may be exponentially long. p-simulation between argument systems is also never defined.

**Proposed fix.** Write: 'a time bound t (in unary)'; 'Fix a consistent, Sigma_1-complete theory T (e.g. ZFC)'; 'the Delta_0(exp) sentence ...'; 'it p-simulates every argument system V' for which T proves soundness of V' clocked at its polynomial time bound: an argument pi' maps to (V'(.,.,pi'), t, delta, instance of the T-proof), of size |T-proof| + poly(|C|,|pi'|)'.

### 4. [minor] sec-part2.tex, 'Uniquely satisfiable circuits have p=2^{-n}, certified exactly by one witness'

**Problem.** A single witness certifies only the lower bound L = 2^-n = p. Certifying p exactly, that is, the upper bound, needs a proof of uniqueness, which is a coNP statement and is in general impossible by thm:ma. In a section that also discusses two-sided exact certification, 'certified exactly' is misleading. The claim also needs D uniform.

**Proposed fix.** Say: 'Uniquely satisfiable circuits have p=2^{-n} under the uniform distribution, and one witness certifies the tight lower bound L=p.'

### 5. [minor] sec-part2.tex, 'Under one-way permutations ... C_y(x,r)=[(\pi(x),r,<x,r>)=y] gives an average-case version with (1+eps)-tight certificates'

**Problem.** The construction is correct: p(C_y) is in {0, 2^-2k}, confirmed by brute force. The statement is imprecise, though. (a) The instance distribution and the conclusion are never given. (b) '(1+eps)-tight' is ambiguous. The lower-bound certificate (x,r) is exact. A two-sided tight certificate would need a proof, in the verifier's system, that pi is injective. (c) pi clashes with the symbol for the argument string.

**Proposed fix.** State it as: 'For y=(pi(x),r,b) with x,r,b uniform, p(C_y)=2^{-2k}[b=<x,r>]. The preimage certifies L=p exactly on positive instances, while any sound poly-time procedure gives L>0 on positive instances with probability at most delta+negl(k), by Goldreich-Levin.' Rename the permutation (e.g. f). Optionally note: if the verifier's proof system proves pi injective, negative instances are certified as well (reveal x), giving a two-sided average-case separation. This is relevant to open problem 4.

### 6. [minor] sec-part2.tex, canonical-pair and padding paragraph ('Z_P^m be the circuits C with a P-refutation of size m(|C|)', 'p>=2^{-n-1} when C is satisfiable', 'Task-1')

**Problem.** (i) 'Task-1 procedure' is defined nowhere in the paper. (ii) The direction 'procedure gives separator' needs D uniform, or at least of full support. Under a product D with some q_i in {0,1}, a satisfiable C can have p=0. (iii) 'of size m(|C|)' should read 'of size at most m(|C|)'. (iv) For a factor of exactly 2^a, the threshold argument (unsat: U <= 2^a L <= 2^a p = 2^-(n+1); sat: U >= p) needs the strict bound p >= 2^-(n+1) * (1+2^-a) > 2^-(n+1). The stated p >= 2^-(n+1) leaves a tie at equality. (v) Pudlak's equivalence is stated for the canonical pair (REF(P), SAT*) with a size parameter 1^m. The fixed slice (Z_P^m, SAT) is equivalent to it for linear m by padding with fresh satisfiable clauses, and this is worth one sentence.

**Proposed fix.** Replace 'Task-1 procedure' with 'sound procedure without a prover (Definition def:sound)'. Fix D uniform throughout the paragraph. Write 'p >= 2^{-n-1}(1+2^{-a}) > 2^{-n-1}', and say 'complete within any factor <= 2^a'. Add the slice and padding remark.

### 7. [minor] sec-part2.tex, 'Two-sided, exact' paragraph

**Problem.** The construction is correct: p(C_u) = 2^-(k+1) * (1+[u in L]). (i) The hypothesis '0^k never a witness' is unnecessary. beta separates the two disjuncts, and brute force with the witness at 0^k gives the same p. (ii) 'decides L' needs eps<1, because the two values differ by a factor of 2: threshold on L > 2^-(k+1). (iii) For the factoring language, uniqueness needs a canonical fixed-length encoding of the sorted factorisation, padding included. Using AKS rather than Pratt certificates, so that the witness stays unique, is right and worth saying.

**Proposed fix.** Drop '0^k never a witness'. Add 'for eps<1 (the two values differ by a factor 2), a sound procedure complete on {C_u} decides L by thresholding L at 2^{-k-1}'. Specify the canonical encoding of the factorisation.

### 8. [minor] sec-part2.tex, 'Why not NP-hardness?' paragraph; also sec-intro.tex 'Such separations cannot be based on NP-hardness alone unless coNP in AM'

**Problem.** Both orientations are argued correctly. Orientation 1 is an AMA protocol, which is in AM (Babai), followed by BHZ. Orientation 2 is Goldwasser-Sipser, with gamma >= 1+1/poly after tensoring. The upper-bound completeness must have ratio 1+eps < gamma. The conclusion 'must come from structured, non-NP-hard assumptions' is broader than the argument. The argument covers only randomized many-one (non-adaptive, single-query) reductions to a monotone gap problem. Non-monotone many-one reductions (unsat -> p in [t/2,2t], sat -> p far from t) are handled by the same argument combining the MA upper bound with Goldwasser-Sipser, but the text doesn't say so. Adaptive (Turing) reductions to the promise problem are not covered at all. For promise problems these can behave very differently (Even-Selman-Yacobi).

**Proposed fix.** Qualify it: 'cannot come from NP-hardness under randomized many-one reductions'. Add one sentence saying non-monotone acceptance regions reduce to the two cases. Make the same qualification in the intro.

### 9. [minor] sec-part2.tex, 'Finding the short proofs is the hard part ...' paragraph

**Problem.** (i) Atserias-Muller: no algorithm finds a resolution refutation in time poly(|F| + s(F)) unless P=NP. 'Finding a refutation of size polynomial in the shortest one is NP-hard' leaves out the time bound, and finding such a refutation in exponential time is trivial. Their result concerns automatability, not weak automatability, so it gives no separation of the cor:automatability type for resolution. (ii) The proof-complexity-generator conjectures concern specific generators (Nisan-Wigderson-type, Krajicek's truth-table generator), not every cryptographic PRG. Hardness of refuting tau_y does not by itself stop the verifier certifying intermediate upper bounds through confinement statements C_y => E with D(E) small. Those are non-range statements for restricted generators. 'The verifier cannot beat sampling for upper bounds' is therefore a conjecture on top of a conjecture, and it holds only for PCIS with that P, not for V_T.

**Proposed fix.** Write 'no polynomial-time algorithm finds a resolution refutation in time polynomial in |F| and the size of the shortest one, unless P=NP [Atserias-Muller]'. Rephrase: 'for suitable generators G, it is conjectured that strong proof systems have no short proofs of y not in range(G), nor of its restricted versions. If so, PCIS with such P cannot certify upper bounds below the sampling scale on these circuits.'

### 10. [minor] sec-part2.tex, 'Universality' paragraph (cross-check with sec-limits.tex l.114-118)

**Problem.** The equivalence holds, and I re-derived both directions. Forward: amplify the gap with an AND of k independent copies. Backward: Merlin sends j = ceil(log2(1/p)) and a prMA proof for the gap problem at threshold 2^-j, giving L = 2^-j/gamma >= p/(2*gamma). Three problems remain. [bgm2006] introduces SBP but does not state this equivalence, which is folklore and needs a two-line proof. The oracle claim should cite its source precisely; I believe BGM06 give an oracle with SBP not in MA, and Aaronson-Kothari-Kretschmer-Thaler 2020 strengthen it to QMA. I could not check this online. The derandomisation clause has no citation; prAM = prNP follows from hardness against nondeterministic circuits (Miltersen-Vinodchandran, Shaltiel-Umans). Finally, sec-limits.tex states the same fact for the language classes ('SBP contained in MA', 'is exactly a proof'). That is not known to be equivalent; the promise version given here is the right one.

**Proposed fix.** Add the two-line proof of the equivalence. Cite the oracle separation and the AM=NP derandomisation explicitly. Change sec-limits.tex to prSBP and prMA. Optionally note that a constant factor upgrades to 1+eps by tensoring (k = O(1/eps) copies).
