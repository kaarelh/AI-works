# Fix round: pcis

## Fixer's summary

I rewrote paper/p2-pcis.tex to address every finding from both pcis referees, fixed a real soundness bug in code/rareprob/argument.py, and added code/tests/test_fix_pcis.py.

Paper changes:
- **EXACT rule and determinism.** The verifier now accepts only forms whose determinism it can check: OBDDs, decision-DNNFs, SDDs whose vtree and prime partitions it checks, and d-DNNFs that come with P-refutations c_a AND c_b for every pair of children of every OR-gate. A one-line reduction shows that checking determinism of a DNNF is coNP-hard. An unverified d-DNNF is a plain DNNF.
- **Rules in def:pcis.** SPLIT needs a P-proof and the verifier forms the restrictions itself. The Tseitin variables of F and G are disjoint. Node targets are now q_u, and marginals are q_i. PROD is syntactic; an equivalence goes through a one-child SUM first. Sample caps are written in unary. A failed deductive certificate gives [0,1], a downgraded statistical node keeps only its lower end, and a node that hits its cap keeps a valid interval.
- **ENVELOPE rule.** The certificate is C_v ⊢_P G with G implying phi >= 1. G is supp Phi for integer-valued phi. For the tilted envelope beta^{w.x-theta} (weights in unary), G is [w.x >= theta]. Envelope leaves may be atoms. The rule lists every estimator: thm:envelope, thm:boxes, thm:sis(c), and the weighted KLM.
- **thm:sis**, with a full proof:
  - (a) One shared comparator block per variable, with both polarities range-restricted.
  - (b) Marginals in {0,1} are replaced by constants; A_c = 0 exactly when the branch is unsatisfiable; the identity holds for every f >= 0 under a stated causality condition (martingale proof).
  - (c) New interval clause. D-hat is one FPRAS call (accuracy 1/4, failure probability delta_r). The output [3 D-hat l, 3 D-hat u/(1-eta)] with eta = 2n·gamma is valid with probability at least 1-delta_r-delta_s for every C and every counter meeting the FPRAS guarantee; the lower end alone is always valid. The weight range is now exactly [3/5, 5/3]·D(E).
  - (d) Cost now includes the log log K term.
- **thm:pcis-sound** cites these interval guarantees.
- **prop:calculus** now states its hypotheses: only SUM, SPLIT and PROD nodes on derivations; [0,0] has ratio 1; eps <= 1.
- **thm:certenv.** R_0 is decomposable products of atoms: subcubes, checked-deterministic forms, and affine subspaces when q_i = 1/2 on their variables. Unions are allowed only at the top. DNNF pieces go through a weighted KLM built on thm:sis with clipping and inflation. The proof is complete, the time bound includes log log K and a multiplicative FPRAS cost, and a short paragraph says nested unions are handled only through the derivation-count envelope with K = Z/p.
- **Examples.**
  - Hidden triggers: the resolution claim is now a conjecture backed by CDCL evidence, the Monte Carlo cost is given for general D, the ⊕-tree case is dropped, and the multiplier test is spelled out with a caveat.

The O(sqrt n) bound is claimed only for unit weights, with a rational beta from prop:tilting (which already covers |mu_theta - t| < 1/(k+2), so no extra e^{1/8} factor is needed). General integer weights get no bound, and the unreproducible K=37 number is removed.
- **Sandwiches.** The circuit is binarized first. The base case is stated, ⊕ rules are added, and each check is a constant number of OBDD applies.
- **Opening.** "complete exactly when" becomes "complete whenever".
- **Checks.** Exact Fraction enumeration with adversarial counters (n <= 3) confirmed the unbiasedness for arbitrary f, the bad-mass bound eta·D(C AND E), and the clipped-mean bounds. A numeric check confirmed the 5/3 weight bound. A copy of the paper compiles with latexmk, with no undefined references and no warnings from p2-pcis.

Code: argument.py has no EXACT rule. Its verifier used the prover-supplied envelope supports to check PROD decomposability. A PROD of two x0 leaves with one support falsely marked empty passed the check, got a checking proof, and returned a tight interval around 1/4 while p = 1/2. ### 2. [minor] paper/p2-pcis.tex thm:sis(c) proof l.240-241 ('The walks are i.i.d. given D-hat'); same step in the thm:certenv proof l.295-298

**Problem.** The counter model (l.152-156) and the clause 'for every counter that meets the guarantee above' (l.187) only require accuracy 'given everything computed before the call'. Under that model a counter's error law may depend on earlier walks, so the walks need not be i.i.d., and the KL interval as justified ('i.i.d. samples in [0,1]') does not apply directly. The conclusion is still true. On A, every walk's conditional mean given the past lies in [(1−η)D(C∧E)/M, D(C∧E)/M], because (*) and (b) hold conditionally on the past. The Chernoff/KL bounds hold for adapted [0,1] sequences against these endpoints, since E[e^{λX}|past] ≤ 1−μ+μe^λ is monotone in μ. The proof does not say any of this.

**Proposed fix.** Either state that the counter's output law depends only on its input, which is true for a fixed FPRAS run with fresh coins (then the walks are i.i.d.), or replace 'i.i.d.' with this supermartingale argument: compare the lower tail against μ_lo = (1−η)D(C∧E)/M and the upper tail against μ_hi = D(C∧E)/M, for both the fixed-N interval and the thm:envelope(iv) schedule. Do the same in thm:certenv.

### 3. [minor] paper/p2-pcis.tex def:pcis ENVELOPE rule l.63-74 and thm:sis(c) l.183-191

**Problem.** Several side conditions are unstated.
(i) thm:sis(c) allows any γ, but the upper end Mu/(1−η) needs η = 2nγ < 1.
(ii) The tilted rule allows any integer θ. With θ written in binary and |θ| ≫ Σw_i, the exact mass β^{−θ}∏(...) has exponentially many bits, which breaks the polynomial-time claim (l.88).
(iii) 'when φ is integer-valued' is a semantic condition. The verifier can only check that all constant leaves are integers, and with non-integer constants supp Φ ≠ [φ≥1].
(iv) A downgraded tilted node gets a valid lower end only if W is clipped at 1 (thm:envelope(iii)). def:pcis does not say the verifier clips.
(v) The letter G means three different things: the domination circuit in def:pcis, the side condition in the Thresholds bullet, and the good event in the proof of thm:sis.

**Proposed fix.** Require η < 1, or set the upper end to +∞ when η ≥ 1. Require 0 ≤ θ ≤ Σ_i w_i: a larger θ makes [w·x≥θ] unsatisfiable, so C_v ⊢ ⊥ via REFUTE. Write 'all constants are nonnegative integers'. Say that the verifier always clips W at 1 for real-valued φ. Rename the side condition, for example to H, and the good event, for example to 𝒢.

### 4. [minor] paper/p2-pcis.tex paragraph after thm:certenv, l.317-321 ('such as the product of an affine atom with a union, has in general neither an exact mass nor an FPRAS')

**Problem.** The example contradicts its own claim. Take an affine atom times a union of atoms (or of DNNFs) on disjoint variables. By independence its mass is 2^{−rank}·D(∪). D(∪) has an FPRAS: KLM over atoms with exact masses, exact samplers and membership tests, or Meel–de Colnet for a union of DNNFs, which is itself a DNNF. Distributing the atom over the union also turns the piece into a top-level union of R_0 pieces, whose Λ equals the derivation-count Z. So this example loses nothing, and 'only through the derivation-count envelope' is not illustrated by it.

**Proposed fix.** Use an example with deeper alternation, such as a DNNF-shaped circuit whose leaves are affine atoms, or an ∧ of unions of affine atoms where distributing multiplies the pieces. Alternatively, drop 'nor an FPRAS' and say only that such pieces fall outside the rules of thm:certenv.

### 5. [minor] paper/p2-pcis.tex Sandwiches l.368 ('threshold gates expanded by a counter, sec:setting')

**Problem.** sec:setting (l.13) says threshold gates expand by a counter only when their integer weights are polynomially bounded. It allows general weights, which then 'stay opaque'. For a circuit with large-weight threshold gates, the binarization the paragraph relies on is not polynomial as cited.

**Proposed fix.** Say 'threshold gates with polynomially bounded weights expanded by a counter, others by a binary adder and comparator'. Alternatively, restrict the paragraph to circuits whose threshold gates have polynomially bounded weights.
