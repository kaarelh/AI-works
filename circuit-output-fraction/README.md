# Beating Monte Carlo on rare circuit outputs

Notes by Claude, prompted by Kaarel Hänni. Research note, 27 October 2026; not reviewed by a human. The proofs were checked by AI referees (see [`verification/`](verification/)), not by humans.

**Question.** A Boolean circuit $C:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$ (gates $\wedge,\vee,\neg,\oplus$ and thresholds) and an input distribution $D$ (a product distribution with rational marginals; a distribution given by a sampler circuit reduces to the uniform one) are given, and $p=\Pr_D[C(x)=1]$ is tiny. Monte Carlo needs about $1/p$ samples. (1) Is there a polynomial-time procedure, one that checks some property of the circuit and then samples, that estimates $p$ faster than sampling on a reasonable class of circuits, while never being wrong on any circuit? (2) The same, when an unbounded prover may send one argument to a polynomial-time randomized verifier that must never be convinced of a falsehood (beyond probability $\delta$), on any circuit.

A threshold gate with weights $w_j$ and threshold $t$ computes $[\sum_j w_j y_j\ge t]$ exactly, in rational arithmetic and with no tolerance; in the code the weights and threshold may be Python ints, floats (taken at their exact binary value) or Fractions, and constant gates are 0 or 1 (other values are rejected). Soundness, in the paper and in the code, is for this function.

**Paper:** [`paper/main.pdf`](paper/main.pdf) (LaTeX sources in [`paper/`](paper/)).

## Short answer

**Upper bounds need deduction; lower bounds need proposals; sampling pays for whatever surprise is left.**

- Throughout, $D$ is a product distribution with rational marginals. An *envelope* is a tractable function $\varphi\ge\mathbf 1_C$ whose mass $Z=\mathbb E_D\varphi$ can be computed exactly and from which one can sample (a DNF, a DNNF, a subcube, a Chernoff bound, …). Importance sampling from $D\varphi/Z$ has weights in $[0,1]$, so it gives *two-sided* intervals after $O(K(\log(1/\delta)+\log\log K)\varepsilon^{-2})$ samples, where $K=Z/p$ is the envelope's looseness. The envelope explains $\log_2(1/Z)$ bits of the surprise $\log_2(1/p)$ deductively; sampling pays $2^{\log_2 K}$ for the rest.
- **Without a prover**, the procedure *DFSB* (Decompose–Force–Sample–Branch) splits the event by exact rules (independence of variable-disjoint parts, literals forced by propagation and Gaussian elimination, Shannon expansion) and samples only at leaves that are dense or have tight envelopes. It is sound on every circuit, and costs $\mathrm{poly}\cdot K$ on every circuit whose ideal $K$-explanation tree is polynomial: read-once formulas (exactly), read-once compositions of non-constant sub-circuits each *component-dense* in the polarity in which it occurs, also after restriction by any literals it implies (unit propagation and independence split it into exact or dense pieces), triggers that propagation or Gaussian elimination exposes, circuits with small cutsets (exactly), and versions of these disguised inside cones of logarithmic support. DNFs and DNNFs, whose ideal trees can be exponential, are covered by their own envelopes as envelope leaves ($K\le$ the number of terms for a DNF) and by the #DNNF FPRAS of Meel–de Colnet, which the meta-procedure runs. Beyond DFSB:
  - every circuit whose NNF has no opaque gates (thresholds with polynomially bounded integer weights) and whose AND gates are bracketed with logarithmic *context width* compiles to a polynomial-size DNNF, of size $O(|N|(\mathrm{cw}+1)2^{\mathrm{cw}})$ for its NNF $N$, so (with the #DNNF FPRAS) it has an FPRAS; this covers, e.g., ORs of bounded-pathwidth CNFs, each bracketed as an AND-chain along a path decomposition (found in linear time for constant width), whose exact counting is #P-hard;
  - functions of independent *modules* (thresholds, majorities) are estimated through their quotients; the log-ratio of the final interval is at most the sum $S$ of those of the quotient estimates (in the polarities the parents use) when each parent is monotone or antitone in each input or the interval for that input's used polarity lies in $[0,\tfrac12]$, and at most $e^{O(dS)}S$ in general, for $dS=O(1)$ and $d$ the depth of the module tree;
  - for unate circuits the *presumption of independence* is certified: Harris and Janson inequalities give $M\le p\le Me^{\Delta/(1-\max_i b_i)}$ for $M=\prod_i D(A_i)$ ($b_i = D(\neg A_i)$, $\Delta$ the probability carried by dependent pairs). For unate CNFs both ends are computed exactly. For unate ANDs of arbitrary sub-circuits, a sampling procedure certifies $p$ to ratio $1+\varepsilon$ whenever $\Delta\le\ln(1+\varepsilon)/64$ and $\max_i b_i\le1/4$, using samples linear in $\sum_i b_i \le \ln(1/p)+\Delta/(1-\max_i b_i)$ plus $O(\varepsilon^{-1}\log(1/\delta))$ per dependent pair, independent of $1/p$. A covariance correction makes the error third order, and pairwise data provably cannot do better than $e^{0.29\Delta}$;
  - *certified telescoping*: annealing becomes sound with exact coupling-from-the-past samplers whose truncation is certified empirically;
  - *local thresholds*: counts of overlapping local sub-circuits (bounded arity and degree, expander-like overlaps allowed) by exponential tilting with a heat-bath sampler whose mixing is certified locally (Dobrushin), or by coupling from the past for supermodular counts; e.g. $p=10^{-20.19}$ certified to a factor 1.66 with about $7\cdot10^4$ chain samples, where Monte Carlo would need about $2\cdot10^{22}$;
  - *affine structure*: certified affine gates and an exact affine/triangular presolve remove the parity-check part of the surprise exactly when the pivots can be chosen on uniform coordinates (under biased marginals each pivot costs a factor up to $(1+2\eta)/(1-2\eta)$ in samples, $\eta$ the pivot's bias); unions of affine subspaces give envelopes. For events that fill a $1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$ fraction of the solution set of $m\le n/6$ random parity checks, with high probability, decomposition-based methods (envelopes with small leaves, context-width compilation, DFSB's completeness guarantee) cost Monte Carlo's price up to polynomial factors, and any use of a DNNF envelope at least its square root; under biased product distributions, small ANDs of parity checks admit no sound procedure that is complete on them unless NP ⊆ BPP;
  - bounded treewidth (exactly) and thresholds of unit-weight independent blocks (exponential tilting with $K\le\tfrac43(2\sqrt k+1)$ for $\mathbb E_D S\le t<k_+$, $k_+$ the number of blocks that can be 1).
- **With a prover**, *proof-carrying importance sampling* checks implications in any propositional proof system (DRUP, DRAT/LRAT, cutting planes, extended Frege) and samples the rest. It is sound against every argument, and complete on every circuit whose rarity has a short proof of confinement to a tractable region in which it is not rare. Lower bounds need no proof at all. XOR-propagation-tree arguments add parity reasoning (gate propagation and Gaussian elimination, affine pieces under product distributions), and Gibbs envelopes add tilted proposals with overlapping scopes, whose mixing certificate the verifier recomputes. Verifier cost is $\mathrm{poly}(|\pi|)+2^{\sigma_u}$ (up to polynomial factors), where $|\pi|$ is the size of the argument's deductive part (envelopes, proofs, circuits; not its unary sample caps) and $\sigma_u$ is the surprise it leaves unexplained. Under the uniform distribution both terms have a sharp meaning for verifiers that use (part of) $C$ only through evaluations: in the worst case (planted subcubes), each bit of argument saves at most a factor two in evaluations for lower bounds, and once an envelope is certified, a verifier that accesses the rest of $C$ only through evaluations needs about $2^{\sigma_u}$ of them for two-sided intervals on some circuits, whatever further argument it reads; the envelope estimator achieves this.
- **Limits.** No procedure beats sampling on all circuits (unless NP ⊆ BPP; and with exponentially hard PRGs, not by more than $p^{-o(1)}$). Under the uniform distribution (or any $D$ whose collision probability $\sum_x D(x)^2$ is much smaller than $1/T$), a verifier that only evaluates $C$ cannot certify an upper bound below about $1/T$ with $T$ evaluations, whatever the argument. No argument system is complete for upper bounds unless coNP ⊆ MA. On circuits with short refutations in the verifier's proof system (and, for resolution, cutting planes and Frege-type systems, padded versions with exactly certified $p>0$), a procedure without a prover can be complete iff the corresponding slice of the system's canonical pair is separable in randomized polynomial time; so the prover helps strictly for TC⁰-Frege, Frege and extended Frege if Blum integers cannot be factored by polynomial-size circuits (and two-sidedly, with a verifier that checks proofs in a theory such as ZFC, if factoring is not in BPP). Whether weak automatability removes the prover's advantage on general envelope classes is open. Upper-bound and two-sided separations cannot rest on NP-hardness under randomized many-one reductions to gap problems with gap at least $1+1/\mathrm{poly}$, unless coNP ⊆ AM. With interaction (sum-check) the problem becomes trivial, so the one-message model is the interesting one.
- **Experiments** (pure Python with numpy; times are wall-clock on a shared 4-core machine and indicate orders of magnitude only). An 80-bit trigger ($p\approx4\cdot10^{-25}$) is computed exactly with no samples, and one on a dense 20-variable body ($p\approx2.7\cdot10^{-25}$) to ratio 1.03 with $1.8\cdot10^5$ evaluations; an obfuscated DNF with $p=2.2\cdot10^{-16}$ costs $1.6\cdot10^5$ evaluations, where Monte Carlo needs $1.5\cdot10^{19}$; an OR of eight window-CNFs on 800 variables ($p\approx4\cdot10^{-46}$) is estimated to 0.4% from 1,024 samples after context-width compilation; a unate CNF with $2\cdot10^5$ clauses is pinned to $[6.18,7.35]\cdot10^{-22}$ deterministically in 71 s. When $d$ dense leaves must be sampled and multiplied, the cost grows like $d^3$, and Monte Carlo is cheaper on ANDs of up to three dense 16-variable blocks. With SAT-solver proofs as arguments, the verifier certifies a 20-bit SPN-hidden trigger on which Part 1 gives no answer within 900 s; the solver's DRUP proofs grew from 329 to 32,910 steps as the trigger length went from 12 to 24, even for linear hashes (evidence, not a lower bound). In 2,250 coverage trials, 750 of them with dishonest arguments (300 with malformed envelopes), no interval missed $p$; reference values are exact rationals (enumeration, products of block probabilities, Poisson-binomial tails) and are compared with the interval ends without slack.

## Contents

- **`paper/`**: the paper (`main.pdf`) and its LaTeX sources.
- **`code/rareprob/`**: the Python implementation.
  - `circuit.py`, `dist.py`: circuits with bit-parallel evaluation, threshold gates evaluated exactly (stored in an equivalent integer form) and constants normalised to 0/1; product distributions with exact Bernoulli sampling.
  - `exact.py`: exact output probabilities by enumeration (in rational arithmetic, or in floating point for speed), exact DNF probabilities and Poisson-binomial tails, and an independent gate-by-gate reference evaluator, for tests and reference values.
  - `nnf.py`: negation normal form, restriction, small-cone canonicalisation.
  - `envelope.py`, `compile.py`: envelope circuits, the envelope estimator with dense-leaf boxes, and the sound envelope compiler.
  - `dfsb.py`: Decompose–Force–Sample–Branch (atom size fixed at 12 variables). `meta.py`: the meta-procedure (variable elimination, Harris–Janson, tilting, tilted Glauber sampling for local thresholds, the affine presolve and affine envelopes, context width and DFSB, each at its share of δ, intersected).
  - `ctxwidth.py`: context-width compilation of any circuit into a DNNF. `cftp.py`: bounding-chain coupling from the past and certified telescoping for unate CNFs.
  - `tilting.py`: exponential tilting for thresholds of blocks, and proposal-based lower bounds. `janson.py`: the Harris–Janson sandwich. `ve.py`: exact variable elimination (greedy min-degree order).
  - `localthr.py`: local threshold circuits (overlapping scopes): Dobrushin certificates, systematic-scan heat-bath and monotone CFTP samplers, the thermodynamic-integration normaliser interval, and the sound estimator TG.
  - `affine.py`: GF(2) machinery, certified affine gates, the exact affine/triangular presolve, affine-union envelopes with Karp–Luby–Madras sampling, the linear-order dynamic program for affine subspaces under product distributions (masses with proved floating-point error bounds), and the verifier for Part-2 XPT arguments.
  - `cnf.py`, `rup.py`, `argument.py`: Tseitin encodings, unit propagation, Gaussian-elimination forcing, a DRUP checker, and the Part-2 verifier with an honest prover.
  - `stats.py`: KL confidence intervals (allowing for floating-point rounding), the sequential estimator, the DKLR stopping rule (no estimate if its sample cap is reached first).
  - `families.py`: benchmark circuit families.
- **`code/tests/`**: pytest suite (exactness of masses and samplers, interval coverage, checker soundness, end-to-end arguments).
- **`experiments/`**: `exp_speedup.py`, `exp_coverage.py`, `exp_arguments.py`, `exp_ctx.py`, `exp_janson.py`, `exp_cftp.py`, `exp_local.py`, `exp_affine.py`, `check_second_order.py`, `check_independence_numbers.py`, and their outputs in `results/`; `make_figures.py` and `make_tables.py` regenerate the paper's figures and the tables of its experiments section (the local-threshold and affine tables are transcribed from `results/local.json` and `results/affine.json`).
- **`verification/`**: the AI referee reports on the theorems, and how each finding was resolved.
