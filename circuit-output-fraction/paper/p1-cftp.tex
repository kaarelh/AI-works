\subsection{Certified telescoping with Las Vegas samplers}\label{sec:cftp}

Annealing estimators write $p=\prod_kr_k$ with $r_k=\Pr_{\pi_{k-1}}[\neg B_k]$ and
$\pi_{k-1}=D\mid F_{<k}$, and estimate each ratio by sampling from $\pi_{k-1}$. They
are sound only if the samples really come from $\pi_{k-1}$; Markov chains give
no certificate of that. \emph{Exact} Las Vegas samplers do, if their running time
is certified empirically:

\begin{proposition}[certified truncation]\label{prop:truncation}
Let $\mathcal A$ halt with probability $1$ and output exactly $\pi$, with running
time $\tau$. Truncated at $T$ and conditioned on halting, its output law
$\pi_T$ satisfies $d_{\rm TV}(\pi_T,\pi)\le\Pr[\tau>T]$. If at most $\eta R/2$ of
$R=\lceil8\ln(1/\delta)/\eta\rceil$ truncated runs fail, then $\Pr[\tau>T]\le\eta$ except
with probability $\delta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\pi=(1-\theta)\pi_T+\theta\pi_{>T}$ with $\theta=\Pr[\tau>T]$, so
$\pi_T-\pi=\theta(\pi_T-\pi_{>T})$. If $\theta>\eta$, then by Chernoff at most
$\eta R/2$ failures occur with probability at most $e^{-\eta R/8}\le\delta$.
\end{proof}

Let $C$ be unate (\cref{sec:independence}), flipped so that the events $B_i$ are
increasing and $q_v$ are the flipped marginals. Then every
$F_X=\bigwedge_{i\in X}\neg B_i$ is a down-set, and $D(F_X)\ge\prod_{i\in X}(1-b_i)>0$
by Harris when all $b_i<1$. Fix $F=F_X$ with $D(F)>0$ and let $\phi_v(x)=1$ iff no event
$B_i$, $i\in X$, with $v\in S_i$ occurs at $x^{v\to1}$; $\phi_v$ is decreasing. The
heat-bath Glauber dynamics for $D\mid F$ picks a uniform site $v$ and a uniform
$u\in[0,1]$ and sets $x_v\leftarrow[u<q_v]\wedge\phi_v(x)$. It is anti-monotone in the
sense of Häggström and Nelander: $\Pr[x_v=1\mid x_{-v}]$ is decreasing in $x_{-v}$.
Their bounding-chain coupling from the past \cite{hn1998,pw1996} runs, with common
randomness, a lower chain $L$ and an upper chain $U$ from $L=0$, $U=\one$ at time
$-T$, updating $L_v\leftarrow[u<q_v]\wedge\phi_v(U)$ and $U_v\leftarrow[u<q_v]\wedge\phi_v(L)$.
Then $L\le X\le U$ for every chain $X$ started in $F$, so if $L=U$ at time $0$ that
state is an exact sample of $D\mid F$; otherwise $T$ is doubled, reusing the
randomness of times $-1,\dots,-T$. It halts almost surely: in any window of $n$
steps, with positive probability the sites with $q_v<1$ are updated first, each
with $u\ge q_v$, and then the sites in $W=\{v:q_v=1\}$; after the first phase both
chains are $\le\one_W$, and $\one_W\in F$ (as $D(F)>0$ and $F$ is a down-set), so
every update of the second phase sets $1$ in both chains, and $L=U=\one_W$ at the
end. Disjoint windows are independent.

\begin{theorem}[certified telescoping]\label{thm:cftp}
Let $C$ be unate, flipped as above, $0<\eps\le1$, $\eps'=\ln(1+\eps)$ and
$0<\delta<1$. Let $b_i^+\ge b_i$ be upper bounds: $b_i^+=b_i$, a product of
marginals, if $C$ is a CNF; otherwise the upper end of the Hoeffding--KL interval
(\cref{sec:envelopes}) for $b_i$ from samples of $x_{S_i}$, at level $\delta/(3m)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item If some $b_i^+=1$, output $[0,0]$ if $C$ is a CNF and $[0,1]$ otherwise.
\item \emph{Stages:} scan the events in a fixed order, adding each to the current
stage unless that brings $\rho_k=\prod_{i\in S_k}(1-b_i^+)$ below $1/2$, in which
case it opens a new stage. Let $S_1,\dots,S_K$ be the stages, $\rho=\min_k\rho_k$,
$\Lambda=\sum_k1/\rho_k$ and $\eta=\eps'\rho/(16K)$. Let $\mathcal A_k$ be the
sampler above for $\pi_{k-1}=D\mid F_{<k}$, $F_{<k}=F_{S_1\cup\dots\cup S_{k-1}}$, with
horizons doubling up to $T$, and $\theta_k=\Pr[\mathcal A_k$ does not halt$]$.
\item \emph{Certificates:} for each $k$, run the test of \cref{prop:truncation} on
$\mathcal A_k$ at level $\eta$, with $\delta/(3K)$ in place of $\delta$; if one
fails, output $[0,1]$.
\item \emph{Estimates:} in each of $R=\lceil8\ln(3/\delta)\rceil$ rounds and for each
$k$, run $\mathcal A_k$ until $N=\lceil256\Lambda/\eps'^2\rceil$ runs have halted, and
let $\hat r_k$ be the fraction of their outputs at which no event of $S_k$ occurs.
Output $[\hat Me^{-\eps'/2},\min\{1,\hat Me^{\eps'/2}\}]$, with $\hat M$ the median
over the rounds of $\prod_k\hat r_k$.
\end{enumerate}
This procedure is sound for every unate circuit and every $T$; unless it outputs
$[0,1]$, its output is exact or has $U\le(1+\eps)L$. If $\theta_k\le\eta/16$ for all
$k$, it outputs $[0,1]$ in step~(3) with probability at most $\delta/3$; this holds when
$\gamma=1-q_{\max}d_{\max}(k_{\max}-1)>0$ and $T\ge(n/\gamma)\ln(16n/\eta)$, where
$q_{\max}=\max_vq_v$, every variable lies in at most $d_{\max}$ supports and
$k_{\max}=\max_i|S_i|$. It uses $K\lceil8\ln(3K/\delta)/\eta\rceil$ runs for the
certificates and, if all $\theta_k\le\eta$, at most $RKN/(1-\eta)$ runs in expectation
for the estimates, each of at most $2T$ updates of two chains. If
$\max_ib_i^+\le1/2$, then $\rho\ge1/2$, $\Lambda\le2K$ and $K\le4\mu^++1$ with
$\mu^+=\sum_ib_i^+$, so this is
$O\big(K^2\log(K/\delta)/\eps+K(\mu^++1)\log(1/\delta)/\eps^2\big)$ runs, and
$T=O((n/\gamma)\log(nK/\eps))$ suffices when $\gamma>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Soundness.} In step~(1) for a CNF, $b_i=1$ and $p=0$. Otherwise all $b_i^+<1$,
so $\rho>0$. Let $G$ be the event that $b_i^+\ge b_i$ for all $i$ (certain for a
CNF, of probability at least $1-\delta/3$ otherwise) and that every $\mathcal A_k$
whose certificate passes has $\theta_k\le\eta$ (by \cref{prop:truncation} and a union
bound, given the stages, this fails with probability at most $\delta/3$). Assume $G$
and that step~(4) is reached. By Harris, as the $\neg B_i$ are decreasing,
$r_k=D(F_{\le k})/D(F_{<k})\ge D(F_{S_k})\ge\prod_{i\in S_k}(1-b_i)\ge\rho_k$. Halted
runs of $\mathcal A_k$ output i.i.d.\ samples of a law $\pi'_{k-1}$ with
$d_{\rm TV}(\pi'_{k-1},\pi_{k-1})\le\theta_k$, whatever the stopping rule, so
$r'_k=\pi'_{k-1}(F_{S_k})$ has $|r'_k-r_k|\le\eta\le\rho_k/16$.
\emph{Bias:} $Z'=\prod_kr'_k$ satisfies $Z'/p\in[(1-\eta/\rho)^K,(1+\eta/\rho)^K]
\subseteq[1-\eps'/16,e^{\eps'/16}]$. \emph{Variance:} in a round the $\hat r_k$ are
independent with $\E\hat r_k=r'_k$ and relative variance
$(1-r'_k)/(Nr'_k)\le(1-\rho_k+\eta)/((\rho_k-\eta)N)\le2/(\rho_kN)$, so
$\hat Z=\prod_k\hat r_k$ has $\E\hat Z=Z'$ and
$\E\hat Z^2/Z'^2\le e^{2\Lambda/N}\le e^{\eps'^2/128}\le1+\eps'^2/64$; by Chebyshev,
$\Pr[|\hat Z/Z'-1|>\eps'/4]\le1/4$. By Hoeffding, at least half of the $R$
independent rounds deviate with probability at most $e^{-R/8}\le\delta/3$; otherwise
$\hat M/Z'\in[1-\eps'/4,1+\eps'/4]$. As $\eps'\le\ln2$,
$(1-\eps'/4)(1-\eps'/16)\ge e^{-\eps'/2}$ and $(1+\eps'/4)e^{\eps'/16}\le e^{\eps'/2}$,
so $p\in[\hat Me^{-\eps'/2},\hat Me^{\eps'/2}]$, and $U/L\le e^{\eps'}=1+\eps$. The three
failure events have total probability at most $\delta$.

\emph{Certificates.} If $\theta_k\le\eta/16$, the number $X$ of failures among the
$R_k=\lceil8\ln(3K/\delta)/\eta\rceil$ runs has $\E X\le\eta R_k/16$, so by Chernoff
$\Pr[X>\eta R_k/2]\le(e/8)^{\eta R_k/2}\le(e/8)^{4\ln(3K/\delta)}\le\delta/(3K)$.

\emph{Coalescence.} Let $D_t$ be the set of sites where $L$ and $U$ differ. An update
of $v$ leaves $L_v\ne U_v$ only if $u<q_v$ and $\phi_v(L)\ne\phi_v(U)$, which needs a
site $w\in D_t\setminus\{v\}$ sharing a support with $v$; each $w$ has at most
$d_{\max}(k_{\max}-1)$ such $v$. So
$\E[|D_{t+1}|\mid D_t]\le(1-1/n)|D_t|+(q_{\max}/n)d_{\max}(k_{\max}-1)|D_t|=(1-\gamma/n)|D_t|$,
and $\theta_k\le\E|D_0|\le ne^{-\gamma T/n}\le\eta/16$ for the chains started at
$-T$. (Coalescence from $-T'$ implies coalescence from $-T\le-T'$: the updates
preserve $L'\le L\le U\le U'$ for the pairs $(L,U)$ started at $-T$ and $(L',U')$
started at $-T'$.)

\emph{Cost.} Horizons doubling up to $T$ cost at most $2T$ updates per run, and a run
halts with probability $1-\theta_k$. A stage accepts a further event only if
$\rho_k$ stays $\ge1/2$, so an event with $b_i^+>1/2$ is a stage of its own and every
other stage has $\rho_k\ge1/2$. If $\max_ib_i^+\le1/2$, then $\rho\ge1/2$ and
$\Lambda\le2K$; with
$s_i=-\ln(1-b_i^+)\le2\ln2\,b_i^+$ (convexity on $[0,1/2]$), stage $k<K$ was closed
because $\sum_{i\in S_k}s_i+s_{j}>\ln2$ for the first event $j$ of $S_{k+1}$, and
summing over $k<K$ gives $(K-1)\ln2<2\sum_is_i\le4\ln2\,\mu^+$. In general the same
count gives $\Lambda\le8\mu^++2+2\sum_{i:\,b_i^+>1/2}(1-b_i^+)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

This covers, for instance, hard-core-type instances where Janson's $\Delta$ is
extensive and the local-lemma conditions fail; beyond the provable regime the method
stays sound and decides empirically whether it can answer.
The implementation (\texttt{cftp.py}, for unate CNFs) changes only the bookkeeping:
it takes $N$ rather than $\eps$, pools the estimation runs of stage $k$ into a
Hoeffding--KL upper bound $\theta_k^+$ on $\theta_k$ instead of the test of
\cref{prop:truncation}, requires $\theta_k^+<\rho_k$, and widens the interval by the
bias factors $e^{\pm a_k}$, $a_k=\theta_k^+/(\rho_k-\theta_k^+)$ (as
$r_k/r'_k\in[1/(1+\theta_k/r_k),1/(1-\theta_k/r_k)]$), and by a Chebyshev bound
computed from the per-stage relative variances
$(1-\rho_k+\theta_k^+)/((\rho_k-\theta_k^+)n_k)$, with $n_k$ the realised numbers of
halted runs; the same argument applies, and \texttt{telescoping\_estimate} states the
resulting guarantee, including the effect of floating-point rounding. Karp--Luby--Madras
sampling should extend it to ORs of unate $\wedge$-circuits with different
polarities, given estimates of each disjunct's probability from \cref{thm:cftp} and
certified samplers for each disjunct's conditional law (at truncation level
$O(\eps/t)$ for $t$ disjuncts, as the KLM summand is at least $1/t$); we have not
worked out that error budget.
