\title{Beating Monte Carlo on rare circuit outputs:\\ envelopes, proof-carrying sampling, and their limits}
\author{Claude (Anthropic), prompted by Kaarel H\"anni\\[2pt]
\small Research note, October 2026. Draft; proofs checked by AI referees, not by humans.}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Given a Boolean circuit $C$ and a product distribution $D$, how can one estimate the
tiny probability $p=\Pr_D[C(x)=1]$ faster than Monte Carlo, whose cost grows like
$1/p$, without ever being wrong? We study procedures that are sound on \emph{every}
circuit (their confidence intervals are valid with probability $1-\delta$) and
efficient on as wide a class as possible, first without help and then with an
unbounded prover who sends a single argument to a randomized polynomial-time
verifier. The organising principle is that \emph{upper bounds need deduction, lower
bounds need proposals, and sampling pays for the surprise that is left}. An
\emph{envelope}, a tractable $\varphi\ge\one_C$ with computable mass, turns a
deduction into an importance sampler with bounded weights whose cost is the
looseness $K=\E_D\varphi/p$. Without a prover, a Decompose--Force--Sample--Branch
procedure is sound everywhere and costs $\poly\cdot K$ on every circuit whose ideal
$K$-explanation tree is polynomial: products and read-once compositions of dense
sub-circuits, backdoor triggers, small cutsets, DNFs and DNNFs, and their locally
disguised versions. Every circuit of logarithmic \emph{context width} compiles to a
polynomial-size DNNF and so has an FPRAS (covering, e.g., ORs of bounded-pathwidth
CNFs, whose exact counting is $\#\mathsf P$-hard). For unate circuits the
presumption of independence is certified by Harris--Janson sandwiches (to second
order, with a matching barrier for pairwise data), and annealing becomes sound with
exact Las Vegas samplers whose truncation is certified empirically. Further modules
cover bounded treewidth and thresholds of independent blocks (exponential tilting,
$K=O(\sqrt k)$). With a prover, proof-carrying importance sampling, checking
implications in any Cook--Reckhow proof system, is sound against every argument and
complete for circuits whose rarity has a short proof of confinement to a tractable
region; its cost is $\poly(|\pi|)+2^{\sigma_u}$ with $\sigma_u$ the unexplained
surprise, and each bit of argument buys at most one bit of surprise. We prove the
limits: no procedure beats sampling on all circuits (unless $\NP\subseteq\BPP$, and by
more than $p^{-o(1)}$ under exponentially hard PRGs); verifiers that only evaluate $C$
cannot certify upper bounds below $1/T$; no argument system is complete for upper
bounds unless $\coNP\subseteq\MA$; and the prover's advantage for upper bounds is
exactly the non-weak-automatability of its proof system. A Python implementation
illustrates the gains (e.g.\ $p\approx 4\cdot10^{-46}$ to $0.4\%$ from $10^3$
samples) and the soundness of the intervals.
\end{abstract}
