\subsection{How much stronger is the prover?}\label{sec:separations}

\paragraph{A universal verifier.} Fix a consistent theory $T$ that proves every
true $\Delta_0(\exp)$ sentence (an arithmetic sentence with exponentiation all of
whose quantifiers are bounded), for instance any consistent extension of
$I\Delta_0+\exp$, such as ZFC. A $V_T$-argument is a randomized program $A$, a time
bound $t$ written in unary, a budget $\delta_A\le\delta$, and a $T$-proof of the
$\Delta_0(\exp)$ sentence ``at most $\delta_A2^t$ strings $r\in\bits^t$ make $A$,
run for $t$ steps on $(C,D)$ with coins $r$, fail to output an interval containing
$\sum_xD(x)C(x)$''. $V_T$ checks the proof and runs $A$ for $t$ steps (outputting
$[0,1]$ if $A$ has not halted), in time polynomial in the length of the argument.
It is sound: if the sentence were false, its negation would be a true
$\Delta_0(\exp)$ sentence, which $T$ also proves, and $T$ would be inconsistent. It
$p$-simulates every argument system $V'$ with a polynomial time bound $t'$ for which
$T$ proves, as one universally quantified sentence, that $V'$ clocked at $t'$ is
sound: in polynomial time an argument $\pi'$ for $V'$ becomes the $V_T$-argument made
of $V'(\cdot,\cdot,\pi')$, $t=t'(|C|,|\pi'|)$, $\delta$ and the instance of that
$T$-proof at $(C,D,\pi')$ (of polynomial size, with numerals in binary), on which
$V_T$ has the output law of $V'$ clocked at $t'$. So $V_T$ can use any FPRAS,
compiler, mixing-time bound or combinatorial theorem as a certified rule. This is
the analogue, for estimation, of proof systems that are optimal relative to a
theory \cite{kp1989}.

\paragraph{Lower bounds: the prover helps unless $\NP\subseteq\BPP$.}
Under the uniform distribution a uniquely satisfiable circuit has $p=2^{-n}$, and
one witness certifies the tight lower bound $L=p$ (a witness list,
\cref{sec:lower}). If a sound efficient procedure without a prover outputs some
$L>0$ with probability at least $1-\delta$ on every uniquely satisfiable circuit,
then $\NP\subseteq\BPP$: run it with $\delta$ of order $1/n$ on $O(n)$ independent
Valiant--Vazirani restrictions of a formula \cite{vv1986}, which include a uniquely
satisfiable one with probability at least $3/4$ if the formula is satisfiable and
are all unsatisfiable otherwise, and answer ``satisfiable'' iff some run gives
$L>0$. The matching upper bound needs a proof of uniqueness: a system certifying
$p$ within a factor below $2$ on every uniquely satisfiable circuit would put
$\coNP$ in $\MA$, since $\mathrm{pad}_0$ (below) maps unsatisfiable circuits to
uniquely satisfiable ones with $p=2^{-n-1}$ and satisfiable ones to circuits with
$p\ge2^{-n}$ (argue as for \cref{thm:ma}).

One-way permutations give an average-case version. Let $f$ be a one-way
permutation of $\bits^k$ and $C_y(x,r)=[(f(x),r,\langle x,r\rangle)=y]$ on $2k$
inputs. For $y=(f(x_0),r_0,b)$ with $x_0,r_0,b$ uniform,
$p(C_y)=2^{-2k}[b=\langle x_0,r_0\rangle]$, and on positive instances the preimage
$(x_0,r_0)$ certifies $L=p$. A sound polynomial-time procedure without a prover
outputs $L>0$ on a random positive instance $(f(x_0),r_0,\langle x_0,r_0\rangle)$
with probability at most $\delta+\mathrm{negl}(k)$: otherwise, run on
$C_{(f(x_0),r_0,b)}$ for a random bit $b$, answering $b$ if $L>0$ and $1-b$
otherwise, it would predict the Goldreich--Levin bit $\langle x_0,r_0\rangle$ from
$(f(x_0),r_0)$ \cite{gl1989}. Two-sided certificates on these instances also need a
proof that $f$ is injective (for $V_T$, a $T$-proof).

\paragraph{Upper bounds on refutable circuits.} For a proof system $P$ and a
polynomial $m$ let $Z_P^m$ be the set of circuits $C$ with a refutation
$C\vdash_P\bot$ (\cref{sec:pcis}) of size at most $m(|C|)$, and $\mathrm{SAT}$ the
set of satisfiable circuits; $(Z_P^m,\mathrm{SAT})$ is a slice of the canonical
disjoint $\NP$ pair of $P$ \cite{razborov1994pairs}. For a set $\mathcal Z$ of
unsatisfiable circuits, $(\mathcal Z,\mathrm{SAT})$ is \emph{separable in randomized
polynomial time} if a randomized polynomial-time algorithm accepts every
$C\in\mathcal Z$ and rejects every $C\in\mathrm{SAT}$, each with probability at
least $2/3$. For $a\ge0$ let
\[
\mathrm{pad}_a(C)(\beta,x,w)=(\beta\wedge x=0^n\wedge w=0^a)\vee(\neg\beta\wedge C(x))
\]
on $1+n+a$ inputs and $\mathrm{pad}_a(\mathcal Z)=\{\mathrm{pad}_a(C):C\in\mathcal
Z\}$; under the uniform distribution $p(\mathrm{pad}_a(C))=2^{-n-a-1}+s\,2^{-n-1}$ if
$C$ has $s$ satisfying inputs.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:canonical}
Let $\mathcal Z$ be a set of unsatisfiable circuits and $a\ge0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item A sound efficient procedure without a prover that is complete on $\mathcal Z$
exists iff $(\mathcal Z,\mathrm{SAT})$ is separable in randomized polynomial time.
\item If $(\mathcal Z,\mathrm{SAT})$ is separable in randomized polynomial time, some
sound efficient procedure without a prover is complete on $\mathrm{pad}_a(\mathcal
Z)$. Conversely, if some sound efficient procedure without a prover is complete on
$\mathrm{pad}_a(\mathcal Z)$ under the uniform distribution for some $\eps<2^a$,
then $(\mathcal Z,\mathrm{SAT})$ is separable in randomized polynomial time.
\item If $\mathcal Z\subseteq Z_P^m$ and $P$ is resolution, cutting planes or a
Frege-type system ($\mathrm{TC}^0$-Frege, Frege, extended Frege), the PCIS verifier
with $P$ outputs $[p,p]$ on every circuit of $\mathcal Z$ and of
$\mathrm{pad}_a(\mathcal Z)$, with arguments of size $m(|C|)+\poly(|C|+a)$ ($p=0$
on $\mathcal Z$, and $p=2^{-n-a-1}$ on $\mathrm{pad}_a(\mathcal Z)$ under the uniform
distribution).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Given a separator, amplify it to error $\delta$ by a majority vote, and output
$[0,0]$ if it accepts $C$ and $[0,1]$ otherwise. This is sound for every $C$ and
$D$: if $p_D(C)>0$ then $C$ is satisfiable and $[0,0]$ is output with probability at
most $\delta$, and if $p_D(C)=0$ both outputs contain $p$. On $\mathcal Z$ it
outputs $[0,0]$ with probability at least $1-\delta$. Conversely, run the procedure
under the uniform distribution with $\eps=1$ and $\delta=1/8$, and accept iff $U=0$.
On $C\in\mathcal Z$, $p=0$, and a tight interval containing $p$ has $U\le2L\le2p=0$,
so this happens with probability at least $1-2\delta$. On $C\in\mathrm{SAT}$,
$p\ge2^{-n}$, so $U=0$ means $p\notin[L,U]$, which has probability at most $\delta$.

(b) Given a separator, output $[0,1]$ unless the circuit has the syntactic form
$\mathrm{pad}_a(C)$; then output $[q,q]$ with $q=D(\beta=1,x=0^n,w=0^a)$ if the
amplified separator accepts $C$, and $[0,1]$ otherwise. Since $p=q+D(\beta=0)p_D(C)$,
$p\ne q$ only if $C$ is satisfiable, so this is sound, and it is complete on
$\mathrm{pad}_a(\mathcal Z)$. Conversely, run the procedure on $\mathrm{pad}_a(C)$
under the uniform distribution with $\delta=1/8$, and accept iff
$U<\tau=2^{-n-1}(1+2^{-a})$. On $C\in\mathcal Z$, $p=2^{-n-a-1}$, and with probability
at least $1-2\delta$, $U\le(1+\eps)L\le(1+\eps)p<(1+2^a)2^{-n-a-1}=\tau$. On
$C\in\mathrm{SAT}$, $p\ge\tau$, so $U<\tau$ has probability at most $\delta$.

(c) A \textsc{refute} node with the given refutation outputs $[0,0]$ on $C$. For
$\mathrm{pad}_a(C)$, \textsc{split} on $\beta$: the restriction to $\beta=1$ is
equivalent to the subcube $x=0^n,w=0^a$, an \textsc{exact} node whose two
implications have proofs with $O(n+a)$ steps; the restriction to $\beta=0$ gets a
\textsc{refute} node whose refutation derives $[C=1]$ from the gadget in $O(1)$ steps
and then uses $C$'s refutation. The root interval is $[q,q]$ with $q$ as in (b).
\end{proof}

A proof system $P$ is \emph{weakly automatable} if for some proof system $Q$ an
algorithm finds $Q$-refutations of every unsatisfiable $F$ in time polynomial in
$|F|$ and the size of the shortest $P$-refutation of $F$. Pudl\'ak
\cite{pudlak2003pairs} shows that $P$ is weakly automatable iff its canonical pair,
formulas with a $P$-refutation of size at most $k$ versus satisfiable formulas
(each paired with $1^k$), is separable in \emph{deterministic} polynomial time.
Every slice $(Z_P^m,\mathrm{SAT})$ reduces to it, by
$C\mapsto(\mathrm{Tseitin}(C)\wedge[C=1],1^{m(|C|)})$. Hence, if $P$ is weakly
automatable, the prover gives no advantage on the tasks of \cref{prop:canonical} with $\mathcal Z=Z_P^m$; and, for
the systems of \cref{prop:canonical}(c), it strictly helps on them whenever some
slice is not separable in randomized polynomial time. The two conditions differ by
derandomisation: a randomized separator does not give Pudl\'ak's automatable system,
whose proofs must be checked deterministically. Whether weak automatability of $P$
removes the prover's advantage on larger classes, such as the classes
$\mathcal K_P(m,K)$ of \cref{thm:certenv}, is open: there the argument also
supplies the envelope pieces and the shape of the DAG, which a procedure that can
only test given formulas for short refutations does not obviously find.

\paragraph{Hard slices.} Let $(A,B)$ be a disjoint $\NP$ pair,
$A=\{u:\exists y\,\alpha_{|u|}(u,y)\}$ and $B=\{u:\exists z\,\beta_{|u|}(u,z)\}$
with polynomial-size formulas $\alpha_\ell,\beta_\ell$, such that
$\alpha_\ell(u,y)\wedge\beta_\ell(u,z)$, with $u,y,z$ all variables, has
$P$-refutations of polynomial size. Suppose $P$ is closed under restrictions and
passes from a refutation of a formula to one of its Tseitin encoding with
polynomial overhead (as $\mathrm{TC}^0$-Frege, Frege and extended Frege do, by
deriving the equivalence of each gate variable with its subformula). For $u\in B$
with witness $z_0$, restricting such a refutation by $(u,z_0)$ (the true closed
formula $\beta_\ell(u,z_0)$ has a short proof) shows that the circuit
$C_u=\alpha_{|u|}(u,\cdot)$ lies in $Z_P^m$ for a polynomial $m$; for $u\in A$ it
is satisfiable. So $u\mapsto C_u$ reduces $(A,B)$ to $(\mathcal
Z,\mathrm{SAT})$ with $\mathcal Z=\{C_u:u\in B\}\subseteq Z_P^m$ (cf.\
\cite{razborov1994pairs,pudlak2003pairs}), and a randomized polynomial-time separator
of $(\mathcal Z,\mathrm{SAT})$ separates $(A,B)$ in randomized polynomial time,
hence, by Adleman's argument \cite{adleman1978} (amplify the success probability,
then fix coins that are good for all $u$ of each length), by polynomial-size
circuits. Bonet, Pitassi and Raz \cite{bpr2000} construct such a pair, based on the
Diffie--Hellman function, with polynomial-size $\mathrm{TC}^0$-Frege refutations,
which no polynomial-size circuits separate unless Blum integers can be factored by
polynomial-size circuits (their failure of feasible interpolation); Kraj\'{\i}\v{c}ek
and Pudl\'ak \cite{kp1998} had constructed one for extended Frege from RSA. Frege and
extended Frege $p$-simulate $\mathrm{TC}^0$-Frege, so \cref{prop:canonical} gives:

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:automatability}
Suppose Blum integers cannot be factored by polynomial-size circuits, and let $P$
be $\mathrm{TC}^0$-Frege, Frege or extended Frege. Then there are a polynomial $m$
and a family $\mathcal Z\subseteq Z_P^m$ such that for every $a\ge0$ the PCIS
verifier with $P$ certifies $p$ exactly, with polynomial-size arguments, on
$\mathcal Z$ and on $\mathrm{pad}_a(\mathcal Z)$, while no sound efficient procedure
without a prover is complete on $\mathcal Z$, nor on $\mathrm{pad}_a(\mathcal Z)$
under the uniform distribution for any $\eps<2^a$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $\mathcal Z=\{C_u:u\in B\}$ for the pair of \cite{bpr2000}. Certification is
\cref{prop:canonical}(c). An excluded procedure would, by
\cref{prop:canonical}(a,b), separate $(\mathcal Z,\mathrm{SAT})$ in randomized
polynomial time, and hence $(A,B)$ by polynomial-size circuits.
\end{proof}

If LWE is hard for quantum polynomial-time algorithms, Arteche, Carenini and Gray
\cite{acg2024} show that no polynomial-time quantum algorithm weakly automates
$\mathrm{TC}^0$-Frege. We do not claim the quantum analogue of
\cref{cor:automatability}: it would need that no bounded-error quantum algorithm
separates a slice $(Z_P^m,\mathrm{SAT})$, which does not obviously follow, since a
separator need not output proofs that can be checked deterministically.

Finding the short proofs is a different matter. No polynomial-time algorithm finds
a resolution refutation of a CNF $F$ in time polynomial in $|F|$ and the size of its
shortest refutation unless $\Ptime=\NP$ \cite{atserias2020automating}. This concerns
automatability; \cref{prop:canonical} needs only separability of the slices, which
would follow from weak automatability, and whether resolution is weakly
automatable is, as far as we know, open, so no separation for resolution follows.
For preimage circuits $C_y=[G(x)=y]$ of suitable generators $G$, it is conjectured
that strong proof systems have no short refutations of $C_y$ for
$y\notin\mathrm{range}(G)$ (proof complexity generators
\cite{abrw2004,krajicek2004dual}); if so, the PCIS verifier with such $P$ cannot
certify $p=0$ on them by \textsc{refute}. Excluding certified small positive upper
bounds there would need the stronger hypothesis that there are no short proofs
$C_y\vdash_PG$ for envelope circuits $G$ of mass $n^{-\omega(1)}$.

\paragraph{Two-sided, exact.} Let $\Lambda\in\mathsf{UP}\cap\coNP$, with a
polynomial-time verifier $A(u,v)$, $v\in\bits^k$ ($k$ polynomial in $|u|$), that has
exactly one witness if $u\in\Lambda$ and none otherwise, and a verifier $B$ for the
complement. Let $C_u(\beta,v)=(\beta\wedge v=0^k)\vee(\neg\beta\wedge A(u,v))$, so
that $p(C_u)=2^{-k-1}(1+[u\in\Lambda])$ under the uniform distribution. If $T$ proves
that $A$ has at most one witness and that no $u$ has witnesses for both $A$ and $B$,
then $V_T$ certifies both values exactly, from the witness of $A$ or of $B$ and the
instances of these two theorems. A sound efficient procedure without a prover that
is complete on $\{C_u\}$ for some $\eps<1$ decides $\Lambda$ with error at most
$2\delta$, by answering $u\in\Lambda$ iff $L>2^{-k-1}$ (the two values differ by a
factor $2$). For the bits of the prime factorisation, encoded canonically (prime
factors in increasing order with multiplicity, padded to a fixed length, primality
checked by AKS \cite{aks2004} rather than by certificates, so that the witness is
unique), ZFC proves both theorems, and this gives a two-sided separation under the
worst-case assumption ``factoring $\notin\BPP$'', with $V_T$ complete on both sides.

\paragraph{Why not NP-hardness?} Suppose SAT reduces, by a randomised many-one
reduction, to the gap problem ``$p\ge\gamma t$ versus $p\le t$'' ($\gamma>1$). If
unsatisfiable formulas go to the small side and some MA system certifies
$U<\gamma t$ there (for instance, is complete there with $1+\eps<\gamma$), then
$\coNP\subseteq\AM$: Arthur samples the reduction (repeated in parallel), Merlin
sends arguments, and Arthur accepts if most of them certify $U<\gamma t$; this is an
$\mathsf{AMA}$ protocol, and $\mathsf{AMA}=\AM$ \cite{babai1985}. If unsatisfiable
formulas go to the large side, the set lower bound protocol \cite{gs1986} (after
replacing the circuit by a conjunction of independent copies to make $\gamma$ a
constant) already puts the complement of SAT in $\AM$. A reduction that maps
unsatisfiable formulas to a middle band of $p$ and satisfiable ones outside it is
handled by combining the two protocols. In each case $\PH$ collapses
\cite{bhz1987}. So upper-bound and two-sided separations cannot be derived from
$\NP$-hardness by randomised many-one reductions (adaptive reductions to the gap
problem are not covered); here they come from structured assumptions, such as the
hardness of factoring, while lower-bound separations may come from
$\NP\not\subseteq\BPP$.

\paragraph{Universality.} Upper bounds: no argument system is complete for all
circuits unless $\coNP\subseteq\MA$ (\cref{thm:ma}). Lower bounds, under the uniform
distribution: an argument system complete for lower bounds within a constant factor
on all circuits exists iff $\mathrm{pr}\SBP\subseteq\mathrm{pr}\MA$, for the class
$\SBP$ of \cite{bgm2006}. If $V$ is such a system, with factor $c$, and a promise
problem has a randomized polynomial-time machine that accepts with probability
above $2^{-f}$ on yes-instances and below $2^{-f-1}$ on no-instances, the
conjunction $C$ of $k\ge\log_2c$ runs with independent coins has $p(C)>2^{-kf}$,
respectively $p(C)<2^{-kf}/c$, so accepting iff $V$ outputs $L\ge2^{-kf}/c$ is an
$\MA$ protocol.
Conversely, ``$p(C)>2^{-j}$ versus $p(C)<2^{-j-1}$'' is in $\mathrm{pr}\SBP$; given
an $\MA$ protocol for it, the argument consisting of $j=\lfloor\log_2(1/p)\rfloor+1$
and Merlin's proof, with output $L=2^{-j-1}$ if Arthur accepts and $L=0$ otherwise,
is sound and complete within the factor $4$. A constant factor improves to $1+\eps$
by applying the system to the conjunction of $O(1/\eps)$ independent copies of $C$
and taking the corresponding root of $L$. The inclusion fails relative to an oracle
(one with $\SBP\not\subseteq\Sigma_2^p$ \cite{bgm2006}, while $\MA\subseteq\Sigma_2^p$
relativizes). It holds if $\mathrm{pr}\AM=\mathrm{pr}\NP$, since
$\mathrm{pr}\SBP\subseteq\mathrm{pr}\AM$ by the set lower bound protocol
\cite{gs1986}; the hitting-set derandomisation of Miltersen and Vinodchandran
\cite{mv2005}, which applies to promise problems, gives this if some language in
$\mathsf{NE}\cap\mathsf{coNE}$ needs nondeterministic circuits of size
$2^{\Omega(n)}$, and the verifier's soundness then rests on that hypothesis.
