\subsection{Specialised modules}\label{sec:modules}

Some structures are cheaper to detect and use directly. Each module below is
sound on every input, recognises its class syntactically, and runs in polynomial
time (it declines when its parameter exceeds the stated bound):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Bounded treewidth.} Variable elimination on the Tseitin factor graph
along an elimination order of width $w$ computes $p$ exactly in time
$O(|C|\,2^{w+1})$ \cite{darwiche2009,samer2010}. We use the order of the
5-approximate tree decomposition of \cite{bodlaender2016} (width at most
$5\,\mathrm{tw}+4$, found in time $2^{O(\mathrm{tw})}|C|$), or a greedy order if
that is narrower, and decline if $w>c\log|C|$; so the module is complete when
$5\,\mathrm{tw}+4\le c\log|C|$. (Our implementation uses only a greedy min-degree order
with $w\le18$, and floating point with a proved relative error bound: each table entry
is a mantissa with an integer exponent, so nothing underflows.)
\item \emph{Thresholds of independent blocks.} Exponential tilting
(\cref{prop:tilting}): $K\le\frac43(2\sqrt k+1)$ for $k$ unit-weight blocks;
arbitrary weights under product distributions via the branching-program FPTAS of
\cite{gkm2010} (see also \cite{gopalan2011fptas}).
\item \emph{Unate circuits with sparse overlaps or fast coalescence.}
\Cref{thm:janson,thm:janson-general,thm:cftp}, the last with a polynomial
truncation time $T$ and the cut-off $\Lambda_{\max}=24|C|+3$ (notation of
\cref{thm:cftp}); it is then complete when $\Lambda\le\Lambda_{\max}$ and
$\gamma=1-q_{\max}d_{\max}(k_{\max}-1)>0$ with
$T\ge(n/\gamma)\ln(256n\Lambda_{\max}^2/\eps')$, which implies the condition of
\cref{thm:cftp} on $T$ as $\eta\ge\eps'/(16\Lambda^2)$. The condition on $\Lambda$
holds for CNFs with all $b_i\le1/2$ and, except with probability $\delta/6$, for
other unate circuits with all $b_i\le1/8$, since then $\Lambda\le24\mu+3$ and
$\mu\le m\le|C|$. Clauses are canonicalised first: repeated literals merged,
tautologies dropped.
\item \emph{Small context width.} \Cref{thm:cw}, declining if
$\mathrm{cw}>c\log|C|$, with the \#DNNF FPRAS or, in practice, the envelope
estimator on the compiled DNNF.
\item \emph{Local-lemma regime.} Under the uniform distribution, $k$-CNFs whose
variables lie in at most $d\le2^{c'k}$ clauses, for an absolute constant $c'>0$, admit
FPRASs \cite{moitra2019,fgyz2021,jpv2021,hwy2022}; for $k$-CNFs, $d\lesssim2^{k/4.82}$
suffices up to factors polynomial in $k$ \cite{wangyin2024}. More generally, so do
local CSPs in which every constraint is violated with probability at most $\beta$
and shares variables with at most $\Delta$ others, if $\beta\Delta^{c}\lesssim1$ for
an absolute constant $c$ \cite{fhy2021,jpv2021,hwy2022}. The conditions are
syntactic (for constraints on $O(\log|C|)$ variables, $\beta$ is computed exactly).
\item \emph{Local thresholds.} Unit-weight thresholds of sub-circuits with
overlapping scopes of bounded size and degree, possibly $\wedge$ an arbitrary circuit
(declining if $dk>c\log|C|$): tilted Glauber sampling with a locally checked Dobrushin
certificate (\cref{thm:local}), or monotone coupling from the past for supermodular
statistics (\cref{prop:local-cftp}).
\item \emph{Affine and quadratic structure} (\cref{sec:affine}). Certified affine and
solved-form conjuncts are factored out exactly (\cref{prop:presolve}), unions of affine
subspaces serve as envelopes (\cref{thm:affenv}), affine systems of bounded width are
counted and sampled exactly under every product distribution (\cref{prop:bw-dp}), and
together with one linear threshold they have an FPTAS and an exact sampler
(\cref{prop:affine-ltf}); widths above $c\log|C|$ are declined. Under the uniform
distribution an affine system with at most one quadratic equation is counted exactly:
Gaussian elimination substitutes the affine system into the quadratic polynomial, and
Dickson's normal form of the result gives the exact count and an exact sampler
\cite{ek1990}; the quadric changes the probability inside the affine space by at most
a factor $4$ unless it makes it $0$ (\cref{prop:fourier}). (Systems of several
quadratic equations are $\NP$-hard even to decide.)
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[meta-procedure]\label{thm:meta}
Fix the polynomial parameters of the procedures: $K=|C|^{\alpha}$, $m=|C|^{\beta}$ and
the rule budget $B$ for DFSB; the cut-offs $c\log|C|$ above; for \cref{thm:cftp} a
truncation time $T=|C|^{c}$ and the cut-off $\Lambda_{\max}=24|C|+3$; and the sample
caps and list budgets of the other modules. Run DFSB and every applicable module,
$M$ procedures in all, each at confidence $\delta/(3M)$ and at an accuracy
parameter for which its completeness gives $U\le(1+\eps)L$ (e.g.\
$\eps'=\ln(1+\eps)$ for \cref{thm:janson-general}), with exact modules computing in
rational arithmetic or in floating point with certified error bounds, rounded
outwards. Output the intersection of their intervals, or $[0,1]$ if it is empty.
The result is sound on every circuit, runs in polynomial time, and is complete on
the union of the classes above (for these parameters and the given $\eps$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With probability at least $1-\delta/3$ all $M$ intervals are valid simultaneously,
and then so is their intersection, which is nonempty. Each procedure runs in
polynomial time (\cref{thm:dfsb}, the cut-offs and caps; \cref{thm:cftp} draws
$\poly(|C|,\Lambda_{\max},1/\eps,\log(1/\delta))$ samples and runs, each run of
at most $2T$ updates). If $C$ lies in the class of one procedure, its interval is
tight with probability at least $1-2\delta/(3M)\ge1-2\delta/3$, and a nonempty
intersection $[L',U']\subseteq[L,U]$ with $U\le(1+\eps)L$ has $U'\le(1+\eps)L'$; so
the output is tight with probability at least $1-\delta$.
\end{proof}

Skipping the remaining procedures once some interval is tight changes neither
property, and neither does returning at once an interval that is valid and tight
with certainty. Our implementation does both. In this order it runs variable
elimination, the Janson sandwich for unate CNFs, tilting, the presolve (with DFSB on
$\tilde R$ when all pivots are uniform) and affine envelopes when they explain at
least one bit (presolve factor and envelope mass at most $1/2$), TG, context width and
DFSB; it returns at once the result of variable elimination, and $p=0$ or $p=1$ when
the affine structure or the attainable sums of a block threshold decide $C$; and it
omits the other modules.

\subsection{What remains out of reach}\label{sec:part1-limits}

\Cref{prop:worstcase} rules out beating sampling on all circuits. Under the
randomized exponential-time hypothesis (no bounded-error randomized algorithm
decides 3-SAT in time $2^{o(n)}$; cf.\ \cite{ip2001eth}), no sound procedure that
is complete on all satisfiable 3-CNFs has cost $2^{o(\log(1/p))}\poly(|C|)$ on them:
since $p\ge2^{-n}$ there, running it for $2^{o(n)}\poly(|C|)$ steps and answering
``satisfiable'' iff $L>0$, as in the proof of \cref{prop:worstcase}(a), would decide
3-SAT in time $2^{o(n)}$. A speed-up exponential in the surprise is still possible:
exact model counting by branching on the literals of an unsatisfied clause takes
time $O^*(1.84^n)$, against about $2^n$ samples when $p=2^{-n}$. Three natural kinds of
rarity also defeat the syntactic tools:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Distributed surprise.} In a random 3-CNF with $m=\alpha n$ clauses,
$\alpha>1/3$ small enough that it is satisfiable with high probability, every
clause is satisfied with probability $7/8$ and the rarity comes from $m$
overlapping weak constraints. Any drop-conjuncts relaxation keeps at most $n/3$
pairwise-disjoint clauses, so $D(E)\ge(7/8)^{n/3}$, while $\E p=(7/8)^m$, so that by
Markov's inequality $p\le n(7/8)^m$ with probability at least $1-1/n$; then
$K\ge(8/7)^{m-n/3}/n$, exponential. Annealing or multilevel-splitting estimators
work well in practice here, but their accuracy rests on the mixing of Markov
chains, for which no polynomial-time certificate is known in this regime; exact
Las Vegas samplers (\cref{thm:cftp} for unate circuits, partial rejection
sampling \cite{gjl2019} for CNFs) are known to be fast only in restricted regimes,
and random 3-CNFs are not unate.
\item \emph{Pseudorandom rarity.} Preimages $[G(x)=y]$ of a PRG $G$: by
\cref{prop:worstcase}(b), if $G$ has security $2^{(1-o(1))k}$, nothing beats
sampling on them by more than a factor $2^{o(k)}$.
\item \emph{Algebraic rarity outside the decomposable world.} For events that fill a
$1/\poly(n)$ fraction of the solution set of $m\le n/6$ random parity checks, the
decomposition-based methods of
this section (envelopes with small leaves, context width, DFSB's completeness
guarantee) cost Monte Carlo's price up to polynomial factors, and any use of a DNNF
envelope at least its square root (\cref{cor:barrier-methods}). Gaussian elimination
handles such checks under the uniform distribution, but under $\mathrm{Bern}(1/3)$
inputs small $\wedge$'s of parity checks on $N$ inputs admit no sound procedure complete
within a factor $2^{N^{1-\gamma}}$ (fixed $\gamma>0$) unless $\NP\subseteq\BPP$, and no
argument system complete
within a factor $2$ unless $\coNP\subseteq\MA$ (\cref{thm:biased-hard}).
\end{itemize}
