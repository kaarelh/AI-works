\subsection{Lower bounds need no deduction}\label{sec:lower}

\begin{proposition}[trace-reconstructible proposals]\label{prop:proposal}
The prover supplies a seed law $P_R$ on $\bits^r$ in \emph{self-certifying}
form: circuits $q_i(r_{<i})$, $i\le r$, that output a numerator and a denominator in
binary (an autoregressive law, $\Pr[r_i=1\mid r_{<i}]=q_i$; the verifier reads a zero
denominator as $0$ and clamps every value to $[0,1]$); or a smooth decomposable
probabilistic circuit with nonnegative rational weights, which the verifier normalises
itself (it computes partition functions bottom-up and samples a sum child $c$ with
probability proportional to $w_cZ_c$, as in \cref{lem:envmass}); or the law $D\varphi/Z$
of an envelope (\cref{lem:envmass}); if its total mass is $0$, the lower bound is $0$.
The verifier samples $R\sim P_R$ with its own coins and computes $P_R(R)$ from the
same object and the same clamped or normalised values, so sampler and pmf agree by
construction and need no check, and all numbers have polynomial bit length. The
prover also supplies circuits $\psi:\bits^r\to\bits^n$ (generator) and
$\phi:\bits^n\to\bits^r$ (inverter), and a clip level $\tau>0$. For
$R\sim P_R$ and $X=\psi(R)$ let
\[
W=\one[\phi(X)=R]\,C(X)\min\big(D(X)/P_R(R),\,\tau\big)\in[0,\tau].
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item (Soundness.) For every $C$ and every such $P_R,\psi,\phi,\tau$,
\[
\E W=\sum_{x\in C^{-1}(1):\,\psi(\phi(x))=x}\min\big(D(x),\,\tau P_R(\phi(x))\big)\le p,
\]
so $\tau$ times the lower end of a KL interval for $W/\tau$ at level $\delta$
is a lower bound on $p$ with probability at least $1-\delta$.
\item (Completeness.) If $\tau\le Kp$ and the inputs $x\in C^{-1}(1)$ with
$\psi(\phi(x))=x$ and $D(x)\le\tau P_R(\phi(x))$ have total $D$-mass at least
$(1-\eps/2)p$, then after $N=O(K\eps^{-2}\log(1/\delta))$ samples this lower
bound is at least $(1-\eps)p$ with probability at least $1-\delta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) $\psi$ is injective on $R^*=\{r:\phi(\psi(r))=r\}$ (if
$\psi(r)=\psi(r')=x$ then $r=\phi(x)=r'$) and maps it onto
$\{x:\psi(\phi(x))=x\}$. Hence $\E
W=\sum_{r\in R^*}P_R(r)\,C(\psi(r))\min(D(\psi(r))/P_R(r),\tau)$, and the
substitution $x=\psi(r)$ gives the identity; each term is at most $D(x)$ and
each $x$ occurs once. (Without the trace check each $x$ would be counted once
per preimage and $\E W$ could exceed $p$.) (b) The inputs named in the
hypothesis contribute $D(x)$ each, so $\E W\ge(1-\eps/2)p$. Their inverter
images are distinct, so their mass is at most $\tau\sum_rP_R(r)=\tau$; hence
$\tau\ge(1-\eps/2)p$ and $\mu=\E[W/\tau]\ge(1-\eps/2)/K\ge1/(2K)$. By the
multiplicative Chernoff bound for $[0,1]$-valued variables, after
$N=cK\eps^{-2}\log(1/\delta)$ samples the KL lower end for $W/\tau$ is at least
$(1-\eps/2)\mu$ with probability $1-\delta$, and $(1-\eps/2)^2\ge1-\eps$.
\end{proof}

Special cases: autoregressive models and probabilistic circuits ($r=n$,
$\psi=\phi=\mathrm{id}$), an envelope used without a domination proof (whose
importance weights then give the lower end of \cref{thm:envelope}(iii)),
witness lists (the verifier checks $C(x^{(i)})=1$ for distinct $x^{(i)}$: an
exact lower bound $\sum_iD(x^{(i)})$, no sampling), and generators with
inverters driven by an autoregressive seed law. What must \emph{not} be
accepted is a pmf and a sampler supplied separately: their agreement is a
semantic property the verifier cannot check, and a sampler that over-produces
a seed whose claimed pmf is tiny inflates $\E W$ arbitrarily.

\subsection{Surprise accounting}\label{sec:surprise}

Write $\sigma=\log_2(1/p)$ for the surprise of the event. An envelope argument
explains $\sigma_e=\log_2(1/Z)$ bits deductively and leaves
$\sigma_u=\log_2(Z/p)$ bits to sampling, where $Z$ is the mass of the envelope
actually sampled: $Z=D(E)$ for a deterministic envelope or one sampled by
\cref{thm:sis}, $Z=\E_D\varphi$ for \cref{thm:envelope}, and
$Z=\Lambda=\sum_jD(E_j)$ for a union (\cref{thm:certenv}). Under completeness,
\[
\text{verifier time}\;\le\;\poly(|\pi|,|C|)\;+\;O\big(2^{\sigma_u}\eps^{-2}(\log(1/\delta)+\log(1+\sigma_u))\big)\cdot c_s,
\]
where $|\pi|$ is the size of the deductive part of the argument (envelopes or
pieces, proofs, circuits), not counting the unary sample caps, which an honest prover
sets to the samples needed so that reading them costs no more than the second term;
and $c_s$ is the cost of one sample: $O(|C|+|\Phi|)$ for \cref{thm:envelope} (with
$\Phi=E$ for a deterministic envelope), $|C|$ plus $2n$ counter calls for
\cref{thm:sis}, and $O(|C|+m+n^2+nT)$ for a union (\cref{thm:certenv}). For a
skeleton of $t$ independent components (\textsc{prod} at the top) estimated
separately, each leaf needs ratio $1+\eps/(2t)$ and confidence $\delta/t$
(\cref{prop:calculus}), so the second term becomes
$O(t^2\eps^{-2}(\log(t/\delta)+\log(1+\sigma_u)))\sum_\lambda2^{\sigma_u(\lambda)}\,c_s$:
unexplained surprise spread evenly over $t$ independent components costs about
$t^32^{\sigma_u/t}$ rather than $2^{\sigma_u}$. Both kinds of bits have a sharp
meaning in black-box models. Say that a verifier \emph{accesses} a function $f$
\emph{only through $T$ evaluations} if it computes $f$ at $T$ points, chosen
adaptively by any randomized rule, and uses $f$ in no other way.

\begin{theorem}[proof bits and surprise bits]\label{thm:surprise}
Let $D$ be uniform.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item (Lower bounds.) Let a verifier read an argument of exactly $\ell$ bits and
access $C$ only through $T$ evaluations. Fix a set $I$ of $k$ coordinates and let
$S_b=[x_I=b]$ for $b\in\bits^k$. If the verifier is $\delta$-sound for lower
bounds on all circuits and, for each $b$, some argument makes it output $L>0$
on $\one_{S_b}$ with probability at least $1-\delta$, then $T\cdot2^\ell\ge(1-2\delta)2^k$.
This is tight up to the factor $\eps^{-2}\log(1/\delta)$: an argument consisting of $j$
bits of $b$ lets the verifier sample $x$ uniformly with those bits fixed, which
certifies $L\ge(1-\eps)p$ (\cref{prop:proposal}) after
$T=O(2^{k-j}\eps^{-2}\log(1/\delta))$ evaluations.
\item (Two-sided intervals inside a certified envelope.) Let the inputs be pairs
$(y,x)$ with at least $2k$ $x$-coordinates, and $E=E(y)$ any function of $y$ with
$D(E)>0$. Let a verifier be given $E$ explicitly (as after a checked domination
proof), read an argument of any length, and estimate $p(E\wedge G)$ for a function $G$
that it accesses only through $T$ evaluations. Suppose it is $\delta$-sound for upper
bounds on all circuits $E\wedge G$, i.e.\ on all $C$ with $C\Rightarrow E$, and that
for $G_0=S=[x_I=b]$ with $|I|=k$, so that $p_0=D(E\wedge S)=D(E)2^{-k}$, some argument
$\pi_0$ makes it output with probability at least $1-\delta$ an interval $[L,U]\ni
p_0$ with $U\le(1+\eps)L$, $\eps<1/2$. Then
$T\ge(1-2\delta)D(E)/p_0=(1-2\delta)2^{\sigma_u}$, where $\sigma_u=\log_2(D(E)/p_0)$
is the surprise left by $E$. Conversely, if $D(E)$ is computable and $D\mid E$ can be
sampled exactly (as for the atoms of \cref{thm:certenv}), the estimator of
\cref{thm:envelope} with $\varphi=\one_E$, which reads no argument and evaluates $G$
only at its samples, is sound on all these circuits and meets the hypothesis with
$T=O(2^{\sigma_u}\eps^{-2}(\log(1/\delta)+\log(1+\sigma_u)))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) Couple the run on $\one_{S_b}$ with argument $\pi_b$ and the run on the
zero function with the same argument and the same coins; they differ only if a
query lands in $S_b$. Soundness on the zero function bounds $\Pr[L>0]$ there by
$\delta$, so under $\pi_b$ the run on the zero function hits $S_b$ with
probability at least $1-2\delta$. For a fixed argument $\pi$ that run does not
depend on $b$, and since the $S_b$ partition the cube each query lies in
exactly one of them: $\sum_b\Pr_\pi[\text{hit }S_b]\le T$. Summing over $b$
and over the $2^\ell$ arguments gives $2^k(1-2\delta)\le2^\ell T$. (b) Fix
$i_0\in I$ and a set $J$ of $k$ $x$-coordinates outside $I$, and for uniform
$b''\in\bits^k$ plant $S'=[x_{i_0}=1-b_{i_0}]\wedge[x_J=b'']$, which is disjoint from
$S$, has $D(S')=2^{-k-1}$, and contains each fixed point with probability at most
$2^{-k}$. Then $G_1=S\vee S'$ gives $p_1=D(E\wedge G_1)=1.5\,p_0$ for every $b''$
(as $E$ depends only on $y$), and $E\wedge G_1\Rightarrow E$, so soundness applies to
it. Run the verifier with $\pi_0$ and the same coins on $G_0$ and on $G_1$; the runs
differ only if a query lands in $S'$. On $G_0$, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
$U\le(1+\eps)L\le(1+\eps)p_0<p_1$; on $G_1$, soundness gives $U\ge p_1$ with
probability at least $1-\delta$. So the runs differ with probability at least
$1-2\delta$. The run on $G_0$ does not depend on $b''$; averaging over $b''$, the
expected number of its queries in $S'$ is at most $T2^{-k}$. Hence
$T2^{-k}\ge1-2\delta$. Conversely, $\one_E$ dominates every $C\Rightarrow E$, so
\cref{thm:envelope}(ii) gives soundness; on $E\wedge G_0$ the looseness is
$D(E)/p_0=2^{\sigma_u}$, so \cref{thm:envelope}(iv) at level $\delta/2$ gives the
count; and each sample $X\in E$ costs one evaluation, $C(X)=G(X)$.
\end{proof}

So, up to polynomial factors, certifying a rare event costs $|\pi|+2^{\sigma_u(\pi)}$
for the argument $\pi$ used: a two-part, MDL-like cost in the spirit of surprise
accounting \cite{hilton2024surprise} (which adds bits, $|\pi|+\sigma_u$, where
here the unexplained bits are paid exponentially). \Cref{thm:surprise} gives
the two halves a precise sense: each bit of argument reduces the number of
evaluations needed for a lower bound by at most a factor $2$; and once an
envelope $E$ is certified, a verifier that accesses the rest of $C$ only through
evaluations needs about $2^{\sigma_u}$ of them for a two-sided interval, whatever
further argument it reads, and the envelope estimator achieves this. (\Cref{thm:blackbox}
does not give (b): its planted circuits do not imply $E$.) Neither part bounds
white-box verifiers, which may need no argument and no sampling at all (an explicit
subcube is counted exactly).
