\section{Without a prover: sound procedures and their classes}\label{sec:part1}

A procedure without a prover must find the structure itself. Ours look for
exactly the structure that \cref{sec:envelopes} can exploit: independence
(variable-disjoint sub-circuits), forcing (literals implied by $C(x)=1$),
small cutsets, dense residuals, and a few algebraic and analytic patterns. All
steps are either exact identities under product distributions or Monte Carlo
estimates with valid intervals, so the procedures are sound on every circuit;
which circuits they are \emph{complete} on is guaranteed by the size of a
canonical ``explanation tree''.

\subsection{Decompose--Force--Sample--Branch}\label{sec:dfsb}

Fix $a=\lfloor c\log_2|C|\rfloor$ for a constant $c$ (our implementation fixes
$a=12$), and call a node an \emph{atom} if its support has at most $a$ variables.
Work with the \emph{canonicalised NNF} $N$: the NNF of $C$ in
which every gate of $C$ whose support has at most $a$ variables has been replaced by
its reduced OBDD (rule (R8) of \cref{sec:rules}), flattened with duplicate children
removed, except that an atom is never merged into a larger parent, and with
constants propagated
($x\wedge\neg x\mapsto0$, $x\vee\neg x\mapsto1$); restricting a node by a partial
assignment $\rho$ simplifies it in the same way. Opaque gates
(\cref{sec:setting}) are leaves of this NNF: (F) gets no clauses from them, and (B)
branches on their smallest variable. A node $h$ is handled by the first rule that
applies:

\begin{description}[style=sameline,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item[(T)] $h$ is a constant or a literal: exact value.
\item[(T$'$)] $h$ is an atom: exact value, by summing $D$ over the
$2^{|\sv(h)|}\le|C|^c$ assignments of $\sv(h)$.
\item[(F)] Unit propagation on $\mathrm{Tseitin}(h)\wedge[h=1]$, repeated on the
restricted node until it derives nothing new, derives a nonempty set of input
literals $F$ (at the root, also failed-literal probing and Gaussian elimination on
conjuncts that are affine over $\F_2$): $D(h)=D(F)\cdot D(h|_F)$, since
$h\Rightarrow F$ and $h|_F$ does not depend on the variables of $F$. If it derives a
conflict, $D(h)=0$.
\item[(D)] The children of $h$'s top gate fall into at least two
variable-disjoint groups: $D(h)=\prod_jD(h_j)$ for $\wedge$ and
$D(h)=1-\prod_j(1-D(h_j))$ for $\vee$.
\item[(S)] Dense test: a pilot of $N_0=64K\lceil\ln(2m/\delta)\rceil$ samples
finds at least $N_0/(4K)$ hits; then $h$ is a Monte Carlo leaf.
\item[(E)] Envelope test: a syntactic envelope $\Phi_h$ (\cref{sec:rules})
passes the analogous pilot for $\E_{\hat Q}[h/\varphi]$ (at least $N_0/(4K)$ total
weight in $N_0$ samples of its proposal $\hat Q$); then $h$ is an envelope leaf. If
$\Phi_h$ has boxes, at most $L$ on a derivation and $r_h$ in all, the pilot is run
twice: first with the rough masses found by (R7) (failing it fails the test), then
with masses estimated, each at level $\delta/(4mr_h)$ and with at most
$N(16K,1/(2L),\delta/(4mr_h))$ samples (defined below), to intervals with
$u_j\le(1+\frac1{2L})\ell_j$ and $\ell_j\ge1/(16K)$; if these estimates fail, or
the (truncated) rejection sampler of a box fails, so does the test.
If $\Phi_h$ has no boxes and mass $0$, then $D(h)=0$.
\item[(B)] Otherwise branch on the variable shared by the most children (ties:
smallest index): $D(h)=(1-q_x)D(h|_{x=0})+q_xD(h|_{x=1})$.
\end{description}
Phase 1 builds the tree with a node budget $m$; nodes beyond the budget become
``loose'' leaves. Phase 2 estimates every leaf with fresh samples at confidence
$\delta/\ell$, where $\ell$ is the number of leaves and $d$ the largest number of
leaves on one derivation (all children at (D)-$\wedge$, one child elsewhere), and
combines the leaf intervals through the exact node operations, all of which are
monotone. Let $\eta=\eps/(3d)$. Every estimator is the doubling rule of
\cref{thm:envelope}(iv) run towards ratio $1+\eta'$ at level $\delta'$ and capped at
$N(K',\eta',\delta')=c_0K'\eta'^{-2}(\log(1/\delta')+\log\log K')$ samples, a size by
which it has stopped with probability $1-\delta'$ whenever its mean is at least
$1/K'$ ($c_0$ an absolute constant); at the cap it outputs its current interval,
which is still valid. (S)-leaves (Monte Carlo) and box-free (E)-leaves use
$(16K,\eta,\delta/\ell)$. An (E)-leaf with boxes runs the procedure of
\cref{thm:boxes} (importance sampling towards ratio $1+\eta/4$ at level
$\delta/(2\ell)$, each box towards $1+\eta/(8L)$ at level $\delta/(2\ell r_h)$), with
the cap $\max\{N(64K,\eta/4,\delta/(2\ell)),\,N(16K,\eta/(8L),\delta/(2\ell r_h))\}$
for every stage; each box fill of its rejection sampler is truncated after
$T_{\rm f}=\lceil16K\ln(8N_{\rm f}/\delta)\rceil$ draws, where $N_{\rm f}$, the sum over
these leaves of $L$ times the cap, bounds the number of fills, and a truncated leaf
outputs $[0,1]$.
A loose leaf is estimated by Monte Carlo with any cap up to $N(16K,\eta,\delta/\ell)$.
So DFSB runs in time $\poly(|C|,K,m,B,1/\eps,\log(1/\delta))$ on every circuit, with
$B$ the rule budget of \cref{sec:rules}.

\begin{definition}[ideal explanation tree]
$T^*_K(C)$ is the tree built by the same rules with the pilot test (S)
replaced by the exact predicate $D(h)\ge1/K$ and without (E).
$m_K(C)=|T^*_K(C)|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[DFSB]\label{thm:dfsb}
For every circuit $C$ and product distribution $D$, the output interval
contains $p$ with probability at least $1-\delta$. If $m_K(C)\le m$, then with
probability at least $1-2\delta$ the output is tight ($U\le(1+\eps)L$), and the
number of evaluations of nodes of the canonicalised NNF $N$ is
\[
O\big(mK\log(m/\delta)\big)+\tilde O\Big(\ell\cdot K\,d^2\,\eps^{-2}\log(\ell/\delta)\Big),\qquad
\ell\le m,\ d\le n,
\]
plus $s_7B$ for the (R7) pilots of each (E)-test, and
$\tilde O(r_hL^2Kd^2\eps^{-2}\log(mr_h/\delta))$ box evaluations for each
(E)-test or (E)-leaf whose envelope has boxes. An evaluation costs $O(|N|)$, where
$|N|=O(2^a|C|)$ for $\{\wedge,\vee,\neg,\oplus\}$-circuits (threshold counters add
their size), and $O(|N|+|\Phi_h|)$ at an (E)-leaf plus $O(LK\max_j|B_j|)$ in
expectation for rejection sampling. The remaining work is $O(2^a|C|^2)$ for (R8) and
$O\big(m(n+2^a+B)|N|+n^2(|C|+|N|)+n^3\big)$ arithmetic operations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Soundness. (T), (T$'$), (F), (D), (B) are exact identities under a product
distribution (independence of variable-disjoint sub-circuits, the law of total
probability, and $h\Rightarrow F$), and an envelope of mass $0$ forces $D(h)=0$
because $\varphi\ge\one_h$. Phase 2 runs on a fixed tree with fresh samples; each
leaf interval is valid with probability $1-\delta/\ell$ whatever its cap
(\cref{thm:envelope}(iv), \cref{thm:boxes}); a truncated rejection sampler agrees
with an untruncated one until it stops, and then $[0,1]$ is output. A union bound
over the $\ell$ leaves and interval arithmetic through monotone operations give a
valid root interval. Phase 1 only shapes the tree.

Completeness. Let $X$ be a pilot's hit count and $\mu=\E X$. If $D(h)\ge1/K$ then
$\mu\ge N_0/K$ and $\Pr[X<N_0/(4K)]\le e^{-(3/4)^2\mu/2}\le(\delta/2m)^{18}$. If
$D(h)<1/(16K)$ then $\mu<N_0/(16K)$ and, by the Chernoff bound
$\Pr[X\ge t]\le e^{-\mu}(e\mu/t)^t$, $\Pr[X\ge N_0/(4K)]\le(e/4)^{N_0/(4K)}\le(\delta/2m)^{6}$.
The same two bounds hold for the envelope pilot, whose summands lie in $[0,1]$. So
with probability at least $1-3m(\delta/2m)^6\ge1-\delta/8$ every pilot (at most three
per node) accepts every node of density (or $\E_{\hat Q}W$) $\ge1/K$ and none of
density $<1/(16K)$, and with probability at least $1-\delta/4$ all pilot box intervals
of (E) are valid. On these events the actual tree is a pruning of $T^*_K(C)$ (where
the ideal tree stops, the actual one stops; where it continues, the actual one
continues identically or stops early at an (S)- or (E)-leaf; a failed or truncated
(E)-test leaves the node to the rules of $T^*_K$), so it has at most $m$ nodes and
no loose leaves.
Every (S)-leaf is $16K$-dense; a box-free (E)-leaf has looseness $K_h=Z/p_h$ at most
$16K$; and for an (E)-leaf with boxes, the proof of \cref{thm:boxes} gives
$\E_{\hat Q}W\le(\rho_{\max}/\rho_{\min})/K_h$ for the pilot proposal, where every
derivation has $\rho\in[(1+\frac1{2L})^{-L},(1+\frac1{2L})^{L}]$, so $K_h\le16eK$ and
the boxes are $16K$-dense. In Phase 2, with probability $1-\delta/2$ the box intervals
are valid, and then the importance-sampling mean of an (E)-leaf with boxes is at
least $e^{-\eta/4}/K_h\ge1/(64K)$ (proof of \cref{thm:boxes}). As the boxes have mass at
least $1/(16K)$, each of the at most $N_{\rm f}$ fills exceeds $T_{\rm f}$ draws with
probability at most $(1-\frac1{16K})^{T_{\rm f}}\le\delta/(8N_{\rm f})$, so with
probability $1-\delta/8$ no leaf is truncated. Hence, with probability
$1-\delta$ more, every leaf estimator stops before its cap, with ratio at most
$1+\eta$ (for boxes via \cref{thm:boxes}), which gives the stated counts; and
$(1+\eps/(3d))^d\le e^{\eps/3}$, so the root ratio is at most $1+\eps$: products multiply leaf ratios; positive
combinations with exact weights do not increase them; and for
$f(x)=1-\prod_j(1-x_j)$, which is monotone and concave along rays with
$f(0)=0$, $x'\le bx$ implies $f(x')\le bf(x)$ and $x'\ge ax$ implies $f(x')\ge
af(x)$, so (D)-$\vee$ nodes do not increase ratios either. The failure
probabilities add up to $\delta/8+\delta/4+\delta/2+\delta/8+\delta=2\delta$.
Costs: each evaluation reads the cone of a node of $N$; (R8) replaces at most $2|C|$
gates (one per polarity) by OBDDs of $O(2^a)$ nodes, which gives the bound on $|N|$,
and tabulating each costs $O(2^a|C|)$. Per tree node, (F) costs $O(n|N|)$ (at most $n$
rounds of unit propagation), (T$'$) $O(2^a|N|)$, and building $\Phi_h$ $O(B|N|)$ besides
the pilot evaluations of (R7) (at most $B$ rule applications, $s_7$ evaluations
each); the root's probing and elimination cost $O(n^2(|C|+|N|)+n^3)$.
\end{proof}

With $K=K(|C|)$ and $m=m(|C|)$ fixed polynomials, DFSB therefore beats sampling
on $\{C:m_K(C)\le m\}$, which contains circuits with $p=2^{-\Omega(n)}$. The
tree-size condition is sufficient, not necessary: (E)-leaves can stop the tree
early, and loose leaves under a rare $\vee$-branch need not spoil tightness.
Without (E), on the good event of the proof the actual tree also contains
$T^*_{16K}(C)$ as a pruning, so it avoids loose leaves only if $m_{16K}(C)\le m$. Since
$m_K$ is nonincreasing in $K$, one large $K$ suffices, at a cost proportional to $K$.
The class contains:

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:dfsb}
Let $s=|C|$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Component-dense circuits.} Let the \emph{component tree} of $C$ be the
tree built by (T), (T$'$), (F) and (D) alone. If each of its leaves that is not
exact (by (T), (T$'$) or a refutation) has probability at least $1/K$, then $m_K(C)=O(s)$. This
includes read-once $\{\wedge,\vee,\neg\}$-formulas, computed exactly, and read-once
compositions $F(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_t)$ on disjoint variable sets in which each
$\lambda_i$, in the polarity in which it occurs in $F$ (positive if $F$ is monotone),
satisfies the hypothesis itself: for instance, is an atom, or is $K$-dense and not
decomposable after (F) (forcing does not lower its probability:
$D(\lambda\mid F)=D(\lambda)/D(F)\ge D(\lambda)$ for $\lambda\Rightarrow F$). E.g.\ an
$\wedge$ of $t$ such blocks of probability $1/2$ has $p=2^{-t}$; it costs no samples
if the blocks are atoms, and otherwise $\tilde O(Kt^3\eps^{-2}\log(m/\delta))$
evaluations ($d=\ell=t$, plus the pilots).
\item \emph{Backbones.} If (F) derives a set $F$ with $D(C\mid F)\ge1/K$ (for
instance trigger circuits $R\wedge[x_A=a]$, however the test is written, as long
as propagation sees it), then $m_K(C)=1+m_K(C|_F)$. If $C|_F$ is an atom or not
(D)-decomposable, this is $2$; the cost is then $0$ samples for an atom and otherwise
$O(K\eps^{-2}(\log(1/\delta)+\log\log K)+K\log(m/\delta))$ evaluations (one leaf at
$d=1$, and one pilot), against $2^{|A|}K\eps^{-2}\log(1/\delta)$ for Monte Carlo.
\item \emph{Cutsets.} Let $\mathrm{Conf}(C)$ be the set of input variables that lie
in the supports of two different children of some non-atom gate of the NNF. If
the NNF has no opaque gates and $|\mathrm{Conf}(C)|=r$, then $m_K(C)=O(s\,3^r)$ for
every $K$, and with (S) and (E) disabled and a node budget $m$ at least the size of the
resulting tree, DFSB is exact cutset conditioning \cite{pearl1988}: $O(s\,3^r)$ nodes
and no sampling.
\item \emph{Global variables.} If fixing some set $Z$ of $r$ variables makes $C$
satisfy (a), the variant that first enumerates $Z$ uses $O(2^rs)$ nodes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The formula at a tree node is a restriction of an NNF node, so its support is
contained in that node's; and since atoms are never merged into larger parents,
two distinct children of a non-atom tree node descend from two different children
of a non-atom NNF gate (the gate at which their paths diverge).

(c) Disable (S) and (E). (B) applies only at a non-decomposable non-atom node, on
a variable shared by two of its children, which lies in $\mathrm{Conf}(C)$ by the
remark above; once $\mathrm{Conf}(C)\cap\sv(h)$ is assigned, every non-atom node
below $h$ is decomposable, so all leaves are exact ((T), (T$'$), or $0$). For a
tree node $t$ let $\sigma_t\in\{0,1,*\}^{\mathrm{Conf}(C)}$ record the values given
to $\mathrm{Conf}(C)$ on the path to $t$. Call $t$ \emph{terminal} if its formula is
not constant but all its children's formulas are. Two distinct nodes with the same
$\sigma$, each a (B)-node or terminal, are not ancestor and descendant, and their
lowest common ancestor is a (D)-node (an (F)-node has one child, and a (B)-node
would separate their $\sigma$); so their formulas lie in different groups and have
disjoint nonempty supports. Hence there are at most $s$ such nodes per $\sigma$,
$O(s3^r)$ in all. The other nodes are constant leaves (at most two per (B)-node or
terminal node), (D)-nodes (fewer than the leaves) and (F)-nodes (each with a
non-(F) child, as (F) runs to a fixpoint); so the tree has $O(s3^r)$ nodes, and
$T^*_K$, which stops no later, is a pruning of it.

(a) In $T^*_K$ every leaf of the component tree that is not exact is accepted by
(S), so $T^*_K(C)$ is the component tree, which has $O(s)$ nodes by the count in
(c) (it has no (B)-nodes, so all $\sigma$ are empty). In a read-once formula every
non-atom gate is decomposable, so all leaves are exact. In a read-once composition
(F) and (D) act on each $\lambda_i$ as on $\lambda_i$ alone, since the $\lambda_i$ share
no variables, so the leaves of the component tree of $C$ are those of the $\lambda_i$.
(b) and (d) are immediate.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Envelope leaves and sound rewriting rules}\label{sec:rules}

Nodes that are rare but not decomposable get an envelope from the following
rules, each of which produces $\varphi\ge\one_h$ pointwise, so any combination is
sound (\cref{thm:envelope}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R\arabic*)]
\item literal $\mapsto$ literal; $\vee(c_1,\dots,c_k)\mapsto\sum_j\varphi_{c_j}$ (union bound);
\item $\wedge$ with forced literals $F$ $\mapsto\prod_{\ell\in F}[\ell]\cdot\varphi_{h|_F}$;
\item $\wedge$ with variable-disjoint children $\mapsto\prod_j\varphi_{c_j}$;
\item $\wedge$ with a shared variable $u$ (at most a fixed number of times on a
path, and only while $|\Phi|$ is below a fixed polynomial budget)
$\mapsto\sum_b[u=b]\cdot\varphi_{h|_{u=b}}$ (Shannon expansion);
\item $\wedge$ otherwise $\mapsto$ product over a greedy variable-disjoint subset of
conjuncts, most informative first (dropping conjuncts);
\item anything $\mapsto1$, used whenever the rules give mass $>1$, and for every
further node once $B$ rules have been applied ($B$ a fixed polynomial);
\item a dense node $\mapsto$ a box (\cref{thm:boxes}), when a pilot of $s_7$
samples finds hit rate at least $\tau$ ($s_7$ and $\tau$ fixed constants; the hit rate
is the box's rough mass) and, unless the node is opaque, the other rules give more
than twice that mass;
\item \emph{small-cone canonicalisation}: every gate whose support has at most
$a$ variables is replaced, before anything else, by its reduced OBDD in the order
of the variable indices, computed by tabulation; in DFSB these are atoms, exact
leaves by (T$'$). Equal functions have equal reduced OBDDs, which hash-consing
identifies, so replacing a sub-circuit computing a gate (whose internal gates feed
nothing else) by an equivalent circuit with the same support of at most $a$
variables changes neither the canonicalised NNF nor, hence, the tree of DFSB and
membership in the classes above, as long as $a$, $K$ and $m$ stay fixed (they depend
on $|C|$, which such a rewrite can change); and gadgets such as $(x\oplus r)\oplus r$
become the literal $x$. Rewritings of cones with more than $a$ variables are not covered:
$A\wedge B\mapsto(A\wedge B)\wedge(A\vee B)$ makes the top gate non-decomposable.
\end{enumerate}
Our implementation uses $s_7=512$, $\tau=0.05$, $B=2\cdot10^5$ and at most three
Shannon expansions per path.

\begin{proposition}[DNNF envelopes]\label{prop:dnnf-env}
If the NNF of $C$ (after (R8)) is a DNNF, rules (R1)--(R3) produce its own
arithmetic circuit $\varphi$, whose positivity set is $C^{-1}(1)$; so
$K\le\E_D[\varphi\mid C=1]$, its expected ambiguity, with equality unless (R6)
replaces a sub-envelope. For a DNF with $m$ terms (contradictory terms and repeated
literals are removed by the simplification of \cref{sec:dfsb}), $K\le m$
(Karp--Luby--Madras). Under product distributions with rational marginals, the
FPRAS of \cite{meel2026dnnf} with the sampler of \cref{thm:sis} removes the
dependence on the ambiguity (its looseness is $D(E)/p=1$), in time
$\poly(|C|,n,\max_i\log b_i,1/\eps,\log(1/\delta))$ with $b_i$ the denominators of
the marginals. So all DNNFs admit polynomial-time relative-error estimation, and so
do all circuits that (R1)--(R6) and (R8) turn into a DNNF envelope whose function
$E$ has $D(E)\le Kp$ with $K\le\poly(|C|)$.
\end{proposition}
