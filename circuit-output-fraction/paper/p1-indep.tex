\subsection{The presumption of independence, certified}\label{sec:independence}

Let $C=\bigwedge_{i=1}^mA_i$ be the top $\wedge$-tree of $C$ with arbitrary
sub-circuits $A_i$ of supports $S_i$, $B_i=\neg A_i$, $b_i=D(B_i)$, $\mu=\sum_ib_i$,
and $G$ the dependency graph ($i\sim j$ iff $i\ne j$ and $S_i\cap S_j\ne\emptyset$).
The presumption of independence estimates $p$ by $M=\prod_i(1-b_i)$.

Call $C$ \emph{unate} if the following top-down propagation of polarities from
the output reaches no input variable with both polarities: $\wedge$ and $\vee$ pass on the polarity they receive and $\neg$ flips it; a
$\oplus$-gate passes both polarities to each input; a threshold gate passes its
polarity to the inputs of nonnegative weight and the flipped one to the inputs of
negative weight (a negative weight counts as a negation), whatever the size of the
weights; any other gate disqualifies $C$. This is a syntactic check: one top-down
pass computes the set of polarities with which each gate is reached, and together
with the supports $S_i$ it takes $O(|C|n)$ time. If $C$ is unate, flip the variables
that occur positively ($x_v\mapsto1-x_v$, $q_v\mapsto1-q_v$). By induction from the
inputs, every gate reached only positively (negatively) now computes a decreasing
(increasing) function, and a gate reached with both polarities has no variable in its
cone; so every $A_i$ is decreasing and every $B_i$ increasing, and $D$ is still a
product distribution. (Harris's inequality needs only that all $B_i$ be monotone in the same
direction.)

\begin{theorem}[certified independence, first order]\label{thm:janson-general}
Let $C$ be unate with $\max_ib_i<1$ (if some $b_i=1$ then $p=M=0$). For any ordering
of the events and $d_i=\sum_{j<i,\,j\sim i}D(B_i\wedge B_j)$,
\[
M\le p\le\prod_i(1-b_i+d_i)\le M\exp\Big(\sum_i\frac{d_i}{1-b_i}\Big)\le
M\exp\Big(\frac{\Delta}{1-\max_ib_i}\Big),
\]
where $\Delta=\sum_id_i=\sum_{\{i,j\}:\,i\sim j}D(B_i\wedge B_j)$ is a sum over unordered
dependent pairs (the convention throughout).
Let $0<\eps\le1$, $\eps'=\ln(1+\eps)\ge\eps\ln2$ and $0<\delta<1$. The following
procedure is sound on every circuit:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item if $C$ is not unate, output $[0,1]$;
\item \emph{pilot:} draw $N_0=\lceil8\ln(4/\delta)\rceil$ samples of $x_{S_i}$ for each
$i$; let $\hat\mu_0$ be the sum over $i$ of the empirical frequencies of $B_i$;
\item \emph{pre-test on $\Delta$:} for each edge $\{i,j\}$ of $G$ draw
$N_\Delta=\lceil26\ln(4/\delta)/\eps'\rceil$ samples of $x_{S_i\cup S_j}$; let $\hat\Delta$
be the sum over edges of the empirical frequencies of $B_i\wedge B_j$; if
$\hat\Delta>\eps'/16$, output $[0,1]$;
\item \emph{main stage and pre-test on $b$:} in each of $R=\lceil8\ln(4/\delta)\rceil$
rounds $r$ draw $N=\lceil560(2\hat\mu_0+1)/\eps'^2+72\ln(4mR/\delta)\rceil$ fresh samples
of $x_{S_i}$ for each $i$, giving frequencies $\hat b_i^{(r)}$; if some
$\hat b_i^{(r)}>1/3$, output $[0,1]$; otherwise let
$\hat M=\exp\big(\mathrm{median}_r\sum_i\log(1-\hat b_i^{(r)})\big)$ and output
$[\hat Me^{-\eps'/4},\min\{1,\hat Me^{3\eps'/4}\}]$.
\end{enumerate}
It is complete, with probability $1-\delta/2$, on unate circuits with
$\max_ib_i\le1/4$ and $\Delta\le\eps'/64$. Its expected cost is
$O\big(\sum_i|A_i|\big[\big((\mu+1)\eps^{-2}+\log(m/\delta)\big)\log(1/\delta)
+\deg_G(i)\,\eps^{-1}\log(1/\delta)\big]\big)$ gate operations after the syntactic
checks. As $\mu\le\log(1/p)+\Delta/(1-\max_ib_i)$, this is linear in $\mu$, hence in
$\log(1/p)$ on the complete class, with no dependence on $1/p$ (which can be
$e^{\Theta(\mu)}$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Sandwich.} Flip as above. Write $p=\prod_i(1-c_i)$ with
$c_i=\Pr[B_i\mid F_{<i}]$, $F_X=\bigwedge_{j\in X}\neg B_j$ (by Harris
$\Pr[F_{<i}]\ge\prod_{j<i}(1-b_j)>0$). $B_i$ is increasing and $F_{<i}$ decreasing, so
$c_i\le b_i$ (Harris), giving $p\ge M$. Split the earlier events into neighbours $N$
and non-neighbours $R$ of $i$ (Boppana--Spencer \cite{boppanaspencer1989}): $B_i$ is
independent of $F_R$ (disjoint supports), so $c_i\ge\Pr[B_iF_N\mid F_R]\ge
b_i-\sum_{j\in N}\Pr[B_iB_j\mid F_R]\ge b_i-d_i$, the last step by Harris
($B_iB_j$ increasing, $F_R$ decreasing). Then $1-b_i+d_i\le(1-b_i)e^{d_i/(1-b_i)}$.
(If $b_i=0$ then $c_i=d_i=0$ and the $i$-th factors are $1$.)

\emph{Procedure.} $\hat M\le1$, so the output lies in $[0,1]$, and when it is not
$[0,1]$ its ratio is at most $e^{\eps'}=1+\eps$. Four failure events have probability
at most $\delta/4$ each.
(a) \emph{Pilot.} $N_0\hat\mu_0$ is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables with mean
$N_0\mu$. If $N_0\mu\ge8\ln(4/\delta)$, Chernoff gives
$\Pr[\hat\mu_0\le\mu/2]\le e^{-N_0\mu/8}\le\delta/4$; otherwise $\mu<1$. So
$\mu\le2\hat\mu_0+1$, and hence $N\ge560\mu/\eps'^2$, except with probability $\delta/4$.
(b) \emph{Pre-test on $\Delta$.} $N_\Delta\hat\Delta$ is a sum of independent Bernoulli
variables with mean $\lambda=N_\Delta\Delta$. If $\Delta>\eps'/4$, then
$\Pr[\hat\Delta\le\eps'/16]\le\Pr[N_\Delta\hat\Delta\le\lambda/4]\le e^{-9\lambda/32}\le
e^{-9N_\Delta\eps'/128}\le\delta/4$. If $\Delta\le\eps'/64$, then with
$\lambda'=N_\Delta\eps'/64\ge\lambda$, $\Pr[\hat\Delta>\eps'/16]\le
e^{-\lambda'}(e/4)^{4\lambda'}=e^{-(1+4\ln(4/e))\lambda'}\le\delta/4$, as
$26(1+4\ln(4/e))>64$.
(c) \emph{Pre-test on $b$.} If some $b_i>1/2$, Hoeffding gives
$\Pr[\hat b_i^{(1)}\le1/3]\le e^{-N/18}\le\delta/4$. If $\max_ib_i\le1/4$, the
probability that some $\hat b_i^{(r)}>1/3$ is at most $mRe^{-N/72}\le\delta/4$.
(d) \emph{Main stage.} Let $\max_ib_i\le1/2$ and $N\ge560\mu/\eps'^2$; fix a round, and
let $F$ be the event that all its $\hat b_i\le1/3$. With $Y_i=\hat b_i-b_i$,
$\Lambda=-\sum_iY_i/(1-b_i)$ and $Q=\sum_iY_i^2$ we have
$\mathrm{Var}\,\Lambda=\sum_ib_i/(N(1-b_i))\le2\mu/N$ and $\E Q\le\mu/N$. On $F$, Taylor's
theorem with $\hat b_i,b_i\le1/2$ gives $|\log(1-\hat b_i)-\log(1-b_i)+Y_i/(1-b_i)|\le2Y_i^2$,
so $|\sum_i\log(1-\hat b_i)-\log M|\le|\Lambda|+2Q$, and by Chebyshev and Markov (with
$\eps'\le\ln2$)
\[
\Pr\Big[F\wedge\Big|\sum_i\log(1-\hat b_i)-\log M\Big|>\frac{\eps'}4\Big]\le
\frac{2\mu/N}{(\eps'/8)^2}+\frac{2\mu/N}{\eps'/8}\le\frac{140\,\mu}{N\eps'^2}\le\frac14.
\]
If every round passes the pre-test and the median is off by more than $\eps'/4$, at
least $R/2$ of the independent rounds have this property, which has probability at
most $e^{-R/8}\le\delta/4$.
Outside these events, a non-trivial output has $\max_ib_i\le1/2$, $\Delta\le\eps'/4$ and
$|\log\hat M-\log M|\le\eps'/4$, so by the sandwich
$\hat Me^{-\eps'/4}\le M\le p\le Me^{2\Delta}\le\hat Me^{3\eps'/4}$, and $p\le1$. On the
complete class only (b) and (c) can produce $[0,1]$. Cost: $\E\hat\mu_0=\mu$, so
$\E N\le560(2\mu+1)/\eps'^2+72\ln(4mR/\delta)+1$; a sample of $x_{S_i}$ costs $O(|A_i|)$,
and the pre-test on $\Delta$ uses $N_\Delta$ samples per edge. Finally
$\mu\le-\log M\le\log(1/p)+\Delta/(1-\max_ib_i)$. The constants are not optimised.
\end{proof}

For a unate CNF all quantities are products of marginals and the sandwich is
deterministic (\cref{thm:janson}). For triangle-freeness of $G(N,q)$ ($B_i$: the
$i$-th triangle is present) at $N=10^4$ and $\mu=10$, $\Delta=0.023$ and the window is
$[M,Me^{0.023}]$. The bound needs only \emph{syntactic} structure (unateness,
supports); semantic monotonicity or independence is coNP-hard to check and
invisible to sampling.

\paragraph{Second order.} Correcting by covariances,
$M_2=\prod_i(1-b_i+C_i)$ with $C_i=\sum_{j<i,j\sim i}\mathrm{Cov}(B_i,B_j)$, the same
Harris/Boppana--Spencer argument together with Bonferroni inequalities gives,
when $\max_ib_i<1$ and every $\beta_i=\sum_{j<i,j\sim i}b_j<1$,
\[
|\log p-\log M_2|\le E_3=\sum_i\frac{\Theta_i+\Theta'_i+\beta_iC_i}{(1-\beta_i)(1-b_i)},
\]
where $\Theta'_i=\sum D(B_iB_jB_k)$ and $\Theta_i=b_i\sum D(B_jB_k)$, both over the
triples $\{i,j,k\}$ with $j,k<i$ that are connected in $G$ (\cref{thm:second-order}).
So $\sum_i\Theta'_i$ is the total probability of connected triples, whatever the
order. On triangle-freeness of $G(10^4,q)$ with $\mu=100$ the first-order window is
$e^{1.07}$, while $E_3\le0.0184$ for every order, so $\log p=-98.93\pm0.02$. Here the
symmetric local lemma holds ($e\,b\,(d+1)\approx5\cdot10^{-5}$, $d=3(N-3)$ the maximum
degree of $G$), but the counting conditions of the local-lemma module of
\cref{sec:modules}, which with $b$ in place of $2^{-k}$ allow each variable to lie in
about $b^{-1/4.82}\approx80$ events, fail (each edge lies in $N-2$ triangles), and so
does the condition of \cref{prop:kp} ($r=N-2$). All numbers in this section are
computed by \texttt{experiments/check\_independence\_numbers.py}. For principal up-sets
Mousset, Noever, Panagiotou and Samotij \cite{mnps2020} give a full hierarchy of such
bounds in terms of joint cumulants; the statement for arbitrary monotone sub-circuits
and its proof are in \cref{app:second-order}.

\begin{proposition}[pairwise data is not enough]\label{prop:pairwise-barrier}
Let all variables be Bernoulli$(q)$, $q\le1/2$. The ``stars'' $B_i=y\wedge z_i$
and the ``triangle'' $B_1=z_1z_2,B_2=z_2z_3,B_3=z_3z_1$ have the same dependency
graph, the same $b_i=q^2$ and the same $D(B_i\wedge B_j)=q^3$; taking $M'$ disjoint
copies, $\log(p_{\rm stars}/p_{\rm triangles})=M'\log\big(1+\frac{q^3(1-q)}{1-3q^2+2q^3}\big)\ge0.29\,\Delta$.
So no certificate computed from single and pairwise probabilities can have a
window narrower than $e^{0.29\Delta}$: the window $e^{\Delta/(1-\max_ib_i)}$ of
\cref{thm:janson-general} is optimal up to the constant in the exponent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$p_{\rm stars}=(1-q)+q(1-q)^3$ and $p_{\rm triangles}=(1-q)^3+3q(1-q)^2=1-3q^2+2q^3$;
their difference is $q^3(1-q)$. Each copy contributes $3q^3$ to $\Delta$, and $0.29$ is
the minimum over $q\in(0,1/2]$ of $\log(1+q^3(1-q)/(1-3q^2+2q^3))/(3q^3)$, which is
$0.2939$, attained at $q=0.279$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Beyond pairs.} Two further certified routes need no monotonicity.
(i) \emph{Interface dynamic programming.} If $Z$ contains every variable shared by
two of the $A_i$, then
\[
p=\sum_{z\in\bits^Z}D_Z(z)\prod_ia_i(z_{I_i}),\qquad a_i(y)=\Pr[A_i\mid x_{I_i}=y],\ I_i=S_i\cap Z,
\]
computed by variable elimination along a tree decomposition of
the interface hypergraph $\{I_i\}$ with Monte Carlo (or recursively estimated) factor
tables. The expression is a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients in the
$\sum_i2^{|I_i|}$ table entries, so interval arithmetic on confidence intervals for
all entries (with a union bound) is sound for every circuit. If every entry's
interval has ratio at most $e^{\eps'/m}$, the output's ratio is at most $e^{\eps'}=1+\eps$;
an entry then costs $O(m^2\eps^{-2}\log(\sum_i2^{|I_i|}/\delta)/a_i(y))$ samples, so the
route is complete and efficient when all entries are at least $1/\poly(|C|)$ and
$\max_i|I_i|$ and the width of the decomposition are $O(\log|C|)$. It handles strong,
extensive correlations: on a chain of $300$ modules with $p\approx3\cdot10^{-115}$,
where independence is off by $e^{64}$, the certified interval for $\log p$ (at
$\delta=0.05$) has width $7.3$ with $10^5$ samples per table entry and $0.073$ with $10^9$. This is DFSB's cutset
rule organised by a tree decomposition.
(ii) \emph{The Möbius expansion.} Let $Z_U=\Pr[\bigwedge_{i\in U}A_i]$ and, when all
$Z_U>0$, $\kappa(T)=\sum_{U\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T\setminus U|}\log Z_U$. Then
$\log p=\sum_{T\subseteq[m]}\kappa(T)$, and $\kappa(T)=0$ unless $T$ is connected in $G$
(the $Z_U$ factorize over the components of $T$). The terms $\kappa(\{i\})=\log(1-b_i)$
are the presumption of independence, and the higher ones are log-interaction
corrections computed from non-rare probabilities. Let $d\ge1$ be the maximum degree
of $G$ and $r$ the maximum number of supports $S_i$ containing a variable ($r\le d+1$).

\begin{proposition}[cluster expansion]\label{prop:kp}
Let $B_1,\dots,B_m$ be arbitrary events with supports $S_i$ under a product
distribution, with $\max_ib_i\le(2e^3d)^{-r}$. Then $Z_U\ge e^{-|U|/e}$ for every $U$, and
$\sum_{|T|\ge s}|\kappa(T)|\le me^{-s}$ for every $s$. Hence the following is sound on
every circuit: certify $\max_ib_i\le(2e^3d)^{-r}$ by upper confidence bounds (else
output $[0,1]$); compute confidence intervals for $\log Z_U$ for all $U\subseteq T$, $T$
connected with $|T|<s=\lceil\ln(4m/\eps')\rceil$; combine them by interval arithmetic
and widen by $me^{-s}\le\eps'/4$. If every $\log Z_U$ is known within
$\pm\eps'/(4m(2ed)^s)$, the interval for $\log p$ has width at most $\eps'$. For bounded
$d$ this is an FPRAS on the instances with $\max_ib_i\le(2e^3d)^{-r}/2$, using
$(m/\eps)^{O(1+\log d)}\log(1/\delta)$ samples.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Expanding $\prod_i(1-\one_{B_i})$, $p=\sum_{T}(-1)^{|T|}\Pr[\bigwedge_{i\in T}B_i]$,
and $\Pr[\bigwedge_TB_i]$ factorizes over the connected components of $G[T]$. So $p$
is the partition function of the polymer model whose polymers are the connected
sets $\gamma$ of $G$, with weights $w(\gamma)=(-1)^{|\gamma|}\Pr[\bigwedge_{i\in\gamma}B_i]$,
two polymers being compatible when they are disjoint and non-adjacent. By Finner's
generalized Hölder inequality for product measures \cite{finner1992}, as every
variable lies in at most $r$ supports, $|w(\gamma)|\le\prod_{i\in\gamma}b_i^{1/r}\le\beta^{|\gamma|}$
with $\beta=(\max_ib_i)^{1/r}\le1/(2e^3d)$. A polymer incompatible with $\gamma$ meets one of
the at most $(d+1)|\gamma|$ vertices of $\gamma$ and its neighbourhood, and at most
$(ed)^{s-1}$ connected sets of size $s$ contain a given vertex, so
\[
\sum_{\gamma'\not\sim\gamma}|w(\gamma')|e^{2|\gamma'|}\le(d+1)|\gamma|\sum_{s\ge1}(ed)^{s-1}(e^2\beta)^s
\le(d+1)|\gamma|\,e^2\beta\sum_{s\ge1}2^{1-s}\le\frac{d+1}{ed}|\gamma|\le|\gamma|.
\]
This is the Kotecký--Preiss condition \cite{kp1986} with $a(\gamma)=|\gamma|$ and decay
$|\gamma|$. It gives an absolutely convergent cluster expansion
$\log p=\sum_X\Phi(X)$ over clusters $X$ (multisets of polymers with connected
incompatibility graph) with $\sum_{X\ni i}|\Phi(X)|e^{\|X\|}\le1$ for every $i$, where
$X\ni i$ means $i\in\mathrm{supp}\,X=\bigcup_{\gamma\in X}\gamma$ and $\|X\|=\sum_{\gamma\in X}|\gamma|\ge|\mathrm{supp}\,X|$. The
condition holds for the sub-model on every $U$, so $Z_U>0$ and
$\log Z_U=\sum_{\mathrm{supp}\,X\subseteq U}\Phi(X)$; by Möbius inversion $\kappa(T)$ is the
sum of $\Phi(X)$ over the clusters with support exactly $T$. Hence
$\sum_{|T|\ge s}|\kappa(T)|\le\sum_i\sum_{X\ni i,\|X\|\ge s}|\Phi(X)|\le me^{-s}$ and
$|\log Z_U|\le\sum_{i\in U}\sum_{X\ni i}|\Phi(X)|\le|U|/e$. With $s=\lceil\ln(4m/\eps')\rceil$ the
truncation error is at most $\eps'/4$; the connected $T$ with $|T|<s$ have at most
$m(2ed)^s$ subsets $U$ in all, so the estimation intervals add up to a width of at
most $\eps'/2$.
Each $Z_U\ge e^{-s/e}$ needs $O(e^{s/e}m^2(2ed)^{2s}\eps^{-2}\log(m(2ed)^s/\delta))$ samples, and
$(2ed)^s=(m/\eps)^{O(1+\log d)}$; certifying $\max_ib_i$ costs
$O((2e^3d)^r\log(m/\delta))$ samples per event, and $(2e^3d)^r=O(1)$ for bounded $d$.
\end{proof}
