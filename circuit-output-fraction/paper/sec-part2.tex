\section{With a prover: proof-carrying importance sampling}\label{sec:part2}

By \cref{thm:blackbox} a verifier can certify an upper bound below the
sampling scale only by reading the circuit, and by \cref{thm:ma} no argument
system does so for every circuit. The system below makes the deductive part as
strong as any propositional proof system and leaves everything else to
sampling. It is sound on every circuit and every argument, and complete
exactly when the rarity of $C(x)=1$ has a short \emph{explanation}: a proof
that the event is confined to a tractable region in which it is not rare.

\subsection{The argument language}\label{sec:pcis}

Fix a Cook--Reckhow proof system $P$ for propositional unsatisfiability
(resolution with RUP/DRAT/LRAT certificates, cutting planes, Frege, extended
Frege, \dots). For circuits $F,G$ write $F\vdash_PG$ for a $P$-refutation of
$\mathrm{Tseitin}(F)\wedge[F=1]\wedge\mathrm{Tseitin}(G)\wedge[G=0]$, which
certifies $F\Rightarrow G$.

\begin{definition}[PCIS arguments]\label{def:pcis}
An argument is a finite DAG fixed before the verifier flips any coin. Every node
$v$ carries a circuit $C_v$ (the root carries $C$) whose target is
$q_v=D(C_v)$, and one rule:
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em]
\item[Deductive rules (checked by the verifier, no randomness).]
\textsc{refute}: $C_v\vdash_P\bot$, so $q_v=0$.
\textsc{exact}: $C_v$ is (or $P$-provably equivalent to) a d-DNNF, OBDD or
SDD $R$, so $q_v=D(R)$ exactly.
\textsc{sum}: $P$-proofs of $C_v\Leftrightarrow\bigvee_iC_i$ and of
$C_i\wedge C_j\vdash_P\bot$; then $q_v=\sum_iq_i$.
\textsc{prod}: $C_v=C_1\wedge C_2$ with syntactically disjoint supports; then
$q_v=q_1q_2$.
\textsc{split}: $q_v=(1-q_x)q_{v|x=0}+q_xq_{v|x=1}$, and $C_v\Rightarrow\ell$
gives $q_v=D(\ell)q_{v|\ell}$.
\textsc{mono}: $C_v\vdash_PC_w$ gives $q_v\le q_w$.
\item[Statistical rules (each with a confidence budget $\delta_v$ and a sample
size written in the argument).]
\textsc{envelope}: an envelope $\Phi$ (\cref{def:envelope}) with
$C_v\vdash_P\supp\Phi$, estimated by \cref{thm:envelope,thm:boxes} or by the
FPRAS-guided sampler of \cref{thm:sis}.
\textsc{proposal}: a lower bound from a proposal (\cref{sec:lower}).
\textsc{mc}: plain Monte Carlo on $C_v$.
\end{description}
The verifier checks every certificate and that $\sum_v\delta_v\le\delta$,
evaluates the statistical nodes with fresh coins, combines intervals by
interval arithmetic through the deductive rules (all of which are monotone
in their arguments), and outputs the root interval. If a domination proof
is missing or wrong, the node is downgraded to a lower bound (upper end
$+\infty$).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[soundness]\label{thm:pcis-sound}
For every circuit $C$, product distribution $D$ and string $\pi$,
$\Pr[p\notin V(C,D,\pi)]\le\delta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each deductive rule states a true identity or inequality between the true
targets, by soundness of $P$ and independence of variable-disjoint
sub-circuits under $D$; interval arithmetic with nonnegative endpoints is
inclusion-monotone. Let $B_v$ be the event that statistical node $v$ returns
an interval missing $q_v$. Conditionally on everything computed before $v$
starts, $v$'s target and sampling law are fixed and it uses fresh coins, so
$\Pr[B_v\mid\text{history}]\le\delta_v$ by \cref{thm:envelope} (resp.\
\cref{thm:boxes,thm:sis,prop:proposal}); a downgraded node fails only if its
lower end exceeds $q_v$, which has the same bound. Hence
$\Pr[\bigcup_vB_v]\le\sum_v\delta_v\le\delta$, and on the complement every
interval, including the root's, contains its target.
\end{proof}

Adaptive choices inside a node (sequential stopping) are allowed; what must
be fixed in advance is the set of nodes and their budgets, or a summable
schedule $\delta_v=\delta\,2^{-v}$.

\begin{proposition}[relative-error calculus]\label{prop:calculus}
If every statistical leaf returns an interval of ratio at most $1+\eta$ and
every derivation of the root from the leaves (one child at \textsc{sum},
\textsc{split}; all children at \textsc{prod}) uses at most $d$ leaves, the
root interval has ratio at most $(1+\eta)^d$. With $\eta=\eps/(2d)$ the
ratio is at most $1+\eps$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sums and convex combinations with exact weights do not increase the worst
ratio of their summands; products multiply ratios.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two-sided workhorse: certified envelopes}

When the envelope is an arbitrary DNNF $E$ given by the prover, the
derivation-weighted estimator of \cref{thm:envelope} can be needlessly slow:
its relative variance is
$\frac{D(E)}{p}\,\E[\varphi\mid E]\,\E[1/\varphi\mid C]-1$, which depends on the
\emph{dispersion} of the number of derivations, and for
$E=\bigwedge_{i\le m}(x_i\vee y_i)$ is $(10/9)^m-1$ under every reweighting of
the $\vee$-edges, although $D(E)=(3/4)^m$ is known exactly. The
Meel--de Colnet FPRAS for DNNF \cite{meel2026dnnf} removes the dispersion,
through a Knuth-style sequential sampler whose weights are exactly unbiased
whatever errors the counter makes.

\begin{theorem}[FPRAS-guided envelope sampling]\label{thm:sis}
Let $E$ be a DNNF and $D$ a product distribution with rational marginals.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item (Weighted counting.) Smoothing $E$ and replacing each literal by a
comparator on $\lceil\log_2b_i\rceil$ fresh bits gives a DNNF $E'$ with
$\#E'=(\prod_ib_i)D(E)$; so the FPRAS approximates $D(E\mid\rho)$ for every
partial assignment $\rho$.
\item (Sampler.) Draw $x_1,\dots,x_n$ in turn: with counter outputs
$A_c\approx D(E\mid\rho,x_i=c)$ (zero iff $E\mid\rho,x_i=c$ is unsatisfiable, which is
decidable in linear time), set $x_i=c$ with probability
$D_i(c)A_c/S$, $S=\sum_cD_i(c)A_c$, and multiply the weight $w$ by $S/A_{x_i}$.
Then for every circuit $C$ and \emph{every} law of the counter's errors,
$\E[C(x)\,w]=D(C\wedge E)$.
\item (Cost.) With counter accuracy $1/(4n)$ and confidence $\eps/(16n)$ per
call, $w\in[0.58,1.70]\cdot D(E)$ outside an event of weighted mass
$(\eps/8)D(C\wedge E)$; clipping at $3\hat D(E)$ and running the stopping rule
gives a $(1\pm\eps)$ estimate of $D(C\wedge E)$ with $O(K\log(1/\delta)/\eps^2)$
samples, $K=D(E)/D(C\wedge E)$, each costing one evaluation of $C$ and $2n$
counter calls.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(a) Smoothing makes every derivation mention every variable exactly once,
so the substituted comparator blocks are all constrained; then each $x$ has
$(\prod_ib_i)D(x)$ preimages. (b) At each step the probability of choosing $c$
times the weight factor is $D_i(c)$, whatever $A_0,A_1$ are (Knuth's
estimator \cite{knuth1975}); pre-drawing
the counter outputs for every prefix and summing over paths gives
$\E[C(x)w]=\sum_xD(x)C(x)\one_E(x)$ (the walk only enters satisfiable
prefixes). (c) On the event that all $2n$ counts on the path are accurate,
the weight telescopes to $D(E)/D(E\mid x)=D(E)$ up to $e^{\pm2.1/(4n)}$ per
step; apply (b) to the indicator of the bad event to bound its contribution,
then the stopping rule \cite{dklr2000}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[certified envelopes]\label{thm:certenv}
Let $\mathcal R$ be the closure under decomposable products and finite unions
of subcubes, OBDDs, SDDs, d-DNNFs and, under the uniform distribution, affine
subspaces, together with DNNFs via \cref{thm:sis}. Let
$\mathcal K_P(m,K)$ be the class of circuits $C$ for which there are
$E_1,\dots,E_r\in\mathcal R$ and a $P$-proof $C\vdash_P\bigvee_jE_j$, all of
total size at most $m$, with $\sum_jD(E_j)\le K\,p$. The PCIS verifier is sound
on all circuits and complete on $\mathcal K_P(m,K)$, in time
$\poly(m,|C|)+O(K\log(1/\delta)/\eps^2)\cdot(|C|+m)$ (plus FPRAS overhead for
DNNF pieces). Without the proof the same samples still certify a lower
bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Soundness is \cref{thm:pcis-sound}. For completeness, sample $j\propto
D(E_j)$, $x\sim D\mid E_j$, and set $W=C(x)\Lambda/\mathrm{cov}(x)$ with
$\Lambda=\sum_jD(E_j)$ and $\mathrm{cov}(x)=|\{j:x\in E_j\}|$ (Karp--Luby--Madras
\cite{klm1989}). Then $x$ has density $D(x)\mathrm{cov}(x)/\Lambda$, $0\le
W\le\Lambda$, and $\E W=D(C\wedge\bigcup_jE_j)$, which is $p$ under the proved
domination. Apply \cref{thm:envelope} with $Z=\Lambda$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{thm:certenv} lifts every tractable counting class from \emph{syntactic}
envelopes (\cref{sec:part1}) to \emph{provable} ones. Examples:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Hidden triggers.} $C=R\wedge T$ where $T$ is a test that holds only
on a subcube $x_A=a$ but is not written as one: through a linear hash $[Mx_A=Ma]$
over $\F_2$, through two differently built $\oplus$-trees for the same parities,
through a multiplier. A SAT solver produces a refutation of $C\wedge(x_A\ne a)$;
with $K=1/\Pr[R\mid x_A=a]$ the verifier needs $O(K/\eps^2)$ samples, Monte
Carlo $2^{|A|}K/\eps^2$. Resolution needs exponential size for dense linear
hashes, so here the proof system matters (\cref{sec:experiments}); extended
resolution, or an affine envelope, handles them in polynomial size.
\item \emph{Planted unions.} $C=\bigvee_j(T_j\wedge G_j)$, $T_j$ subcubes of
large codimension, $G_j$ dense: $K\le$ (overlap)$/\min_j\Pr[G_j\mid T_j]$.
\item \emph{Thresholds with side conditions.} $C=[w\cdot x\ge\theta]\wedge G$
with integer $w$: a cutting-planes proof of $w\cdot x\ge\theta$ from $C$ and the
soft envelope $\beta^{w\cdot x-\theta}$ ($\beta>1$ rational) give $K=O(\sqrt n)$
(\cref{prop:tilting}); numerically $K=37$ at $n=800$, $p=4\cdot10^{-125}$.
\item \emph{Hidden independence.} A proof that $C\Leftrightarrow\bigwedge_jC_j(x_{S_j})$
with disjoint $S_j$ turns a product of $k$ moderately rare events into $k$
separate estimations (\textsc{prod}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Sandwiches.}
A certificate may also give, for every gate $g$, OBDDs $L_g\subseteq
g^{-1}(1)\subseteq U_g$ in a fixed variable order, checked gate by gate
($L_{a\wedge b}\Rightarrow L_a\wedge L_b$, $U_a\wedge U_b\Rightarrow U_{a\wedge b}$,
$L_{\neg a}\wedge U_a\equiv\bot$, $\neg L_a\Rightarrow U_{\neg a}$, and dually for
$\vee$), each check an OBDD operation. Then $p\in[D(L_{\rm out}),D(U_{\rm
out})]$ deterministically, and sampling $D\mid(U_{\rm out}\setminus L_{\rm out})$
estimates the gap with $O\big(\frac{D(U\setminus L)}{p}\eps^{-2}\log\frac1\delta\big)$
samples. No SAT proof is needed.

\subsection{Lower bounds need no deduction}\label{sec:lower}

\begin{proposition}[trace-reconstructible proposals]\label{prop:proposal}
The prover supplies a seed law $P_R$ on $\bits^r$ with computable pmf and an
exact sampler, a generator $\psi:\bits^r\to\bits^n$, an inverter
$\phi:\bits^n\to\bits^r$, and a clip level $\tau$. For $R\sim P_R$ and
$X=\psi(R)$ let $W=\one[\phi(X)=R]\,C(X)\min(D(X)/P_R(R),\tau)\in[0,\tau]$.
Then for every $C$ and every choice of the other objects,
\[
\E W=\sum_{x\in C^{-1}(1):\,\psi(\phi(x))=x}\min\big(D(x),\tau P_R(\phi(x))\big)\le p,
\]
so the lower end of a KL interval for $\E W$ is a sound lower bound. It is
within $1-\eps$ of $p$ after $O(K\log(1/\delta)/\eps^2)$ samples if
$\tau\le Kp$ and the pairs $x$ with $D(x)\le\tau P_R(\phi(x))$ and
$\psi(\phi(x))=x$ carry $(1-\eps/2)p$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\psi$ is injective on $R^*=\{r:\phi(\psi(r))=r\}$ (if $\psi(r)=\psi(r')=x$
then $r=\phi(x)=r'$) and maps it onto $\{x:\psi(\phi(x))=x\}$; substitute
$x=\psi(r)$. Without the trace check each $x$ would be counted once per
preimage and $\E W$ could exceed $p$.
\end{proof}

Special cases: autoregressive models or probabilistic circuits ($r=n$,
$\psi=\phi=\mathrm{id}$, $P_R=Q$), witness lists (exact lower bound
$\sum_iD(x^{(i)})$, no sampling), an unproved envelope, and generators with
inverters such as normalising flows.

\subsection{Surprise accounting, made exact}

Write $\sigma=\log_2(1/p)$ for the surprise of the event. An envelope argument
explains $\sigma_e=\log_2(1/D(E))$ bits deductively and leaves
$\sigma_u=\log_2(D(E)/p)$ bits to sampling:
\[
\text{verifier time}\;=\;\poly(|\pi|,|C|)\;+\;O\big(2^{\sigma_u}\eps^{-2}\log(1/\delta)\big)\cdot(|C|+|E|).
\]
For a skeleton of independent components (\textsc{prod} at the top) the
second term is $\sum_\lambda2^{\sigma_u(\lambda)}$ rather than
$2^{\sum_\lambda\sigma_u(\lambda)}$: unexplained surprise spread over $t$
independent components costs about $t\,2^{\sigma_u/t}$. Both terms are
necessary in the black-box sense:

\begin{theorem}[proof bits and surprise bits]\label{thm:surprise}
Let a verifier access $C$ only through $T$ evaluations (adaptive, from any
distributions) and read an $\ell$-bit argument; let $D$ be uniform.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item If it is $\delta$-sound for lower bounds on all circuits and, for each
subcube $S$ of codimension $k$, some argument makes it output $L>0$ on
$\one_S$ with probability $1-\delta$, then $T\cdot2^\ell\ge(1-2\delta)2^k$.
Each bit of argument buys at most one bit of surprise, and this is tight:
revealing $j$ coordinates of $S$ leaves $T=O(2^{k-j}\eps^{-2}\log(1/\delta))$.
\item Inside a certified envelope $E$, distinguishing $C_0=E\wedge[x_I=b]$
from $C_1=E\wedge([x_I=b]\vee[x_{I'}=b'])$ (factor $1.5$ apart) needs
$T\ge(1-2\delta)D(E)/p$ evaluations: the $2^{\sigma_u}$ term cannot be avoided
by any method that treats the unexplained part of $C$ as a black box.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) Couple the runs on $\one_S$ and on the zero function with the same coins;
they differ only if a query lands in $S$, which for a fixed query has
probability $2^{-k}$ over a uniformly random $S$ of the family. Soundness on
the zero function forces the event ``some query lands in $S$'' to have
probability at least $1-2\delta$ under the argument $\pi_S$; averaging over
$S$ and summing over the at most $2^\ell$ arguments gives $1-2\delta\le
2^\ell T2^{-k}$. (b) The same coupling, with $C_0$ in place of the zero
function and the random subcube $[x_{I'}=b']$ planted.
\end{proof}

So the cost of certifying a rare event is governed by
$\min_\pi\big(|\pi|+2^{\sigma_u(\pi)}\big)$, a two-part, MDL-like cost of the
kind that ``surprise accounting'' \cite{hilton2024surprise} proposes for
heuristic explanations, here with a sound verifier and a matching lower bound.

\subsection{How much stronger is the prover?}\label{sec:separations}

\paragraph{A universal verifier.} Fix a consistent theory $T$ (say ZFC). Let a
$V_T$-argument be a randomized program $A$, a time bound $t$, a budget
$\delta_A\le\delta$, and a $T$-proof of the bounded arithmetic sentence
``at most $\delta_A2^t$ coin strings make $A^{(t)}(C,D)$ output an interval
missing $\sum_xD(x)C(x)$''; $V_T$ checks the proof and runs $A$. It is sound
whenever $T$ is (a false bounded sentence has a true $\Sigma_1$ negation,
which $T$ would refute), and it $p$-simulates every argument system whose
soundness $T$ proves, by instantiating that proof. So it can use any FPRAS,
compiler, mixing-time bound or combinatorial theorem as a certified rule. This
is the analogue, for estimation, of proof systems that are optimal relative to
a theory \cite{kp1989}.

\paragraph{Lower bounds: the prover helps unless $\NP\subseteq\BPP$.}
Uniquely satisfiable circuits have $p=2^{-n}$, certified exactly by one
witness. A sound Task-1 procedure that outputs any $L>0$ on all of them
decides SAT via Valiant--Vazirani \cite{vv1986}. Under one-way permutations
(with a Goldreich--Levin hard-core bit \cite{gl1989}),
$C_y(x,r)=[(\pi(x),r,\langle x,r\rangle)=y]$ gives an average-case version
with $(1+\eps)$-tight certificates.

\paragraph{Upper bounds: the prover helps exactly when $P$ is not weakly
automatable.} For a refutation system $P$ let $Z_P^m$ be the circuits $C$ with
a $P$-refutation of size $m(|C|)$. A sound Task-1 procedure that outputs
$[0,0]$ on all of $Z_P^m$ exists iff the canonical pair $(Z_P^m,\mathrm{SAT})$ is
separable in randomized polynomial time (if it outputs $[0,0]$, the circuit
cannot be satisfiable, by soundness; conversely run the separator); separability of
the canonical pair is weak automatability of $P$ \cite{pudlak2003pairs}. Padding
turns this into a separation for positive, exactly certified $p$: with
$\mathrm{pad}_a(C)(\beta,x,w)=(\beta\wedge x=0^n\wedge w=0^a)\vee(\neg\beta\wedge
C(x))$, $p=2^{-n-a-1}$ exactly when $C$ is unsatisfiable (certified by one point
and $P$'s refutation), while $p\ge2^{-n-1}$ when $C$ is satisfiable, so any Task-1
procedure complete within a factor $2^a$ separates the canonical pair. Known
results then give:

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:automatability}
If factoring Blum integers is hard for polynomial-size circuits, then for
$P\in\{\mathrm{TC}^0\text{-Frege},\text{Frege}\}$ \cite{bpr2000}, and for
extended Frege if RSA is secure \cite{kp1998}, there are circuit families on
which the PCIS verifier with $P$ certifies $p$ exactly while no sound
polynomial-time procedure without a prover is complete, even within a factor
$2^a$. Under LWE the same holds against quantum procedures for
$\mathrm{TC}^0$-Frege \cite{acg2024}.
\end{corollary}

Finding the short proofs is the hard part: even for resolution, finding a
refutation of size polynomial in the shortest one is $\NP$-hard
\cite{atserias2020automating}. And for preimage circuits $[G(x)=y]$ of
pseudorandom generators, short proofs that $y\notin\mathrm{range}(G)$ are
conjectured not to exist in strong proof systems (proof complexity generators
\cite{abrw2004,krajicek2004dual}), so there the verifier cannot beat sampling for
upper bounds either.

\paragraph{Two-sided, exact.} Let $L\in\mathsf{UP}\cap\coNP$ with verifiers $A$
(unique witness, $0^k$ never a witness) and $B$, and
$C_u(\beta,v)=(\beta\wedge v=0^k)\vee(\neg\beta\wedge A(u,v))$, so
$p(C_u)=2^{-k-1}(1+[u\in L])$. With $T$ proving uniqueness and disjointness,
$V_T$ certifies both values exactly (by the witness of $A$ or of $B$), while a
Task-1 procedure complete on $\{C_u\}$ decides $L$. For bits of the
prime factorisation (AKS-certified primes) this gives a two-sided separation
under ``factoring $\notin\BPP$''.

\paragraph{Why not NP-hardness?} If SAT reduces (randomised, many-one) to the
gap problem ``$p\ge\gamma t$ versus $p\le t$'' with unsatisfiable formulas
mapped to the \emph{small} side, and some MA system is complete for upper
bounds on that side, then $\coNP\subseteq\AM$: Arthur samples the reduction,
Merlin sends arguments, Arthur accepts if most of them certify $U<\gamma t$.
With the opposite orientation, Goldwasser--Sipser already puts the problem
in $\AM$. Either way $\PH$ collapses \cite{bhz1987}. So upper-bound and
two-sided separations must come from structured, non-$\NP$-hard assumptions
(factoring, RSA, LWE), as above, while lower-bound separations may come from
$\NP\not\subseteq\BPP$.

\paragraph{Universality.} Upper bounds: complete for all circuits only if
$\coNP\subseteq\MA$ (\cref{thm:ma}). Lower bounds: complete for all circuits
within a constant factor iff $\mathrm{prSBP}\subseteq\mathrm{prMA}$
\cite{bgm2006}; this fails relative to an oracle, and holds under derandomisation
hypotheses strong enough to fool nondeterministic circuits, with a verifier
whose soundness then depends on the hypothesis.
