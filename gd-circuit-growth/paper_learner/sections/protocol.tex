\section{How it is evaluated}\label{sec:protocol}

This section gives the tasks, the references, the verdict rules and the budgets, and what is held out for v3. The names of learners, members, references, sets and targets are collected in \cref{app:names}.

\subsection{Tasks}\label{sec:tasks}

\paragraph{Toy Boolean targets.} Inputs are uniform on $\cube$ with $d=30$, and labels are $\pm1$ values of a target $f$. There are 11 suites, 138 targets in all, summarized in \cref{tab:status} and defined in \repo{study/boolfuncs.py}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{designed} (21) and \code{random} (20). Parities, majorities, halfspaces, staircases, compositions, a multiplexer, a DNF, a decision list and random small formulas.
\item \code{dlheld}, \code{dlheld2}, \code{dlheld3} (12, 7, 10). Dense halfspaces and majorities, $\chi_5$, multiplexers, symmetric functions such as $[|x_1+\dots+x_9|\le1]$, mixtures of parities with gates, and random formulas.
\item \code{dlsymdev} (8). Symmetric and signed-sum targets used for development.
\item \code{dlheld4}, \code{dlheld5}, \code{dlheld6} (12 each). Signed sums with threshold, band, interval, residue-class and arbitrary accept sets, some mixed with parities and gates, plus 2--3 non-symmetric controls per suite.
\item \code{dlfamdev} and \code{dlheld7} (12 each). Two fresh draws (seeds 61616 and 70707) from the blind family below.
\end{itemize}
\emph{The blind family} is a random generator of signed-sum targets, written in Part 9 by an agent that had not seen DFG's sum-block design (source: \repo{study/part9/heldout6/FAMILY.md}). It has 9 structured slots and 3 controls: sums, sums times a parity, sums with a gate, and compositions of sums. Each block's accept set is drawn uniformly from five types, one of them a residue class mod 3 or 4, except in the inner-term form $u=s+a\tau$, which uses a threshold or a band. \code{dlheld6}, \code{dlfamdev} and \code{dlheld7} are three draws from it. v3's toy builder developed on \code{dlheld6} and \code{dlfamdev}, so \code{dlheld7} tests v3 on fresh targets from a distribution its designer had seen.

The \emph{sum-structured targets} are the 54 targets of \code{dlsymdev}, \code{dlheld4}--\code{dlheld7} and \code{dlfamdev}, minus their 14 non-symmetric controls; \cref{tab:dlheld7,tab:sumdefs,tab:sumdefs2} define them. Every suite puts the relevant inputs first. The discrete learners therefore see the columns in a fresh random order in every fit, so that tie-breaking by column index cannot help them.

\paragraph{Tabular data.} There are 26 PMLB datasets \cite{olson2017pmlb}: 5 for development, 11 held out in Part 7 (including mushroom), and 10 fresh in Part 8 (HELDOUT2). They have continuous, categorical, binary and mixed features and 2--26 classes.

\paragraph{Images.} MNIST \cite{lecun1998mnist}, Fashion-MNIST \cite{xiao2017fashion} and five 10-class sets of QuickDraw bitmaps \cite{ha2017sketch}. The QuickDraw sets use 50 categories drawn at random from the 345 (seed 9191) and split into five disjoint sets of ten:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{qd_dev}, development;
\item \code{qd_a} and \code{qd_b}, held out in Part 9;
\item \code{qd_c} and \code{qd_d}, held out in Part 10.\footnote{\code{qd_c}: hospital, submarine, telephone, bathtub, hockey stick, key, snake, bowtie, hat, binoculars. \code{qd_d}: basketball, purse, train, pencil, remote control, camel, onion, harp, squiggle, beard.}
\end{itemize}
Each set has 6000 training and 1000 test drawings per class, so a 60000-point training pool and a 10000-point test set. The 784 pixel columns are scaled to $[0,1]$ and permuted by one fixed permutation (seed 9192). Every reference MLP is invariant to that permutation.

\subsection{References}\label{sec:refs}

\paragraph{Networks on the toy targets.} These are the companion paper's $\mu$P two-hidden-layer ReLU MLPs \cite{gdcircuit2026,yang2021feature}: width 128, Adam at learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 64, 20000 steps on a fixed training set, read at the final step. There are five variants:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{nets}: square loss;
\item \code{nets_wd}: decoupled weight decay 0.1;
\item \code{nets_cewd}, \code{nets_cewd0.3}, \code{nets_cewd1.0}: logistic loss with weight decay 0.1, 0.3 and 1.0.
\end{itemize}
Not every variant was run on every suite (\cref{tab:status}). \code{nets_best} is, per target, the variant with the smallest $\nstar$ among those run: a hindsight choice that favours the networks. \code{lin} (cross-validated L2 logistic regression on the raw inputs; a separate run, not v3's member \code{lin_C}) and \code{hgb} (scikit-learn's histogram gradient boosting, default settings \cite{pedregosa2011sklearn}) are calibration references. The networks are a fixed recipe, not tuned per target or suite. On \code{dlheld7} a learner that searches no features (\code{hgb} or \code{lin}) already beats \code{nets_best} on 5 of the 12 targets (\cref{sec:res-dlheld7}). The comparison is therefore with a standard gradient-trained network, not with the best network for each target (\cref{sec:disc-limits}).

\paragraph{MLPs on standard tasks.} All are ReLU MLPs with softmax cross-entropy:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{mlp}: 256-256, Adam, learning rate and stopping point chosen on validation;
\item \code{mlp_reg}: the same with an L2 penalty chosen on validation;
\item \code{mlp_strong}: quantile-transformed inputs, AdamW with cosine decay, weight decay in $\{0,0.1,1\}$ chosen on validation, dropout 0.1, an ensemble of 3;
\item \code{mlp_wide}: the same at width 512 with weight decay in $\{0.1,1,3\}$;
\item \code{mlp_xl}: width 1024 at $n=60000$, at the weight decay \code{mlp_wide} chose there. Its CPU time per fit is close to the discrete learners'.
\end{itemize}
\code{mlp_best} is the per-cell hindsight minimum over the Part 7--8 variants, and \code{mlp_best+} adds the wider ones. Two further learners are \emph{not} references:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{mlp_pool}, \code{mlp_strong} given v2's correlation-pooled sums as extra inputs, is a control for how much of the image result is representation;
\item \code{cnn}, a small convolutional network on the \emph{unpermuted} pixels, is context: it uses the layout that every other learner here is denied. It is one network, not an ensemble: conv $3\times3$ with 16 channels and ReLU, max-pool 2, conv $3\times3$ with 32 channels and ReLU, max-pool 2, dense 128 with ReLU and dropout 0.1; AdamW at learning rate $10^{-3}$ with cosine decay, decoupled weight decay fixed at 0.1, batch 128, $\min(10^4,\max(2000,\text{50 epochs}))$ steps, no augmentation, seed 0 only (\repo{study/standard_tasks/cnn_ref.py}). It ran on \code{qd_c} and \code{qd_a} at $n=1000$ and $10^4$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Earlier versions.} v1 (Part 8) and v2 (Part 9) are run as references. v2 is run on every toy suite and at every image cell of v3. v1 is run at every image cell of v3 and on every toy suite except \code{dlfamdev} and \code{dlheld7}. On tabular data without DFG, v1's, v2's and v3's code paths are the same.

\subsection{Metrics, verdicts and budgets}\label{sec:metrics}

\paragraph{Toy targets.} $\nstar$ is the training-set size at which the seed-mean test error reaches 0.1. It is interpolated in $\log n$ on the grid $n=16,32,\dots,16384$, with 3 seeds (2 on \code{random}) and one 4096-point test set per target. A comparison is the $\log_2$ ratio $\log_2(\nstar_{\rm learner}/\nstar_{\rm ref})$. A target is \emph{clearly better} or \emph{clearly worse} when the ratio is below $-0.25$ or above $+0.25$, or when only one of the two reaches 10\% error. Suite means are taken over targets both learn. Unlike the cell rule below, this verdict has no noise term: it does not compare the difference with a standard error.

\paragraph{Standard tasks.} Test error is measured on a fixed test set at $n\in\{100,300,1000,\allowbreak3000,10000,\allowbreak30000,60000\}$ (up to the pool size). There are 3 seeds, except on images at $n\ge30000$, where every learner runs seed 0 only, except the baseline \code{mlp}, which has 3 seeds at every $n$. The control \code{mlp_pool} and the context \code{cnn} ran seed 0 only at every $n$, and \code{mlp_xl} at $n=60000$ only. A \emph{cell} is a (set, $n$) pair. A cell is \emph{better} or \emph{worse} when the seed-mean errors differ by more than 10\% relative and by more than 2 standard errors. The s.e.\ comes from the seed spread of both learners and is at least the test set's binomial noise; otherwise the cell is a \emph{tie}. Errors are floored at 0.002 in $\log_2$ ratios. At $n=60000$ on images the s.e.\ is the test set's binomial noise alone, about 0.34 points at an error of 13\%, so 2 s.e.\ of a difference is about 1.0 point. The 10\% criterion asks for 1.3 points there, so it is the binding one.

\paragraph{Budgets.} Compute is counted in FLOPs, everything a learner does in a fit included, prediction excluded. The budget caps the discrete learners only.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Toy fit:} one training run of one toy network, $1.62\times10^{11}$ FLOPs at $d=30$. Each network variant spends exactly one budget, and \code{nets_best} is a hindsight minimum over up to five such runs.
\item \emph{Real-data fit:} three training runs of the baseline \code{mlp}, which is the baseline's own cost including its learning-rate search: $1.24$, $2.42$ and $6.21\times10^{12}$ FLOPs at $n=1000$, $10^4$ and $6\times10^4$ on the image sets.
\end{itemize}
The references run their standard recipes uncapped. On the held-out image cells \code{mlp_strong} spends 2.0 budgets per fit, \code{mlp_wide} 5.0, \code{mlp_pool} 5.9 and \code{mlp_xl} 9.2 (\cref{sec:res-img-compute}). ``No more FLOPs'' therefore means that the discrete learner spends no more than the network it is compared with. Because equal FLOPs are far from equal CPU time, CPU time per fit is reported separately, and \code{mlp_xl} is the reference matched to v3's CPU time.

\subsection{How v3 was built and what is held out for it}\label{sec:discipline}

\paragraph{Pre-registration.} The Part 10 protocol (\repo{study/part10/PROTOCOL.md}) was committed before any design work (\texttt{dc70266c}). It fixed six things:
\begin{itemize}
\item the fresh held-out sets \code{dlheld7}, \code{qd_c} and \code{qd_d}, and a development draw \code{dlfamdev};
\item the budgets;
\item three builder tasks on development data only: a toy component (DFG's sum-feature search) and two image components from different angles;
\item an \emph{integration rule}. The toy component is adopted if its mean $\log_2\nstar$ ratio against v2 on \code{designed}, \code{dlsymdev} and \code{dlfamdev} is below $-0.05$, with no target worse by more than 0.5. The image component with the lower mean $\log_2$ error ratio against v2 over 8 development cells is adopted if that mean is below $-0.05$ and, at \code{qd_a} with $n=60000$, its error is within 5\% of v2's, its CPU time is at most $1.5\times$ v2's, and it stays within the FLOP budget;
\item a \emph{best-learner rule}. v3 replaces v2 as the learner of this paper if its mean $\log_2$ error ratio against v2 on \code{qd_c} and \code{qd_d} at $n\ge10000$ is below 0, and its mean $\log_2\nstar$ ratio against v2 on \code{dlheld7} is at most $+0.1$;
\item the held-out reference runs.
\end{itemize}
The builders froze their files, recorded md5 sums and ran the fixed development evaluation. The rules were then applied mechanically (\cref{sec:decisions}). v3 was assembled and checked identical to its sources on every input type, then frozen (md5 \texttt{b045fafe}). Only then were held-out results of any Part 10 learner read.

\paragraph{What is in-sample.} v3 is the last of several pre-registered steps, and every step used the earlier held-out sets as development data. For v3 as a whole, only \code{dlheld7}, \code{qd_c} and \code{qd_d} are untouched (\cref{tab:status}). The other suites still measure the learner, but they are not held out for its design: the toy builder developed on \code{dlheld6}, and the image builders on validation rows of \code{qd_dev}, \code{qd_a}, \code{qd_b} and Fashion-MNIST (and MNIST for topology diagnostics). On tabular data the claim is narrower and cleaner: v3's code path there is v1's. HELDOUT2 was fixed before any Part 8 design work (commit \texttt{04a215b2}), and v1 was frozen before it was run there, so HELDOUT2 is held out for that code.
