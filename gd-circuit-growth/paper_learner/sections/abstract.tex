\noindent
This paper describes v3 (\code{hyb_portfolio3}), the best learner so far of a study that compares discrete learners with gradient-descent-trained networks at matched FLOPs, and tests it on data fixed before it was designed. v3 is a stacked portfolio whose members are chosen by input type. On Boolean inputs the main member is DFG, which enumerates Fourier characters and indicator features of signed sums of inputs, admits each one by a union-bound test against the current residual, and reads the admitted features out linearly. On images the main members are gradient-boosted trees over fixed subset sums of pixels, blobs and short ``bars'' whose pixel sets are chosen from pixel correlations, so the pixel layout is never read. No member fits a feature direction by gradient descent. On a fresh 12-target Boolean suite ($d=30$), drawn from a family written by an agent blind to the design, v3 needs clearly less data than the per-target best of three pre-registered network variants on 10 targets and more on none. The mean $\log_2$ ratio of the training-set sizes at 10\% test error is $-1.50$, about $2.8\times$ less data. On five of the ten, default gradient boosting or logistic regression also beats the networks, and one target, a residue class mod 3 of a 14-input count, is learned by no learner. On two fresh 10-class QuickDraw sets with permuted pixels, v3 has 12.8\% and 12.6\% test error at $n=60000$. It is clearly better than an ensemble of three MLPs at all six cells ($n=1000$, $10^4$, $6\times10^4$; errors 17--23\% lower) and than an MLP given its predecessor's pooled features at both cells where that ran. But it is only 8.6--9.6\% below a CPU-matched ensemble of wider MLPs, a tie by the pre-registered rule. Against its predecessor v2 it ties all six cells, although its error is lower at each. Descriptive interpretability analyses (seed 0, independently verified) find three things. DFG's model comes to hold the target's exact signed block on 49 of 54 sum-structured targets. Its data cost is set by when the residual's degree-1 and degree-2 statistics point to the block, not by its test. The mod-3 targets fail because they have no weight at those degrees, although their block would pass the test at 134--155 times the threshold. On images the pooling sets are local neighbourhoods of the true pixel grid, and the bar member that v3 adds has the lowest out-of-fold error of all members in every analysed fit. The limitations are as large as the results. The held-out sets are small: 12 targets from one family, and two image sets from one source with one seed at $n=60000$. v3 is a portfolio, not one mechanism. Its image members recover the pixel locality that the protocol withholds, and a small CNN that sees the layout has lower error at both cells where it has run. A fit takes 23--24 CPU minutes at $n=60000$. On tabular data v3 is a classical stack whose margin over MLPs is not significant.
