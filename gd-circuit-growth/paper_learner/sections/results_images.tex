\section{How well it does: images}\label{sec:results-img}

On images v3 runs its image members and the classical ones (\cref{tab:members}); DFG does not run. So on images v3 is v2 with one more member, the bar booster \code{cgbt}, and a stacker refitted over all members (\cref{sec:image}). This section gives v3's test error on the two QuickDraw sets held out for it (\cref{sec:res-img-heldout}) and on the image sets it was developed on (\cref{sec:res-img-dev}). It then asks where the gain over v2 comes from (\cref{sec:res-img-gain}) and what a fit costs (\cref{sec:res-img-compute}). Errors, verdicts, stacking weights, FLOPs and CPU seconds come from \repo{study/part10/LEARNER_RESULTS.json} and from the result files and \texttt{.info.json} records in \repo{study/standard_tasks/results/}; \cref{app:repro} says which file stands for v3 on each set. Cells and verdicts are those of \cref{sec:metrics}, where the noise at $n=60000$ is discussed.

\subsection{The held-out sets \code{qd_c} and \code{qd_d}}\label{sec:res-img-heldout}

\code{qd_c} and \code{qd_d} are ten fresh QuickDraw classes each (\cref{sec:tasks}). They were fixed in a commit before any Part 10 design work. No result of a Part 10 learner on them was read before v3 was frozen (\cref{sec:discipline}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Runs.} v3, v2, v1, \code{hgb}, \code{mlp_strong} and \code{mlp_wide} ran 3 seeds at $n=1000$ and $n=10000$ and seed 0 at $n=60000$. \code{mlp_xl} ran at $n=60000$ only. The control \code{mlp_pool} ran on \code{qd_c} at $n\in\{10000,60000\}$, seed 0, and the context \code{cnn} on \code{qd_c} at $n\in\{1000,10000\}$, seed 0. These are the pre-registered cells. The Part 7 \code{mlp}, which enters \code{mlp_best}, was also run, with 3 seeds at every $n$ (not pre-registered).
\item \emph{Where to look.} \Cref{tab:img-cells} gives every held-out cell, \cref{tab:img-summary} the verdicts, and \cref{fig:images} the learning curves.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Against the MLP references} \status{Numerical}. At all six held-out cells v3's error is lower than every reference's and the control's (the context \code{cnn}'s is lower at both \code{qd_c} cells where it ran).
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{mlp_strong} (ensemble of three 256-256 networks): v3 is clearly better at all 6 cells. Its error is 17--23\% lower, and the mean $\log_2$ ratio is $-0.31$ ($-0.32$ at $n\ge10000$).
\item \code{mlp_wide} (three 512-512 networks): clearly better at 5 cells. The sixth, \code{qd_c} at $n=60000$, is a tie: 12.76\% against 14.12\%, 9.6\% lower, just inside the 10\% threshold. Mean $-0.25$.
\item \code{mlp_xl} (three 1024-1024 networks, the CPU-matched reference; $n=60000$ only): two ties. v3 is 9.6\% lower on \code{qd_c} (12.76\% against 14.12\%) and 8.6\% lower on \code{qd_d} (12.64\% against 13.83\%). Mean $-0.14$.
\item \code{mlp_best} equals \code{mlp_strong} at all six held-out cells, so v3 is clearly better at all six (mean $-0.31$). \code{mlp_best+} adds the wider ensembles: 4 cells better and 2 ties, the two $n=60000$ cells (mean $-0.25$). v3 is also clearly better than the Part 7 baseline \code{mlp} at all six cells (mean $-0.53$).
\end{itemize}

So at $n\le10000$ v3 is clearly better than every MLP reference. At $n=60000$ the two widest ensembles come within 10\% of it on \code{qd_c}, and \code{mlp_xl} also on \code{qd_d}.

\paragraph{Against the other references} \status{Numerical}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{hgb}: v3 is clearly better at all 6 cells, with errors 25--30\% lower (\code{qd_c} 29--30\%, \code{qd_d} 25--28\%); mean $\log_2$ ratio $-0.47$.
\item v1, the Part 8 portfolio: clearly better at all 6 cells, by 13.5--20.9\% (\code{qd_c} 16.1\%, 19.4\%, 19.1\% and \code{qd_d} 13.5\%, 16.9\%, 20.9\% at $n=1000$, 10000 and 60000); mean $-0.28$.
\item v2: all 6 cells are ties. v3's error is lower at every cell, by 2.2--3.3\% at $n=1000$ and by 6.4--9.6\% at $n\ge10000$, but never by more than 10\%. The mean $\log_2$ ratio is $-0.10$, and $-0.125$ at $n\ge10000$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The control and the context} \status{Numerical}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{mlp_pool} is \code{mlp_strong} given v2's pooled subset sums as extra inputs (seed 0, \code{qd_c} only). v3 is clearly better at both cells: 16.17\% against 19.37\% at $n=10000$, and 12.76\% against 14.48\% at $n=60000$ (16.5\% and 11.9\% lower). v2 ties it at both cells (17.79\% and 13.63\%, which are 8.2\% and 5.9\% lower), as it did on \code{qd_a} and \code{qd_b} in Part 9. This control matches v2's representation, not v3's: no MLP was given the bar sums (\cref{sec:res-img-gain}).
\item \code{cnn}, a small convolutional network on the unpermuted pixels (\cref{sec:refs}), is context, not a reference. Its \code{qd_c} runs (seed 0) give 24.36\% at $n=1000$ and 14.84\% at $n=10000$, 8.4\% and 8.2\% below v3's errors (26.58\% and 16.17\%): two ties by the cell rule, but lower at both cells.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The best-learner rule}\label{sec:res-img-rule} \status{Numerical}. The pre-registered rule does not use cell verdicts (\cref{sec:discipline}). It makes v3 the learner of this paper if two conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item the mean $\log_2$ error ratio of v3 to v2 over the four held-out image cells with $n\ge10000$ is below 0. It is $-0.125$: the cells give $-0.138$ and $-0.095$ on \code{qd_c} ($n=10000$, 60000) and $-0.122$ and $-0.146$ on \code{qd_d};
\item the mean $\log_2\nstar$ ratio of v3 to v2 on \code{dlheld7} is at most $+0.1$. It is $-0.004$ (\cref{sec:res-dlheld7}).
\end{itemize}
Both hold, so v3 replaced v2. The rule was applied by \repo{study/part10/compare_part10.py} (output: \repo{study/part10/RESULTS.md}) after every held-out cell of v3 and v2 was in, and the decision is recorded in \repo{study/part10/PROTOCOL.md} (28 October, 04:25 UTC).

\paragraph{How firm the gain over v2 is.} By the cell rule it is not established at any single cell. Its direction is consistent: in all 14 seed-paired fits (same training sample, same test set), v3's error is below v2's, by 0.47 to 1.86 points \status{Numerical}. This is a description made after the results were in, not a pre-registered test. The 14 fits share two test sets, so they are not independent.

\Cref{tab:img-cells-dev,tab:img-summary-dev} in \cref{app:further-img} give v3 at the cells of its fixed development evaluation (one fit each, seed 0). \paragraph{The same members, plus one} \status{Numerical}. In each of the 14 held-out fits, v3's and v2's records give every shared member the same out-of-fold error, and \code{ggbt} the same number of boosting rounds. \code{cgbt} draws from its own random stream, so v3's other members are v2's fits. v3 and v2 therefore differ only by \code{cgbt} and by the stacking weights. The final refits also differ where a weight moves between zero and a positive value, because a member with weight 0 is not refitted. \Cref{tab:img-stack} gives the weights and the out-of-fold errors.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{\code{cgbt} is the better booster.} Its out-of-fold error is below \code{ggbt}'s in all 14 fits: by 0.2--4.0 points at $n=1000$, and by 0.13--0.20 in $\log_2$ at $n\ge10000$. It is the lowest of all members in 13 of the 14 fits. The exception is \code{qd_c}, $n=1000$, seed 1, where nearest-neighbour votes on pooled sums are lower (30.1\% against 31.8\%).
\item \emph{It takes \code{ggbt}'s place in the stack.} At $n\ge10000$, \code{cgbt} has the largest family weight in all 8 fits (0.32--0.49). \code{ggbt}'s weight falls from 0.25--0.50 in v2 to 0--0.12 in v3. At $n=1000$, the nearest-neighbour votes on pooled sums keep the largest family weight in 5 of 6 fits, in v3 as in v2.
\item \emph{The stack improves.} v3's stacked out-of-fold error is below v2's in 12 of the 14 fits. At \code{qd_d}, $n=1000$, it is equal in one fit and higher in one (27.2\% against 26.3\%).
\end{itemize}
The weights multiply log-probabilities of different calibration, and the convex stacker splits weight between two nearly collinear boosters, so ``\code{ggbt} gets weight 0'' describes one fit, not the learner (\cref{sec:interp-img-members}).

\paragraph{What \code{cgbt} changes: the bank, not the schedule} \status{Numerical}. \code{cgbt} has \code{ggbt}'s tree settings and early-stopping rule. It differs from \code{ggbt} in three ways: its bank adds bar sums at two lengths, it drops the 300 prototype-pair projections, and its per-tree column fraction is 0.05 instead of 0.12 (\cref{sec:image}). The builder's development runs separate these effects (one booster alone, $n=5000$, validation rows, seed 0; \repo{study/part10/p10_codes/DESIGN.md}, \S4.2). On \code{qd_dev}:
\begin{itemize}
\item v2's bank gave 17.66\% error, and dropping the pairs gave 17.20\%;
\item adding the bars at both lengths gave 15.92\%, and the lower column fraction then gave 15.82\%.
\end{itemize}
On \code{qd_a} the full change gave 17.68\% $\to$ 16.78\%. So on QuickDraw most of the gain is the bars. On Fashion-MNIST the bars did not help. In \code{cgbt}'s fitted trees the bars, two thirds of its columns, carry 0.72--0.80 of the split gain on QuickDraw and MNIST (development fits; \cref{sec:interp-img-members}). Split gain describes how the trees split, not what the predictions depend on.

\paragraph{Compared with Part 9} \status{Numerical}. In Part 9, v2 tied the CPU-matched \code{mlp_xl} at $n=60000$ on \code{qd_a} and \code{qd_b}, and the control \code{mlp_pool} matched v2, so most of v2's large-$n$ advantage over the MLPs was the pooled-sum representation, which an MLP uses as well as the trees do. The Part 10 held-out sets repeat this for v2: it ties \code{mlp_xl} on both sets (13.63\% against 14.12\%; 13.99\% against 13.83\%) and \code{mlp_pool} at both \code{qd_c} cells. v3 moves past the control but not past \code{mlp_xl} (\cref{sec:res-img-heldout}).

\paragraph{Reading} \status{Heuristic}. On images v3's gain over v2 is a better fixed representation for gradient-boosted trees: sums over short runs of pixels along lines recovered from pixel correlations, used at fixed positions. The gain is not evidence that discrete search beats gradient-based learning on images, for three reasons.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The control for v3's own representation was not run.} No MLP was given the bar sums as well as the pooled sums. Part 9's control matched v2 once it had v2's features, so part of v3's margin over the MLPs may again be available to a network given the same features.
\item \emph{Gradient-trained networks that learn local structure were not run.} For example, an MLP trained to learn sparse local connectivity \cite{neyshabur2020convolutions} would be the information-matched comparison.
\item \emph{A network told the layout is better.} The small \code{cnn} (seed 0) has lower error at all three cells where both have run: clearly at \code{qd_a}, $n=10000$, and within the tie band at both \code{qd_c} cells (\cref{sec:res-img-heldout,sec:res-img-dev}).
\end{itemize}
What v3 shows is narrower: a learner that never reads the pixel layout, and whose features are fixed subset sums chosen by rank statistics, reaches 12.6--12.8\% error on fresh QuickDraw classes at $n=60000$. That is 8.6--9.6\% below a CPU-matched MLP ensemble, a tie by the cell rule, and clearly below an MLP given v2's representation (\code{qd_c}).

\paragraph{FLOPs} \status{Numerical} (\cref{tab:img-compute}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{v3 and v2.} By its own count v3 uses 0.30--0.58 of the budget on the held-out cells, the most at $n=60000$. v2 uses 0.22--0.42. \code{cgbt} itself is charged 0.14--0.22 of the budget; the net increase over v2 is 0.05--0.22, because \code{ggbt} is not refitted where its weight is 0.
\item \emph{Near the guard at $n=3000$.} On the development sets, at $n=3000$ (no held-out cell has this size), v3 used 0.85 of the budget on \code{qd_a} and on \code{qd_b}. That is close to the guard at 0.9, which skips members whose projected cost would exceed it (\cref{sec:portfolio}). Part 9's compute audit found that the FLOP charge of Part 8's \code{gbt} member is too low by a factor of 1.14--1.46 at this size, and it projected v2's $n=3000$ QuickDraw cells at about 0.72 (0.69--0.81) of the budget, against v2's own count of at most 0.62. v3 keeps that charge, and no compute audit was made of v3. Applying the audit's factor to \code{gbt} alone (0.28 of the budget by v3's count) puts these two cells at 0.88--0.98 \status{Heuristic}. So v3 is within the budget at $n=3000$ by its own count, but not with a clear margin.
\item \emph{The MLP references.} Every MLP reference spends more FLOPs than the budget: \code{mlp_strong} 2.0 budgets per fit (six training runs), \code{mlp_wide} 5.0, \code{mlp_pool} 5.9 and \code{mlp_xl} 9.2. So by FLOPs v3 is cheaper than every MLP reference at every cell, by a factor of at least 3.4 (\code{mlp_strong} at \code{qd_d}, $n=60000$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{CPU time} \status{Numerical}. Equal FLOPs are far from equal CPU time.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{v3 and v2.} A v3 fit takes 229--291 CPU s at $n=1000$, 402--785 s at $n=10000$, and 1361 and 1464 s at $n=60000$. v2 takes 144--175, 284--602, and 1296 and 1531 s.
\item \emph{v3 against v2 at $n=60000$.} v3 takes about as long as v2 (1.05$\times$ on \code{qd_c}, 0.96$\times$ on \code{qd_d}) although it fits a second booster, because it sets LightGBM's column-wise layout, which leaves the predictions unchanged and which Part 9 found but did not apply to v2. Like for like, the second booster about doubles the CPU time of a fit (\cref{app:further-compute}).
\item \emph{The MLP references.} At $n=60000$ they take 963 and 422 s of wall time (\code{mlp_strong}), 1168 and 989 s (\code{mlp_wide}) and 2196 and 1335 s (\code{mlp_xl}); \code{mlp_pool} takes 716 s. So \code{mlp_xl} takes 0.9--1.6$\times$ v3's time (wall against CPU seconds), which is the sense in which it is CPU-matched. The other ensembles take 0.3--0.9 of v3's time. These are wall-clock seconds on a shared machine and vary with its load (\cref{app:further-compute}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Prediction} is not charged for any learner. A v2 query costs about $2.8\times10^8$ FLOPs at $n=60000$, about $500\times$ an MLP's, because the nearest-neighbour members scan the training set (Part 9). v3 adds \code{cgbt}'s bank projection, about $1.2\times10^7$ FLOPs per query, and up to about 4000 more tree traversals (\repo{study/part10/p10_codes/DESIGN.md}, \S6).
