### 1.3 Evaluation

The rules are Part 8's (`compare_hybrid.py`, HYBRID.md §1).
- **Toy suites.**
  - Inputs are Boolean, d = 30.
  - Grid: n = 16, 32, …, 16384, with 3 seeds.
  - The learners see the same training sets and the same 4096-point test set as the networks.
  - The discrete learners' input columns are permuted per fit (`-perm`).
  - n* is the training size at which the seed-mean test error reaches 0.1, log-interpolated.
  - A target is clearly better (or worse) if |log2 n* ratio| > 0.25, or if one side never reaches 10% error.
- **Images.**
  - Grid: n = 100, 300, 1000, 3000, 10000, 30000, 60000, on the fixed 10000-point test sets.
  - Every learner runs 3 seeds at n ≤ 10000 and seed 0 only at n ≥ 30000 (memory, compute).
  - A cell is better (or worse) if the seed-mean errors differ by more than 10% relative and by more than 2 s.e. The
    s.e. comes from the seed spread of both learners, and is at least the binomial test-set noise.
  - log2 ratios floor errors at 0.002.

**Why permutation invariance.** The comparator is an MLP, which is invariant to a fixed permutation of its input
columns. A method that used pixel adjacency would have to be compared with a CNN. So the image members may not use the
pixel layout, and the QuickDraw sets are stored with their columns permuted.

## 3. Sum features (builder S: DFG v1.2)

`p9_sum/dfg12.py` is DFG v1.1 (`discrete_learner/dfg.py`, md5 `162e4202…`) plus sum-feature blocks. `p9_sum/learner.py`
is the Part 8 portfolio with its DFG member loaded from `dfg12.py`.

### 3.1 The features

A **block** is three things: a subset S of the inputs, signs s_i ∈ {−1, +1}, and a fold flag. Its statistic is the
integer t(x) = Σ_{i∈S} s_i x_i, or |t(x)| when folded.
- **Value groups.** The training values of t are cut into contiguous groups, each holding at least max(3, 0.005 n)
  training points. Rare tail values are merged inward.
- **Features.** The block's features are the group indicators φ_v(x) = [t(x) ∈ group v], with the most populous group
  dropped.
- **What it covers.** Every function of t, including every symmetric function of the signed inputs in S, is then a
  linear combination of the block's features, up to the merged rare values.

There are three variants:
- **additive:** φ_v as above;
- **modulated:** χ_T(x) · φ_v(x), for a character χ_T already in the model, with T's inputs excluded from S;
- **product:** the indicators of the joint cells (group of block A, group of block B), for two blocks with disjoint
  supports.

**Integer combinations with a character.** Take an additive block and a modulated block on the same (S, s), and one
character χ_T. Together they represent g(t + w χ_T) for any integer w, because that equals A(t) + χ_T · B(t). This
covers forms such as [|x1 + … + x5 + 2 x6x7| ≤ 1]. Thresholds of such combinations, like thrsum, are also covered by
v1.1's logistic readout on the inputs and the character.

### 3.2 The test

A candidate block is accepted when the residual's projection onto the block's group indicators passes a χ² tail bound,
with a union bound over the whole class.
- **The statistic.** With r the residual and s² = mean(r²),
  T = Σ_v n_v · mean_v(r)² / s².
- **The threshold.** The block passes if T > m + 2√(m t) + 2t (the Laurent–Massart bound). Here m is the number of
  occupied groups minus 1, and
  t = ln(4K · C(d, k) · 2^{k−1} · 2 · (1 + #modulators) · k_max / δ)
  for a block of size k, with K = 4, δ = 0.1 and k_max = 24.
- **Why the adaptive search is allowed.** The bound covers every (S, s) of every size up to k_max, both fold variants and every
  modulator. So the adaptive search below does not invalidate it.
- **What it costs.** At d = 30 and k = 5–11, t is about 24–34 nats, and the χ² threshold is about 63 (k = 5), 74 (k = 7)
  and 95 (k = 11). Since T ≤ n, no block of 7 inputs can pass below n ≈ 75, even for a perfect sym7.
- **Beyond the round's characters.** A block must also pass the same test on the residual projected off the constant
  and off the characters accepted in the same round. This was added after a block {x1, noise input}, carried by x1's
  signal, was accepted on a dev target.

### 3.3 The search for (S, s)

The search uses only the residual on the training points, never the target's identity. Scores use the first
min(n, 2048) points; the final test uses all n.
1. **Pair-matrix seeds.** M = mean(r x xᵀ) with the diagonal removed; DFG v1.1 already computes it.
   - An even symmetric function on (S, s) makes M restricted to S rank one, with sign pattern s.
   - For the top 2 eigenvectors of M, order the inputs by |u_i|, take s_i = sign(u_i), and score every prefix.
2. **Degree-1 seeds.** Order the inputs by |mean(r x_i)| and score every prefix. These are for sums that are not even,
   such as ex3of8.
3. **Greedy growth.** Start from the 3 strongest pairs of M (both relative signs) and from the 2 strongest single
   inputs. Add the input and sign that maximize T, until 5 steps pass without improvement or k_max is reached.
4. **Local refinement.** For the 3 best candidates, take the best improving move (remove an input, add one with either
   sign, flip a sign) until none improves.
5. **Final test.** Test the best refined candidate on all n.

**Per DFG round**, after v1.1's character searches:
- up to 2 additive blocks, deflating the residual after each;
- one product block, by a conditional route or a split route, accepted only if its margin beats the first additive
  candidate's;
- a modulated block for each of the 4 characters with the largest weights;
- products of pairs of additive blocks accepted in earlier rounds.

Accepted blocks become least-squares columns, within DFG's p ≤ n/2 limit. The readout, stopping rule and budget caps are
v1.1's. A "forced block" for small n was tried and is off in the frozen file.

### 3.4 What is discrete

| part | what it is | continuous? |
|---|---|---|
| support and signs (S, s) | a subset of inputs and a sign vector, chosen by prefix scoring, greedy steps, local moves and a union-bound test | no |
| value groups | contiguous sets of integer values of t, from training counts | no |
| modulator χ_T | a character already accepted by DFG's tests | no |
| readout | least squares + 15 Newton steps of L2 logistic regression (v1.1, unchanged) | convex readout |
| ordering heuristic | eigenvectors of the residual pair matrix, used only to rank inputs for candidate prefixes | inside the search, not in the hypothesis |

The hypothesis is a weighted sum of characters, group indicators of integer signed sums (optionally times a character),
the inputs, and v1.1's spectral (u·x)² features. The spectral features are unchanged, and they are still the one
feature with a continuous direction, as in Part 8.

### 3.5 Development (dev data only)

Every change in p9_sum/DESIGN.md §4 was driven by a dev-target failure:
- **Local refinement**, for band11: the eigenvector ordering had put a noise input into the block.
- **Degree-1 and single-input seeds**, for ex3of8, which has no pair signal.
- **The conditional and split product routes**, for twosym.

### 4.1 p9_gates (builder I1): pooled subset sums and gate trees

**Two discrete objects**, built from the training data of the fit:
- **Pooling sets.** For every non-constant column i and k ∈ {4, 9, 16}, S_i(k) is the k columns most correlated with
  column i over the first ≤ 20000 training rows, with i itself first.
  - The pooled feature is the subset sum p_i(x) = Σ_{j∈S_i(k)} x_j, a signed sum with all signs +1, over a pixel set
    chosen by a data statistic.
  - On images the most correlated pixels are in practice the spatial neighbours, so p_i acts as a blob detector found
    from the data matrix. It never uses adjacency or the 28 × 28 layout.
- **Prototype pairs.** 300 pairs (a, b) of training points of different classes, drawn uniformly. The feature is
  (x_a − x_b) · x. A threshold on it is a two-prototype comparison: the hyperplane at (|x_a|² − |x_b|²)/2 separates
  points closer to a from points closer to b.

**Members:**

| member | what it is |
|---|---|
| `gknn_<k>`, k ∈ {1, 3, 9, 27} | k-nearest-neighbour votes in the pooled representation at scale 9: Euclidean distance on the ≈ 640–700 subset sums p_i. Leave-one-out votes serve as out-of-fold predictions, as for Part 8's `knn`. |
| `ggbt` | LightGBM on the gate bank [x, p(x) at scales 4, 9 and 16, (x_a − x_b) · x], about 3000–3200 columns. |

Each split of `ggbt` is a gate [u · x > t]: u is a pixel, a pooling set or a prototype pair, and t is a histogram bin
boundary (data quantile, 63 bins). Its settings:
- 31 leaves, lr 0.1, 12% of the columns per tree;
- bagging 0.5 above 20000 rows; `min_data_in_leaf` = clip(n/25, 2, 20);
- 100 rounds below 5000 training points. Otherwise it stops early on the error of an inner validation split (patience
  50 rounds, 150–400 rounds).

**Refits:**
- k-fold regime (n ≤ 3000): `ggbt` is refitted on all n with 100 rounds.
- n = 10000 (n·d ≤ 1e7): refitted on all n with the early-stopped round count.
- n·d > 1e7: the holdout-trained booster is final. At n = 60000 it was trained on 48600 points.

**What is discrete.**
- Features: pixels, subset sums over correlation-chosen pixel sets, and prototype-pair projections. All have weights in
  {−1, 0, +1} on pixels or on two training points, and no weight is fitted.
- Thresholds: data quantiles, chosen by the booster's exhaustive split search. Trees are conjunctions of gates.
- Continuous fits: only boosting's Newton leaf values on fixed partitions and the stacker's convex weights, the same
  allowances as Part 8.
- Not used: neural networks, gradient descent on a feature parameter, continuous kernels, eigenvectors or class means.

**Permutation invariance** (`p9_gates/test_perm.py`).
- **Exact for the pooling sets and `gknn`.** Permuting the columns permutes the sets exactly. Correlations are rounded
  to 1e-9, and ties are broken by a column fingerprint, never by column index. On three random permutations of
  `qd_dev` and MNIST, the test finds identical sets, identical leave-one-out votes and identical pair projections.
- **The tie fix.** It was needed: index tie-breaking had changed 1 of 644 pooling sets under a permutation.
- **In distribution only, for `ggbt`.** LightGBM draws the per-tree column subsets by index, so `ggbt` is invariant in
  distribution, not bit for bit. The same holds for Part 8's `gbt` and `et`.

**Budget.**
- *What is counted* (multiply-add = 2):
  - pooling sets: the correlation matrix and the ranking;
  - the pooled features for `gknn`;
  - `gknn`'s distances (v1's kNN count);
  - `ggbt`'s projections and binning;
  - every trained tree, including the early-stopping patience rounds. Each tree is charged by the Part 8 compute
    audit's per-tree formula, with the bin counts read from the built `Dataset`.
- *The guard.* Each of the block's two parts is skipped if its projected cost would take the total above 0.9 of the
  budget. That never happened on dev.
- *Measured use.* On the 9 dev cells the portfolio with `p9_gates` uses 0.25–0.46 of the budget (3 MLP training runs),
  against v1's 0.16–0.34 (§7).

**Development** (validation rows; p9_gates/DESIGN.md §5):
- A convex readout over a random bank of quantile gates was tried first and abandoned. Its cost is capacity-limited: at
  n = 50000 on MNIST, 4000 gates gave 3.99% validation error.
- Boosted trees over the augmented gate bank were kept. On MNIST at n = 10000:

  | features | validation error |
  |---|---|
  | raw pixels | 4.52% |
  | raw + pooled (k = 9) + 300 pairs | 3.86% |
- kNN in the pooled space beat raw kNN on MNIST and `qd_dev`, and scale 9 was kept for `gknn`.
- Early stopping on validation log-loss stopped `ggbt` at 57 rounds on MNIST at n = 10000, while the error was still
  falling. It was replaced by stopping on validation error: 179 rounds there, and validation error 3.42% → 3.30%.
- The last change before the freeze added the multi-scale bank. It was tested lightly: as LightGBM alone on `qd_dev` and

### 4.2 p9_bank (builder I2): screened prototype-pair threshold bits

**Features.** Each feature is a pair of training points (a, b) of different classes and a threshold t, giving the bit
φ(x) = [(x_a − x_b) · x > t].
- a is drawn uniformly. b is the nearest point of another class to a, within a random reference sample of ≤ 4000
  training points.
- t lies halfway between an order statistic of the projection, at a random quantile level, and the next larger
  distinct value. Each direction gets 3 thresholds at stratified levels in [0.1, 0.9].

**Screening.** The bank is built in 8 rounds on a subsample of ≤ 15000 rows.
- Each round draws a fresh pool of 6k candidate bits (k = F/8) and drops bits that fire on < 1% or > 99% of the rows.
- The candidates are scored by squared standardized correlation with the current multi-output ridge residual, and the
  best k are kept.
- The ridge fit is updated after each round, so later rounds look for what the earlier bits miss.

**Readout.** A closed-form ridge regression of the one-hot labels on the bits.
- The penalty is chosen on a 15% internal validation split from {1, 10, 100, 1000}, then refined ×3 and /3.
- The class probabilities are softmax(τ · scores), with τ fitted on the same split.

**Size and invariance.**
- *F from the budget.* F is the largest value in {8000, 7000, …, 500, 250} whose projected cost fits
  min(0.6 × budget, 0.9 × budget − spent − 0.7 × the LightGBM charge). On the dev images that gives F = 2496 at
  n = 1000, 5000 at n = 10000, 4496 at n = 50000 and 4000 at n = 60000.
- *Exact invariance.* The member uses only Euclidean distances, inner products with differences of training images,
  per-feature quantiles and the labels. With float64 projections and between-value thresholds, `test_perm.py` gives a
  maximum score difference of 0 and identical features under a random column permutation.
- *The earlier version.* The killed session's version used float32 and interpolated thresholds. It failed the same test,
  with prediction agreement 0.939.

**What is discrete.** The directions are pairs of data points, not fitted vectors, although their entries are
continuous. The thresholds are order statistics. Only the convex ridge readout, the 1-d temperature and the stacker are
fitted continuously.

    sampled columns for every tree. At n = 30000 a tree takes 198.5 ms, against 73.2 ms with `force_col_wise=True`.
  - *The fix.* The setting leaves the predictions bit-identical (checked on `qd_dev` at n = 8000) and cuts `ggbt`'s
    time about 2.2–2.7×. It was not applied (§8, item 5).
  - *FLOP count.* Not affected, because the copies are data movement.
- **Memory at n = 60000.** p9_gates peaks at about 6 GB process RSS: 6.2 GB on Fashion-MNIST and 4.4 GB on MNIST,
  against v1's ~3 GB. p9_bank peaks at 2.55 / 2.56 / 2.75 GB (MNIST / Fashion / `qd_dev`).
- PLACEHOLDER: held-out compute (budget fractions and CPU of v2 on `qd_a`, `qd_b` and `dlheld4/5`).

**Prediction cost** (not counted, as for every learner):

| component | cost per query | comparison |

## 9. Limitations

PLACEHOLDER: the final wording of the claim, written after the held-out results.

Limitations already known from development:
- **The image member is still a tree ensemble plus kNN.** `ggbt` is LightGBM over a richer discrete feature bank, and
  `gknn` is nearest neighbours in a pooled representation. Neither learns a continuous direction.
- **"Layout-free" holds in the letter, not in spirit.** The pooling sets are built from pixel correlations alone and are
  exactly permutation-equivariant. But on images the most correlated pixels are the spatial neighbours, so the sets
  recover local neighbourhoods from the data. An MLP is free to learn the same.
- **Invariance in distribution only.** `ggbt`'s column sampling makes it invariant in distribution, not bit for bit.
- **Borderline discreteness.** The prototype-pair directions x_a − x_b have continuous entries, though they are data
  points, not fitted vectors. DFG's spectral (u·x)² block is unchanged and is still the one feature with a continuous
  direction. The sum-feature search ranks inputs by eigenvectors of the residual pair matrix, although the hypothesis
  holds only the discrete (S, s).
- **Sum features cover ±1 weights only.** Blocks have ±1 weights, at most one modulating character, and products of at
  most two blocks. The union bound costs samples at small n: no 7-input block passes below n ≈ 75. Weak blocks are
  limited by the search: band11 is found reliably only from n ≈ 1000.
- **Cost.**
  - *CPU.* At n = 60000 a v2 image fit takes 22–29 CPU-min, against tens of seconds for the MLP runs the FLOP budget
    corresponds to.
  - *Memory.* About twice v1's.
  - *Prediction.* About 500× an MLP per query.
  - FLOP counts of library trees are analytic estimates.
- **Small n on images was not developed.** The dev grid started at n = 1000. With 5 more members the stacker has about
  22 inputs, and at n = 100–300 its noise may grow. A crash at small n was found only after the freeze (§8, item 6).
- **Power.** Each toy test suite has 12 targets, and there are two fresh image sets with one seed at n ≥ 30000. `dlheld4`
  is not blind for the sum-feature component.
- **References.** The image comparators are MLPs: no convolution, augmentation or tuned architecture, by design (§1.4).
  The toy networks are width-128 MLPs at one learning rate and 20000 steps, as in Part 8. `nets_best` and `mlp_best`
  are hindsight minima that favour the networks.
