# A discrete portfolio against gradient-descent-trained networks, on the toy suites and on standard tasks

Part 8 of the study (26 October 2026). Tables: [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md), generated by `compare_hybrid.py` from the
result files, and [`ABLATIONS.md`](ABLATIONS.md), post hoc, generated by `ablations.py`. Protocol and designation:
[`PROTOCOL.md`](PROTOCOL.md). Code audits, run while the primary's held-out jobs were running (§8):
[`audit/`](audit/) (`protocol.md`, `compute.md`, `discreteness.md`, `fairness.md`). Designs: `hyb_portfolio/`,
`hyb_stage/`, `hyb_union/`, each with a `DESIGN.md`. Result files: `results/toy__<learner>__<suite>.json` (toy),
`../standard_tasks/results/<learner>__<dataset>.json` (real; `err[n][seed]`, `flops`, `cpu_seconds`) and
`results/nets_final_err.json` (the toy networks' final-step test errors, exported by `nets_export.py`).

**Question (from the user).** "Can you come up with a discrete method that beats NN gradient descent on both the toy
tasks considered earlier and also these real tasks you looked at now?"
- *Toy tasks:* the Boolean d = 30 suites of Parts 1–6. The score is n*, the training size at which test error reaches
  10%, against the study's networks.
- *Real tasks:* Part 7's MNIST, Fashion-MNIST and 16 PMLB datasets, against MLPs. For this part, 10 further PMLB
  datasets were added and kept unseen.

**Short answer: partly** (Numerical). One `fit()` that dispatches by input type was built: `hyb_portfolio`. It
combines Part 6's Fourier-character search (DFG) with LightGBM, ExtraTrees, kNN votes and logistic regression, stacked
on out-of-fold predictions. Its fits stay within the FLOP budget, by its own counts and after the compute audit's
correction; prediction is not counted (§6). Its results:
- **Toy (fresh suite `dlheld3`, inputs permuted).** It needs less data than the best network variant on 9 of 10
  targets. The mean log2 n* ratio is −1.41 (about 2.7× less data) against a per-target best of three network
  configurations chosen with hindsight. Against the single configurations `nets_cewd` and `nets_wd` it is −1.55 and
  −1.67.
  - The exception is sym9 (|x1 + … + x9| ≤ 1). It needs 1229 samples there, against 350 for a network trained with
    logistic loss and weight decay.
  - On the other four toy suites, which are development data or not blind for DFG, it is clearly better on 57 of 60
    targets, tied on 3, and clearly worse on none.
- **Tabular (10 fresh PMLB datasets).** Against the hindsight-best MLP per cell, its mean error is lower on 8 of 10
  datasets (two-sided sign test p = 0.11).
  - The mean log2 error ratio is −0.17, a geometric-mean error about 11% lower. 10 of 53 cells are better and 2 worse.
  - Against a strong regularized MLP ensemble alone, the mean log2 ratio is −0.28; against one network of that recipe,
    −0.32. The sign test is 8 of 10 (p = 0.11) against all three references, so none of this is significant.
  - It loses on texture and on krkopt.
  - On the Part 7 tabular set (11 datasets, references known to the designers) it is −0.11 against the hindsight-best
    MLP, with 12 better and 3 worse of 59 cells, and lower on 8 of 11 datasets (p = 0.23). Its mean error is higher
    there on nursery, pendigits and connect_4.
- **Images (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST; no fresh image set).** Its seed-mean error is below every MLP's at n ≤ 3000, by
  about 1–13% relative, but only MNIST n = 3000 resolves as better. At n = 10000 they are about equal. At n = 30000 and
  60000 (seed 0) it is clearly behind the strong MLP, a 3-network ensemble that uses twice its budget: at 60000, 1.98%
  against 1.43% on MNIST and 10.7% against 9.7% on Fashion-MNIST. Against one network of that recipe only MNIST
  n = 30000 resolves.

So the method needs clearly less data than the study's gradient-descent-trained networks on 9 of 10 fresh toy targets.
On held-out tabular data its error is lower than the MLPs' on average, but the sign tests are not significant (8 of 10
fresh datasets, p = 0.11; 8 of 11 Part 7 datasets, p = 0.23). On images its error is slightly lower at n ≤ 3000
(resolved at 1 of 8 cells), level at n = 10000, and clearly above a well-regularized MLP ensemble at n ≥ 30000. It is
an AutoML-style portfolio, not one discrete mechanism:
- the toy half's large margins come from exhaustive character search;
- the real half is carried by tree ensembles, kNN and stacking. DFG runs on 1 of the 23 held-out real datasets.

"Discrete" here means *no continuous feature parameters*. Gradient boosting is functional gradient descent over trees,
and DFG's spectral block uses continuous eigenvector directions (§9).

## 1. Question and protocol

**Pre-registration** ([`PROTOCOL.md`](PROTOCOL.md), written before any design result was seen):
- **Data access.** Development may use the toy `designed` suite (21 targets) and the 5 standard DEV datasets
  (optdigits, magic, kr_vs_kp, satimage, spambase). The final evaluation uses:
  - the toy suites `random` (20), `dlheld` (12), `dlheld2` (7) and the fresh `dlheld3` (10);
  - the Part 7 held-out set HELDOUT (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, 11 PMLB);
  - the fresh HELDOUT2 (10 PMLB).

  `dlheld3` and HELDOUT2 were fixed in commit 04a215b2, before any Part 8 design work.
- **Budgets.** Everything is counted in FLOPs, model selection included.
  - Toy: one network training run per fit, 1.62e11 FLOPs at d = 30 (`common.toy_budget`).
  - Standard tasks: 3 MLP training runs per fit (`common.budget_real`). That is the baseline MLP's own cost, including
    its learning-rate selection.
  - Prediction is not counted, as for the networks. §6 discloses it.
- **Designs.** Three builders worked independently, on dev data only, each from a different design angle. Each design
  was frozen (md5 sums in PROTOCOL.md) and run once on the full dev evaluation.
- **Designation rule**, fixed in advance and applied to the dev scores before any held-out run:
  - *Dev scores.* Toy: the mean log2(n* learner / n* networks). Real: the mean log2(err / min(err mlp, err mlp_l2)),
    with errors floored at 0.002. Each comes with better / worse counts.
  - *"Beats GD on dev"* (the protocol's label): both means are below 0 and there are more better than worse targets
    or cells.
  - *Primary:* among the designs that beat GD on dev, the lowest sum of the two means.

| design | toy mean log2 n* ratio | toy better / worse (of 21) | real mean log2 err ratio | real better / worse (of 24) | sum |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hyb_stage | −2.79 | 21 / 0 | −0.122 | 1 / 0 | −2.91 |
| hyb_union | −2.52 | 21 / 0 | −0.127 | 0 / 0 | −2.65 |
| hyb_portfolio | −2.98 | 21 / 0 | −0.248 | 1 / 0 | −3.23 |

hyb_union has no better real cell, so it does not beat GD on dev. **The primary design is hyb_portfolio.** It is also
best on each side separately. These dev scores are in-sample: the builders tuned on the same dev test sets (protocol
audit F8).

**Evaluation.**
- **Toy.**
  - Grid: n = 16, 32, …, 16384, with 3 seeds (2 on `random`).
  - The learners see the same training sets and the same 4096-point test set as the networks.
  - n* is the training size at which the seed-mean test error reaches 0.1, log-interpolated over the grid.
  - A target is *clearly better* (or worse) if |log2 n* ratio| > 0.25, or if the reference never reaches 10% error.
  - The headline runs permute the input columns per fit (`-perm`, §3.3). The natural-order runs are also reported.
- **Real.**
  - Learning curves from n = 100 to the full pool, on Part 7's fixed test sets, with 3 seeds. On images, seed 0 only at
    n = 30000 and 60000, for the discrete learners and for the strong MLP alike.
  - A cell is *better* (or worse) if the seed-mean errors differ by more than 10% relative and by more than 2 s.e.
    The s.e. comes from the seed spread of both learners, and is at least the binomial test-set noise.
  - *Median data factor:* the n at which the learner reaches the reference's error, divided by n.
  - *Datasets with lower mean error:* the per-dataset mean over n of the log2 ratio is below 0, with a two-sided exact
    sign test. This was added to the protocol before any held-out run.

**What is blind** (protocol audit F2, F3). The headline evidence is the two fresh sets.

| set | status | role here |
|---|---|---|
| toy `designed` (21) | development | dev score |
| toy `random` (20) | development data for DFG in Part 6 | supporting, labelled |
| toy `dlheld` (12) | post hoc for DFG v1.1 | supporting, labelled |
| toy `dlheld2` (7) | blind for DFG v1.1, but already used once (Part 6) | supporting, labelled |
| **toy `dlheld3` (10)** | fresh, fixed before any design | **toy headline** |
| Part 7 HELDOUT, 11 tabular | not blind at the reference level: the builders read Part 7's per-dataset tables | supporting, labelled |
| Part 7 HELDOUT, MNIST and Fashion-MNIST | same; there is no fresh image set | the only image evidence |
| **HELDOUT2 (10 PMLB)** | fresh, fixed before any design, never read by a builder | **tabular headline** |

**References.**
- **Toy networks.** All are μP MLPs d-128-128-1, trained with Adam at batch 64 for 20000 steps on the fixed training
  sets, and read at the final step.
  - `nets`: the study's networks (square loss, no weight decay).
  - `nets_wd`: decoupled weight decay 0.1, the value the fairness auditor fixed a priori for its dev probe (not tuned).
    Added after the fairness audit; same width, steps and FLOPs; run on all five suites.
  - `nets_cewd`: logistic loss plus weight decay 0.1; on `dlheld3` only.
  - `nets_best`: per target, the variant with the smallest n*. This is chosen with hindsight and favours the networks.
- **MLPs.** All are 256-256 MLPs as in Part 7.
  - `mlp`: the baseline (lr ∈ {1e-3, 3e-4}, early stopping on a 10% validation split).
  - `mlp_reg`: an L2-regularized MLP selected on validation. This is `mlp_l2` on tabular data and `mlp_l2unit` (raw
    [0, 1] pixels) on images, at every n.
  - `mlp_strong` (`../standard_tasks/mlp_strong.py`, added after the fairness audit). Its settings follow the
    auditor's recommendation and standard practice; it was smoke-tested on dev data only (magic, spambase) and not
    tuned on any held-out result. Unlike the audit's proposal, lr is fixed at 1e-3 (not chosen from {1e-3, 3e-4}) and
    the weight-decay grid includes 1:
    - quantile-transformed continuous inputs, or [0, 1] pixels;
    - AdamW, lr 1e-3 with cosine decay to 0;
    - decoupled weight decay ∈ {0, 0.1, 1} chosen on validation;
    - dropout 0.1;
    - an ensemble of 3 networks.

    That is 6 training runs per fit, twice the hybrids' budget. Its first network alone is `mlp_strong1`.
  - `mlp_best`: per cell, the lowest seed-mean test error over every MLP variant run on that cell. This is chosen with
    hindsight on the test set and favours the MLPs. The variants available differ by split (fairness audit F9):

    | split | variants in `mlp_best` |
    |---|---|
    | HELDOUT2 | mlp, mlp_l2, mlp_strong, mlp_strong1 |
    | images | mlp, mlp_unit (n ≤ 10000), mlp_l2unit, mlp_strong, mlp_strong1 |
    | Part 7 tabular | mlp, mlp_plus (n ≤ 3000), mlp_l2, mlp_strong and mlp_strong1 (n ≤ 10000) |
    | dev | mlp, mlp_l2 |

    `mlp_plus` is effectively an lr-grid variant, since its weight decay is negligible.
- **Calibration references** (not part of the comparison):
  - `lin`: L2 logistic regression on the raw inputs, with λ cross-validated (`refs.py`). Shown on the toy suites to
    separate wins that need no feature search.
  - `hgb`: sklearn histogram gradient boosting with default settings.

## 2. The primary design and the two others

**hyb_portfolio** ([`hyb_portfolio/DESIGN.md`](hyb_portfolio/DESIGN.md)). Each member is a learner without continuous
feature parameters that outputs class probabilities:

| member | what it is | when it runs | intended for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dfg`, `dfg_forced` | DFG v1.1 unchanged (Part 6): exhaustive Fourier characters of degree ≤ 4 (5–6 on a subsample), union-bound tests on the residual, extensions, local Walsh search, linear and spectral blocks, least-squares + logistic readout; `dfg_forced` is DFG's small-n variant (n ≤ 512) | only if C = 2 and every column is binary or categorical (≤ 256 bits) | parities, staircases, multiplexers, sparse polynomials: features with no marginal signal |
| `gbt` | LightGBM, 100 rounds, lr 0.1, 31 leaves | always | thresholds and interactions; ANDs and DNFs |
| `et` | ExtraTrees, 100 trees; out-of-bag probabilities are its out-of-fold predictions | always | smooth structure at small n |
| `knn_<metric>_<k>` | k-nearest-neighbour vote fractions, k ∈ {1, 3, 9, 27}, under the MLP's encoding (`std`) and under one common scale (`raw`); leave-one-out votes are the out-of-fold predictions | always (`raw` when the scales differ) | images, prototype-like data |
| `lin_<C>` | multinomial L2 logistic regression, C ∈ {0.05, 1} (only C = 1 above n = 10000) | always | linear structure |

**Stacking.** The score is s_c(x) = b_c + Σ_m w_m L_m(x)_c, with w ≥ 0. Here L_m are member m's out-of-fold
log-probabilities. The weights are fitted by L2-penalized multinomial log-loss, which is convex.
- *Stacking set.* n ≤ 500: 10-fold cross-validation. n ≤ 3000: 5-fold. n > 3000: one stratified holdout of
  min(n/5, 6000) points.
- *Refits.* DFG, LightGBM and logistic regression are refitted on all n. kNN and ExtraTrees already use all n.

**Budget split.**
- DFG: its out-of-fold fits share 35% of the budget, and its final fit(s) get at most 50%.
- Every other member is skipped if its projected cost exceeds 90–95% of the budget. This never triggered on dev.

**Large inputs.** When n·d > 1e7 (MNIST and Fashion-MNIST at n ≥ 30000):
- float32 encodings;
- LightGBM with row subsampling 0.5 and column subsampling 0.25;
- no LightGBM refit.

These settings were chosen from synthetic timing runs and had never run on real data before the held-out evaluation.

**On the 22 held-out real datasets other than mushroom**, the design is {LightGBM, ExtraTrees, up to 8 kNN members, 1–2
logistic regressions} plus a stacker, by construction.

**The two other designs.** Both were frozen and evaluated, with scope cuts (§8):
- **hyb_stage** ([`hyb_stage/DESIGN.md`](hyb_stage/DESIGN.md)), stagewise.
  - *Stage 1:* a multi-class port of DFG's search, over characters on binary, one-hot and thermometer bits, plus linear
    and spectral blocks and kNN votes. It is read out by penalized logistic or softmax regression.
  - *Stage 2:* LightGBM on the stage-1 margins.
  - The stage set {stage 1, stage 1 + trees, trees alone} is chosen by cross-validation.
  - Its spectral (u·z)² features have continuous directions and fire on real data.
- **hyb_union** ([`hyb_union/DESIGN.md`](hyb_union/DESIGN.md)), unified boosting.
  - It starts from a cross-validated logistic regression and stacked honest kNN, ExtraTrees and linear blocks.
  - Each round adds the best of four families — a tree, a Fourier character, a linear step, or one honest block. The
    families are compared by a penalized Newton statistic.
  - It stops on a 480 s wall-clock limit, among other rules (§6).

### 3.3 What changed with input permutation and with stronger networks

**Permutation** (protocol audit F1).
- *Why it was needed.* Every toy suite puts the relevant inputs first, and all three designs break exact ties toward low
  column indices (DFG's enumeration order, LightGBM's split choice). The networks are symmetric under input
  permutation. So every held-out toy suite was re-run for the primary with the columns permuted per fit, and that run
  is the headline (the secondary designs were re-run this way on `dlheld3` only).
- *Effect on suite means.* Small: the mean log2 ratio against `nets` changes by 0.01–0.04 per suite (table above).
- *Effect on single targets.* Up to 0.64 in log2, in both directions. The largest is par5 on `dlheld`: 539 in natural
  order, 839 permuted. The natural-order runs are kept in RESULTS.md.

**Stronger networks** (fairness audit F1). The audit's probe measured that weight decay 0.1, or logistic loss plus
weight decay, cuts the networks' n* by about 2× at the same compute. In the full runs the largest cut is 3.7×, on poly4
(10199 to 2745).
- *Margin shrink.* Against `nets_best` the portfolio's suite-mean margins shrink by 0.65 (designed), 0.66 (random),
  0.49 (dlheld), 0.63 (dlheld2) and 0.72 (dlheld3) in log2 (from unrounded means).
- *Which targets shrink.* Every kind of target. The largest per-target shrinks are on `dlheld3`: poly4 by 1.9 in log2,
  thrint by 1.5 and sym9 by 1.2. On the other suites the largest are 0.8–1.0 (e.g. par3, thr10, andxor, inter2,
  thr20). poly4's and thrint's margins stay negative, and the sparse parity-type margins survive.
- *Sign of the comparison.* The per-target sign is the same against every reference: negative on 9 targets, positive
  on sym9. sym9 is already +0.64 against the unregularized `nets` (787).
- *What `nets_best` is.* On `dlheld3` it is a hindsight minimum over three configurations, and no single network
  configuration achieves it. `nets_cewd` is best on 5 targets and `nets_wd` on 5. `nets_cewd` is worse than plain
  `nets` on mux11 (1699 against 1527), dlist8 (108 against 104) and h3rf0 (113 against 109). On the other four suites
  `nets_best` equals `nets_wd`.

### 3.4 sym9, the one loss

- *Errors.* The portfolio's seed-mean test error on sym9 is 0.39 at n = 256, 0.20 at 512, 0.13 at 1024 and 0.014 at
  2048, giving n* = 1229. The logistic-loss, weight-decayed networks reach 10% at about 350.
- *Who carries it.* DFG alone gets 1220, and the portfolio without DFG gets 7113 (ABLATIONS.md). So the loss is DFG's.
- *hyb_stage* (591) is closer to the networks, but still worse than `nets_cewd`.

Heuristic reading, not tested here:
- sym9 depends on the inputs only through s = x1 + … + x9, so a network can represent it with units on the single
  direction (1, …, 1).
- Its Fourier weight is spread over many characters of degrees 2, 4, 6 and 8 with equal coefficients within each
  degree. A character search pays for each character separately.
- DFG's spectral block could in principle produce (u·x)² with u ∝ (1, …, 1). Whether it fires on sym9 was not checked.

- *Images.* With enough data, a regularized MLP's learned continuous directions beat prototype memory and axis-aligned
  trees. This is the same reading as in Part 7.

## 8. Audits, deviations from the protocol, scope cuts

**Audits.** Four independent code-and-process audits (protocol and leakage, compute accounting, discreteness, and
fairness of the baselines) ran while the primary's held-out jobs were running. The auditors were instructed not to read
held-out results of the Part 8 learners, and each reports that it read none. By the time the audit-driven changes were
decided, however, the orchestrating session had already seen parts of the primary's held-out results in the job logs:
- the per-dataset error curves of 7 tabular datasets (phoneme, ring, twonorm and waveform_40 from the Part 7 set;
  texture, wine_quality_white and sleep from HELDOUT2);
- the full MNIST learning curve (all n, next to the MLP references then available), and a few single-seed cells
  (connect_4 n = 30000, sleep n = 10000 and 30000);
- the summaries of the first two `random` toy shards (4 of the 20 targets).

It had also seen the held-out toy results of the `hgb` reference. Every change made on the audits' findings
strengthens a reference, removes a bias in the learner's favour, or narrows the claim:
- the references `nets_wd` (wd = 0.1, the value the fairness auditor fixed for its dev probe), `nets_cewd` and
  `mlp_strong` (settings from the auditor's recommendation and standard practice, smoke-tested on dev data only);
- the input permutation;
- the restriction of the headline to the fresh sets.

No learner was changed. None of the audits found test-data use inside a learner or a held-out access by a builder.
The freezes hold, and a re-run of the frozen primary reproduced its dev errors exactly at the cells re-run.

**Job scheduling.** To give the reference runs priority, the secondary design's running jobs (hyb_stage on

was paused. Two duplicate reference runs (`nets_wd` on `dlheld`, `mlp_strong` on pendigits) were started by mistake
and stopped; one copy of each was kept.

## 9. Limitations, and the honest wording of the claim

**What "discrete" means here** (discreteness audit).
- *What the hypothesis is made of:* a weighted sum of tree cells, Fourier characters, nearest-neighbour votes, the raw
  inputs, and DFG's spectral features.
- *What is fitted continuously:* only convex readouts and stacking weights, plus boosting's Newton leaf values on fixed
  partitions.
- *What it does not contain:* no neural network and no gradient descent on a continuous feature parameter.

Borderline components:
- **Gradient boosting.** It is functional gradient descent over trees. The line between this method and the networks
  is "no continuous feature parameters", not "no gradients".
- **kNN votes.** Nearest-neighbour memory under a fixed Euclidean metric. A Gaussian-weighted version would be a
  continuous kernel smoother.
- **Logistic regression on continuous inputs.** A linear model, not a discrete feature.
- **DFG's spectral block** (and hyb_stage's). Its features are (u·x)², with u a continuous eigenvector of the
  residual-weighted second-moment matrix: one closed-form step of continuous feature learning.
  - In the primary it acts only through DFG, so only on Boolean C = 2 inputs (the toy suites and mushroom).
  - Part 6 records it as DFG's first route on majxor (`dlheld`).
  - It was not ablated.

**Honest wording** (proposed):

> One learner that never trains a neural network or any continuous feature parameter needs clearly less data than the
> study's gradient-descent-trained networks (width-128 MLPs, 20000 Adam steps, three loss and regularization settings)
> on 9 of 10 fresh toy Boolean targets, and clearly more on one symmetric function (sym9). On the 60 targets of the four
> non-fresh toy suites it is clearly better on 57 and tied on 3. On 10 fresh tabular datasets its error is lower on
> average than the hindsight-best MLP's (8 of 10 datasets, sign test p = 0.11, not significant). On MNIST and
> Fashion-MNIST, for which there is no fresh set, its seed-mean error is slightly below every MLP's at n ≤ 3000
> (resolved at 1 of 8 cells) and level at n = 10000. At n ≥ 30000 it is clearly above a weight-decayed,
> dropout-regularized ensemble of 3 MLPs that uses twice its FLOP budget; against one such network only MNIST
> n = 30000 resolves. The only exception to "no continuous feature parameters" is DFG's spectral features, whose
> directions come from one eigendecomposition.
>
> The learner is an AutoML-style portfolio: Part 6's exhaustive Fourier-character search plus a standard stack of
> boosted trees, ExtraTrees, nearest-neighbour votes and logistic regression. Its two halves are carried by different
> parts:
> - on the toy suites, by the character search, with linear and tree members on dense targets;
> - on real data, by the classical stack. The character search runs on 1 of 23 held-out real datasets.

**Limitations.**
- **Two mechanisms, not one.** The method does not unify the structure classes. On real data it restates the known
  strength of trees, nearest neighbours and stacking against plain MLPs at small-to-medium n. The new ingredient, DFG,
  acts on the toy side.
- **Power.**
  - Toy: the fresh toy suite has 10 targets.
  - Tabular: the fresh tabular set has 10 datasets, and its sign test gives p = 0.11 against `mlp_best`,
    `mlp_strong` and `mlp_strong1`. The tabular "yes" is "on average", not significant at conventional levels on the
    fresh data alone. The Part 7 set (8 of 11 datasets against `mlp_best`, p = 0.23; 10 of 11 against `mlp_strong` at
    n ≤ 10000, p = 0.012, a margin inflated by its weakness on pendigits and ring) is not blind at the reference level.
  - Images: there is no fresh image set.
- **The references are finite.**
  - Toy: the toy networks are width-128 MLPs at one learning rate and 20000 steps. Wider networks, longer training,
    better checkpoint choice and tuned regularization would plausibly lower their n* further (fairness audit F2–F4):
    checkpoint choice mostly on dense threshold and majority targets, width mostly on order-≥ 4 parities, and 2–4×
    longer training by about 0.1–0.3 in log2 across the board. The untuned weight decay and logistic loss already
    applied shrank the margins on every kind of target, most on poly4 (1.9 in log2), thrint (1.5) and sym9 (1.2).
  - Real: the strongest MLP is a 256-256 ensemble with standard regularization. It has no tuned architecture,
    augmentation or convolution.
- **Hindsight references favour the networks.** `nets_best` and `mlp_best` are per-cell minima chosen on the test set.
  Against single, non-hindsight references the portfolio's margins are larger on the toy and fresh tabular sets: on
  `dlheld3` −1.55 against `nets_cewd` and −1.67 against `nets_wd`; on HELDOUT2 −0.28 against `mlp_strong` (twice the
  budget) and −0.32 against `mlp_strong1`. On images they are not larger: `mlp_strong` is `mlp_best` at every n
  (+0.05), and `mlp_strong1` gives +0.00. On the Part 7 set the single-reference margins (−0.34, −0.39) are inflated by
  the strong recipe's weakness on pendigits and ring.
- **Compute is FLOPs only.** On images the portfolio uses minutes of CPU per fit where the MLP budget corresponds to
  tens of seconds. Its prediction is up to 350× an MLP's per query (§6). Library FLOP counts are analytic estimates.
- **Load dependence.** hyb_union's results at large n depend on machine load.
- **Large-input settings.** The settings used for MNIST and Fashion-MNIST at n ≥ 30000 were chosen without real-data
  validation.
