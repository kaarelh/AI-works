AUDIT: APPENDICES K AND L, AND RELATED CLAIMS ON SLIDES 30 AND 35
Source: nnbayes-generalization.pdf, slides 104--110 (PDF pages); audited 2026-10-15.

Executive status
---------------
K's deterministic formula-MDL conjunction ladder is essentially correct, once d>=3,
a fixed finite gate vocabulary, and deterministic tie-breaking are specified. Its
Bayesian weighted-majority comparison is also correct for the stated prefix-code
prior. Several extensions in K are unsupported or need materially narrower wording;
in particular that prefix-code mistake bound is not automatically a theorem for the
strong-Gaussian-prior limit. K is tangential to the requested strong-prior PAC paper,
so omission, with a clear scope statement, is appropriate.
L correctly explains the assumptions used by the main proof. Its last paragraph
understates the extra assumptions required for G and H.
The additional total iid mistake, reverse-complexity, and rank-equivalence claims
should not be included as established theorems.

K.1. Rigorous deterministic conjunction ladder
---------------------------------------------
Take X={0,1}^d, d>=3, and formulas with binary AND, OR, XOR gates of cost 4,
unary NOT of cost 2, constants of cost 3, and variable leaves of cost
v=2+ceil(log_2 d). A formula's cost is the sum of token costs; a function's cost
is the minimum formula cost. This cost admits a prefix code: token Kraft sum
is at most d*2^{-v}+3*2^{-4}+2^{-2}+2*2^{-3} <=15/16, and node arities
make complete prefix-notation formulas self-delimiting. Fix deterministic
function tie-breaking and choose a minimum-cost consistent function.

Target f=AND_{i=1}^d x_i. Present 1^d, then 0^d. Constants are cheaper than
variables for d>=3. Thus at most the first of these two points is predicted
correctly; 0^d is mispredicted by the surviving constant 1.

At stage k=1,...,d-1, all points of Hamming weight below k have been shown,
and 1^d was shown positive. For a consistent nonzero h, its GF(2) multilinear
polynomial has coefficients c_S = XOR_{T subset S} h(1_T), so c_S=0 for
|S|<k. Hence it has a monomial involving at least k distinct variables; any
formula for h uses at least k variable leaves and k-1 binary gates, and costs
at least kv+4(k-1).

If equality holds, there are exactly k distinct variable leaves, no constant
or NOT leaves, and exactly k-1 binary gates. The function is supported on
these k variables. Any assignment other than all ones can be padded by zeros
to a point of Hamming weight <k, where h vanishes. Its value at the all-ones
assignment must be 1. Therefore h is precisely AND_S for a k-element set S.

At every intermediate stage, the surviving k-conjunctions are exactly those
whose point 1_S has not yet been shown: AND_{S'}(1_S)=1 for |S|=|S'|=k iff
S=S'. Reveal 1_S for the currently selected conjunction. Each such prediction
is wrong and eliminates exactly that conjunction among the minimum-cost
survivors. All m=2^d points are eventually shown, with m or m-1 mistakes
according to the initial constant tie. Also C(f)=dv+4(d-1)=Theta(d log d).
Thus the worst-case deterministic mistake count is 2^{Theta(C/log C)}.

Qualifications:
* The literal "all but at most one" statement is false for some small d and
  allowed ties. At d=1 variable x_1 is cheaper than a constant and is already
  correct; at d=2 variables tie constants and an initial variable can make
  both the initial all-ones and all-zeros examples correct. Restrict d>=3.
* This is a formula-size theorem for the explicitly given costs. It is not a
  proof for arbitrary circuit conventions, especially arbitrary shared DAGs.
* The ordering can be fixed in advance for deterministic tie-breaking: simulate
  the deterministic learner. Its short description requires that the learner's
  tie-breaking rule itself have a fixed short description. An arbitrary
  permutation tie-break can encode exponentially much information.
* The alleged exact-DP computation was not provided; the proof above does not
  rely on that unverified computation.

K.2. The four prediction rules
------------------------------
(a) Prefix-code weighted majority. For pi(h)=2^{-C(h)} (unnormalized), Kraft
says initial total weight W_0<=1. Every majority mistake removes at least half
of the surviving weight. Since target weight 2^{-C(f)} is never removed,
M<=C(f), for every adaptive realizable input sequence. This is correct.
With normalized arbitrary pi, the general bound is
M<=log_2(1/pi(f)). No stochastic-input assumption is needed.

(b) Uniform consistent sampling with a doubling description budget. For a
fixed finite class containing f, let q_t be the fraction eliminated by the
next label. The sampled prediction errs with probability q_t and
q_t <= -log(1-q_t). Summing gives expected mistakes <=log |H|. Restarting
when a budget is exhausted, and doubling the budget, gives O(C(f)) expected
mistakes, with an additive constant per restart. The adversary may use past
randomness but cannot choose the current input after seeing the current
private random prediction. A precise algorithm is required to state this.

(c) Randomized MAP on the particular fixed conjunction-ladder ordering.
If the learner draws uniformly from the surviving cheapest functions at each
step, the k-th level contributes H_{binom(d,k)} expected mistakes: each
point removes exactly one cheapest survivor, and has probability 1/r of
being the currently sampled one when r remain. Summing k gives Theta(d^2),
or Theta(C^2/log^2 C), plus the initial constant steps. This justifies the
soft-Theta(C^2) claim for this particular oblivious ordering. It does not
establish the table's apparent universal "worst case over orderings" claim.
An adversary who sees the current sampled function can instead force every
step to be a mistake, so the adversary protocol matters.

(d) Strong-prior majority on the ladder. If the only surviving minimum-cost
functions at level k are the k-conjunctions and all have equal prior mass,
then a level makes at most one majority mistake: its displayed point is
positive for exactly one current candidate, and hence gets a positive majority
only at the end (with a possible additional tie-dependent mistake when two
remain). Thus O(d) is valid for the ideal uniform-minimum-cost formula model;
allowing arbitrary binary tie resolution gives at most two per level.

This does not transfer to neural minimum norm merely because same-size
conjunctions have equal kappa_N. Gaussian input-permutation symmetry DOES give
equal induced prior masses to these conjunction functions, and conditioning
preserves their ratios among the survivors. But it does NOT prove that they
are the cheapest surviving neural functions, or that all other functions
have negligible posterior mass. Appendix K correctly notes it is not a
neural theorem; the intermediate "exactly what rescues" language should be
qualified accordingly.

The finite-code bound M<=C(f) uses prior 2^{-C}; the strong-prior family
pi_sigma instead has log(1/pi_sigma(f)) generally diverging as sigma tends
to zero. That bound cannot be imported unchanged into the strong-prior limit.

K.3. Random ordering, descending ordering, and smoothing
-------------------------------------------------------
Uniform random permutation of the finite input domain IS safe for any
consistent learner constrained to a finite candidate set S of size M.
Let m=|X|. After t uniformly selected distinct examples, a fixed hypothesis
with r errors survives with probability at most exp(-tr/m). If its remaining
error rate exceeds eps, then r>eps(m-t). Union bounding over S gives
P(max surviving remaining error>eps)<=M exp[-t eps(m-t)/m]. Integrating in
eps bounds expected next error by
min(1,(1+log M)m/[t(m-t)]) for 1<=t<m.
Summing, expected total mistakes <=2+2(1+log M)H_{m-1}.
For m=2^d and a prefix-code budget C, this is O((C+1)d), which establishes
(and slightly improves) the displayed O((C+d)d) random-order bound.
This is for a uniform random permutation, not an arbitrary iid distribution.

"Descending weight gives exactly d+1" is not valid for the stated general
AND/OR/XOR formula language and arbitrary tie-breaking. For d=3, after 111
and all three weight-2 points, AND_3 and XOR_3 have equal minimum formula
cost, both fit these observations, and both depend on all three variables.
If the tie chooses XOR_3, the next weight-1 point adds an additional mistake.
With initial constant 0 and the preceding cheapest conjunctions, this is at
least five mistakes, exceeding d+1=4. Even the initial constant tie changes
any exact count. Descending weight gives the familiar d+1-type guarantee
for the restricted monotone-conjunction learner, with suitable conventions.

The "smoothing by w helps only by factor w" assertion has no defined
smoothing operation or quantifiers and cannot be verified as stated.

Slide 30. Online and reverse-complexity additions
------------------------------------------------
An unlimited total expected iid mistake bound polynomial in (d,C_L(f)) is
NOT established by the main counting/PAC proof. This audit does NOT supply
a counterexample to that statement for the actual Gaussian neural prior;
it identifies a missing independent theorem, and a false generic inference.

General reason iid does not fix the issue: for any desired first-occurrence
ordering x_1,...,x_m, choose p_j proportional to eps^{j-1}. In the iid stream,
Poissonization gives independent first-arrival times T_j~Exp(p_j), so
P(T_{j+1}<T_j)=p_{j+1}/(p_j+p_{j+1})<=eps. Union bounding shows the entire
first-occurrence ordering follows the specified order with probability
at least 1-(m-1)eps. Repeated points impose no new noiseless constraints.
Thus an arbitrarily bad deterministic first-occurrence order can be induced
with probability arbitrarily close to one by a sufficiently skewed iid D.
The uniform-random-permutation argument does not apply to arbitrary D.

A stronger counterexample to the abstract count inference, not to the neural
model, uses target f=0 and point functions h_j=1_{x_j}. Set strictly increasing
costs kappa(h_j)=j/(m+1), kappa(f)=1, and prior proportional to
exp(-beta*kappa). For sufficiently large beta, the least-cost surviving
h_j outweighs the sum of all remaining candidates. On input order x_1,...,x_m,
posterior majority makes m mistakes. Here log |S|=log(m+1), yet total errors
are m; the skewed iid construction makes expected total errors near m.
For m=2^d all these Boolean functions have ordinary circuits of size O(d).
This example shows exactly why count/relevance alone is insufficient.

A finite-horizon result that DOES follow from the relevance hypotheses:
if f in S, |S|<=M, and pi(H\S)<=tau*pi(f), then after T iid rounds,
E[number of majority mistakes] <=2(1+log M)H_T+2tau.
Proof: at time n, let R_n be the maximum population error of a surviving
hypothesis in S. The exact union bound P(R_n>u)<=min(1,M(1-u)^n)
implies E R_n <=(1+log M)/(n+1). Tail expected error is bounded by
sum_{h notin S} [pi(h)/pi(f)] e_h(1-e_h)^n; summing n>=0 gives at most tau.
A majority error is at most twice the posterior error. Sum over n<T.
Classical Bayesian total mistakes <=log_2(1/pi_sigma(f)) is also valid,
but is not uniform as sigma decreases. Recommend omitting the stronger claim.

The claimed reverse implication "C_L of the learned function <=poly(d,
number of mistakes)" lacks quantifiers and is unsupported. If "learned
function" means the target or a globally recovered target, a distribution
supported on one point provides no information about the target's values
elsewhere: arbitrary high-complexity targets can be indistinguishable and
have identical mistake behavior on the support. Low population error alone
therefore cannot certify low global target complexity. If the phrase means
the actual returned posterior-majority function, a separate theorem and a
precise complexity convention are required; the sentence does not state one.

Slide 35. Polynomial equivalence of log-indices
-----------------------------------------------
Pointwise polynomial equivalence of C_L and kappa_N, together with upper
bounds on sublevel cardinalities, does NOT by itself imply polynomial
relations between their ranks. A generic counterexample even has linear
complexity equivalence: on 2^{2^d} objects choose distinguished g_0,f, put
C(g_0)=kappa(g_0)=1, C(f)=R=2^d, kappa(f)=2R, and for every other object
C=R+1, kappa=R. The measures are within a factor of two, and both have
log sublevel counts <=O(their complexity), yet f's log-ranks are 1 and 2^d
(using base-2 logs). This does not disprove the intended statement for the
particular neural/circuit measures, but proves the stated derivation needs
additional lower counting/density information or a different rank theorem.

The displayed lower bound (s d)^{1/a} also has the wrong dependence for
constant-rank examples: if both log-ranks stay 1 as d grows, this lower bound
would exceed 1. Typical equivalence bounds would need additive constants
and a polynomial factor of d on the other side, e.g. s' >=s^{1/a}/poly(d),
but no such corrected theorem has been proved here.
Prior ordering adds an additional subtlety: different minimum norm values
are ordered in the small-sigma limit, whereas equal norms need not have
comparable subexponential prefactors. Norm equivalence does not resolve
ordering inside equal-norm blocks.

L. Activation assumptions
--------------------------
Valid core content:
* Global boundedness controls summands in width-independent subsampling.
* Global Lipschitz continuity gives parameter-space separation and propagates
  activation errors layer by layer. Differentiability is not required;
  replace notation |phi'|<=const by an explicit Lipschitz condition.
* Continuous nonpolynomial activations satisfy the finite interpolation
  theorem used to realize the fixed finite gate vocabulary. Boundedness
  plus nonconstancy already excludes polynomial activations on all of R.
* tanh satisfies all the assumptions mentioned, including the stronger ones.

Corrections/qualifications:
* Bounded, Lipschitz, and nonpolynomial do not imply saturation, phi(0)=0,
  or phi'(0)!=0. Examples: sin is bounded/Lipschitz/nonpolynomial but not
  saturating; tanh(t)^3 is bounded/Lipschitz/nonpolynomial/saturating/odd
  but has derivative zero at zero; 3/4+(1/4)tanh(t) is bounded in absolute
  value by 1 and never vanishes. Hence the assumptions used in G/H ARE
  additional hypotheses for their given constructions, not consequences
  of the main hypotheses. Some may be arranged by recentering/reparametrizing
  with constant channels, but that reduction must be proved, with its effects
  on the norm and the quantization condition.
* Oddness is indeed an extra symmetry assumption for the H construction.
* ReLU being bounded on each bounded interval is insufficient to apply the
  given global bounded-activation proof uniformly in width. The relevant
  interval can depend on N under this prior scaling. A ReLU extension needs
  another concentration argument or explicit width-independent bounds.

Recommendation for the requested paper
---------------------------------------
Use L's valid observations to explain the hypotheses succinctly. Keep the
paper focused on the finite-width strong-prior PAC theorem, state that it
makes no adversarial-order or unlimited-online-mistake guarantee, and avoid
slides 30/35's extra assertions. K's rigorous formula-MDL counterexample is
optional separate background, not needed to prove the paper's result.
