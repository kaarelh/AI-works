Appendices G and H: independent mathematical audit

Scope and conventions
The audit concerns the supplied slides, Appendix G (slides 70--75) and H
(slides 76--80). Both constructions implicitly specialize the activation to
tanh, despite the broader bounded/Lipschitz/non-polynomial assumptions in
the main talk. The paper can use tanh only in a separately stated example.
The proof fragment below uses L-1 tanh hidden layers and a linear readout,
with first-layer coefficient d/N and readout coefficient N. With a final
tanh, change the readout constant to give any fixed output margin; the
asymptotic conclusions are unchanged.

APPENDIX G: salvageable and proved

A rigorous, slightly cleaner replacement is proved in the file
/workspace/strong-prior-paper/depth-lower-bound.tex.

For a universal constant A, L >= 4, 1 <= q <= d and N >= 2^(q+1), EVERY
Boolean function of a specified q coordinates is realized with output
exactly +/-1 and weighted squared norm at most

 A [ d q^2 log^2(2q) + (L-2) 2^(2q/(L-2)) ].

Thus a ball of that radius contains 2^(2^q) distinct functions. In
particular, for d=q>=2 and L=d+2, radius O(d^3 log^2 d) suffices for all
Boolean functions on d bits. A counting bound polynomial jointly in d,K,L
is impossible. This remains a norm-ball statement, not a lower bound on
sample complexity for an easy target.

Proof repairs:
1. The soft-bump dictionary is controlled by a diagonally dominant
   interpolation matrix, rather than informally neglecting all tails.
   With gamma=(log(16q))/2 and
   T_xp=(1+tanh(gamma(<p,x>-(q-1))))/2,
   ||T-I||_infty<1/4, so ||T^-1||_infty<=4/3. A bank of tanh(1) constant
   neurons implements the constant offset exactly at the desired cost.
2. The amplification uses exact tanh recurrences, not repeated
   approximations tanh(z) approximately z. Define target signal sizes t_j,
   and use gain atanh(t_j)/t_(j-1). Uniformly for t_j <=3/4 these gains
   differ from t_j/t_(j-1) by a constant factor. This keeps constants
   uniform in depth and removes an unquantified accumulation of error.
3. Integer fan-out populations are rounded up. Their ratios are bounded
   by twice the ideal ratio, giving a constant factor in each layer's
   additive cost, not a factor exponential in depth.
4. Arbitrary larger widths use unequal cloning and preserve cost within
   factor 2. Exact norm preservation at every larger width, asserted in
   the slides, is generally unavailable unless the relevant clone
   multiplicities divide the width. Reserving a constant factor fixes it.
5. The reader and amplification costs are optimized together, retaining
   the L-2 term explicitly. This supports the advertised Theta(L)
   exponent with no reliance on the slide's informal replacement of
   L-3 by L.

Main logical qualification: the packing/cardinality lower bound implies
an obstruction to polynomial-size compression only if the proposed
compressed class has an appropriate finite counting bound. Norm-bounded,
margin-preserving compression has this property via the paper's packing
lemma. A claim that arbitrary real weights intrinsically carry only a
finite number of bits requires a separate capacity argument.

Numerical sanity check (not the proof): q=1,2,3,4 and m=L-3=1,2,5 were
checked with the exact interpolation and scalar amplification formulas.
The dictionary row errors were .11765, .09094, .08166, .07695; all outputs
were +/-1 to floating-point accuracy and the universal bound with A=32
held. The proof does not depend on this check.

APPENDIX H: correct counterexample for quantization margins, not a proof
of the advertised positive robust-prior theorem

There are two distinct properties:
(a) On each Boolean input, every pre-rounding neuron activation is at
    least h/4 away from a rounding boundary.
(b) A neuron's rounded output stays fixed when all of its INPUT
    activations are independently perturbed by up to h/4.
Appendix H establishes (a), and explicitly exploits the failure of (b).
This distinction must remain explicit. If 'robust' means (b), the
construction is not robust and does not prove the stated counterexample
for that class. If the intended notion is (a), the construction works.
The body uses ambiguous wording about perturbing 'activations'; the
paper should define which activations are perturbed before making any
claim involving robustness.

A rigorous margin-only construction can be recovered as follows. Fix
h=1/r, integer r>=2, let Q_h round to the nearest multiple of h, and take
alpha=h, z_t=atanh(h), c'=z_t/h. Use M=2^d point bumps, each repeated n
times, and nM constant neurons, so N=2nM. Choose gamma with
 tanh(gamma)>1-h/4.
After rounding, the bump bank takes value +1 at its point and -1 off it,
and the constant bank equals +1. Its weighted norm is exactly
 gamma^2 d(d^2-d+2)/2.
For target g let S=sum_p g(p), mu=1+S^2/M^2 in [1,2]. Each of n_2 reader
neurons gives every copy of bump p the weight z_t g(p)/(2n), and every
constant neuron weight z_t S/(2nM). Its preactivation is exactly z_t g(x),
so its activation is h g(x), exactly a rounding representative. The reader
cost is
 R = mu c'^2 h^2 M^2 n_2/(2N).
Subsequent populations n_j of identical h g signals use weights c'/n_j,
with layer cost c'^2 n_(j+1)/n_j. If their total fan-out is F and there are
m=L-3 such layers, equal population ratios give fan cost
 c'^2 m F^(1/m)
up to integer-rounding factors at sufficiently large width. A linear
readout with output +/-1 costs
 O = N/(h^2 F n_2),
and hence
 R O = mu c'^2 M^2/(2F).
For a final tanh readout with preactivation magnitude z_o, multiply O and
this product by z_o^2. Population fractions can be chosen so that R and O
are comparable, provided F is not too large relative to h^4 M^2.
Optimizing gives the advertised depth exponent: for fixed h and suitable
large width, norm at most
 O_h(d^3 + (L-1) 2^(2d/(L-1)))
suffices (with harmless changes in constants and choices of populations).
In particular, for L=Theta(d), the cost is O_h(d^3), yet every Boolean
function on d coordinates is represented. All internal rounding margins
are at least h/4.

Why input perturbation robustness fails: the reader has absolute sum of
incoming bump weights M z_t/2. Adversarial perturbations of size h/4 to
these inputs can change its preactivation by h M z_t/8. This grows
exponentially in d despite the perfect rounding margin at unperturbed
inputs. For instance, an appropriately chosen parity target has balanced
S=0, avoiding even the constant-bank contribution. This is the exact
mechanism being exhibited, not a technical rounding issue.

Two corrections to H's wording/calculations:
1. The assertion that the required margin 'forces' h=1/r is too strong.
   That choice conveniently makes 0,+/-h,+/-1 exact representatives, but
   a margin condition does not require every value to be a representative.
   E.g. h=.31 gives 0 and +/-h centered, while values sufficiently near
   +/-1 remain >h/4 from the neighboring grid boundaries. The saturated
   bank then rounds to +/- .93; rescaling the reader weights accommodates
   it. Keeping h=1/r as a stated sufficient assumption is sound.
2. The displayed reader/output budget inequality suppresses constants
   that depend on the final readout convention and on whether B bounds
   their sum or each separately. If B=R+O, AM-GM gives RO<=B^2/4; for a
   linear +/-1 readout the preceding exact identity yields
       M^2 <= B^2 F/(2 mu c'^2),
   rather than the printed 2 B^2 F/(mu c'^2). This is only a constant-factor
   error for the qualitative lower bound. A tanh readout additionally
   introduces z_o^2.

The no-large-relative-weights condition does exclude this particular
construction: a live n_j by n_(j+1) block has
 max|W|/(||W||_F/N) = N/sqrt(n_j n_(j+1)).
At the first fan-out layer this is at least sqrt(F), if populations grow
monotonically and N>=F n_2. Thus a polynomial bound on this ratio forces
F polynomial and kills this example's exponential advantage. This does
NOT establish the positive polynomial-depth learning theorem. That
requires a complete compression and prior argument absent from H.

Recommendation for the requested first paper
Include the rigorous tanh-only G proposition as a short optional appendix
about why depth is fixed. Omit H's quantized model from this first paper:
it changes the model and is unnecessary for the strong-prior theorem.
Report its margin/input-robustness distinction, the overstated grid
necessity, and the missing positive theorem proof to the user.
