\begin{abstract}
We study Gaussian Bayesian learning of Boolean functions by fully connected
tanh networks. We restrict the prior using stability of the output clipped
at the training margin, under simultaneous small internal perturbations.
The condition permits arbitrarily small clean outputs on unobserved inputs
and ignores changes between sufficiently confident outputs of the same sign.
For every layered Boolean target circuit, the resulting posterior has a
sample bound polynomial in circuit width, input dimension, and network depth,
simultaneously at every network width above circuit width and at every
Gaussian exponent $\gamma\ge1$, including the finite-variance mean-field
scale. The noise calibration is explicit, and its constant can be varied
without changing the qualitative theorem. We also prove a weaker
margin-band version and a localized version allowing network-dependent
exceptional regions of controlled complexity. We give self-contained proofs
and an empirical assessment on networks trained by gradient methods, with
selected exact-arithmetic certificates. The evidence is mixed: exact
certificates establish global stability for some fully fitted image models
and full-domain stability for two 64-hidden-layer Boolean classifiers.
Another fitted image model has a verified confident-input counterexample,
although exact certificates establish stability on all 10,000 of its
official held-out images at the same radius.
An exact gradient-descent construction shows that
on-distribution stability alone cannot imply the global condition.
The empirical calibration may decrease polynomially with depth without
losing the polynomial sample theorem.
The empirical question is whether trained parameterizations satisfy this
specified support condition; it is distinct from a theorem about the
generalization of gradient descent or an experiment sampling the Bayesian
posterior.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and scope}

Small parameter perturbations, small internal activation perturbations,
and stability of classification are different properties. A network may
have stable confident predictions despite changes in the magnitude of a
large output. Conversely, a network can fit its training set accurately
and be highly sensitive at inputs away from the data distribution. A useful
prior restriction must make its input domain, disturbance model, and output
criterion explicit.

We use the output margin already present in the likelihood to define a
bounded prediction score. Training requires signed output greater than
$1/2$. We clip raw outputs at $\pm1/2$, and require a score change of at
most $1/16$ under a specified ball of simultaneous internal perturbations.
The test guarantee controls signed outputs at most $1/4$. Thus stability
is calibrated to the gap between training and test margins. It does not
forbid every sign change: moving from $0.02$ to $-0.02$ changes the score
by only $0.04$. The statistical proof shows that weak or incorrect test
outputs receive little posterior mass after sufficiently many examples.

The main theorem concerns a particular restricted Gaussian prior and an
exact margin-conditioning likelihood. Its comparator complexity is the
width of a layered Boolean circuit. It does not assume that every small
analog neural circuit has a robust implementation of the same width and
depth. Nor does it assert that ordinary gradient descent samples this
posterior. These distinctions matter when assessing the empirical evidence.

Compression through noise stability and all-layer margins has precedents
in work of Arora et al.\ and Wei and Ma~\cite{arora,weima}.
Wei, Chen, and Ma~\cite{weichenma} prove polynomial circuit-size sample
bounds for overparameterized feedforward networks using a suitable
all-layer-margin surrogate and robust circuit simulation. Thus the general
idea that robust simulation can avoid exponential depth factors predates
this paper. Here the object being analyzed is the posterior of a specified
Gaussian prior, including its target probability and uniformity over widths
and finite Gaussian scales.
Our proof uses Gaussian entropy and transport to construct one finite
candidate class shared by all widths and scales. We include that construction
and the Gaussian target-mass bound, so the result can be read independently.
The polynomial constants are deliberately loose and do not provide practical
sample estimates. The mathematical arguments have been reviewed independently
by AI agents; a companion Lean development now verifies the qualitative main learning theorem for the exact robust-only posterior, with a looser polynomial bound from a Pinsker-and-energy construction. It does not certify every quantitative constant, extension, or empirical certificate in this manuscript.

Published experiments support internal noise stability as a research
direction, with different quantifiers from our support condition.
Arora et al.\ report attenuation of injected Gaussian activation noise
in a VGG-19 trained on CIFAR-10 and measure their stability quantities
on training inputs~\cite{arora}. Yu and Tomasi report improved noise
stability in residual networks relative to matched plain
networks~\cite{yutomasi}. Neither establishes stability at every input
under every simultaneous disturbance in our calibrated ball. The
experiments below test that particular condition directly in the tanh
architecture of the theorem. They do not establish its prevalence in
modern convolutional networks or transformers.
