15 October 2026, allwidth_adversarial_review

STATUS: PASS. The key is to use the stronger intermediate metric theorem,
not to call a one-sided function a norm. The direct source checked is James
R. Lee, Spectral hypergraph sparsification via chaining, arXiv:2209.04539v3,
Section 2.4, especially equations (2.17), (2.20) and the proof following it.
https://arxiv.org/html/2209.04539

1. EXACT DETERMINISTIC BOUND
Let a_1,...,a_n in R^D, R=max_i ||a_i||_2, and
 B=sup_{u in T}(sum_i <a_i,u>^2)^{1/2},
where T is any compact subset of the Euclidean unit ball containing zero.
Then for independent Rademacher signs epsilon_i,

 E_epsilon sup_{u in T}|sum_i epsilon_i (<a_i,u>_+)^2|
   <= C R sqrt(log(2(n+D))) B.                       (1)

The constant is universal. No symmetry of T, no symmetry of the a_i, and
no probability law for the a_i is required. The same result holds for
individual maps psi_i with psi_i(0)=0 and Lipschitz constant at most one,
replacing (<a_i,u>_+)^2 by psi_i(<a_i,u>)^2.

Proof. Put N_i(u)=|<a_i,u>|, genuine seminorms, and
 d(u,v)^2=sum_i <a_i,u-v>^2(<a_i,u>^2+<a_i,v>^2).
The norm formulation in Lee permits the rank-one case explicitly (see the
paragraph after (1.5)); alternatively add an arbitrarily small Euclidean
norm and pass to the limit. Let
 lambda=max_i(E N_i(g)^2)^{1/2}=R,
 kappa=E max_i N_i(g)<=C R sqrt(log(2n)).

After normalizing B=1, Lee's equation (2.20) and the rest of its proof give
 gamma_2(T,d)<=C(lambda sqrt(log(2D))+kappa).
Restoring the scale gives
 gamma_2(T,d)<=C(lambda sqrt(log(2D))+kappa)B.        (2)
Indeed N_i -> N_i/B divides d by B^2 and lambda,kappa by B.
The zero case B=0 is immediate. For general T one first passes to its
closed convex hull, which remains in the Euclidean unit ball and has the
same value of sup sum_i N_i^2, by convexity. Thus the stronger metric bound
really does cover arbitrary T.

For psi(0)=0 and Lip(psi)<=1, we have
 |psi(s)^2-psi(t)^2|
 <= |s-t|(|s|+|t|),
so the squared increment metric of the centered Rademacher process
 X_u=sum_i epsilon_i psi_i(<a_i,u>)^2
is bounded by sqrt(2)d(u,v). Standard subgaussian generic chaining and (2)
bound E sup X_u. Applying the same argument to -X_u bounds the absolute
supremum by twice that amount; X_0=0 ensures the two suprema are nonnegative.
This proves (1). In particular, it never assumes ReLU is a seminorm.

2. SMALL SOURCE-PROOF PRESENTATIONAL GAP AND ITS DIRECT REPAIR
In the source, after normalization B=1, equation (2.26) is printed as
 gamma_2(T,d) <= C(1/c*+1).
The subsequent sentence absorbs the last 1 into lambda sqrt(log D)+kappa.
Such absorption is not automatic: lambda and kappa can both be small for
many small rank-one rows. This does not invalidate the claimed bound; the
preceding diameter and packing estimates directly sharpen to O(lambda).

For rank-one N_i, N_i(u)<=lambda ||u||_2. Thus, on T subset B_2,
 ||u||_N:=max_i N_i(u)<=lambda.
Under B=1, the source's equation (2.23) gives
 d(u,v)<=sqrt(2)||u-v||_N,
so diam_d(T)<=2sqrt(2)lambda.
Any d-separated collection at separation a is ||.||_N-separated at
separation a/sqrt(2), and lies in the ||.||_N ball of radius lambda.
The volume bound therefore gives K<=(C lambda/a)^D whenever K>=2.
For seminorms apply this in the quotient by the common kernel (dimension
at most D). Thus the parameter L in Corollary 2.11 is C lambda, not merely
an absolute constant. The right side of (2.26) becomes
 C(1/c*+lambda),
which is absorbed into C(lambda sqrt(log(2D))+kappa). The same repair works
for true norms by the Gaussian domination sup_{||u||2<=1}N_i(u)<=C lambda.
All other parts of the proof use genuine N_i and are unchanged.

3. UNIFORM SAMPLING CONSEQUENCE
Suppose a(X) in R^D is isotropic, E a(X)a(X)^T=I_D, and ||a(X)||^2<=D.
(The Walsh evaluation vector multiplied by an arbitrary +/-1 label has
exactly these properties.) Let X_1,...,X_n be iid. If
 n>=C D log(2(n+D)),
then

 E sup_{||u||<=1}| n^-1 sum_i (<a(X_i),u>_+)^2
                     - E(<a(X),u>_+)^2 |
 <= C sqrt[D log(2(n+D))/n].                        (3)

Proof: symmetrize, apply (1) conditionally on the sample with R<=sqrt D,
and use E||sum_i a_i a_i^T||op<=C n. The last estimate follows from the
standard bounded isotropic covariance bound (matrix Bernstein), or from
Rudelson's inequality plus its self-consistent second-moment bound. Jensen
then gives E B<=C sqrt n.

By Markov, for any fixed delta,eta>0, a sufficiently large constant in
 n>=C delta^-2 eta^-2 D log(2(n+D))
ensures, with probability at least 1-delta, that for EVERY u,
 |P_n(<a,u>_+)^2-E(<a,u>_+)^2|<=eta ||u||_2^2.        (4)
Positive homogeneity extends from the unit ball to all u. The constant
need not be sharp; the applications require fixed confidence and fixed
relative coercivity. For n polynomial in D, this is D log D sampling.

4. WALSH/PARITY CONSEQUENCE
Let V_r be the degree-at-most-r Walsh space, D=dim V_r, f full parity,
and a(x)=-f(x)(chi_U(x))_{|U|<=r}. Then ||a(x)||^2=D and isotropy holds.
Thus (4) applies to (-fP)_+^2 for EVERY P in V_r.
The previously audited discrete-derivative identity gives
 |E[f P|P|]|<=2sqrt(r/d)||P||_2^2,
so
 E(-fP)_+^2 >= (1/2-sqrt(r/d))||P||_2^2.
For example r<=d/100 and eta=1/10 imply, on the event (4),
 P_n(-fP)_+^2 >= (3/10)||P||_2^2
simultaneously for every P in V_r. This is the desired one-sided squared
coercivity at D log D rather than D log^3 D sample size.

AUDIT LIMIT
This note checks the metric theorem, its application, and the sampling
corollary. It does not by itself establish the downstream all-n positive-
gamma or gamma-zero lower bounds; their cutoff, spectral, and posterior
arguments must be checked separately.

5. INDEPENDENT RECHECK OF THE STRONGER CLIPPING STEP
The exact argument in research-round3/clipping-laplacian-reduction.txt also
passes independently. Write R for the uniformly chosen coordinate flip,
Delta=(d/2)(I-R), q=fP, and e=(2/d)fDelta P. Then Rq=-q+e. Jensen and
invariance of the uniform measure imply
 E|Rq+a|<=E|q+a|,
whereas the triangle inequality gives E|Rq+a|>=E|q-a|-||e||1. Apply the
same argument with -a to conclude
 |E|q+a|-E|q-a||<=||e||1.
The exact clipping identity gives
 |E f clip(P/a)|<=||Delta P||1/(ad).
For degree-at-most-r P, Parseval yields ||Delta P||2<=r||P||2. Therefore

 |E f clip(P/a)| <= (r/d)||P||2/a.                  (5)

The r/d coefficient is stronger than the earlier sqrt(r/d) estimate.
No unproved endpoint Bernstein inequality is needed: L1<=L2 is in the
correct direction here. This is sufficient to make the entropy-based
Gaussian-endpoint scaling decay even at beta=1/2.
