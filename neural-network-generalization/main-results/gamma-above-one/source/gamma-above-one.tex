\begin{abstract}
A neural-network learner can be much larger than the network needed to
represent its target. We prove that, for a family of Gaussian weight priors,
this excess width does not increase the number of examples that suffice for learning.
The model is a fully connected network with bounded Lipschitz hidden
activations and a linear readout. The prior retains only networks whose
outputs have absolute value greater than $1/2$ on the entire Boolean input
cube, and learning conditions on noiseless labels. Internal weight variance
is $N^{-\gamma}$ at width $N$, with corresponding input and readout scalings.
For every fixed depth and every fixed $\gamma>1$, the sample bound is
polynomial in the input dimension and the target's bounded-weight network
complexity, uniformly at every width at least that complexity. The theorem
holds at the specified positive variances, without taking an infinite-width
or zero-variance limit. It controls both posterior average error and the
fraction of inputs receiving correct-label probability at most $2/3$.
The proof compares the prior mass of a small target with the total mass of
functions lacking a small description, using a Gaussian neighborhood
construction, singular-value bounds, and network compression. We also relate
normalized negative log prior mass to bounded-weight network complexity.
The polynomial degree may depend on depth and on $\gamma$; a bound polynomial
in depth is not established here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Suppose a Boolean function can be computed by a small neural network, but
the learner uses a much wider network. Can a Gaussian weight prior still
favor that simple target strongly enough to learn it from few examples?
A bound based only on the learner's number of parameters cannot answer this
question: that number grows with width even when the target is unchanged.
The desired guarantee instead depends on the size of a small implementation
of the target, uniformly over the learner's excess width.

We prove such a guarantee for noiseless Bayesian classification. The learner
places independent Gaussian priors on its weights, restricts this prior to
networks with an absolute output margin on every Boolean input, and then
conditions on agreement with the training labels. The prior family is
indexed by an exponent $\gamma$: the input, internal, and readout variances
are respectively $N^{1-\gamma}/d$, $N^{-\gamma}$, and $N^{-\gamma-1}$.
The value $\gamma=1$ is the mean-field scaling in this convention;
$\gamma>1$ makes all three variances smaller by the common factor
$N^{1-\gamma}$.

Our main result is that, for every fixed depth $L$ and fixed $\gamma>1$,
this learner has sample complexity polynomial in the input dimension $d$,
the target complexity $C$, the inverse desired error, and the logarithm of
the inverse failure probability, for every width $N\geq C$.
Here $C=C_L(f)$ is the smallest width of a depth-$L$ implementation of the
target that has strict output margin greater than $1/2$ and weights bounded
in absolute value by $C$. The training and test inputs may follow any common
distribution on the Boolean cube. With sufficiently many examples, the
posterior assigns the correct label probability greater than $2/3$ on all
but the desired fraction of test inputs; it also has small average label
error. The precise statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:all-width-gamma-gt-one}, together
with the confidence remark following its proof.

The width is finite and can be as small as the target complexity. The
variance is the specified nonzero value at that width; it is not sent to
zero after fixing the network. Depth, however, is held fixed: the degree of
the sample polynomial may depend on $L$ and $\gamma$. The theorem also
concerns the exact Bayesian posterior, without an assertion that it can be
computed efficiently.

\paragraph{Why the prior learns small targets.}
For a Bayesian learning bound, it is not enough to exhibit one inexpensive
set of target weights. We need to compare the probability of the target
with the probability of competing functions. The proof has three steps.
First, replicate the neurons of a small target network to embed it at any
larger width, and lower-bound the Gaussian probability of a neighborhood
that still computes the target. Second, use $\gamma>1$ to show that, apart
from an event of small total probability, a margin-separated draw has a
low-norm implementation after truncating small singular values. Third,
compress every such implementation into a network whose description length
is independent of learner width. A finite-class argument then excludes
inaccurate functions using the training labels. A direct target-probability
bound covers the smaller widths where the spectral estimate is unavailable.

This develops the strong-prior argument in Vaintrob and H\"anni's lecture
notes~\cite{notes} and the finite-$\gamma$ draft~\cite{fable}. The presentation
includes the model, probability bounds, and complete compression argument;
no companion paper is needed for the learning proof. The external tools
are standard Gaussian concentration and isoperimetry. Section~\ref{sec:mass-complexity}
on prior mass also makes precise a relation between function probability and
bounded-weight network complexity.

\section{Model and prediction convention}\label{sec:model}
Fix $d\geq1$, $L\geq2$, and $\mathcal X=\{-1,1\}^d$. Let $\bar x=(1,x_1,\ldots,x_d)$, and let $\phi:\R\to[-1,1]$ be $1$-Lipschitz. The architecture has $L-1$ hidden layers of common width $N$ and a linear readout:
\[
 h^0(x)=\bar x,\qquad h^s(x)=\phi(W^s h^{s-1}(x))\quad(1\leq s<L),
 \qquad z_w(x)=W^Lh^{L-1}(x).
\]
There are no additional biases beyond the constant input coordinate. Tanh satisfies the activation assumptions. Non-polynomiality is not needed for the theorem when the target is assumed representable.

For a Boolean function $g$, set
\[
 U_{N,g}=\{w:g(x)z_w(x)>1/2\text{ for every }x\in\mathcal X\},
 \qquad \mathcal H_N=\{g:U_{N,g}\ne\varnothing\},
 \qquad U_N=\bigcup_{g\in\mathcal H_N}U_{N,g}.
\]
Define
\[
 \kappa_N(w)=\frac dN\|W^1\|_F^2+
 \sum_{s=2}^{L-1}\|W^s\|_F^2+N\|W^L\|_2^2,
 \qquad \kappa_N(g)=\inf_{w\in U_{N,g}}\kappa_N(w).
\]
Let $C_L(g)$ be the smallest integer $C\geq1$ for which a width-$C$ network in $U_{C,g}$ has every weight bounded in absolute value by $C$. We assume $C_L(f)<\infty$ for the target. This is bounded-weight network complexity at fixed depth; it does not silently identify arbitrary Boolean circuit size with fixed-depth network size.

Under the raw law $\mu_{N,\gamma}$ all weights are independent centered Gaussians with standard deviations
\[
 \sigma_1=N^{(1-\gamma)/2}/\sqrt d,\qquad
 \sigma_s=N^{-\gamma/2}\ (2\leq s<L),\qquad
 \sigma_L=N^{-(\gamma+1)/2}.
\]
The density is proportional to $\exp[-N^\gamma\kappa_N(w)/2]$.
The function prior is the raw law conditioned on $U_N$:
\[
 \pi_{N,\gamma}(g)=\frac{\mu_{N,\gamma}(U_{N,g})}{\mu_{N,\gamma}(U_N)}.
\]
Given $S_n=((x_i,f(x_i)))_{i=1}^n$ with independent $x_i\sim D$, the posterior $\Pi(\cdot\mid S_n)$ restricts $\pi_{N,\gamma}$ to functions satisfying all observed labels. For any distribution $D$ on $\mathcal X$, write
\[
 \operatorname{err}_D(g)=\Pr_{x\sim D}\{g(x)\ne f(x)\},
 \qquad R_D(\Pi)=\E_{g\sim\Pi}\operatorname{err}_D(g).
\]
The global margin condition applies before training, including at unobserved inputs. It is part of the learner, and differs from imposing margins only on the training sample. The theorem controls the posterior probability of an incorrect label, averaged over a fresh input.

\section{Scope of the result}
The theorem supplies an explicit finite-variance extension of the strong-prior argument. For large enough width the relevant-class sample bound depends on $L,d,C,\log(1/\varepsilon)$ but not on $\gamma$; $\gamma$ determines when the spectral cutoff lies above the Gaussian bulk. Covering all widths uses a polynomial whose degree grows as $\gamma$ approaches one. This is a limitation of the present argument, rather than a lower bound asserting that such deterioration is necessary.

The cloning bound is uniform over $\gamma\geq1$, including the endpoint.
At $\gamma=1$ the internal Gaussian matrices have a bulk of constant
operator norm, so the small-cutoff argument used here does not establish a
full-depth compression theorem. A separate mean-field paper~\cite{critical}
proves polynomial sample complexity for tanh at every width
$N\geq C_L(f)$, at arbitrary fixed depth, using finite-width covariance
estimates and a different compression argument. That result is not needed
for the present proof. For one hidden layer, a direct chi-square argument
already gives the every-width conclusion for all $\gamma\geq1$ and any
activation satisfying the assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:model}. Indeed, when
$L=2$ there are no internal matrices, and the event $G_K$ in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-structural-tail} consists of the two chi-square
bounds. These need only $KN^\gamma\geq8N(d+1)$, which holds at every width
and every $\gamma\geq1$ once $K\geq8(d+1)$. Every draw in $G_K\cap U_N$ then
has $\kappa_N\leq2K$, and $\mu_{N,\gamma}(G_K^c)\leq2e^{-KN^\gamma/4}$.
With $A=\mathcal A_2(d,C)$ and $K=\max\{8(d+1),2A+4\log(4/\varepsilon)\}$,
the large-width argument in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:all-width-gamma-gt-one} therefore applies at every $N\geq C$
and every $\gamma\geq1$, with a sample polynomial independent of $\gamma$.

For $\gamma<1$, tanh's diverging preactivation variance leads to a saturated, iterated-arcsine kernel. Under global margin conditioning its learning limit depends further on the sign of $\gamma$. The companion kernel note~\cite{subcritical} treats these limits and explains why a lower bound for regression using the posterior mean cannot be substituted for a classification lower bound under the present conventions. For tanh at every fixed $\gamma<1$, a separate paper~\cite{subexp} proves an $\exp(\Omega(d))$ sample lower bound for full parity under uniform training inputs, for the correct-label confidence criterion, with width taken to infinity before dimension.
