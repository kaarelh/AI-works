\begin{abstract}
We construct small Boolean targets and fixed nonrepeating input
sequences on which exact Bayesian learning with a one-hidden-layer
hardtanh network makes superpolynomially many online mistakes.
The weight prior is independent Gaussian and isotropic within each
layer. It is conditioned from the start on an absolute output margin
at every Boolean input, and training conditions on exact labels.
At every sufficiently large fixed learner width, the correct-label
posterior probability tends to zero on each designated round as the
Gaussian scale tends to zero.

The targets have same-depth hardtanh teachers of width $C$, margin one,
and coefficients bounded by $C$, while the construction forces
$\exp(\Omega(C^{1/3}))$ designated mistakes. The polynomial exponent
is a convenient bound, not an optimization claim. The calibration
prefix and sufficient learner width are large.

The proof gives an exact unequal-rail parity norm for hardtanh neurons
and polynomial stability into an odd Fourier subspace. A shared carrier
bit converts hardtanh coefficients into even-degree ReLU coefficients.
Cauchy--Schwarz and a monotone finite-difference estimate then
control all affine neurons. The resulting parity costs
have positive inverse-degree curvature, so the cheapest competing
scores use a fixed negative overshoot. An explicit dual identifies
these scores, square-root independence orders their costs, and integer
neuron replication transfers the strict cost gaps to the actual
Gaussian posterior. 
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and result}

Write $\clip(t)=\max(-1,\min(1,t))$. On the full Boolean cube
$X=\{-1,1\}^d$, append a constant input $\bar x=(1,x)$ and use
\[
 F_\theta(x)=\sum_{j=1}^N v_j\clip(u_j\cdot\bar x),\qquad
 (u_j)_i\sim\mathcal N(0,N\sigma^2/d),\qquad
 v_j\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2/N),
\]
with all coordinates independent. The prior is first conditioned on
$|F_\theta(x)|>1/2$ at every input. Each revealed example then conditions
on its exact sign label. The strong-prior limit means
$\sigma\downarrow0$ after fixing $N$; no joint width--scale limit is
used. Up to the common factor $1/(2\sigma^2)$, the Gaussian energy is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 K_N(\theta)=\frac dN\sum_j\|u_j\|_2^2+N\sum_jv_j^2.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For all sufficiently large integers $m$, there exist a dimension
$d_m=\Theta(m^2)$, a Boolean function $f_m$ on the full cube, and
a fixed nonrepeating input sequence with $T_m=\exp(\Omega(m))$
designated rounds, with the following properties.
The target has a one-hidden-layer hardtanh score of margin one and
width $C_m=O(m^3)$, all of whose coefficients are bounded by $C_m$.
There is a finite $N_0(m)$ such that, for every fixed $N\ge N_0(m)$,
at every designated round the posterior probability of the correct
label tends to zero as $\sigma\downarrow0$.
Consequently the same sequence forces
$\exp(\Omega(C_m^{1/3}))$ mistakes for posterior-majority prediction
in this limit, and that many expected mistakes under posterior sampling.
\end{theorem}

This is an adversarial-order result. It does not contradict iid
sample-complexity guarantees for small targets. The full-cube margin
restriction and the order of limits are part of the theorem, as are the
large calibration prefix and the potentially very large width threshold.
The proof concerns the full Bayesian posterior, rather than a chosen
minimum-energy representative.

\section{The parity code and its scalar costs}

Take $\ell$ pairs of disjoint rails $B_j^0,B_j^1$ of even sizes,
nonzero columns $a_j\in\mathbb F_2^m$ spanning the logical space,
and one additional physical carrier coordinate $x_\star$.
For $s\in\mathbb F_2^m$ put
\[
 A_s=\{\star\}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}B_j^{a_j\cdot s},
 \qquad n_s=|A_s|.
\]
All $n_s$ are odd. Let $b=x_\star\prod_j\chi_{B_j^0}$, define
$(-1)^{\zeta_j}=\chi_{B_j^0\cup B_j^1}$, and put
$z=\sum_j a_j\zeta_j$. Then $z$ is uniform on $G=\mathbb F_2^m$ and
$\chi_{A_s}=b(-1)^{s\cdot z}$.
The full independent-rail code is the special case $\ell=m$, $a_j=e_j$;
the construction below retains a path subcode of a larger rail family.
The target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 f(x)=b(x)\begin{cases}+1,&z(x)=0,\\-1,&z(x)\ne0.\end{cases}
\end{equation}

For odd $n$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parity-cost}
 \kappa_n=2^{1-n}\binom{n-1}{(n-1)/2},\qquad w(n)=\frac{\sqrt n}{\kappa_n}.
\end{equation}
A hardtanh neuron with unit weights on $n$ coordinates is majority
on those coordinates. Its top parity coefficient has absolute value
$\kappa_n$, so signed averaging over input flips represents parity
with variation cost $w(n)$. For $F=\sum_jc_j\clip(a_j\cdot\bar x)$,
the representation cost is $\sum_j|c_j|\|a_j\|_2$; its infimum is
the hardtanh variation norm $V(F)$. Arithmetic--geometric mean gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variation-energy}
 K_N\ge2\sqrt d\sum_j|v_j|\|u_j\|_2\ge2\sqrt d\,V(F_\theta).
\end{equation}

For a logical score $g(z)=\sum_s\widehat g(s)(-1)^{s\cdot z}$,
write
\[
 B_w(g)=\sum_s w(n_s)|\widehat g(s)|,\qquad
 W(v)=2^{-m}\sum_s(-1)^{s\cdot v}w(n_s).
\]
The atomic theorem in the next section shows that $V(bg)=B_w(g)$.
The following scalar facts identify and order the competing scores.

\subsection{Positive curvature and a dominant pair}

The gamma-ratio identity and its right-half-plane expansion give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost-expansion}
 w(z)=\sqrt{\pi z}\,\frac{\Gamma((z+1)/2)}{\Gamma(z/2)}
 =\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\left(z-\frac14+\frac1{32z}+O(|z|^{-2})\right).
\end{equation}
The remainder is holomorphic and uniform after restricting to a far
right half-plane. If $C=\sqrt{\pi/2}/32$, Cauchy's estimate on the
circle of radius $a(1-1/k)$ gives
\[
 \frac{Ck!}{2a^{k+1}}\le(-1)^k w^{(k)}(a)
 \le\frac{3Ck!}{2a^{k+1}}
\]
for $2\le k\le\ell$ and $a\ge n_0\gg\ell^2$. On the full
independent-index cube, if
$n_s=n_0+\sum_i\delta_i s_i$ and $D_\delta=\sum_i\delta_i$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:walsh-bounds}
 \frac{Ck!\prod_{i\in T}\delta_i}{2^{k+1}(n_0+D_\delta)^{k+1}}
 \le W(T)\le
 \frac{3Ck!\prod_{i\in T}\delta_i}{2^{k+1}n_0^{k+1}}
 \qquad(|T|=k\ge2).
\end{equation}
The degree-one coefficients are negative, since $w'>0$.

The same estimates apply before pushing the independent rail indices
through the column map. In particular, higher-order coefficients are
positive on the independent-index cube; their sums give coefficients
of the retained column code. The only possibly negative contributions
come from individual columns. Section~\ref{sec:path-code} uses this
observation to build and order the negative-overshoot competitors.

\section{Conclusion of the theorem}

The path construction has $m=r+2$ logical coordinates,
$d_m=\Theta(m^2)$ physical coordinates, and a margin-one teacher of
width $C_m=\Theta(m^3)$ with coefficients bounded by $C_m$.
The affine live set contains at least $2^{r/3}=\exp(\Omega(m))$
distinct-cost representatives. The preceding posterior argument
therefore gives $\exp(\Omega(C_m^{1/3}))$ designated mistakes at
every sufficiently large fixed learner width. This completes
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
