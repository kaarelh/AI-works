Specific correctness checks
---------------------------
1. Full-cube affine margin minimizer is unique; zero coefficients correspond
   exactly to inessential coordinates. Every nonzero absolute coefficient is
   >=1, and unequal positive magnitudes are >=1 apart. Signed-coordinate
   swapping plus averaging is the crucial strict convexity argument.
2. Thus r distinct magnitudes force Q>=r^3/3, and support size s<=Q.
3. With a fixed multiplicity list, weighted coefficient vectors live in an
   r-ball of radius sqrt(K). Distinct canonical functions have a vertex with
   opposite labels; both have margin one there, so vectors are 2/sqrt(K)
   separated. Volume packing gives at most (1+K)^r vectors per list.
4. There are <=ceil(K)^r multiplicity lists and <=(2(d+1))^ceil(K) functions
   per signed-augmented-coordinate orbit. This is an upper count of actual
   function orbits, not merely energies or parameter descriptions.
5. Equal orbit masses hold at every positive scale by an exact Gaussian
   symmetry. Equal energy between different orbits is never assumed to imply
   equal mass or prefactors.
6. In each shrinking uniform block, the sum of elimination fractions is at
   most 1+log(initial size), even with arbitrary changing block weights.
   This is a pathwise statement permitting adaptive queries.
7. A single small-scale tail ratio to target mass controls the posterior
   outside Q<=Qtarget uniformly over all histories. Exact consistency makes
   repeated queries error-free, so its total contribution is beta 2^d.
8. The posterior-sampling high-probability claim uses a predictable
   compensator bound, so it does not require independent error indicators
   for adaptive queries.
9. Scalar hidden depth preserves the affine sign class, exact odd symmetry,
   and strictly increasing energy ranking in Q. The small-scale threshold
   may depend on depth. No finite-scale depth-uniform claim is made.
10. The C1 bound requires existence of a bounded-weight strict-margin scalar
    implementation. At sufficiently large depth that premise can be empty;
    the paper says so. AND's N=1 bound is not presented as satisfying the
    bounded-weight admissible-width convention.

Result and limits
-----------------
General scalar target Q<=K: exp(O(K^(1/3)log(K+1)))(1+Klog(d+2)).
C=N=1 target: exp(O(d^(1/3)log(d+2))).
AND at scalar width: exp(O(d^(2/3)log(d+2))).
These rule out exp(Omega(d)) mistakes in the stated narrow cases. They do
not prove polynomial mistakes, rule out all superpolynomial mistakes,
or answer the question for wider networks.

Additional checked points:
- Canonical width-n energy is (d/n)||A||_F²+n||b||², agreeing with the
  repository's original strong-prior Gaussian normalization.
- Close column swaps alter output by <1, while opposite signs with the
  global margin require a difference >=1. The resulting Boolean symmetry
  is exact; the subsequent Taylor approximation only counts functions.
- Sampling-without-replacement moments obey the stated subgaussian bound.
  The elementary-symmetric-polynomial inequality and bounded-variable mgf
  bound are used on centered group entries.
- Raw moments are polynomials in the within-group row power sums. The exact
  partition and distinct-index inclusion-exclusion formulas have no hidden
  dependence on unencoded column ordering.
- Quantized descriptors need not be valid network moments. They only define
  a sign-preserving finite encoding, which is enough to count exact
  Gaussian function-symmetry orbits.
- Partition counts, factorial coefficients, and tanh derivative bounds
  contribute exp(O(q log(q+1))) constants. Combining groups preserves this
  dependence since their moment degrees sum to <=q. Thus q=ceil(log p)
  legitimately gives log grid size O_n(log² p) and the final log⁴ factor.
- The theorem explicitly requires teacher width one and every weight <=1
  in absolute value. An earlier phrase 'bounded-weight' was tightened to
  avoid accidentally including the large-weight comparator construction.
- The final fixed-width rate is exp(O_n(d^(n/(n+2))log⁴(d+2))) for all fixed n.
  The constants depend on n; no uniform claim for widths growing with d is
  made. A separate fixed-q theorem avoids growing-order constants entirely.
