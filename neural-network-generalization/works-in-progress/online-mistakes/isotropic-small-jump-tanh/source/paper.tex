\begin{abstract}
For every fixed $\epsilon>0$, consider the bounded activation
$\phi_\epsilon(t)=\tanh(t)+\epsilon\sgn(t)$. Its uniform distance from
tanh is $\epsilon$, but it has a jump at zero. We construct a shallow
Bayesian neural-network learner with the standard coordinate-isotropic
Gaussian prior that makes exponentially many online mistakes, at every
hidden width in its strong-prior limit. The target has a Boolean circuit
of size $\Theta(k)$ and a teacher of width $\lceil1/\epsilon\rceil$
whose weights are bounded by one. A fixed input permutation begins with
$O(k^2)$ calibration examples and has $2^k-k-1$ designated errors, with
$O(k)$ intervening examples per error. The prior is conditioned on
absolute output margin greater than $1/2$ on the entire Boolean cube,
and then on exact training labels. The proof gives an exact Gaussian
energy minimum from an affine margin certificate, controls every
nonlinear competitor, and obtains a width-uniform energy gap using a
compact relaxation over neuron distributions. Direct width-dependent
prior limits are also covered. For the same learner, a separate energy
compression argument gives IID sample complexity
$O_\epsilon((d^3+\log(1/\beta))/\eta)$ for test confidence greater than
$2/3$ outside input mass $\eta$.
The result uses a fixed nonzero jump; it does not settle the question for
ordinary tanh or any continuous approximation to this activation.
\end{abstract}

\section{The model and main result}

We study an activation arbitrarily close to tanh in the uniform norm
and prove an all-width online lower bound for it. The activation is
\[
 \phi_\epsilon(t)=\tanh(t)+\epsilon\sgn(t),\qquad
 \epsilon>0,\qquad \sgn(0)=0.
\]
The coefficient $\epsilon$ is fixed across input dimensions and learner
widths. The activation is odd, increasing, and bounded by
$M=1+\epsilon$ in absolute value. Its distance from ordinary tanh in
the uniform norm is exactly $\epsilon$.

For $x\in\{-1,1\}^d$, put $\bar x=(1,x)$. A width-$N$ learner has one
hidden layer and output
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
 F(x)=\sum_{j=1}^N v_j\phi_\epsilon(u_j\cdot\bar x),\qquad
 u_{ji}\sim\mathcal N(0,N\sigma^2/d),\qquad
 v_j\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2/N),
\end{equation}
with all Gaussian coordinates independent. The coefficient of the
constant input has the same variance as the other incoming weights.
There is no anisotropy or additional readout penalty.

The initial prior is conditioned on $|F(x)|>1/2$ at every cube input.
After labels are revealed, it is further conditioned on
$f(x)F(x)>1/2$ at those inputs. A fresh posterior sample makes an error
when it assigns the wrong sign. We also consider posterior-label
majority and the convention requiring correct-label posterior
probability greater than $2/3$.

At fixed $N$, the strong-prior limit is $\sigma\downarrow0$.
For direct width limits, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling}
 \sigma_N^2=\tau^2N^{-\gamma},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(u_{ji})=\tau^2N^{1-\gamma}/d,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(v_j)=\tau^2N^{-\gamma-1},
\end{equation}
where $\tau>0$ and $\gamma\ge1$ are fixed.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Fix $\epsilon>0$. Let $k\ge8$, $d=2k+2$, and assume
$d\ge4\max\{1,\epsilon^{-6}\}$. Write the inputs as
$(x,y,z,s)$ with $x,y\in\{-1,1\}^k$ and $z,s\in\{-1,1\}$.
Put $A=2^k$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 f_k(x,y,z,s)=\sgn\left(
 -(A-1)s-Az+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}2^i(x_i-y_i)\right).
\end{equation}
There is a fixed permutation of the full input cube, depending only on
$k$, with these properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The target has Boolean circuit size $\Theta(k)$ and a teacher of
hidden width $\lceil1/\epsilon\rceil$, with all weights bounded by one
and signed output greater than one everywhere.
\item A calibration prefix of at most $2k^2+6k+2$ examples is followed
by $T_k=2^k-k-1\ge2^{k-1}$ designated queries. There are at most $4k$
examples in each query block, including its designated first query.
At every fixed width $N\ge1$, the posterior wrong-label probability
at each designated query tends to one as $\sigma\downarrow0$.
\item For every fixed $\gamma>1$ and $\tau>0$ in
\eqref{eq:scaling}, those same wrong-label probabilities tend to one
as $N\to\infty$.
\item For every $\delta\in(0,1)$ there exists
$\tau_0(k,\epsilon,\delta)>0$ such that, for all
$0<\tau\le\tau_0$, all $\gamma\ge1$, and all $N\ge1$, posterior
sampling makes every designated mistake with probability at least
$1-\delta$. Each designated posterior majority is also wrong when
$\delta$ is sufficiently small, and the $2/3$ correctness criterion
fails there.
\item In the same cold and direct-width regimes, the learner has IID
sample complexity $O_\epsilon((d^3+\log(1/\beta))/\eta)$ for
correct-label posterior confidence greater than $2/3$ outside a set of
input mass at most $\eta$, with sample confidence at least $1-\beta$.
The precise variance and width quantifiers are given in
Theorem~\ref{thm:iid}.
\end{enumerate}
The comparison includes every nonlinear network at every width; no
choice among tied minimum-energy predictors is made.
\end{theorem}

The teacher-width bound is independent of $k$ at fixed activation.
The dimension restriction grows as $\epsilon$ decreases. Neither this
restriction nor the prior limits can be interchanged with
$\epsilon\downarrow0$ to obtain a theorem for ordinary tanh.

\section{What the small jump changes}

For the histories in the construction, the jump supplies a signed
activation of size $\epsilon$ even at arbitrarily small nonzero
preactivation. The tanh component then makes a positive incoming scale
strictly preferable to zero. The resulting energy is a strictly
increasing function of the affine margin norm, despite remaining below
$1/(4\epsilon^2)$. The covariance certificate prevents a nonlinear
mixture of neurons from improving that energy.

The online error count does not come from computational limitations or
a favorable selection of one tied predictor. A positive energy gap
separates the actual wrong scalar output from every network that would
give the correct next label, uniformly over hidden width. The same
prior nevertheless gives polynomial IID sample complexity. The fixed
input order, together with exact global-margin conditioning, is what
distinguishes the two learning guarantees.

This is a theorem about a discontinuous activation uniformly close to
tanh. It is not a theorem about tanh itself, and it does not establish
stability under replacing the jump by a continuous transition. The
corresponding all-width question for ordinary tanh, ReLU, and hardtanh
under the same prior remains separate.
