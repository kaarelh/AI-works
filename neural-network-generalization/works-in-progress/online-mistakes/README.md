These supporting results study the entire noiseless learning process: each input is presented, a prediction is made, and its true label is revealed. A mistake bound counts all errors, including errors on rare inputs that appear much later. It is stronger than achieving a fixed test accuracy after a finite sample. The three original neural regimes retain their all-input absolute-margin support restriction: `|z(x)| > 1/2` before training.

| Question | Result and qualifications |
|---|---|
| Do the good NNBayes regimes make only polynomially many mistakes in total? | **Yes under uniform random Boolean inputs.** All three original positive regimes have width-independent polynomial bounds in expectation and with high probability, for posterior sampling, majority, and the strict `>2/3` confidence criterion. Uniform sampling without replacement is also covered. |
| Do ordinary ReLU and tanh have such counterexamples under the unchanged prior? | **Yes at every sufficiently large fixed width, followed by the strong-prior limit.** One fixed nonrepeating order gives `exp(Ω(√C))` designated mistakes for [ReLU and softplus](isotropic-relu-allwidth/) and `exp(Ω(√C/log C))` for [tanh](isotropic-tanh-allwidth/). Here `C` is a displayed bounded-weight shallow teacher width. All three prediction conventions fail; the calibration history and sufficient learner width are large. See the linked verification records for the distinct Lean coverage. |
| Can a fixed continuous activation give the isotropic counterexample at all large widths? | **Yes, even with strict monotonicity and a global Lipschitz bound.** The [monotone-activation paper](isotropic-monotone-allwidth/) uses `(2t+triangle(t))/3`, slopes `1/3` and `1`. A unit-weight shallow teacher of width `8m−4` on `Θ(m⁶)` inputs has `2^(m−4)` near-certain mistakes at every sufficiently large width. The [bounded triangle-wave version](isotropic-triangle-allwidth/) has teacher width `2m−1`. Calibration is large and the width threshold unquantified; ordinary tanh/ReLU are covered by the separate papers above. |
| Is there an exact finite ordinary-ReLU example? | **Yes.** At every `N≥16`, the [four-input example](relu-fourbit-mistakes/) makes fifteen near-certain mistakes after the first query, with limiting expected total `15.5`. Exact arithmetic checks every Boolean completion. No asymptotic dimension-growing lower bound follows from this example alone. |
| Does this extend to other input distributions? | **Yes under specified structured shifts.** The unchanged Gaussian learners allow adaptive switching among `K` fixed uniform-subset sources, with polynomial total mistakes and no limit on switches. Nonuniform sources pay through their positive disagreement gaps. The [new theorem](neural-function-priors/) charges posterior tails only on first appearances. For arbitrary orders, the unchanged isotropic prior has a scalar exponential counterexample and subexponential upper bounds in specified narrow architectures; the ordinary ReLU/tanh papers above give all-large-width counterexamples. |
| Does choosing a smallest consistent circuit give good online learning under distribution shift? | A deterministic history-based selector can make exponentially many mistakes on an AND target, including with NAND-only circuits. Fresh uniform minimum-circuit sampling instead gives polynomial expected mistakes on every nonanticipating order. For every history-based tie rule, polynomial bounds hold under specified adaptive finite-source and persistent-coverage conditions. See the [new paper](occam-distribution-shift/). |
| Does the big-AND lower bound transfer to NNBayes? | The [AND note](and-gaussian/) proves a growing-depth weak-prior Gaussian lower bound, but a fixed-depth weak-prior upper bound. An arbitrarily small coordinate-variance anisotropy gives a strong-prior lower bound, including an admissible one-neuron majority target. The [scalar isotropic AND upper bound](isotropic-subexponential/) rules out `exp(Ω(d))` mistakes at width one. The [isotropic counterexample](isotropic-exponential/) uses a different target, with `N=1<C_L=2`; the all-large-width tanh result above uses a different target. |
| What is known for ordinary ReLU and the AND target? | The [exact AND paper](relu-and-energy/) determines the full-cube margin energy in every dimension and proves actual posterior identification after the all-plus point and its single-bit neighbors. A quadratic width threshold suffices. It leaves arbitrary input orders open. |
| Is there an unchanged isotropic prior with a counterexample at **every** width above teacher complexity? | **Yes for fixed threshold activations with a residual.** The [residual-threshold paper](isotropic-residual-threshold/) covers bounded `sign+c tanh`, for any fixed `0<c≤1`, at every `N≥C₂=1` in the strong-prior limit. It has linear calibration, exponentially many mistakes, and polynomial IID learning. The activation is discontinuous. |
| Can the counterexample activation be arbitrarily close to tanh? | **Yes, while retaining a jump.** The [small-jump paper](isotropic-small-jump-tanh/) uses `tanh+ε sign` for every fixed `ε>0`, the unchanged prior, and every width. The target has a constant-width unit-bounded teacher at fixed `ε`; calibration and IID learning are polynomial. Dimension must be sufficiently large depending on `ε`. Taking the jump to zero is not justified. |
| Can ordinary tanh have a polynomial total-mistake bound on every input order? | **Yes in shallow mean field when only training margins are imposed.** The [training-only paper](meanfield-without-global-support/) proves `O(d⁴+C⁴)` mistakes at fixed weight bound and prefactor, at all sufficiently large widths, uniformly over adaptive orders. It omits the initial global margin restriction and is not uniform in a subsequent cold limit. |
| Is there an exactly isotropic Gaussian counterexample at all sufficiently large widths? | **Yes for the fixed `sign` activation.** The [threshold-network paper](isotropic-threshold-allwidth/) gives `2^k−2` mistakes on a size-`Θ(k)` Boolean target with a width-one, weight-one teacher. It covers the strong-prior limit, fixed `γ>1`, and small-scale `γ=1`, with polynomial IID learning. The calibration and width threshold can be exponential; discontinuity prevents an automatic tanh transfer. |
| Can ordinary tanh do this with equal input-coordinate variances? | **Yes with an arbitrarily slowly stronger readout prior.** The [readout paper](isotropic-tanh-readout/) divides the usual readout variance by any `A_N→∞`, however slowly. At every fixed `γ≥1` and positive scale, it gives exponential online mistakes after linear calibration at all sufficiently large widths, a polynomial-width shallow teacher with unit-bounded weights, and polynomial IID learning. The unchanged-prior tanh paper above uses a different construction with a larger calibration set. |
| Can ordinary tanh make exponentially many mistakes at every width? | **Yes after arbitrarily small unequal perturbations of the input-weight variances.** The [all-width majority paper](anisotropic-allwidth/) has a one-hidden-layer, unit-weight teacher `C₂=1` and covers every `N≥1`, with `O(d)` calibration and `exp(Ω(d))` mistakes. It also gives a common small mean-field scale, all sufficiently large widths for each fixed `γ>1`, and polynomial IID learning under the same priors. The unchanged, exactly isotropic tanh paper above gives a separate all-large-width result. |
| Can exponential online mistakes persist at arbitrary width with nonlinear activations? | **Yes in two qualified models.** The [nonlinear-variants paper](wide-nonlinear-variants/) covers every `N≥C_L=2` for activations approaching the identity, and for one fixed standard nonlinear layer followed by linear layers without biases. The first also has polynomial IID sample complexity. Neither settles a fixed standard activation in every layer of the usual network. Exact tanh competitors show why scalar energy calculations cannot simply be lifted by replication. |
| Does a small mistake count force a small circuit for circuit induction? | **Yes for specified rules.** Minimum-consistent-circuit mistakes `B` imply `C(f) <= (3d+3)B`. Bayesian sampling under a geometric gate-count/uniform-wiring prior gives `C(f)=O(d M log(d+dM))` for expected mistakes `M`; an explicit prefix prior improves this to `O(dM+1)`. These count total NAND gates without a depth restriction. |
| Does the polynomial converse hold for NNBayes? | In the strong-prior-then-width limit, an output cap gives a fixed-depth polynomial converse. Adding controlled internal amplification gives a [polynomial-depth converse](depth-polynomial-converse/), with fourth-power mistake dependence. The original fixed-width polynomial converse remains open. |
| Can the cumulative bound be polynomial in depth? | Yes under the deterministic neuron-merging restrictions, for layered Boolean-circuit teachers with constant wires and compilation overhead counted. The original internal-noise restriction's current approximate-compression proof does not yet give the desired polynomial-depth cumulative extension. |
| What about ReLU and GELU? | The [unified activation theorem](../unbounded-activations/) supplies the same finite-class premise throughout `gamma >= 1`, at every fixed depth, for every fixed globally Lipschitz activation vanishing at zero. The cumulative argument therefore extends to these cases. |

## Why the upper bounds work

Suppose the posterior puts all but `beta` of its mass, uniformly over every history, on a fixed family of functions whose log cardinality is at most `h`. On a domain of size `m`, the expected total posterior-sampling errors in a uniform random permutation are at most

$$
1+2(h+1)\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac1j+m\beta.
$$

Repeated inputs require no further mistakes under exact conditioning, so the infinite uniform iid stream has the same total-error distribution. On the Boolean cube, choose `beta=2^(-d)`. The neural compression bounds pay only polynomially for its logarithm, giving polynomial total mistakes. Small learner widths use the direct Bayesian log-prior bound instead. Mixtures of tied minimizers are fully allowed. Cumulative high-probability bounds hold for each fixed learner, with constants independent of its width; they do not assert one event controlling independently sampled predictions at every width.

The [upper paper](upper/) supplies the proof, explicit imported neural budgets, logarithmic-confidence bounds, and the relevant distinctions between sampling and confidence errors. A finite class alone does not give the same guarantee under arbitrary input order: strongly unequal prior masses can produce a long sequence of mistakes. The paper gives an explicit counterexample to that general inference, without claiming it is a counterexample for the Gaussian neural prior.

## Why the circuit converses work

A circuit can be overwritten at one Boolean input using at most `3d+3` additional NAND gates. For minimum-consistent-circuit prediction, a correct prediction leaves minimum size unchanged, and a mistake raises it by at most this additive cost. For Bayesian sampling, an injective repair of descriptions bounds the probability of the correct label from below, letting cumulative sampling errors control log loss. The exact encoding and its length tail matter.

All converses require sufficient coverage: after revealing the full cube they bound the full target's circuit complexity; on a smaller support they bound the smallest extension agreeing there. They also hold after an infinite iid sequence with that support. The Bayesian circuit converse is for posterior sampling, not a proved converse for posterior majority. An arbitrary prior or learner can hard-code a complicated target and make no mistakes.

The [neural note](nn-converse.md) identifies the remaining obstruction in transferring the exception argument: a linear readout can produce a large wrong output, so adding a correcting singleton may cost energy proportional to the current energy. It gives an exact tanh singleton construction, handles tied minimizers, and proves the stated weaker and output-capped results with their distinct order of limits.

## Verification

The papers and neural note have independent AI-agent mathematical reviews. The core upper-bound, circuit-converse and gain-restricted polynomial-depth neural-converse statements are Lean verified in the [second batch](../../lean/review/BatchTwo-coverage.md), with the optional extensions and neural Markdown note covered only where explicitly recorded. The original five main results retain their own presentation. Each paper has editable sources, a build script, an independent review, and a publication record. Run `./build.sh` here to rebuild the two original online-learning PDFs; the polynomial-depth converse has its own build script. The [neural-note review](verification/nn-converse-review.md) records its narrower scope.

The [circuit Occam distribution-shift paper](occam-distribution-shift/) has conventional proofs and independent AI-agent audits, with its own [verification record](occam-distribution-shift/verification/README.md). It is not included in the earlier Lean coverage.

The [neural function-prior paper](neural-function-priors/) extends the original Gaussian online bounds to structured shifts and separately proves arbitrary-order bounds after an explicit function-prior transformation. It preserves the distinction between these learners and has conventional reviews, not Lean verification.

The [AND and Gaussian-prior note](and-gaussian/) has conventional proofs and independent AI-agent reviews, not Lean formalization. It explicitly separates the unchanged weak prior from the perturbed strong prior.

The [two isotropic-prior papers](isotropic-exponential/) have conventional proofs and independent reviews, with exact finite affine certificates for the counterexample. Their [upper bounds](isotropic-subexponential/) use exact Gaussian symmetry orbits. Neither paper is Lean formalized.

The [nonlinear all-width variants](wide-nonlinear-variants/) have independent mathematical reviews, an exact rational tanh certificate, and an isolated PDF rebuild. Their precise activation and architecture restrictions are part of the theorem statements; they are not Lean formalized.

The [all-width tanh majority paper](anisotropic-allwidth/) has conventional proofs and independent reviews of the essential-coordinate bound, uniform rigidity gap, actual-posterior lower bound, common mean-field scale, and IID comparison. The small variance asymmetry is part of its assumptions. It is not Lean formalized.

The [isotropic threshold-network paper](isotropic-threshold-allwidth/) and [tanh readout paper](isotropic-tanh-readout/) each have standalone proofs, independent full-manuscript reviews, and isolated PDF rebuilds. They retain their different assumptions: discontinuous `sign` activation for the former, and a diverging readout-variance correction for ordinary tanh in the latter. These papers are not Lean formalized.

A separate [ReLU parity note](relu-parity-energy-width/) proves that attaining the exact minimum Gaussian energy can require exponentially more neurons than a bounded-weight teacher needs. It has two independent mathematical reviews and an isolated PDF rebuild. It does not assert an online-mistake lower bound and is not Lean formalized.

The [canonical-case research notes](isotropic-research-notes/) preserve rigorous obstructions to several attempted proofs for unchanged isotropic tanh. They do not prove an upper or lower bound for the unrestricted learner.

The [ReLU AND paper](relu-and-energy/) has a conventional all-atom dual proof, a polynomial-support construction, equality-case analysis of every minimizer, and an actual Gaussian posterior argument. Independent source-level reviews and an isolated rebuild are recorded in its verification folder. It is not Lean formalized.

The [continuous monotone-activation paper](isotropic-monotone-allwidth/) and [bounded triangle-wave paper](isotropic-triangle-allwidth/) have conventional proofs and independent audits of the atomic norm, exclusion of nonsymmetric completions, strict function ordering, and finite-width Gaussian posterior transfer. The [four-input ReLU paper](relu-fourbit-mistakes/) has optimizer-free exact certificates and independent mathematical and code reviews. These papers are not Lean formalized.

The [ordinary-ReLU and softplus paper](isotropic-relu-allwidth/) has a [dedicated verification record](isotropic-relu-allwidth/verification/README.md). Its precise ReLU theorem uses the literal Gaussian posterior, an explicit sufficient width, and a fixed full-cube input permutation. The standard-softplus extension, including the same input sequence and exact teacher bounds, is also Lean verified, as is the optional ReLU extension to arbitrary positive layer variances. The linked full-paper inventories distinguish main-theorem verification from coverage of standalone appendix results. The [ordinary-tanh paper](isotropic-tanh-allwidth/) has a complete Lean proof of the improved `exp(Ω(√C/log C))` main theorem and its finite-scale prediction consequences. Its [verification record](isotropic-tanh-allwidth/verification/README.md) links the exact statements, proofs, axiom checks, and independent correspondence reviews.
