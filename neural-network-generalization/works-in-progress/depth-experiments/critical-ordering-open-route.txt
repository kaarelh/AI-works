Critical target ordering: what the natural contractions do not prove
Research note, 17 October 2026. Kept separate from the paper's claims.

The requested universal statement J_L(constant) <= J_L(higher parity) for every finite dimension and every depth remains unproved. The successful replacement is the new fixed-depth growing-relevance separation and the all-depth rate profile. The following exact facts explain why extending the shallow proof directly is difficult.

1. Exact gauge reduction, with a changed first reference.
For a target f, let D_f be coordinatewise multiplication by f. Because tanh is odd, M_{D_f Q D_f}=D_f# M_Q. Applying D_f to every feature law and readout therefore preserves every entropy term after the first layer and the readout energy. It turns f into the constant target, but changes the first Gaussian reference from Q0 to D_f Q0 D_f. These two covariance matrices have equal diagonals. Consequently neither can dominate the other in Loewner order unless they are equal: a positive semidefinite matrix with all diagonal entries zero is zero.

For a nontrivial parity, a direct gauge at the first layer is worse than merely expensive: after applying atanh, the original support is the affine Walsh subspace span{1,x_1,...,x_d}, whereas the gauged support is its product by the parity. For degree2 their intersection has dimension2, strictly below d+1; for degree>=3 the intersection is zero. Nondegenerate Gaussian measures on these subspaces are therefore mutually singular. A dictator's direct gauge can also be singular; its exact equality with constants uses the additional projective input permutation.

2. Classification of exact linear tanh intertwiners.
Suppose A:R^m->R^m is linear and A tanh(z)=tanh(Az) for every z. In each row, comparing cubic Taylor coefficients gives (sum a_j z_j)^3=sum a_j z_j^3. Thus at most one row coefficient is nonzero, and it must be+1 or-1. Every row of A is zero or a signed coordinate selection.

Assume every row is nonzero and A Q0 A^T <= Q0. All first-layer diagonal entries equal(d+1)/d, so the two covariance matrices have identical diagonals and the PSD inequality is equality. Distinct rows cannot select the same coordinate with either sign, because Q0 has no distinct inputs with correlation+1 or-1. Hence A is a signed input permutation preserving the augmented-cube Gram matrix.

Such a map must come from a signed permutation of the d+1 augmented coordinates. Indeed preservation of the Gram matrix extends the map on augmented cube vectors to an orthogonal linear map U; adjoining antipodes shows U maps the full hypercube to itself. Each row of U then has l1 norm1 (maximize over cube vertices) and l2 norm1, so it has a single nonzero entry of magnitude1. Therefore the output-sign multiplier is a constant or a dictator. These exact linear Gaussian contractions cannot send a constant target to higher parity. This classification does not rule out nonlinear comparison constructions.

3. Pathwise symmetry averaging lacks the shallow convexity argument.
The transition entropy is not jointly convex in its feature law and previous covariance. Even at the scalar Gaussian level,
 D(N(0,s)||N(0,q))=.5[s/q-1+log(q/s)]
has second q-derivative(2s-q)/(2q^3), negative for q>2s. Tanh is a measurable bijection onto(-1,1), so it preserves this example's entropy exactly.

A more explicit two-input example uses Q_a=diag(a,b) and its coordinate-swapped version Q_b=diag(b,a), with a,b>0. Each unmodified next layer M_{Q_a}, M_{Q_b} has zero transition entropy. Their average feature law, relative to M_{(a+b)I/2}, instead has entropy at least
 .5 log((a+b)^2/(4ab)) - log2,
by the Gaussian-mixture entropy identity and the Jensen-Shannon bound<=log2. This diverges as a->0 at fixed b. Both preceding covariance matrices are realizable by finite-entropy first-layer controls when d=1. Averaging those preceding laws lowers first-layer entropy by at most log2, so even the two-hidden-layer prefix action can increase without bound under the actual input-swap symmetry. This is a failure of a general symmetrization argument, not a counterexample to the target-rate ordering itself.

4. Fourier-mode comparison does not close under a free layer.
A covariance with zero target-parity component can acquire a positive component after a zero-entropy tanh transition: the cubic Hermite term creates degree3 parity from first-degree Gaussian coordinates. Therefore ordering only the initial constant and parity Fourier coefficients cannot be iterated as a scalar target-mode recursion. The separate local-geometry note gives a quantified example with output parity eigenvalue32 epsilon^3+O(epsilon^4).

Positive outcome from this investigation:
For all d,L and augmented relevance k, the new rigorous bound is
 J_L(f)>=(1/64) min{d^(1/(L-1)),(dk)^(1/L)},
while J_L(constant)<=5000L d^(1/L). Thus every growing-relevance sequence has a diverging rate ratio at fixed L. This does not settle small fixed-degree parities at arbitrary L, nor exact ordering for all targets.
