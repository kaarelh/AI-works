FOCUSED FINAL ATTEMPT ON THE GENERAL CIRCUIT/PRODUCT-NORM GAP
2026-10-17. Work-in-progress research note; no new theorem added to the paper.

Question: does every ordinary size-S Boolean circuit admit some globally unit-margin tanh representation of intrinsic all-depth product-Frobenius complexity polynomial in S, or is there an explicit small circuit family with superpolynomial intrinsic product complexity?

Outcome: neither direction closed. The strongest audited results remain those in unrestricted-paper.pdf. In particular, implementation-specific costs must not be interpreted as function-level lower bounds.

Checks that eliminated tempting routes:
1. Threshold-circuit 'energy' lower bounds in Uchizawa--Abe (MFCS2023, arXiv2107.00223) count active gates, not Gaussian quadratic parameter energy or product Frobenius norm. Their depth/energy/rank tradeoff has no proved implication for our intrinsic norm. They therefore cannot simply be imported.
2. Communication-matrix margin/nuclear-norm or rank bounds cannot be polynomially upper-bounded by intrinsic PF in general: Boolean inner product already has a fixed-depth polynomial threshold representation, so our bounded tanh translation gives polynomial PF, while its usual communication-matrix complexity is exponentially large. Any valid bridge must incur enough loss to accommodate this example.
3. Standard formula simulation in parallel keeps a Frobenius factor from every simultaneously live subproblem. Exact cloning cannot remove this for a balanced tree (audited method obstruction). Constant-width branching-program simulation similarly pays a constant >1 Frobenius factor each step; input rereading is not free in our architecture. Near-identity memory transmission has a baseline sqrt(memory dimension) Frobenius cost. These observations obstruct these particular constructions, not alternative representations of the Boolean function.
4. The new depth-independent normal form does not imply TC0 membership: its depth is polynomial in P, not constant. Consequently it does not itself refute the possibility that every small circuit has polynomial intrinsic PF. Conversely, a superpolynomial intrinsic-PF lower bound for an NC1 family would separate that family from TC0, because our fixed-depth threshold simulation gives polynomial intrinsic PF for every TC0 family. Such a lower bound is correspondingly substantial.

Related positive result with a different learner/regularizer:
Colin Wei, Yining Chen, Tengyu Ma, 'Statistically Meaningful Approximation: a Case Study on Approximating Turing Machines with Transformers,' NeurIPS2022. Section3/Theorem3.1 proves polynomial intrinsic-circuit sample complexity for overparameterized feedforward ReLU nets using an all-layer-margin surrogate; width enters only logarithmically. The paper explicitly notes that output-margin norm bounds are exponential for its naive Boolean simulation. It does not establish a polynomial intrinsic product norm or a theorem for the original Gaussian posterior. Primary source checked directly:
https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/4ebf1d74f53ece08512a23309d58df89-Supplemental-Conference.pdf
This supports the separate robustness program, not a resolution of the unrestricted prior.

Immediate quantitative consequences already supported by audited constructions:
- A size-S, depth-h bounded-fanin circuit has intrinsic PF at most S^{O(1+h/loglogS)} after the block representation (S includes input resources).
- A size-S Boolean formula has intrinsic PF at most exp[O(log^2 S/loglogS)] after balancing and blocking, independent of its original formula depth.
- Intrinsic PF controls an ordinary finite Boolean description polynomially, and minimum excess Gaussian energy is logarithmic in intrinsic PF once the architecture is sufficiently large. The converse polynomial circuit/PF comparison remains open.
