\section{Local covariance geometry and a singular-direction obstruction}
\label{sec:critical-local-geometry}

This section records two rigorous ingredients for a possible future
target-relative analysis. They do not establish a continuum limit or
a kernel description of the posterior.

\begin{proposition}[Local Fisher cost at a fixed nonsingular shape]
\label{prop:critical-local-fisher}
Fix a positive-definite $m\times m$ matrix $A$ and a symmetric matrix
$\Delta$. Let $P_\epsilon$ be the law of
$h=\tanh(\sqrt\epsilon G)$, $G\sim\mathcal N(0,A)$, and put
\[
 I_\epsilon(\Delta)=\inf\left\{
 D(\nu\Vert P_\epsilon):
       \mathbb E_\nu hh^T=\epsilon A+\epsilon^2\Delta\right\}.
\]
For all sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$ this constraint is
feasible, and
\[
 I_\epsilon(\Delta)=\frac{\epsilon^2}{4}
 \operatorname{tr}\left[
  \left(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right)^2\right]+O(\epsilon^3),
 \qquad
 B=\Delta+\operatorname{diag}(A)A+A\operatorname{diag}(A).
\]
The constants may depend on $m,A,\Delta$. The expansion is uniform
on compact sets with $A$ bounded away from singularity and $\Delta$
bounded.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the symmetric-matrix statistic $T_\epsilon=hh^T/\epsilon$.
Taylor expansion and Gaussian moments give
\[
 \mathbb E T_\epsilon
 =A-\epsilon[\operatorname{diag}(A)A+
                         A\operatorname{diag}(A)]+O(\epsilon^2).
\]
For example the $(i,j)$ first correction is
$-\epsilon\mathbb E(G_i^3G_j+G_iG_j^3)/3
=-\epsilon(A_{ii}+A_{jj})A_{ij}$.
Let
\[
 \Lambda_\epsilon(\Theta)
 =\log\mathbb E\exp\{\operatorname{tr}(\Theta T_\epsilon)\}.
\]
Since $\|T_\epsilon\|_{\rm op}\le\|G\|_2^2$, these logarithmic
moment generating functions and their required derivatives are
uniformly controlled in a fixed neighborhood of $\Theta=0$.
At $\epsilon=0$ the Hessian is the Gaussian quadratic-form covariance:
\[
 D^2\Lambda_0(0)[C,C]=2\operatorname{tr}(ACAC).
\]
It is positive definite on the space of symmetric matrices.
The implicit function theorem therefore produces a quadratic
exponential tilt having exactly the desired normalized moment
$A+\epsilon\Delta$. Its tilt parameter is
\[
 \Theta_\epsilon=\frac\epsilon2 A^{-1}BA^{-1}
                       +O(\epsilon^2).
\]
Entropy duality makes this tilt the constrained minimizer. Expanding
$\operatorname{tr}[\Theta_\epsilon(A+\epsilon\Delta)]
-\Lambda_\epsilon(\Theta_\epsilon)$ gives the asserted coefficient
and remainder. The same estimates are uniform on the stated compact
sets.
\end{proof}

Formally, if a long cold segment has covariance $A(t)/L$, this local
calculation suggests the controlled equation
\[
 \dot A=B-\operatorname{diag}(A)A-A\operatorname{diag}(A)
\]
and an action proportional to
$L^{-1}\int\operatorname{tr}[(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})^2]/4$.
This is only a formal implication: the proposition does not provide
the compactness, boundary control, or uniformity needed to pass to a
long-horizon variational limit.

\begin{proposition}[Free creation of a previously negligible parity mode]
\label{prop:critical-singular-mode-obstruction}
On the three-dimensional Boolean cube, let $m=8$,
$f(x)=x_1x_2x_3$, and
\[
 Q_\epsilon(x,y)=\epsilon(1+x\cdot y)+\delta_\epsilon\mathbf1_{x=y},
 \qquad \delta_\epsilon=e^{-1/\epsilon}.
\]
This matrix is positive definite and has diagonal
$4\epsilon+\delta_\epsilon\to0$.
Its eigenvalue in the parity direction is $\delta_\epsilon$.
After one unconditioned tanh Gaussian transition, the new covariance
$K(Q_\epsilon)$ has parity eigenvalue
\[
 32\epsilon^3+O(\epsilon^4).
\]
In particular,
\[
 \log\frac{f^TQ_\epsilon^{-1}f}
               {f^TK(Q_\epsilon)^{-1}f}
 =\frac1\epsilon+3\log\epsilon+O(1)\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
although the transition has exactly zero relative entropy.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The characters diagonalize both matrices by cube-translation
invariance. The parity character is orthogonal to the constant and
all three linear characters, giving the initial eigenvalue.
First omit the $\delta_\epsilon I$ term and write the Gaussian field
as $\sqrt\epsilon(Z_0+Z_1x_1+Z_2x_2+Z_3x_3)$, with independent
standard normals. Its parity Fourier coefficient after tanh is
\[
 \frac18\sum_x f(x)
   \tanh[\sqrt\epsilon(Z_0+Z_1x_1+Z_2x_2+Z_3x_3)]
 =-2\epsilon^{3/2}Z_1Z_2Z_3+O_{L^2}(\epsilon^{5/2}).
\]
The linear term is orthogonal to parity; the cubic term contributes
the six permutations of $Z_1Z_2Z_3x_1x_2x_3$. The global Taylor
remainder bound $|\tanh u-u+u^3/3|\le C|u|^5$ and Gaussian
moments justify the stated $L^2$ error. Squaring gives Fourier
variance $4\epsilon^3+O(\epsilon^4)$, and multiplying by $m=8$
gives the covariance eigenvalue.

Adding independent coordinate Gaussian noise of variance
$\delta_\epsilon$ changes this Fourier coefficient by
$O_{L^2}(\sqrt{\delta_\epsilon})$, by the Lipschitz property of
tanh. This is negligible compared with every power of $\epsilon$.
The final logarithmic identity follows from the two eigenvalues.

More generally, fix any sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$ and
replace $\delta_\epsilon$ by $\delta\downarrow0$. The output parity
eigenvalue stays bounded below by $c\epsilon^3$, whereas the input
eigenvalue is $\delta$. Thus the inverse-energy improvement is
unbounded even with zero entropy and all diagonal variances bounded
by the fixed number $5\epsilon$.
\end{proof}

Thus no uniform bound can control improvement of every target's
inverse-covariance energy solely by the accumulated entropy and the
smallness of marginal variances. Free nonlinear creation of modes
must also be included. This does not rule out a comparison with the
entire unconditioned kernel evolution, or a more restricted geometric
argument along actual optimal paths. It explains why the fixed-shape
Fisher expansion cannot simply be applied uniformly near singular
directions.

\begin{proposition}[Free creation of a Boolean gate component]
\label{prop:critical-free-boolean-gate}
There are universal $q_0,c>0$ with the following property. Let
$g,h$ be Boolean vectors on a finite input set, and suppose that a
positive-semidefinite covariance $Q$ has constant diagonal
$q\le q_0$ and
\[
 Q\succeq a(\mathbf1\mathbf1^T+gg^T+hh^T),\qquad a>0.
\]
For every Boolean two-input gate $b:\{-1,1\}^2\to\{-1,1\}$,
the unconditioned tanh covariance satisfies
\[
 K(Q)\succeq c a^3 b(g,h)b(g,h)^T.
\]
This transition again has zero relative entropy.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Hermite expansion, with the normalized cubic Hermite polynomial,
gives
\[
 K(Q)=\sum_{n\ge0}\beta_n(q)^2 q^{-n}Q^{\circ n},\qquad
 \beta_3(q)=-\frac{2}{\sqrt6}q^{3/2}+O(q^{5/2}).
\]
Every summand is positive semidefinite. Therefore
$K(Q)\succeq cQ^{\circ3}$ for small enough $q$. Schur products
preserve positive-semidefinite order when the other factor is
positive semidefinite, so
\[
 Q^{\circ3}\succeq
 a^3(\mathbf1\mathbf1^T+gg^T+hh^T)^{\circ3}
 \succeq a^3\sum_{u\in\{\mathbf1,g,h,gh\}}uu^T.
\]
Here repeated vectors cause no problem: the displayed sum retains
four terms with their multiplicities. Write the exact Boolean Fourier
expansion
$b(s,t)=c_0+c_1s+c_2t+c_{12}st$. Parseval gives
$c_0^2+c_1^2+c_2^2+c_{12}^2=1$. Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yields
$\sum uu^T\succeq b(g,h)b(g,h)^T$, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

This gives a concrete mechanism for creating circuit gates in the
feature covariance. Repeated free gates can have very small
coefficients; controlled amplification would still have to be
quantified. More fundamentally, an absolute upper bound for the cost
of constructing a small circuit would need a target-relative
comparison to yield a posterior sample-complexity theorem. The gate
lemma supplies neither such a comparison nor a learning guarantee.
