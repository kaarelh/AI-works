\section{A concrete data-local stability condition}
\label{sec:local-geometry-condition}

A natural way to allow off-distribution failures is to require stability on
neighborhoods of representative unlabeled inputs. The neighborhoods need
not have the same radii for every network. Their input-set complexity,
rather than the complexity of the network that selects the radii, controls
the statistical price of this adaptation.

Choose $M\ge1$ pilot centers $a_1,\ldots,a_M\in\mathcal X$ using data
independent of the labeled sample and subsequent coverage-validation
sample. Fix an input metric $d_0$ independently of those two samples. For
nonnegative radii $s=(s_1,\ldots,s_M)$ set
\[
 G_s=\bigcup_{j=1}^M\{x:d_0(x,a_j)\le s_j\},\qquad B_s=G_s^c.
\]
A network is admissible if there exists $s$ such that it satisfies the
calibrated clipped-score condition at every input of $G_s$, and the
independent unlabeled validation sample assigns at most $\rho$ mass to
$B_s$. The prior is the raw Gaussian conditioned on existence of such
radii; the radii may differ across posterior networks.

\begin{proposition}[Input complexity of adaptive neighborhood radii]
The family $\{B_s:s\in[0,\infty)^M\}$ has VC dimension at most $M$.
Consequently the controlled-exception theorem applies with polynomial
labeled and unlabeled sample bounds and final Gibbs test-margin error
at most $3\rho+\varepsilon$, uniformly in width and Gaussian scale.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Map $x$ to its fixed distance vector
$(d_0(x,a_1),\ldots,d_0(x,a_M))$. Each $B_s$ is the inverse image of an
upper orthant. If $k$ points were shattered, excluding point $i$ while
including every other point would require a coordinate in which point
$i$ is the unique minimum. Different points require different such
coordinates. Hence $k\le M$.

For completeness, on the finite Boolean domain the number of distinct
exception sets is at most $(2^d+1)^M$. Uniform one-sided Hoeffding bounds
on independent validation and labeled samples give, simultaneously for
all admissible radii,
\[
 D(B_s)\le\rho+u,\qquad \widehat D_S(B_s)\le\rho+2u.
\]
The global robust Boolean target cylinder is admissible by taking radii
large enough to cover $\mathcal X$, so its probability lower bound is
unchanged. Apply entropy compression to the full union-conditioned
posterior. Exceptional inputs add at most one to each score/ramp
comparison, giving
\[
 R(Q)\le\beta+3\alpha+3\rho+5u+
       \frac{8[\log|\mathcal H|+\log(3/\delta)]}{n}.
\]
Taking $u,\alpha,\beta$ proportional to $\varepsilon$ proves the stated
bound. This argument uses the ordinary training-margin likelihood and
controls the empirically selected exception set uniformly; it does not
replace that likelihood by robust training consistency.
\end{proof}

The metric may, for example, be Euclidean distance or distance in a fixed
representation learned from the independent pilot. The theorem does not
promise that a small number of neighborhoods covers a high-dimensional
input distribution while satisfying the stability condition. That is a
substantive empirical question. A common radius makes the exception family
nested and reduces its VC dimension to at most one, but may give much
poorer coverage.

A sound empirical sufficient certificate is a raw output enclosure
$[\ell_j,u_j]$ valid jointly for every input in a neighborhood and every
allowed internal disturbance, with
$T(u_j)-T(\ell_j)\le1/16$. In particular, an enclosure entirely beyond a
saturation threshold has zero clipped-score diameter. This connects the
condition to confidently classified input clusters. A tighter certificate
can bound the noisy-minus-clean score directly and exploit their shared
input.

These are sufficient ways to verify membership for an individual trained
network. They must not silently be made additional restrictions on the
prior: a conservative interval certificate can fail for an actually stable
Boolean target, and a prior restricted to that certificate would need a
new target-mass proof. Likewise, certifying finitely many centers without
certifying their neighborhoods does not establish coverage of a region.
