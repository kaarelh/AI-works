\section{Every fixed initial segment becomes unconditioned}
\label{sec:critical-initial-layers}

The common-rate theorem has a qualitative consequence about where a
near-optimal path can change the features. This is a statement about
variational paths; it does not control the later low-variance layers.

\begin{proposition}[Unconditioned fixed prefixes of deep optimal paths]
\label{prop:critical-unconditioned-prefix}
Fix the input dimension $d$ and a Boolean target $f$. Let $L_n\to\infty$
and choose admissible critical variational paths of costs $A_n$ with
$A_n\to V_*$. Write their feature laws and second moments as
$\nu_{n,j},Q_{n,j}$. Let the unconditioned feature sequence be
\[
 \nu_j^0=\mathsf M_{Q_{j-1}^0},\qquad
 Q_j^0=\int hh^T\,d\nu_j^0(h),
\]
starting from the prescribed first-layer covariance $Q_0$.
For every fixed integer $T\ge1$,
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^T
 D(\nu_{n,j}\Vert\mathsf M_{Q_{n,j-1}})\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad Q_{n,j}\longrightarrow Q_j^0\quad(1\le j\le T).
\]
In fact $\nu_{n,j}\to\nu_j^0$ in total variation for every fixed
$j\ge1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose the signed pair used in the common-rate lower bound, and let
$v_{n,j}$ be its pair variances. Strict dissipation and bounded total
cost show
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{L_n-2}v_{n,j}^2\le C.
\]
Choose an index $i_n\in\{0,\ldots,L_n-2\}$ minimizing these
variances. Then $v_{n,i_n}\to0$. The suffix scalar comparison gives
\[
 0\le\sum_{j=1}^{i_n}
 D(\nu_{n,j}\Vert\mathsf M_{Q_{n,j-1}})
 \le A_n-V(v_{n,i_n})\longrightarrow0.
\]

We claim $i_n\to\infty$. Otherwise pass to a subsequence on which
$i_n=i$ is a fixed nonnegative integer. For $i=0$, its variance is
the fixed positive number $1/d$, a contradiction. For $i\ge1$,
Pinsker's inequality and continuity of the Gaussian feature-covariance
map inductively imply $Q_{n,j}\to Q_j^0$ for $j\le i$.
The first unconditioned feature covariance is positive definite by
the spectrum lemma. Every subsequent one remains positive definite:
if $Q\succ0$, a nonzero linear combination of the coordinatewise
tanh functions is nonzero on an open subset of $\mathbb R^m$,
and a nonsingular Gaussian assigns that subset positive probability.
Thus the selected pair variance in $Q_i^0$ is strictly positive,
contradicting $v_{n,i_n}\to0$. This proves the claim.

For any fixed $T$, eventually $i_n\ge T$, so the prefix entropy
sum through $T$ tends to zero. Pinsker and induction again give the
covariance convergence. At the first layer the reference law is fixed,
so total variation convergence follows directly from Pinsker. At
every later fixed layer, $Q_{j-1}^0$ is positive definite. Gaussian
laws are continuous in total variation under convergence to a positive
definite covariance; applying coordinatewise tanh cannot increase
total variation. A final triangle inequality with Pinsker proves the
claimed convergence of each feature law.
\end{proof}

The result applies to nearly minimizing paths because the upper and
lower common-rate bounds force their costs to tend to $V_*$. It says
that target-specific modification moves beyond every fixed initial
layer as depth grows. It does not say that all later features are
Gaussian, or that the final predictor is a kernel method: a long
low-variance segment can accumulate a target-specific change with
vanishing total entropy cost.
