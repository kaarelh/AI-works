\section{Cheap purification of an approximate rank-one sign component}
\label{sec:critical-purification}

The following lemma addresses amplitude errors in a finite-width
rank-one construction. Its contraction is multiplicative: it has no
permanent additive sampling-error floor.

\begin{lemma}[One-step Gaussian purification]
\label{lem:critical-purification-step}
There are universal constants $q_*,\eta,c_0,c_1,c_2,C>0$, with
$c_0\le c_1/100$, with the following
property. Fix $0<q\le q_*$ and put $s=F^{-1}(q)$, where
$F(s)=\mathbb E\tanh^2(\sqrt sZ)$. Suppose
\[
 |q_0-q|\le c_0 q^2,\qquad
 |a-1|\le\eta,\qquad 0\le r\le\eta^2q_0.
\]
For independent $A\sim\mathcal N(0,s)$ and
$B\sim\mathcal N(0,r)$, define
\[
 D=q_0(a-1)^2+r.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathbb E[\tanh(aA+B)-\tanh A]^2\le(1-c_1q)D.
\]
Moreover, for $N$ independent copies, the empirical squared difference
is at most $(1-c_1q/2)D$ except on an event of probability
$2\exp(-c_2Nq^2)$. When $D=0$ the conclusion holds identically.
The empirical anchor variance $N^{-1}\sum_i\tanh^2 A_i$ differs
from $q$ by at most $c_0q^2$ except on an event of the same form, after
changing the universal constants.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $u=a-1$ and $E=uA+B$. The global Taylor-remainder bound
\[
 \operatorname{sech}^2 t=1-t^2+R(t),\qquad |R(t)|\le C t^4
\]
implies, by integrating the derivative between $A$ and $A+E$ and
squaring, that
\[
 \mathbb E[\tanh(A+E)-\tanh A]^2
 =\mathbb E E^2-2\mathbb E[A^2E^2+AE^3+E^4/3]
                 +O(s^2\mathbb E E^2).
\]
The remainder constant is uniform for $|u|\le\eta$,
$r\le\eta^2q_0$, and small $q$, because all variables are jointly
Gaussian, $q_0\asymp s$, and their moments of each fixed order are
bounded by the corresponding powers of $s$ and $\mathbb E E^2$.
Explicitly, the error after squaring is bounded by a constant times
$\mathbb E[E^2(A^4+E^4+A^8+E^8)]$, which has the stated bound.

The necessary Gaussian moments are
\[
 \mathbb E E^2=su^2+r,\quad
 \mathbb E A^2E^2=3s^2u^2+sr,\quad
 \mathbb E AE^3=3s^2u^3+3sur,\quad
 \mathbb E E^4=3(su^2+r)^2.
\]
Also $F(s)=s-2s^2+O(s^3)$, and hence
$s-q_0=2s^2+O(s^3)+O(c_0q^2)$.
Substitution shows that the squared difference is at most
\[
 D-(4-O(\eta)-O(q)-O(c_0))s^2u^2
       -(2-O(\eta)-O(q))sr.
\]
Choose first $\eta$, then $q_*$ and the allowable anchor tolerance
constant sufficiently small. Since $s\asymp q\asymp q_0$, this
proves a contraction $1-c_1q$ for some universal $c_1>0$.

For concentration, the pointwise Lipschitz bound gives
$|\tanh(A+E)-\tanh A|\le|E|$. The variable $E$ is Gaussian
with variance $su^2+r\le CD$. Thus its squared activation difference
divided by $D$ has a uniformly bounded subexponential norm.
Bernstein's inequality at relative accuracy $c_1q/2$ gives the stated
$2e^{-c_2Nq^2}$ bound. Similarly, $\tanh^2 A\le A^2$ and
$s\asymp q$, so the anchor square divided by $q$ has uniformly
bounded subexponential norm. Bernstein at relative accuracy $c_0q$
gives the anchor assertion. Relabeling constants completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-width iterative purification]
\label{prop:finite-width-purification}
Let $H$ be a matrix of $N$ current feature rows on a finite set of $m$
inputs, and put $Q=H^TH/N$. Fix a reference input $x_0$ and a sign
pattern $f$. Suppose, with $q_0=Q(x_0,x_0)$,
\[
 |q_0-q|\le c_0q^2,\qquad
 Q(x,x)+q_0-2f(x)f(x_0)Q(x,x_0)\le\eta^2q_0
 \quad\text{for all }x.
\]
At each new layer, let
\[
 b=H(\cdot,x_0)/\sqrt{Nq_0},\qquad
 \epsilon=F^{-1}(q)/q_0-1,
\]
and draw its rows independently with weight covariance
$(I_N+\epsilon bb^T)/N$. There are universal constants such that
$0\le\epsilon\le Cq$, and this layer has relative entropy at most
$CNq^2$ from the original conditional Gaussian layer.

Except with probability at most $2T(m+1)e^{-c_2Nq^2}$, after every
one of $T$ such layers the anchor-variance condition persists, and
every signed squared column distance contracts by the factor
$(1-c_1q/2)$ at each step. In particular, at time $T$ every such
distance is at most
\[
 \eta^2(q+c_0q^2)e^{-c_1qT/2}.
\]
Stop the controlled dynamics on first failure and use the original
Gaussian transition thereafter. The relative entropy of this complete
stopped proposal law is at most $CNq^2T$, up to a change of universal
constants. This is not a claim about the entropy after conditioning
the proposal law on success.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The vector $b$ has unit norm. Decompose the current covariance as
\[
 Q=gg^T+R,\qquad
 g=Qe_{x_0}/\sqrt{q_0},\qquad R\succeq0,
 \qquad R(x_0,x_0)=0.
\]
The modified preactivation covariance is exactly
$Q+\epsilon gg^T$. After multiplying the coordinate at $x$ by
$f(x)f(x_0)$, its joint Gaussian law with the anchor can be written
as $(a_xA+B_x,A)$, where
\[
 a_x=\frac{f(x)f(x_0)Q(x,x_0)}{q_0},\quad
 \operatorname{Var}(B_x)=R(x,x),\quad B_x\perp A.
\]
The old signed squared column distance is exactly
$q_0(a_x-1)^2+R(x,x)$. The assumed bound puts each pair in the
preceding lemma's range, after reducing the initial constant $\eta$
if necessary. Its contraction and anchor concentration, followed by
a union bound over inputs and layers, prove the claim. The contraction
preserves the hypotheses because $c_0\le c_1/100$ and $q$ is small:
the reduction of squared distance exceeds the possible relative change
of the anchor variance.

Finally, $F^{-1}(q)=q+2q^2+O(q^3)$ gives
$0\le\epsilon\le Cq$ under the anchor tolerance. The rank-one
Gaussian entropy formula gives per row
\[
 \frac12[\epsilon-\log(1+\epsilon)]\le\epsilon^2/4.
\]
Summing over rows and layers proves the cost bound. If a stopping rule
switches to the original prior on first failure, no later entropy is
incurred, so the same bound holds for the complete controlled law.
\end{proof}
