Target-relative critical research status — final round-three record
17 October 2026, 23:46 UTC

Main new posterior result:
For parity on k coordinates in d dimensions, fixed learner depth L>=2, put
M=min{k,d^(1/(L-1))}. For any fixed eta>0 and n<=M^(1-eta), as M grows,
with sample probability at least1-1/(2M), the width-first globally
margin-conditioned critical posterior has Gibbs error tending to1/2. Its
posterior correct-label probability is below2/3 on a fraction at least1/4
in the limit. Full parity gives n<=d^((1-eta)/(L-1)); if d>=k^(L-1), the
bound is n<=k^(1-eta). The full-parity conclusion also holds for depths
L=o(sqrt(log d/log log d)).

The proof is target-relative: an oriented majority of O(n log M) input
coordinates fits the sample with high probability and has lower critical
rate than every function retaining all target-parity coordinates. Every
remaining function has error exactly1/2, so tied minimizers cause no gap.
This is polynomial, not exponential in parity degree. Width is taken to
infinity separately at each fixed d,L,n. There is no uniform finite-width
threshold and no majority-prediction lower bound at threshold1/2.

Other audited target-relative results:
- At one hidden layer, every nonconstant antipodally even target has
  strictly higher rate than a constant. Exact projective symmetry gives
  the comparison for every parity degree at least2.
- At every fixed depth, the rate ratio to a constant diverges whenever
  augmented relevant-coordinate count grows. The explicit lower is
  (1/64)min{d^(1/(L-1)),(d k_aug)^(1/L)}, while constants have upper
  5000L d^(1/L). This is the source of the sample theorem above.

Absolute-rate depth dependence:
- All target rates tend to a common V_* in(1,2), with inverse-depth lower
  error and upper C[(d+1)^2+log^2(2L)]/L for L>=C(d+1).
- The target-seed version is C[S_f^2+log^2(2L)]/L for L>=C S_f, where
  S_f=1+log(f^T Q_1^-1 f). Constants/dictators have S_f=O(log d), and
  k-juntas have S_f<=14(k+1)log(e(d+1)/(k+1)).
- Uniformly over d,L, every target has rate at least a constant times
  Phi=1+L(d^(1/(2L))-1)^2; constants and dictators have rate at most a
  constant times Phi. Thus their bounded-rate depth is Theta(log^2 d),
  while their one-hidden-layer rate is Theta(sqrt d).
- A common correlated Gaussian transport coupling, not coordinatewise
  couplings, proves the relevance and ambient-dimension lower bounds.

Why absolute flattening does not settle learning:
A universal finite-width upper bounds the whole global-margin event by
2 exp[-N V_*+C N/(L-1)+C(L-1)log(2N)]. Disjointness rules out assigning
all2^(2^d) Boolean functions a lower mass exp[-N V_*-o(2^d)] in the useful
regime. Function-specific entropy corrections remain essential. An
arbitrarily narrow interval of rates can still encode a strong ordering.

Open research routes and rigorously identified failures:
- No general unrestricted polynomial-depth learning theorem or
  exponential-depth sample counterexample has been proved here.
- Near-optimal long-depth variational paths have unmodified fixed initial
  layers, but later cold controls can still create target directions.
  This is not a kernel-posterior theorem.
- At fixed positive-definite covariance, a local Fisher expansion is
  valid. Near singular Fourier directions, free tanh transitions can
  improve inverse-kernel target energy arbitrarily at zero entropy by
  creating higher Hermite modes. An entropy-only kernel-distance bound
  therefore fails.
- A free tanh step can create a two-input Boolean gate from constant and
  gate-input covariance components. This is a construction observation,
  not a general circuit-learning theorem.
- Exact gauge transport changes the singular first-layer affine support;
  no Loewner order follows because all diagonals agree. Nonconvexity in
  the reference covariance blocks naive whole-path symmetrization.
- The scalar distance multiplier does not control the family of full
  correlated feature laws. No Rademacher or compression theorem is
  inferred from it.

All final statements have independent mathematical agent audits. They
are not Lean-formalized.
