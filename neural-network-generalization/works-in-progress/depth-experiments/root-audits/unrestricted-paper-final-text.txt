Representation constraints and polynomial-depth learning
in Gaussian Bayesian networks
Dmitry Vaintrob
Kaarel Hänni
OpenAI Codex
Research draft — 18 October 2026
Abstract
We study Gaussian-prior tanh networks conditioned on a clean output margin throughout
the Boolean cube and on noiseless training signs. For every fixed ζ > 0 and exponent γ ≥1 + ζ,
a posterior trained on n examples at depth L ≥3 and sufficiently large polynomial width assigns
mass at least 1 −β to Boolean functions with same-depth unit-margin representatives whose
product of Frobenius norms is polynomial in d, n. A general polynomial sample theorem would
therefore require a corresponding approximation representation theorem.
Conversely, a target with depth D0 and product norm P has polynomial sample complexity,
simultaneously at every width above its actual bounded-weight network complexity, after only
O(log(d+D0+P)) additional learner layers. The original target width and coefficient precision do
not enter. We prove a norm-preserving width compression theorem and a further global-margin
normal form whose entire Boolean description is polynomial in P, independently of original
depth and width, apart from specifying at most P 2 relevant coordinate indices. At sufficiently
large depth and width, the minimum Gaussian energy above its depth baseline is comparable to
the logarithm of this intrinsic product complexity, together with the input-normalization cost.
The positive results include every Boolean halfspace, independently of its original geometric
margin and coefficient precision, at every learner depth L ≥2. For fan-in-two Boolean circuits
of size s and depth h, put S = max{16, 2 + d + s}. At every learner depth L ≥max{2, h}, we
obtain the upper bound SOζ(1+h/ log log S) times a polynomial in learner depth and statistical
parameters. We give all analytic and representation arguments needed for these results. A
polynomial sample bound for arbitrary small deep circuits, and the original critical prior γ = 1,
remain unresolved.

Corollary 5.3 (Two-state branching programs). Every Boolean function computed by a deterministic
layered width-two branching program of length T has A(f) ≤T +1. Consequently the sample bound in
Corollary 5.2 is polynomial in d, T, L, ε−1, log(2/δ) for this class. The program may query variables
repeatedly, and its two nodes in a layer may query different variables.
Proof. Encode the two states as −1, +1, and let sj(x) be the state after j transitions. Given the
old state +1 or −1, the next state is a Boolean function u+(x) or u−(x) of at most one input bit.

sj+1(x) = aj(x)sj(x) + bj(x),

The Fourier coefficient norm is subadditive and submultiplicative: the latter follows because

at most one, so submultiplicativity and the triangle inequality give the bound. These extensions
concern representation and sample bounds only; no efficient algorithm for identifying a program
from samples is asserted.

Constant-depth threshold circuits and a lower-bound barrier
The coefficient-independent halfspace construction also clarifies which explicit targets could disprove
the general unrestricted sample conjecture. Here a threshold circuit may have arbitrary real weights,
and its size counts wires, including input and constant wires.
Lemma 6.1 (Threshold circuits as bounded-weight tanh networks). There is a universal constant a
such that every depth-h, h ≥1, threshold circuit of wire size at most s on d Boolean inputs has a tanh
realization with linear readout, unit global margin, depth at most 2h, and both width and maximum
absolute physical weight bounded by (2 + d + s)a. It uses no biases after the augmented input. The
bound is independent of the original real coefficients and of every original gate’s geometric margin.
Proof. Replace each threshold gate by its depth-two polynomial-size, polynomial-integer-weight
simulation from Lemma 4.1. Each gate has at most s inputs. The total wire count, coefficient
bound, and maximum weighted fan-in of the resulting circuit are bounded by a universal polynomial
in d + s. Its depth is at most 2h. Subdivide skip wires by identity threshold gates to make the

Formula targets independently of their original depth
Formula balancing is classical, following Spira [3]. We include the elementary construction needed

Lemma 8.1 (Polynomial-size balancing of a Boolean formula). An AND/OR/NOT formula with s
gates and literal occurrences has an equivalent fan-in-two circuit of size polynomial in s + 2 and

Proof. Push negations to the leaves using De Morgan’s laws, eliminate double negations, and simplify
constants. This does not increase the number of literal occurrences. Binarize any larger-fan-in

formula is immediate. Otherwise let n denote its number of literal occurrences, counting repeated
occurrences separately.
For n ≥2 there is a subformula g with between n/3 and 2n/3 literal occurrences. To find one,
descend from the root while a child has more than 2n/3 leaves. At the last such node both children
have at most 2n/3 leaves, and at least one has at least n/3 leaves. Regard this one occurrence of g
as a formal Boolean input. After substituting either value b ∈{0, 1} and simplifying constants, the
remaining formula Fb has at most n −|g| ≤2n/3 literal occurrences. The Shannon identity is
F = (g ∧F1) ∨(¬g ∧F0).

occurrences. Hence the depth is O(log(n + 1)). There are at most three recursive subproblems per
call, so the size is polynomial; for example a bound O((n + 1)4) follows immediately by induction.

What remains unresolved
The necessary condition concerns functions up to approximation, not a particular implementation.
The depth-independent product normal form proves that ordinary Boolean description size is
polynomial in intrinsic product complexity. The converse polynomial comparison is not known,
and even such a comparison would retain the learner-depth qualification in our present sufficiency
theorem. The exact-cloning obstruction above therefore does not supply the missing function-level
lower bound. Conversely, the shallow and halfspace results do not identify shallow representation
size with arbitrary deep circuit size. A joint polynomial theorem for every small deep target, or an
explicit counterexample to it under this posterior, remains open.
The exponent γ = 1 lies outside the Gaussian-distance argument: its typical hidden Gaussian
bulk has order-one operator norm, so the uniform subtraction estimate used here does not become
small with width. This observation is a proof limitation, not a sample lower bound. Removing the
original all-input output-margin conditioning is also outside the present statements.
