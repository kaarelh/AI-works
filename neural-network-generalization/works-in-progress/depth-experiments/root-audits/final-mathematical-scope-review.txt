the test loss is signed output <=1/4. The clipped-score hypothesis is

every width and Gaussian scale. The resulting Gibbs-risk statement

verify the two conditions separately. The empirical-interpretation
section now states this distinction explicitly.

3. Reachable-state covariance refinement: with clean Jacobians J_s,
G_1=I and G_s=I+J_s G_{s-1} J_s^T exactly give the covariance of the
stacked clean linear hidden-noise response. Its norm bound and the
separately propagated Taylor-remainder bound may safely be added.
Using the full noise radius again for scalar output noise is conservative.
Interval matrix arithmetic and certified norm bounds are required for
an exact numerical claim; ordinary float64 versions remain only
numerical sufficient tests.

4. Critical lower bound: the last-exit decomposition uses prefix entropy
at the endpoint of each block, not an unjustified entropy rate per step.
Removing the first crossing step ensures that each remaining pre-state
lies above the scale used in the denominator. The scalar suffix

5. Critical depth obstruction: Gaussian transport is applied jointly on
the covariance range; the same coupling bounds all input differences.
The first-layer projected trace gives the ambient-dimension inequality.

depth. The target-dependent upper construction gives the matching scale

and neither direction supplies a posterior sample lower bound.

layer-energy AM-GM, whose product is d times the squared Frobenius
product. The infimum over representations need not be attained; the
upper argument explicitly chooses a representation within a factor two.
The positive learning statement retains its learner-depth threshold.
Ordinary small circuit size is not identified with product complexity.
