Polynomial depth from stability of clipped neural-network predictions
Dmitry Vaintrob
Kaarel Hänni
OpenAI Codex
18 October 2026
Abstract
We study Gaussian Bayesian learning of Boolean functions by fully connected tanh networks.
We restrict the prior using stability of the output clipped at the training margin, under simulta-
neous small internal perturbations. The condition permits arbitrarily small clean outputs on
unobserved inputs and ignores changes between sufficiently confident outputs of the same sign.
For every layered Boolean target circuit, the resulting posterior has a sample bound polynomial in
circuit width, input dimension, and network depth, simultaneously at every network width above
circuit width and at every Gaussian exponent γ ≥1, including the finite-variance mean-field scale.
The noise calibration is explicit, and its constant can be varied without changing the qualitative
theorem. We also prove a weaker margin-band version and a localized version allowing network-
dependent exceptional regions of controlled complexity. We give self-contained proofs and an
empirical assessment on networks trained by gradient methods, with selected exact-arithmetic
certificates. The evidence is mixed: exact certificates establish global stability for some fully
fitted image models and full-domain stability for two 64-hidden-layer Boolean classifiers. Another
fitted image model has a verified confident-input counterexample, although exact certificates
establish stability on all 10,000 of its official held-out images at the same radius. An exact
gradient-descent construction shows that on-distribution stability alone cannot imply the global
condition. The empirical calibration may decrease polynomially with depth without losing the
polynomial sample theorem. The empirical question is whether trained parameterizations satisfy
this specified support condition; it is distinct from a theorem about the generalization of gradient
descent or an experiment sampling the Bayesian posterior.
1
Introduction and scope
Small parameter perturbations, small internal activation perturbations, and stability of classification
are different properties. A network may have stable confident predictions despite changes in the
magnitude of a large output. Conversely, a network can fit its training set accurately and be highly
sensitive at inputs away from the data distribution. A useful prior restriction must make its input
domain, disturbance model, and output criterion explicit.
We use the output margin already present in the likelihood to define a bounded prediction score.
Training requires signed output greater than 1/2. We clip raw outputs at ±1/2, and require a
score change of at most 1/16 under a specified ball of simultaneous internal perturbations. The
test guarantee controls signed outputs at most 1/4. Thus stability is calibrated to the gap between
training and test margins. It does not forbid every sign change: moving from 0.02 to −0.02 changes
the score by only 0.04. The statistical proof shows that weak or incorrect test outputs receive little
posterior mass after sufficiently many examples.
The main theorem concerns a particular restricted Gaussian prior and an exact margin-
conditioning likelihood.
Its comparator complexity is the width of a layered Boolean circuit.
It does not assume that every small analog neural circuit has a robust implementation of the same

width and depth. Nor does it assert that ordinary gradient descent samples this posterior. These
distinctions matter when assessing the empirical evidence.
Compression through noise stability and all-layer margins has precedents in work of Arora et
al. and Wei and Ma [1, 2]. Wei, Chen, and Ma [4] prove polynomial circuit-size sample bounds
for overparameterized feedforward networks using a suitable all-layer-margin surrogate and robust
circuit simulation. Thus the general idea that robust simulation can avoid exponential depth factors
predates this paper. Here the object being analyzed is the posterior of a specified Gaussian prior,
including its target probability and uniformity over widths and finite Gaussian scales. Our proof
uses Gaussian entropy and transport to construct one finite candidate class shared by all widths
and scales. We include that construction and the Gaussian target-mass bound, so the result can be
read independently. The polynomial constants are deliberately loose and do not provide practical
sample estimates. The mathematical arguments have been reviewed independently by AI agents;
they are not Lean formalizations.
Published experiments support internal noise stability as a research direction, with different
quantifiers from our support condition.
Arora et al. report attenuation of injected Gaussian
activation noise in a VGG-19 trained on CIFAR-10 and measure their stability quantities on training
inputs [1]. Yu and Tomasi report improved noise stability in residual networks relative to matched
plain networks [5]. Neither establishes stability at every input under every simultaneous disturbance
in our calibrated ball. The experiments below test that particular condition directly in the tanh
architecture of the theorem. They do not establish its prevalence in modern convolutional networks
or transformers.

The learning theorem
A width-C, depth-H layered Boolean circuit has at most C Boolean wires at each of its H stages.
We use NAND gates, copies, and negations; constant wires required by internal gate implementations
are carried through the circuit and count toward C. This is a universal Boolean basis. Compiling
another fixed basis into this one may change the width and depth, and those resources must be
counted. Its output is one final wire. The circuit’s logical depth must fit the H hidden layers; idle
stages can be filled with copies.

Variants of the prediction-stability condition
6.1
The tolerance is determined by the margin gap
The constants in the main condition arise from the gap between the training and test margins. They
need not be treated as separate modeling choices.
Corollary 6.1 (General training and test margins). Fix m > 0 and 0 ≤u < m, and put ∆= m−u.

also in m and ∆−1.

6.2
Disturbances confined to valid activation states
One may restrict the support condition to disturbances whose complete noisy hidden trajectory lies
in [−1, 1]N at every layer. This permits perturbations only to values within the range of the tanh
activation. The same main theorem holds. Indeed, before grid rounding, every successful virtual
hidden state in the constructive entropy compressor is a tanh output. Thus the disturbances used
in its prediction comparison satisfy this extra state restriction. The separate output injection and
subsequent grid-rounding error are treated exactly as before. The original target cylinder was stable
even without the state restriction and therefore retains its probability lower bound.
This condition is weaker than unrestricted additive-disturbance stability. A negative empirical
attack for this variant must verify the state constraint at every intermediate layer; an attack outside
the activation range does not refute it. The primary empirical condition can remain the unrestricted
one, with this variant stated separately.

6.4
Amplification is not evidence about unmodified trained weights
Every finite-cube network with no zero clean outputs eventually satisfies the calibrated clipped
condition after a sufficiently large positive readout amplification. To verify this, continuity and
finiteness of the input cube give a hidden-disturbance radius ρ > 0 and b > 0 such that the
unamplified noisy output has its clean sign and magnitude at least b throughout that radius. Scaling
the readout by A makes the calibrated radius tend to zero while the noisy signed output is at least
Ab −rc(w(A)). Eventually this exceeds 1/2, so the clean and noisy clipped scores agree exactly. The
readout energy simultaneously grows by A2. The scale needed may be enormous; this observation
gives no polynomial target-mass bound for arbitrary neural representations and cannot justify
reporting rescaled networks as evidence about ordinary training.

The score measures signed excess beyond the test-error threshold. For example, changing an
output from 0.1 to −0.1 leaves both transformed scores zero. Both outputs already fail the 1/4
margin criterion for either label; forbidding this fluctuation is unnecessary for the proof. In contrast,
movement from an unconfident output into the training-confidence region remains controlled.
Training-margin satisfaction and posterior probability of correct test answers retain their original
meanings; the transform only defines the prior’s stability condition.

What empirical evidence can establish
The prior’s support condition quantifies over every Boolean input and every allowed simultaneous
disturbance. A finite-noise certificate at a held-out image proves stability at that image only. A
successful adversarial search provides a counterexample when both its disturbance budget and its
score change are verified. Failure to find an attack proves neither stability nor membership in the
prior. Floating-point sufficient tests are reported separately from interval or exact-arithmetic checks.
There are two distinct empirical eligibility questions. First, does a trained network satisfy the
stability restriction? Second, does it satisfy the likelihood, which requires every signed training
17

output to exceed 1/2? A stable network that has training mistakes is evidence about the condition’s
occurrence under training, but is not a draw admissible under the noiseless posterior for that training
set. Conversely, successful optimization alone provides no stability guarantee. All declared runs,
including optimization failures, are retained.
The original all-input clean-margin convention imposes a further requirement beyond training
eligibility. The image counterexamples near zero output already fail that clean-margin requirement
at their witness input. They refute automatic global stability of the trained parameterization; they
are not examples already inside that stronger base support. In contrast, selected exhaustive Boolean-
domain positives verify absolute clean margins above 1/2 everywhere, as well as all signed training
margins and internal stability. The separate readout rescaling result explains how a finite-domain
parameterization can enter both supports, but charges the resulting Gaussian energy cost.

Boolean input, or if a separate valid discrete input is found and verified. Likewise, an interpolated
image need not lie on the exact eight-bit grid.
Interpreting calibration.
The factor √1 + HEv is the worst-case noise gain for hidden layers
that are individually nonexpansive in Euclidean norm. Hence every such network satisfies the score
condition when c ≤1/16. Smaller c allows greater amplification relative to this reference. A positive
result only at a small radius should not be described as stability to arbitrary percent-level changes
of every activation. Both the normalized total budget and the readout calibration are part of the
claim.
Global failures motivate studying fixed trusted input regions, chosen independently of the labeled
sample, on which the restriction is imposed. If such a region has input-distribution mass at least
1 −ρ, the statistical proof adds at most ρ to the test error. Proving stability throughout the region
remains necessary; merely observing no failures on a finite set of images does not supply that proof.

Experiments on image classifiers
We tested the support condition on raw parameterizations produced by minibatch optimization.
These experiments do not sample from the Bayesian prior or posterior. In particular, a trained
18

network’s test accuracy does not verify the posterior generalization theorem.
Tasks and optimization.
The data are the official MNIST and Fashion-MNIST training and test
sets. The two-class tasks use all MNIST digits 3 and 8, and all Fashion-MNIST classes 0 and 6. A
harder task uses all 60,000 MNIST training images and 10,000 test images with an even-versus-odd
label. The main architecture is a plain tanh network with width 128, 256, or 512, hidden depth 4,
16, or 32, a first-layer bias, and a scalar linear readout. There are no later hidden biases and no
residual connections. Training uses logistic loss and actual shuffled minibatches of size 128. Vanilla
SGD and Adam are distinguished; a small exploratory ReLU cohort uses momentum SGD and lies
outside the tanh theorem.
Every cohort has a timestamped protocol fixed before that cohort’s training. Later cohorts were
motivated by earlier observations, so the whole research sequence is exploratory. All runs, including
failed fits and collapsed classifiers, are retained. We use the final prescribed checkpoint and never
rescale its readout. Unless separately declared, training lasts 60 epochs. The two depth-32 MNIST
SGD seeds also have separately recorded continuations for 180 further epochs, at a fixed lower
learning rate; both original models remain in the data. Four later SGD runs probe depths 64 and
128 at width 128, with fixed orthogonal initialization gains 1 and 1.1. All four also receive separately
declared 180-epoch lower-rate continuations; the original fixed-budget runs remain in the record.
Continuous pixels are mapped to [−1, 1] in float32; Boolean pixels use threshold 128. Final
metrics and ordinary numerical audits cast those same input values and saved weights to float64.
Thus auditing does not change the represented input. These are small fully connected image models,
rather than modern convolutional networks or transformers.
Eligibility and calibration.
For every model we separately record whether all training signed
outputs exceed 1/2. High accuracy alone does not meet this likelihood condition. The primary
score is clipped at ±1/2, the allowed score change is 1/16, and the radius constants are
c ∈{0.001, 0.003, 0.01, 0.03, 0.0625}.
The primary constant is 0.001; the rest are a fixed calibration study. The cap 1 is an additional
recorded comparison. Neither radius nor readout scale is adjusted to make an individual model
pass.
The final collection contains 70 parameterizations: 60 tanh image models, four ReLU comparisons,
and six Boolean-domain models. Every training margin exceeds 1/2 for 13, three, and six of these,
respectively, for a total of 22. These counts include every prescribed failed fit and every separately
retained continuation.
