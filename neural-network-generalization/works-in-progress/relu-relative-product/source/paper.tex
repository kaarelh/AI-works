\begin{abstract}
For bias-free ReLU layers following an augmented Boolean input, every
nonconstant classifier with global unit margin has Gaussian weighted energy
at least $2L+\log d-2\log2$.  A stronger finite-depth bound is attained
exactly by a dictator at $d=1$.  We also prove a function-level
compression theorem.  If a representation with $H$ hidden layers has
Frobenius product $P$ and
$\beta=\log(dP^2/2^H)$, the same classifier admits a ReLU representation
of depth $O(1+\beta)$ and product $\exp(O(1+\beta))$, independently of
its original depth and widths. The proof combines a simultaneous
separation bound for all opposite-labelled hidden pairs with a
constant-branching cover and an explicit nearest-prototype classifier.
An Occam argument gives a cold-prior sample bound polynomial in the
target's relative product, independently of learner width and depth.
The relative product can be exponential in target depth, so this does
not prove a joint polynomial-depth learning theorem or an exponential
learning lower bound. No corresponding result for tanh is asserted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and statements}
Let $d\ge1$ and $\bar x=(1,x)\in\R^{d+1}$ for
$x\in\{-1,1\}^d$.  A network with $H\ge1$ hidden layers is
\[
 h_0(x)=\bar x,\qquad h_s(x)=\relu(W_s h_{s-1}(x))\ (1\le s\le H),
 \qquad z(x)=v^T h_H(x).
\]
All hidden widths are finite and unrestricted.  There are no separately
supplied biases after the augmented input.  Set $L=H+1$ and
\[
 P=\|v\|_2\prod_{s=1}^H\|W_s\|_\F.
\]
A Boolean function $f$ has global unit margin when $f(x)z(x)\ge1$ for every
cube input.  At common width $N$, the Gaussian weighted energy is
\[
 \kappa=\frac dN\|W_1\|_\F^2+
       \sum_{s=2}^H\|W_s\|_\F^2+N\|v\|_2^2.
\]
The product of these $L$ weighted layer energies is exactly $dP^2$.
All logarithms are natural unless a base is displayed.

\begin{theorem}[Baseline and sharp one-variable example]\label{thm:baseline}
If $f$ is nonconstant and globally unit-margined, then
\[
 P^2\ge (1+d^{-1/H})^H,\qquad
 dP^2\ge(1+d^{1/H})^H\ge2^H\sqrt d.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \kappa\ge L(1+d^{1/H})^{H/L}
 \ge L(2^H\sqrt d)^{1/L}
 \ge2L+\log d-2\log2.
\]
For $d=1$ and $f(x)=x$, the first energy bound is attained at every
common width $N\ge2$.  In particular its exact value is $L2^{H/L}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Compression above the baseline]\label{thm:compression}
Suppose a nonconstant $f$ has a globally unit-margined representation with
$H$ hidden layers and product $P$.  Put
\[
 \beta=\log\frac{dP^2}{2^H},\qquad
 G=1+\beta.
\]
There is a universal constant $C$ such that $f$ has another globally
unit-margined ReLU representation with at most $CG$ hidden layers and
Frobenius product at most $\exp(CG)$.  There is no dependence on the
original widths, individual coefficients, precision, or robustness.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Energy form]\label{cor:energy}
If $\kappa\le2L+B$ with $B\ge0$, then the conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} holds with
\[
 G\le C(1+B).
\]
In particular the new depth is $O(B+1)$ and its product is
$\exp(O(B+1))$, independently of the original depth.  This is a statement about an existing
low-energy representation; it is not a learning theorem.
\end{corollary}

\section{Two-anchor linear replacement}
The Boolean cube graph is connected, so a nonconstant $f$ has a
bichromatic edge.  Orient it as $x_+,x_-$ with labels $+1,-1$.  Write
\[
 X=[\bar x_+,\bar x_-],\qquad
 a_s=h_s(x_+),\quad b_s=h_s(x_-),\quad
 G_s=\begin{pmatrix}\|a_s\|^2&a_s^Tb_s\\a_s^Tb_s&\|b_s\|^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The hidden anchors are nonnegative.  Neither vanishes, and they cannot be
positively proportional: every subsequent layer and the readout preserve
positive proportionality, which would contradict their opposite output
signs.  Thus every $G_s$ is positive definite with nonnegative off-diagonal.
The augmented input Gram has eigenvalues $2d,2$, so
\[
 G_0=X^TX=\begin{pmatrix}d+1&d-1\\d-1&d+1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det G_0=4d.
\]
Its off-diagonal is also nonnegative.

\begin{lemma}[Linear replacement with a squared-error bound]\label{lem:replacement}
Let two linearly independent anchors have Gram
$G=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&C\\C&B\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $C\ge0$.
For any row $w$, there is a minimum-norm linear row $b$ that maps these
anchors to their two ReLU outputs, and
\[
 \|w-b\|^2\le\|w\|^2-\|b\|^2.
\]
Consequently, if $W$ maps the anchors through ReLU to output Gram $G'$, its
least-Frobenius linear replacement $B_*$ satisfies
\[
 \|W-B_*\|_\F^2\le\|W\|_\F^2-\|B_*\|_\F^2,
 \qquad \|B_*\|_\F^2=\tr(G'G^{-1}).
\]
The two nonzero squared singular values of $B_*$ are the eigenvalues of
$G^{-1/2}G'G^{-1/2}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If both outputs are positive, project $w$ onto the anchor span.  If both
are zero, use $b=0$.  In the remaining case, interchange anchors if needed
so that the outputs are $p>0,0$ and the original preactivations are $p,t$
with $t\le0$.  The least-norm replacement has evaluations $(p,0)$.
The difference has evaluations $(0,t)$ plus an orthogonal component, hence
\[
 \langle b,w-b\rangle=-Cpt/\det G\ge0.
\]
Expansion of $\|w\|^2$ proves the claimed inequality.  Apply this rowwise.
The matrix and singular-value formulas follow from the minimum-norm map
$Y X^+$ between two full-rank anchor-column matrices $X,Y$.
\end{proof}

It follows that for every hidden layer, including the first,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gramcost}
 \|W_s\|_\F^2\ge\tr(G_sG_{s-1}^{-1})
                \ge2\sqrt{\det G_s/\det G_{s-1}}.
\end{equation}
The least-norm readout with evaluations $y_+\ge1,y_-\le-1$ costs
$y^TG_H^{-1}y$.  Nonnegativity of the off-diagonal gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:readout}
 \|v\|^2\ge\frac{\|a_H\|^2+\|b_H\|^2+2a_H^Tb_H}{\det G_H}
             \ge\frac2{\sqrt{\det G_H}}.
\end{equation}
Multiplying \eqref{eq:gramcost} and \eqref{eq:readout} already proves
$P^2\ge2^H/\sqrt d$.

\section{The stronger baseline and its sharpness}
For completeness we prove the stronger product inequality in
Theorem~\ref{thm:baseline}.  Replace all hidden matrices by the maps
$B_1,\ldots,B_H$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:replacement}, and let
$B=B_H\cdots B_1$ and $V=\|v\|$.  The product has rank two, transports
$X$ exactly to $[a_H,b_H]$, and vanishes off the input anchor span.
Let $\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2>0$ be the two singular values of $VB$.
The input-edge difference has norm two, while the output difference is at
least two.  Therefore $\sigma_1\ge1$.  Also \eqref{eq:readout} gives
\[
 \sigma_1\sigma_2
   =V^2\sqrt{\frac{\det G_H}{\det G_0}}\ge d^{-1/2}.
\]
The rank-two form of Schatten H\"older gives
\[
 P\ge V\prod_s\|B_s\|_\F\ge\|VB\|_{S_{2/H}},
\]
including when $2/H<1$. Here is a direct proof of the inequality needed.
Let $a_s\ge b_s>0$ be the singular values of the restriction of $B_s$
between its two anchor planes, and let $u_1\ge u_2>0$ be those of $B$.
Area multiplication gives $u_1u_2=\prod_s a_sb_s$, while operator-norm
submultiplicativity gives
\[
 \tau:=\log(u_1/u_2)\le\sum_s\log(a_s/b_s).
\]
Put $t_s=\log(a_s/b_s)\ge0$. Convexity of $\log\cosh$ and monotonicity
of $\cosh$ on the positive half-line imply
\[
 \prod_s\|B_s\|_\F^2
 =2^H\Bigl(\prod_s a_sb_s\Bigr)\prod_s\cosh t_s
 \ge2^Hu_1u_2\cosh(\tau/H)^H
 =\bigl(u_1^{2/H}+u_2^{2/H}\bigr)^H.
\]
Multiplication by $V$ proves the claimed inequality for $VB$ without
requiring a separate quasi-norm theorem. Thus
\[
 P^{2/H}\ge\sigma_1^{2/H}+\sigma_2^{2/H}
  \ge\min_{t\ge1}\{t^{2/H}+d^{-1/H}t^{-2/H}\}
  =1+d^{-1/H}.
\]
The last function is nondecreasing on $[1,\infty)$.  This proves the
product bound.  Weighted-layer AM--GM proves the first energy bound.
Finally
\[
 L(2^H\sqrt d)^{1/L}
 =2L\exp\left(\frac{\tfrac12\log d-\log2}{L}\right)
 \ge2L+\log d-2\log2.
\]

For $d=1$, use the first-layer indicator rails
\[
 \relu((1+x)/2),\qquad\relu((1-x)/2),
\]
then the two-dimensional identity in every internal layer and readout
$(1,-1)$.  The first squared Frobenius norm is one, every internal squared
norm is two, and the readout squared norm is two, giving $P^2=2^H$.
Pad by zero neurons to any width $N\ge2$.  If $e_1,\ldots,e_L$ are the
weighted energies and $g=2^{H/L}$, multiply each corresponding weight block
by $\sqrt{g/e_s}$.  Their product is one.  Positive homogeneity preserves
the output and makes every weighted energy equal to $g$, attaining $Lg$.
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:baseline}.

\begin{remark}
For margin $a>0$, multiply the product-squared lower bounds by $a^2$ and
the energy bounds by $a^{2/L}$.  Constants have a different baseline:
a single positive rail attains $\kappa=Ld^{1/L}$.  Its optimality follows
from $P\ge1$ for any globally unit-margined function: the input second
moment is the identity, and successive zero-preserving contractions imply
$\mathbb E z(x)^2\le P^2$.
\end{remark}

\section{All opposite-labelled hidden pairs remain separated}
The margin inequalities yield more than a bound for one selected edge.
Fix \emph{any} pair of opposite-labelled cube inputs and let $G_s$ be its
hidden Gram.  Both states are nonzero, nonnegative, and nonproportional
at every stage.  Lemma~\ref{lem:replacement} therefore applies throughout
its suffix.  With
\[
 S_j=\prod_{s\le j}\|W_s\|_\F,\qquad
 T_j=\|v\|\prod_{s=j+1}^H\|W_s\|_\F,
\]
for $1\le j\le H$ we have $S_jT_j=P$ and
\[
 T_j^2\ge
 \frac{2}{\sqrt{\det G_H}}
 \prod_{s=j+1}^H2\sqrt{\frac{\det G_s}{\det G_{s-1}}}
 =\frac{2^{H-j+1}}{\sqrt{\det G_j}}.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:area}
 \sqrt{\det G_j}\ge2^{1-j}dS_j^2e^{-\beta}.
\end{equation}
Each hidden state has norm at most $RS_j$, where $R=\sqrt{d+1}$.
The parallelogram area of two vectors is at most the first vector's norm
times their distance.  Dividing \eqref{eq:area} by $RS_j$ proves the
simultaneous normalized separation bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separation}
 \left\|\frac{h_j(x)}{S_j}-\frac{h_j(y)}{S_j}\right\|
 \ge\Delta_j:=\frac{2d}{R}\,2^{-j}e^{-\beta}
 \qquad\text{whenever }f(x)\ne f(y).
\end{equation}
There is no original-depth factor in this estimate.  It bounds precisely
those pairs that require opposite final signs, rather than an unrestricted
suffix Lipschitz constant.  The baseline also gives
$\beta\ge\tfrac12\log d\ge0$.

\section{A cover and an explicit ReLU classifier}
\begin{lemma}[A universal radius-quartering cover]\label{lem:cover}
For $K=17^{64}$, every finite matrix $W$ maps any radius-$r$ ball into at
most $K$ balls of radius $r\|W\|_\F/4$.  The same holds after ReLU.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Normalize $\|W\|_\F=1$.  Retain its largest sixty-four singular directions;
the tail has operator norm at most $1/8$. Cover the unit ball in the
retained space by a $1/8$-net of size at most $17^{64}$. Orthogonality of
the top and tail errors bounds the total radius by $\sqrt2/8<1/4$.
Translation and scaling handle arbitrary balls, and ReLU is one-Lipschitz.
\end{proof}

Set
\[
 j_0=\left\lceil\log_2(64e^\beta)\right\rceil.
\]
If $H<j_0$, the original network already has the depth and product required
by Theorem~\ref{thm:compression}, since
$\log P=(H\log2+\beta-\log d)/2$.  Assume $H\ge j=j_0$.
Repeated application of Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} covers the normalized states
$h_j/S_j$ by at most $K^j$ balls of radius $R4^{-j}$.
The simultaneous separation \eqref{eq:separation} makes every such ball
monochromatic. Replace each nonempty ball center by an actual prefix
image and retain its label. There are $q\le K^j$ prototypes, each of norm
at most $R$, and the covering radius doubles. Since
\[
 \frac{2R4^{-j}}{\Delta_j}
 =\frac{R^2}{d}e^\beta2^{-j}\le\frac1{32},
\]
every query has a correct-label prototype within $\Delta_j/4$, while every
opposite-label prototype is at distance at least $\Delta_j$. Write
$\Delta=\Delta_j$ below.
Let $\mathcal P_+,\mathcal P_-$ denote the two nonempty prototype classes.
Define
\[
 s_a(u)=2\langle a,u\rangle-\|a\|^2,\quad g=\Delta^2/2,
 \quad t_{ab}(u)=\frac{s_a(u)-s_b(u)}g,
\]
for $a\in\mathcal P_+$ and $b\in\mathcal P_-$.  Compute
\begin{align*}
 C_{ab}(u)&=\frac{\relu(t_{ab}(u)+1)-\relu(t_{ab}(u)-1)}2,\\
 A_a(u)&=\relu\left(\sum_{b\in\mathcal P_-}C_{ab}(u)
                                      -|\mathcal P_-|+1\right),\\
 z_*(u)&=2\sum_{a\in\mathcal P_+}A_a(u)-1.
\end{align*}
These formulas use two ReLU hidden layers and one linear readout.
Always $C_{ab},A_a\in[0,1]$.  On a positive query a close positive
prototype beats every negative score by at least
$\Delta^2-\Delta^2/16>g$, so its $A_a$ equals one and $z_*\ge1$.
On a negative query, one close negative prototype beats every positive
score by the same amount; every $A_a$ vanishes and $z_*=-1$.
The comparison cancels the squared query norm, which is why the suffix
cost does not depend on the original feature dimension.

Positive homogeneity computes the normalized prefix using
$W_s/\|W_s\|_\F$.  Carry a constant rail of value one through it, starting
from the augmented input.  Every augmented prefix matrix has Frobenius
norm $\sqrt2$, so its product is $2^{j/2}$.  The comparison, $A$, and
readout norms are respectively at most
\[
 Cq(1+R^2/g),\qquad Cq^{3/2},\qquad C\sqrt q,
\]
including constant-rail rows.  Since
$R^2/g=R^4 4^je^{2\beta}/(2d^2)\le2\cdot4^je^{2\beta}$ and
$q\le K^j$, the new product is bounded by
\[
 2^{j/2}Cq^3(1+R^2/g)\le\exp(C(j+\beta+1))=\exp(CG).
\]
Its hidden depth is $j+2=O(G)$.  This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:compression}.

Finally, if $\kappa\le2L+B$, weighted-layer AM--GM gives
\[
 \frac{dP^2}{2^H}\le2\left(1+\frac B{2L}\right)^L\le2e^{B/2}.
\]
Thus $\beta\le\log2+B/2$, proving Corollary~\ref{cor:energy}.

\section{A strong-prior Occam consequence}
We make the statistical consequence explicit. The result in this section
concerns the cold prior limit at each fixed finite learner width. It is
not a uniform polynomial-width theorem at a fixed exponent gap.

\begin{lemma}[Finite classification capacity]\label{lem:capacity}
For ReLU networks of hidden depth at most $h$, Frobenius product at most
$Q\ge1$, and global unit margin on the $d$-cube, the number of distinct
Boolean functions is at most
\[
 \exp\bigl(\operatorname{poly}(d,h,Q)\bigr),
\]
for one universal polynomial, independently of the original widths.
Consequently the functions with any-depth relative product
$dP^2/2^H\le T$, together with constants, form a class $\mathcal F_T$ with
$\log|\mathcal F_T|\le\operatorname{poly}(d,\max(1,T))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We give the finite-width step to clarify the absence of a width parameter.
For consecutive matrices $W,V$, let $a_i$ be the norm of row $i$ of $W$,
$b_i$ the norm of column $i$ of $V$, and
$A=\sum_i a_i b_i\le\|W\|_\F\|V\|_\F$.
Ignore zero terms. Sample $m$ indices with probabilities $a_i b_i/A$.
Use incoming row $w_i/a_i$ and outgoing column $Av_i/(mb_i)$ for each
sampled neuron. Positive homogeneity makes the new preactivation at the
next layer an unbiased approximation to the original. Its two-layer
Frobenius product is exactly $A$, for every draw.

If the earlier prefix product is $S$, each sampled vector has norm at
most $AS\sqrt{d+1}$. The suffix is bounded by its Frobenius product.
Hilbert-space bounded-difference concentration and a union bound over the
$2^d$ inputs therefore give final-output error at most $\varepsilon/h$
with
$m=\operatorname{poly}(d,h,Q,\varepsilon^{-1})$ samples.
Process consecutive pairs from the first hidden layer through the last,
using the readout as the final outgoing matrix. The product never
increases, all retained widths are polynomial, and the total error is at
most $\varepsilon$. Take $\varepsilon=1/4$ and double the final readout.

Positive homogeneous rescaling now makes every hidden matrix have
Frobenius norm one, while the readout norm is at most $2Q$.
All hidden states have norm at most $\sqrt{d+1}$. Round the polynomially
many matrix entries on an inverse-polynomial mesh. The usual layerwise
one-Lipschitz recurrence gives uniform output error less than $1/4$.
There are only $\exp(\operatorname{poly}(d,h,Q))$ resulting finite
parameter descriptions, and each original function is represented by one
of them. Theorem~\ref{thm:compression} gives the second assertion.
\end{proof}

Fix a target $f$ with a unit-margined depth-$D\ge2$, width-$C$ ReLU
representation of product $P_*$, and put
\[
 T_* =\max\{1,dP_*^2/2^{D-1}\}.
\]
The learner uses depth $L\ge D$ and common width $N\ge\max(C,2)$.
In weighted energy coordinates its raw prior has density proportional to
$\exp(-\kappa/(2\sigma^2))$. Its posterior conditions on strict absolute
margin $|z(x)|>1/2$ at every cube input and agreement with the noiseless
sample labels. The input distribution below is arbitrary.

\begin{theorem}[Width- and learner-depth-independent cold sample bound]
\label{thm:occam}
There is a universal polynomial $p$ such that, for all
$\alpha,\delta\in(0,1)$, the sample bound
\[
 n\ge\frac6\alpha\left[p(d,T_*)+\log\frac1\delta\right]
\]
has the following property. For each fixed admissible $L,N$, and all
sufficiently small positive $\sigma$ (with a threshold allowed to depend
on $L,N,\alpha$ and the target), with sample probability at least $1-\delta$
the posterior assigns the correct label probability strictly greater than
$2/3$ on an input-distribution fraction at least $1-\alpha$.
The displayed sample bound is independent of the learner width and depth.
Its dependence on the target product may be exponential in target depth.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First produce an exact target witness at the learner architecture.
When $L>D$, replace the scalar readout by the two rails
$\relu(z),\relu(-z)$ and final readout $(1,-1)$, and insert two-rail
identity layers as needed. The initial split multiplies the product by
two and adds one weighted layer. Every subsequent identity layer
multiplies its square by two. Thus the padded target product $P_0$
satisfies
\[
 T_0:=dP_0^2/2^{L-1}\le2T_*.
\]
For $L=D$ use the target directly. Pad hidden layers to a common base
width $C_0\le\max(C,2)$. At any $N\ge C_0$, clone every base neuron
$k=\lfloor N/C_0\rfloor$ times and pad the remaining coordinates by zero.
The first Frobenius norm gains $\sqrt k$, internal norms are unchanged,
and the readout norm gains $1/\sqrt k$. Hence the function and its product
are preserved exactly, including at widths that are not multiples of
$C_0$.

Balance the $L$ weighted layer energies by positive homogeneous scaling.
The exact target witness then has energy
\[
 K=L(dP_0^2)^{1/L}=L g,\qquad g\ge1.
\]
Here $g\ge1$ follows from $P_0\ge1$, using the input second moment and
global margin. The witness has strict conditioning margin. A fixed
neighborhood therefore lies in every sample-conditioning event and has
energy at most $K+1/4$. Finite-dimensional Gaussian Laplace comparison
shows, uniformly over all samples,
\[
 Q_{S,\sigma}\{\kappa>K+1\}\longrightarrow0
                  \quad(\sigma\downarrow0).
\]
The denominator can use the same target neighborhood for every sample.
No limit uniform in $L,N$ is asserted.

For a posterior network of energy at most $K+1$, AM--GM gives
\[
 dP^2\le((K+1)/L)^L\le e^{1/g}dP_0^2\le e\,dP_0^2.
\]
Its margin is greater than $1/2$. Doubling its readout gives a unit-margin
representation with relative product at most $4eT_0\le8eT_*$.
Constant classifiers are harmless separately. Lemma~\ref{lem:capacity}
therefore puts all these posterior functions in one class $\mathcal F$
with $\log|\mathcal F|\le p(d,T_*)$, independently of $L,N$.

A union bound over $\mathcal F$ shows that with sample probability at
least $1-\delta$, every sample-consistent member has population error at
most $\alpha/6$. Choose the cold threshold so the posterior mass outside
this class is at most $\alpha/12$. Its Gibbs error is then at most
$\alpha/4$. If the posterior correct-label probability is at most $2/3$
at an input, its error probability there is at least $1/3$.
Markov's inequality bounds the fraction of such inputs by
$3\alpha/4<\alpha$, proving the stated strict-confidence conclusion.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and further consequences}
The structural theorem concerns the represented Boolean function, rather
than the stability of a particular parameterization. It permits arbitrary
original widths and arbitrarily small hidden amplitudes.
The product-to-parallel-circuit theorem in the companion
paper~\cite{parallel} also gives threshold circuits of size $\exp(O(\beta+1))$ and depth
$O(\beta+1)$, and Boolean circuits of the same size with depth
$O((\beta+1)^2)$. Its proof is separate from the arguments above.

The cold sample theorem does not imply a polynomial sample bound in
arbitrary target depth: $T_*$ itself can be exponential in that depth.
An extension at prescribed finite Gaussian scales requires a separate
posterior comparison; it is not proved here. The cold threshold in
Theorem~\ref{thm:occam} is allowed to depend on the fixed architecture.

No natural-activation exponential sample lower bound is proved here.
No assertion about the original unrestricted tanh learning problem
follows from these ReLU results.
