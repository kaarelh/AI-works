1        Introduction
A regression target need not be exactly represented by a small neural network for a complexity-based
learning guarantee to be useful. The relevant question is whether a single Bayesian learner can
compete with the best narrow approximation, even when its own architecture is much wider and
the approximating network has unrestricted coefficient magnitudes.
    The main finite-variance result is Corollary 3.2. Its comparison functions are ordinary real-
valued shallow ReLU networks, with no coefficient or output bound. The learner uses output range

Theorem 7.1 gives the additive C 2 + H resource for shallow teachers at every realizable learner

comparison-width class. The formula-network result in Section 9 is polynomial jointly in teacher
width and depth, at unchanged width; its no-fanout condition is a condition on the comparison

    All these primary results concern the ordinary mean of one final posterior trained on all
observations, with a deterministic likelihood choice. Their sharp coefficient-one conclusion is in
expectation over the sample. Theorem 11.1 provides a different, high-probability guarantee at
general teacher depth: select one variance using independent validation observations and return the
posterior trained on the other half. It does not imply the same bound after an all-data refit.
    Section 12 also supplies an all-data expectation theorem at every realizable width for arbitrary
fixed teacher depth. It uses a finite-parameter argument at smaller learner widths and may prescribe
an extremely small observation variance there. This completes an existence statement, while leaving
open a comparably useful fixed-likelihood theorem at those widths.
    The architecture is fixed before data in every case. There is no prior over network sizes, and no
claim of efficient posterior computation. The prior has full support and finite second moments; its
non-Gaussian shape supplies favorable approximation cost without a support restriction to robust
or sparse networks. General coefficient-free compilation can still be expensive in teacher depth,
although additional learner depth has only an additive polynomial cost. On continuous domains,
input regularity has a concrete role: Proposition 10.1 gives a width-one target on two sufficiently
close inputs for which every realized sample leaves constant posterior-mean error. Thus a uniform
coefficient-free width-only rate cannot hold over all input distributions on a continuous domain.

Related work. Dense independent heavy-tailed Bayesian neural-network priors are established
tools. Castillo and Egels [8] prove posterior and variational contraction for deterministic dense
architectures and independent heavy-tailed weights, including Cauchy laws. Their results concern
prescribed architecture/scaling regimes and structured smoothness classes. Nguyen, Westerhout and
Arbel [9] study sample-size-independent shallow ReLU priors with random finite width. Lévy random
expansions also have a substantial statistical history [10]. Our target is the every-prescribed-width
agnostic comparison with arbitrary-coefficient neural-network approximants, and the coefficient-free
compilers needed to obtain it. Ordinary and fractional Gaussian posteriors differ here only by their
observation variance; that terminology is not a novelty claim. The sharp expectation argument
adapts the leave-one-out exponential-weights method of Høgsgaard, Rebeschini and Wegel [11]; the
arbitrary-prior and conditional sub-Gaussian extension is proved in Appendix C.
   Coefficient reduction itself is also established. Maass [6] proves polynomial-bit, same-size
representations for fan-out-one networks with Boolean outputs on finite rational input sets. Vardi
and Shamir [7] obtain polynomial-weight approximations of arbitrary-weight constant-depth ReLU
networks with polynomial width and a constant-factor depth increase under discrete and regular
continuous input assumptions. Brand, Ganian and Rocton [5] reduce weights on edges leaving
hidden neurons of out-degree one to signs, and use the resulting linear structure for exact training.
We use related reductions for real-valued comparisons, track their prior costs, and combine them
with the Bayesian risk inequalities proved here.

5    A quadratic-width oracle for shallow Boolean-input teachers
A one-hidden-layer ReLU teacher admits a stronger coefficient reduction. Its bounded function on
the Boolean cube has an exact implementation with uniformly bounded coefficient magnitudes and
the same number of nonconstant neurons. This removes the compiled-width threshold entirely.
Lemma 5.1 (A bounded point in an integer polyhedron). Let a nonempty finite system of linear
equalities and inequalities in P ≥ 1 real variables have coefficient entries in {−1, 0, 1} and right-hand
sides in [−1, 1]. It has a feasible point z satisfying

                                                ∥z∥∞ ≤ (2P )2P .

The right-hand sides may be arbitrary real numbers, and the number of constraints does not enter
the bound.
Proof. Choose a feasible point of minimum Euclidean norm. Existence follows by restricting the
closed feasible set to the closed ball determined by any one feasible point. Include every equality
and every tight inequality in its active system. For a vector v in the nullspace of the active rows,
both sufficiently small perturbations z ± tv remain feasible: every inactive inequality has positive
slack, and there are finitely many constraints. Minimality therefore implies z · v = 0. Thus z belongs
to the row span of its active coefficient matrix.
    Choose r ≤ P linearly independent active rows and write the resulting matrix as A. If r = 0,
then z = 0. Otherwise Az = b and
                                          z = A⊤ (AA⊤ )−1 b.
The Gram matrix is an invertible integer matrix, so its determinant is an integer at least one in
absolute value. Its entries have magnitude at most P . Cofactor expansion and the permutation
formula bound every entry of its adjugate by (r − 1)!P r−1 . Since |bi | ≤ 1 and |Aij | ≤ 1,

                                       |zj | ≤ r2 (r − 1)!P r−1 ≤ (2P )2P .
