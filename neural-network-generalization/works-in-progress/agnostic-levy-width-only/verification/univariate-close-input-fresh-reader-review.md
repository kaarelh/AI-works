# Independent review: univariate compiler and close-input obstruction

Reviewed `levy-width-oracle/source/univariate-splines.tex` and `close-input-obstruction.tex` on 18 November 2026. No substantive mathematical gap found.

For the spline compiler, clustering breakpoints with consecutive gaps at most `3δ` and enlarging each cluster by `δ` gives disjoint replacement intervals separated by more than `δ`. Every replacement chord has slope at most `2/δ`. A retained boundary interval can be short, but its underlying affine segment continues to the first original breakpoint at distance greater than `δ`, so its slope obeys the same bound. The total exceptional probability is at most `3κKδ^α`; multiplying by output difference at most two gives the stated `6κKδ^α`. At most two new breakpoints per cluster are needed. The hinge representation uses at most `2K+2` neurons and coefficients at most `4/δ`.

A width-`C` shallow univariate ReLU map has at most `C` raw breakpoints, and clipping contributes at most two more in each of its `C+1` affine intervals. Thus `K≤3C+2`, the stated `δ` gives error at most `ζ`, and `m≤6C+6`. Stable padding then gives the linear-`C`, nearly linear-depth expected oracle, with original coefficient magnitudes eliminated. The all-data posterior is unchanged across all comparison widths. This refinement has threshold `N≥6C+6`, separate from the quadratic-cost same-width result at `N≥C+1`.

For the obstruction, centering and independent reciprocal-fan-in variance imply contraction of prior mean-square increments through every hidden layer. The readout reduces the first-layer `Nδ²/2` to `δ²/2`, independently of depth and width. Bounded predictions and labels give posterior density at most `exp(4n/T)` relative to the prior on every realized sample. At `δ=exp(-2n/T)/sqrt(2)`, the two posterior-mean values differ by at most one half. Their best possible squared risk for labels zero and one is therefore at least `1/16`. The teacher `ReLU(x/δ)` is width one and exact on the two support points. The explicit sample-size-dependent adverse-distribution quantifier is correct; this is a uniform-rate obstruction, not inconsistency on a fixed problem.

The two-point proof concerns atomic input laws on a continuous domain. Calling these “continuous distributions” would be misleading. I sent the owner both the wording correction and an optional atomless strengthening: equally mix uniform laws on `[-ε,0]` and `[δ,δ+ε]`, with `ε=δ/4` and `δ=exp(-2n/T)/(2sqrt(2))`. The same clipped width-one teacher is exactly zero and one on the two intervals. The prior increment bound holds for every pair, and Jensen bounds the squared difference between interval-averaged posterior means by `exp(4n/T)(δ+2ε)²/2=9/64<1/4`. Jensen within each interval yields the same `1/16` risk lower bound. This law has a bounded density for each `n`, but no sample-size-uniform density cap; hence it does not contradict the positive rates' dependence on the anti-concentration constant.
