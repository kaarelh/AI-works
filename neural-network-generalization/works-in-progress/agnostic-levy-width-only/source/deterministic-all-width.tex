\section{An existence result with deterministic all-width calibration}
\label{sec:deterministic-all-width}

The general-depth expectation theorem above requires enough learner width to
contain a coefficient-controlled compilation.  A deterministic likelihood
schedule removes this width allowance.  The additional argument uses finite
parameter complexity at smaller widths, and may prescribe an extremely small,
nonexplicit observation variance there.  It is therefore an existence result,
not a refinement of the fixed-likelihood claim.

\begin{theorem}[All realizable widths with a prescribed likelihood schedule]
\label{thm:deterministic-all-width}
Fix input dimension $d\ge1$ and teacher hidden depth $h\ge1$.  Consider either
arbitrary input laws on $\{-1,1\}^d$, or laws on $[-1,1]^d$ satisfying the slab
condition with fixed $\kappa,\alpha$.  Fix a learner hidden depth $H$ large
enough for the corresponding compiler: $H\ge b(h+1)$ in the Boolean case,
and $H\ge\max\{h,4\}$ in the continuous case.  Here $b$ is the numerical
Boolean compiler constant used in Section~\ref{sec:compiler-resources}.
There is a deterministic positive temperature schedule $T_{n,N}$, depending
only on $n,N,H,d$ and the stated input-regularity constants, with the following
property.  Use the fixed L\'evy parameter-prior family of Section~\ref{sec:levy-model-compilers},
output $f_{w,n}=M_n\operatorname{clip}(z_w,[-1,1])$ with $M_n=n^{1/8}$, and
\[
 d\Pi_{n,N}(w)\ \propto\
 \exp\!\left[-T_{n,N}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n
              (f_{w,n}(X_i)-Y_i)^2\right]d\pi_N(w).
\]
The observation variance is $T_{n,N}/2$.  No training label is changed.
Put $a=2b(b+1)$ and choose
\[
 u=\frac1{16a h(h+1)}\quad\text{on Boolean inputs},\qquad
 u=\frac1{16(dh+1)}\quad\text{under slab regularity},\qquad
 C_n=\lfloor n^u\rfloor.
\]
For every input--label law in the stated class with $\E Y^2<\infty$, every
sufficiently large $n$, and every prescribed $N\ge C_n+1$, the ordinary
posterior mean trained on all $n$ observations obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deterministic-all-width-oracle}
 \E R(\bar f_{n,N})\le
 \inf_{\substack{g\text{ a raw ReLU network}\\
          \text{hidden depth }h,\ \text{width}\le C_n}}R(g)
 +O\!\left(n^{-1/8}\log^2(en)\right)
 +5\E\!\left[Y^2\mathbf1_{\{|Y|>n^{1/8}\}}\right].
\end{equation}
The comparison networks have arbitrary real coefficients and affine biases.
The constant in $O(\cdot)$ may depend on $d,h,H,\kappa,\alpha$ and
$\E Y^2$, but not on $N$ or comparison coefficients.  The schedule uses
neither the data nor a label-moment bound.
\end{theorem}

The schedule equals $256M_n^2$ when $N>\lfloor n^{1/8}\rfloor$.  At smaller
widths it is fixed before observing data through the finite covering argument
below.  No useful numerical lower bound on that small-width variance is
claimed.  There is still one architecture, one prior, one
posterior trained on all data, and no validation or architecture mixture.
The finite-variance shallow theorem has the stronger feature that its
simple variance schedule works at every realizable width.

\subsection{Uniform prior comparisons at a fixed finite architecture}
Let $\mathcal F_{N,H}$ denote the normalized bounded predictions
$\operatorname{clip}(z_w,[-1,1])$ at the specified architecture.  The input
family $\mathfrak P$ is either all laws on the Boolean cube or all laws
with the stated slab constants.  Define
\[
 d_{\mathfrak P}(f,g)=\sup_{P_X\in\mathfrak P}\E_{P_X}|f(X)-g(X)|.
\]
\begin{lemma}[Finite uniform covers and finite prior cost]
\label{lem:finite-architecture-prior-cost}
For every fixed $N,H,d$ and $0<\epsilon<1$, there are finitely many
parameter-space open balls $B_1,\ldots,B_J$ of positive $\pi_N$-mass such
that every $g\in\mathcal F_{N,H}$ has some $j$ for which
\[
 \sup_{w\in B_j}d_{\mathfrak P}
       (\operatorname{clip}(z_w,[-1,1]),g)\le\epsilon.
\]
Consequently, a finite deterministic number $K_{N,H,d,\epsilon}\ge1$
exists such that every such $g$ has a comparison measure $\rho_g$ satisfying
\[
 \KL(\rho_g\Vert\pi_N)\le K_{N,H,d,\epsilon},\qquad
 \sup_{P_X\in\mathfrak P}\E_{\rho_g,P_X}|f_w(X)-g(X)|\le\epsilon.
\]
No polynomial bound on $K_{N,H,d,\epsilon}$ is asserted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a finite Boolean cube, quantize the finitely many output values into
bins of length $\epsilon/4$.  There are finitely many realized bin patterns;
choose one implementing parameter vector for each.  Networks in the same
pattern differ uniformly by at most $\epsilon/4$.

For continuous inputs, the clipped prediction of a depth-$H$, width-$N$
ReLU network is affine on each cell of a finite polyhedral partition.
The number of hidden activation regions is at most
$(N+1)^{dH}$.  Every activation boundary is contained in the zero set of
one of the corresponding affine preactivations, and final clipping adds
at most two affine boundaries per region.  Thus all changes of affine
formula are contained in a union of at most
\[
 J_0=(HN+2)(N+1)^{dH}
\]
affine hyperplanes; identically zero expressions add no boundary.
Use the rational grid of mesh $2/Q$ on $[-1,1]^d$ and quantize prediction
values at its vertices into bins of length $\epsilon/4$.
Choose one network for each realized finite pattern.  For a network and
its chosen representative, discard every closed grid cell meeting one
of their at most $2J_0$ boundary hyperplanes.  Each discarded cell lies
within distance $2\sqrt d/Q$ of a corresponding hyperplane, so its total
probability under any $P_X\in\mathfrak P$ is at most
$2\kappa J_0(2\sqrt d/Q)^\alpha$.  On any remaining cell both predictions
are affine and differ at every vertex by at most $\epsilon/4$; the same
bound holds throughout that cell.  Hence their $d_{\mathfrak P}$ distance
is at most
\[
 \epsilon/4+4\kappa J_0(2\sqrt d/Q)^\alpha.
\]
Choose the finite integer $Q$ so that the second term is at most
$\epsilon/4$.  This produces a finite $\epsilon/2$-cover with centers in
the original architecture.  No wider compiler was used.

For every chosen finite parameter vector, continuity of the network
jointly in parameters and in the compact input domain supplies an open
parameter ball whose predictions differ uniformly from the center by
at most $\epsilon/2$.  Each ball has positive mass because the coordinate
prior has full support.  Set
$K_{N,H,d,\epsilon}=\max\{1,\max_j\log(1/\pi_N(B_j))\}$ and take
$\rho_g=\pi_N(\cdot\mid B_j)$.  Then
$\KL(\rho_g\Vert\pi_N)=\log(1/\pi_N(B_j))$.
All choices depend only on the prescribed function class and regularity
constants, before any data or comparison network is selected.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Expected uniform convergence with finite-variance labels}
\begin{lemma}[Bounded predictions, unbounded square-integrable labels]
\label{lem:finite-variance-vc-expectation}
Let $\mathcal F\subset[-M,M]^{\mathcal X}$ be a pointwise separable class
of pseudodimension at most $V\ge1$, and let $\E Y^2<\infty$.  For iid
samples and a numerical constant $c$,
\[
 \E\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}
 \left|(P_n-P)\bigl[f(X)^2-2f(X)Y\bigr]\right|
 \le c\bigl(M^2+M\sqrt{\E Y^2}\bigr)
             \sqrt{\frac{V\log(en)}n}.
\]
This controls squared-loss differences without requiring $\E Y^4<\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Symmetrize the two terms separately.  Uniform $L^2$ covering bounds for
bounded VC-subgraph classes give
$\log\mathcal N(\epsilon M,\mathcal F,L^2(Q))
\le cV\log(c/\epsilon)$ for every probability measure $Q$.
The map $t\mapsto t^2$ is $2M$-Lipschitz on $[-M,M]$, so the squared term
has expected Rademacher supremum at most $cM^2\sqrt{V\log(en)/n}$.
For the multiplier term, condition on the sample and apply the same
entropy bound with the weighted empirical measure
\[
 Q_Y=\frac{\sum_iY_i^2\delta_{X_i}}{\sum_iY_i^2},
\]
when its denominator is positive.  The Rademacher increment metric is
$\sqrt{\sum_iY_i^2}/n$ times the $L^2(Q_Y)$ metric.  Dudley's entropy
integral therefore bounds its conditional supremum by
\[
 cM\sqrt{V\log(en)}\,\frac{\sqrt{\sum_iY_i^2}}n.
\]
The zero-denominator case contributes zero.  Jensen's inequality gives
the stated expectation.
For neural networks, rational parameter vectors form a countable
pointwise dense class, and dominated convergence extends the estimate
to all parameters.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Construction and proof of the schedule}
For $N\le\lfloor n^{1/8}\rfloor$, choose the finite cost in
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-architecture-prior-cost} at $\epsilon_n=n^{-8}$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-width-deterministic-temperature}
 T_{n,N}=\frac{n^{-3}}{1+K_{N,H,d,\epsilon_n}}.
\end{equation}
The prior used to define this cost is exactly the prescribed width-$N$
prior.  In the continuous case its dependence on $\kappa,\alpha$ is
understood.  For larger $N$, set $T_{n,N}=256n^{1/4}$.

Write $\mathcal F^M_{N,H}=M\mathcal F_{N,H}$ and
$\widehat R(f)=P_n(f-Y)^2$.  Gibbs' variational identity gives, for each
sample and every comparison measure $\rho$,
\[
 \E_{\Pi}\widehat R(f)
 \le \E_\rho\widehat R(f)+\frac{T_{n,N}}n\KL(\rho\Vert\pi_N).
\]
Since $\widehat R(f)-\widehat R(0)$ equals
$P_n[f^2-2fY]$, Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-variance-vc-expectation} and
Jensen's inequality imply
\begin{align*}
 \E R(\bar f_{n,N})
 &\le \E_\rho R(f)+\frac{T_{n,N}}n\KL(\rho\Vert\pi_N)\\
 &\quad+c(M_n^2+M_n\sqrt{\E Y^2})
           \sqrt{V_{N,H,d}\log(en)/n}.
\end{align*}
The common term $P_nY^2-\E Y^2$ cancels after expectation; no fourth moment is used.
Here $V_{N,H,d}\le c_{d,H}(N+1)^2\log(e(N+1))$ by the usual ReLU
pseudodimension bound~\cite{pdim}; saturation and multiplication by
$M_n$ preserve this upper bound.

Take any raw comparison network $g$ of width at most $C_n$ and hidden
depth $h$.  If $N\ge C_n+1$, a constant wire and nonnegative ReLU
identity relays embed it at depth $H$ and width $N$.
Thus $\operatorname{clip}(g/M_n,[-1,1])\in\mathcal F_{N,H}$.
The comparison measure from Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-architecture-prior-cost}
has squared $L^2(P_X\times\rho)$ error at most $2M_n^2\epsilon_n$
relative to $\operatorname{clip}(g,[-M_n,M_n])$.  Cauchy--Schwarz bounds
its risk increase by
\[
 2(M_n+\sqrt{\E Y^2})M_n\sqrt{2\epsilon_n}
       +2M_n^2\epsilon_n.
\]
The entropy term under~\eqref{eq:small-width-deterministic-temperature}
is at most $n^{-4}$.  Since $N\le n^{1/8}$ and $M_n=n^{1/8}$,
the uniform-convergence remainder is
$O(n^{-1/8}\log(en))$, uniformly over these widths.
The projection inequality in Appendix~\ref{sec:finite-variance-transfer}
adds at most $\E[Y^2\mathbf1_{\{|Y|>M_n\}}]$ when the clipped
comparison risk is replaced by $R(g)$.

At larger widths, apply the finite-variance expectation theorem and the
coefficient-free compilers to the normalized teacher $g/M_n$.
Use normalized precision $O((n+1)^{-4}(1+M_n)^{-4})$.
For the choices of $u$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:deterministic-all-width}, both
the compiled width and its logarithmic-prior resource are
$O(n^{1/16}\log^{c_{d,h,H}}(en))$.  Consequently the compiled width is
at most $n^{1/8}$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
The all-data expected cost is
$O(M_n^2n^{1/16}\log^{c_{d,h,H}}(en)/n)
=O(n^{-11/16}\log^{c_{d,h,H}}(en))$, together with the five-fold label-tail
term from Appendix~\ref{sec:finite-variance-transfer}.
At fixed $d,h,H$, this smaller remainder is absorbed by
$O(n^{-1/8}\log^2(en))$.  The two branches therefore prove
\eqref{eq:deterministic-all-width-oracle} uniformly over every
$N\ge C_n+1$.  Taking deterministic comparison near-minimizers and
then an infimum does not change the learner or its schedule.
