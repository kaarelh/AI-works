\section{Real-valued labels with only a finite second moment}
\label{sec:finite-variance-transfer}
A growing bounded output range permits arbitrary real-valued labels.
No labels need be clipped in the algorithm or in the proof, and no
conditional noise model is required.

\begin{theorem}[A sharp all-data oracle with a marginal tail remainder]
\label{thm:finite-variance-posterior}
Let $\pi$ be any probability prior on jointly measurable functions
$f_w:\mathcal X\to[-M,M]$, with $M>0$. Let $(X,Y)$ have any joint
law with $\E Y^2<\infty$, and let the observations be IID.
Fix $T\ge256M^2$ and define the ordinary final posterior and mean by
\[
 dQ_{S_n}(w)=Z_{S_n}^{-1}
  \exp\!\left[-T^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n(f_w(X_i)-Y_i)^2\right]d\pi(w),
 \qquad \bar f_{S_n}=\int f_w\,dQ_{S_n}.
\]
For every deterministic comparison law $\rho\ll\pi$,
\begin{equation}
 \E R(\bar f_{S_n})
 \le\int R(f_w)\,d\rho(w)
     +\frac{T\operatorname{KL}(\rho\|\pi)}{n+1}
     +4M\E[|Y|\mathbf1_{\{|Y|>M\}}].
 \label{eq:finite-variance-posterior}
\end{equation}
In particular, the final term is at most
$4\E[Y^2\mathbf1_{\{|Y|>M\}}]$.
The prior and output range may be predetermined functions of $n$;
the theorem is applied with those choices held fixed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a probability law $q$ on $U\in[-M,M]$, with mean $\mu$ and
variance $v$. For $L_y(u)=(u-y)^2$, write
\[
 dp_y(u)=\frac{e^{-L_y(u)/T}}{\int e^{-L_y/T}\,dq}\,dq(u).
\]
The same covariance calculation as for the bounded-label inverse-tilt
inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 \int L_y\,dp_y
 \ge L_y(\mu)+[1-A_M(y)]v,
 \qquad
 A_M(y)=\frac{4(M+|y|)^2}{T}
              e^{4M(M+|y|)/T}.
 \label{eq:finite-variance-pointwise-cooling}
\end{equation}
For completeness, for independent $U,U'\sim q$ the difference
$\int L_y\,dq-\int L_y\,dp_y$ equals
\[
 \frac{\E[(L_y(U)-L_y(U'))
        (e^{-L_y(U')/T}-e^{-L_y(U)/T})]}
      {2\int e^{-L_y/T}\,dq}.
\]
The bounds
$|L_y(u)-L_y(u')|\le2(M+|y|)|u-u'|$ and
$\operatorname{osc} L_y\le4M(M+|y|)$, together with the mean-value
theorem and $\E(U-U')^2=2v$, give the subtracted quantity at most
$A_M(y)v$. Since $\int L_y\,dq=L_y(\mu)+v$, this proves
\eqref{eq:finite-variance-pointwise-cooling}.

If $|y|\le M$, then
$A_M(y)\le e^{1/32}/16<1$, so
$L_y(\mu)\le\int L_y\,dp_y$.
If $|y|>M$, the squared-loss range over $[-M,M]$ has length exactly
$4M|y|$. Both $L_y(\mu)$ and $\int L_y\,dp_y$ lie in this range.
Therefore, for every real $y$,
\begin{equation}
 L_y(\mu)\le\int L_y\,dp_y
              +4M|y|\mathbf1_{\{|y|>M\}}.
 \label{eq:finite-variance-one-step}
\end{equation}
This step requires no conditional tail bound or independence between
$q$ and the input.

Introduce $n+1$ observations, retaining exactly the same prior,
output range $M$, and temperature $T$ as the $n$-sample learner.
Let $Q^+$ be the enlarged-sample posterior and $Q^{-i}$ the posterior
with observation $i$ omitted. Conditional on the other observations
and $X_i$, push $Q^{-i}$ forward through $w\mapsto f_w(X_i)$ to get
$q$. Updating by the omitted observation gives exactly the pushforward
of $Q^+$ through the same map. Apply
\eqref{eq:finite-variance-one-step}, average, and sum over $i$.
Exchangeability and independence of the held-out observation give
\[
 \E R(\bar f_{S_n})
 \le\E\int\frac1{n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(f_w(X_i)-Y_i)^2\,dQ^+(w)
       +4M\E[|Y|\mathbf1_{\{|Y|>M\}}].
\]
For each realized enlarged sample the Gibbs variational inequality
bounds the posterior average total empirical loss by the comparison
average total empirical loss plus
$T\operatorname{KL}(\rho\|\pi)$; the posterior KL is nonnegative
and may be dropped. Take expectations and divide by $n+1$.
All risks and averages are integrable because predictions are bounded
and $\E Y^2<\infty$. The posterior normalizer is positive for every
realized sample. This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projection of an arbitrary real-valued comparator]
\label{lem:finite-variance-projection}
Let $s_M$ clip to $[-M,M]$. For every pair of real numbers $a,y$,
\begin{equation}
 (s_M(a)-y)^2\le(a-y)^2+(|y|-M)_+^2.
 \label{eq:pointwise-projection-transfer}
\end{equation}
Consequently $R(s_M\circ g)\le R(g)+
\E[Y^2\mathbf1_{\{|Y|>M\}}]$ for every real-valued comparator.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $|y|\le M$, projection cannot increase distance to $y$.
If $y>M$, clipping $a<-M$ also decreases its distance to $y$;
for $a>M$ the loss increase is
$(y-M)^2-(a-y)^2\le(y-M)^2$.
The case $y<-M$ is symmetric.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A coefficient-one real-valued oracle]
\label{cor:finite-variance-comparator}
Use an output family $f_{w,M}=M s(z_w)$, with the same parameter
prior for every $M$, and temperature $T=256M^2$.
Suppose a deterministic real-valued comparator $g$ admits a
comparison law with
\[
 \E_\rho\left\|\frac{f_{w,M}-s_M\circ g}{M}\right\|_{L^2(P_X)}^2
 \le\eta_{n,M}:=\frac1{(n+1)^4(1+M)^4},
 \qquad \operatorname{KL}(\rho\|\pi)\le K_{n,M}(g).
\]
Then, for a numerical $A$,
\begin{align}
 \E R(\bar f_{S_n,M})\le R(g)
 &+\frac{256M^2 K_{n,M}(g)}{n+1}
 +5\E[Y^2\mathbf1_{\{|Y|>M\}}]\notag\\
 &+\frac{A(1+\sqrt{\E Y^2})}{(n+1)^2}.
 \label{eq:finite-variance-real-oracle}
\end{align}
Infima over all deterministic comparators may be taken on the right.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $g_M=s_M\circ g$. Its risk is at most
$2M^2+2\E Y^2$. The comparison MSE is at most $M^2\eta_{n,M}$,
so Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \int R(f_{w,M})\,d\rho
 \le R(g_M)+2M\sqrt{R(g_M)\eta_{n,M}}+M^2\eta_{n,M}.
\]
The last two terms are at most
$A(1+\sqrt{\E Y^2})/(n+1)^2$, since
$M(M+\sqrt{\E Y^2})/(1+M)^2\le1+\sqrt{\E Y^2}$.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-variance-posterior} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-variance-projection}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Continuous compilers and prior comparisons.}
A continuous-domain compiler that only controls normalized
$L^1(P_X)$ error is enough. Both normalized functions lie in $[-1,1]$,
so their squared difference is at most twice their absolute difference.
Use compiler tolerance at most $\eta_{n,M}/16$ and a native comparison
MSE at most $\eta_{n,M}/4$; the triangle-square inequality gives the
hypothesis of Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-variance-comparator}.
The required inverse tolerances have logarithm
$O(\log(n+1)+\log(1+M))$. Thus the L\'evy coefficient-free compilers
and logarithmic-entropy comparisons preserve their polynomial
resources, with only these additional logarithms. No bound on
$\E[Y\mid X]$ is used in this transfer.

\paragraph{Tail regimes.}
For a sub-Gaussian marginal envelope
$\Pr(|Y|>t)\le2e^{-t^2/(2K^2)}$, choosing
$M_n=K\sqrt{16\log(e(n+1))}$ makes the tail term
$O(K^2/(n+1)^2)$ and changes the complexity remainder only by a
logarithmic factor. If $\E|Y|^p\le V_p$ for some $p>2$, the tail is
at most $V_p M^{2-p}$, giving an explicit tradeoff with the
$M^2K_{n,M}/n$ term. With only a finite second moment,
any deterministic $M_n\to\infty$ makes the tail vanish, without a
uniform rate over all such label laws.

\begin{corollary}[Universal squared-risk consistency at arbitrary widths]
\label{cor:levy-universal-consistency}
Let $X$ have any Borel probability law on a compact cube and let
$\E Y^2<\infty$. Use the fixed small-intensity L\'evy fan-in
coordinate prior and ReLU hidden activation. Prescribe any finite
width sequence $N_n\to\infty$, at any rate, and fix a hidden depth
$H\ge1$. Choose $0<b<1/2$, output range $M_n=n^b$, and
\[
 f_{w,n}=M_n s(z_w),\qquad T_n=256M_n^2.
\]
Use all observations with their unchanged real labels in the
likelihood $\exp[-\sum_i(f_{w,n}(X_i)-Y_i)^2/T_n]$ and return its
ordinary posterior mean. Then
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\E R(\bar f_{S_n,n})
 =\E\bigl(Y-\E[Y\mid X]\bigr)^2.
 \label{eq:levy-universal-consistency}
\end{equation}
No input-density or anti-concentration assumption is needed.
There is one prescribed architecture at each sample size and no
prior over architectures.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fix any finite-width shallow ReLU function $g$ with finite
coefficients. On the compact input cube it is bounded. Its
nonnegative hidden features pass exactly through any fixed number
of ReLU identity layers. For all sufficiently large $n$, $N_n$
contains this fixed implementation and $M_n$ exceeds its output
bound. The normalized comparison $g/M_n$ has no larger coefficient
cap than a fixed constant depending on $g$.
The width-independent uniform L\'evy comparison theorem, used at the
precision in Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-variance-comparator}, therefore
has $K_{n,M_n}(g)\le A_g\log(n+1)$.
The complexity term is
$O_g(n^{2b-1}\log(n+1))\to0$, and the label-tail term vanishes by
integrability of $Y^2$. Thus
\[
 \limsup_n\E R(\bar f_{S_n,n})\le R(g)
\]
for every such $g$.
Shallow ReLU networks with a constant input are uniformly dense in
continuous functions on a compact cube. Continuous functions are
dense in $L^2(P_X)$, and $\E[Y\mid X]\in L^2(P_X)$ by Jensen's
inequality. Hence the infimum of $R(g)$ over these networks equals
the Bayes squared risk. Every predictor has risk at least that
quantity, proving \eqref{eq:levy-universal-consistency}.
\end{proof}

The same proof permits varying learner depth $H_n$ whenever
\[
 (H_n+1)\log\{(n+1)(H_n+1)\}=o(n^{1-2b}),
\]
using the stable-padding comparison for each fixed shallow teacher.
In particular, polynomially growing depths with exponent strictly
less than $1-2b$ are allowed. The conclusion is consistency, with no
uniform convergence rate over all finite-second-moment label laws.
The quantitative coefficient-free polynomial-width oracle retains
the Boolean-input or continuous anti-concentration assumptions of
its compiler.
