# Independent review of the statistical interface

Reviewed on 18 November 2026 by a separate research agent from the statistical proof's author. This is a mathematical audit, not Lean verification.

The complete all-depth integration checks. The Gaussian Jacobian moment follows by convexity over the covariance eigenvalues, the inequality t(1+r)<=exp(tr), and the independent readout moment. The Morrey exponent and dimension-dependent constants yield a function dictionary common to all width/scale pairs. The small-effective-width branch has polynomially many parameters and preserves a fixed output envelope. Evidence and prior fourth-moment bounds control the raw posterior-risk tail.

The shallow comparison is uniform over the bounded coefficient class because the Gaussian feature balls have a uniform lower mass. Its readout factor depends on the fixed output envelope rather than teacher width. Dyadic likelihood calibration gives finitely many common dictionary events covering the entire prefactor interval. The fixed population minimizer suffices for the leading-one oracle; no uniform teacher-class concentration is silently assumed.

The narrow tanh relay has polynomial coefficients. The translated Gaussian cylinder has the stated normalized entropy cost: only first/readout coordinates and group-constant row directions are constrained, while unrestricted Gaussian bulk is handled by its operator-norm event. Symmetry justifies the translation lower bound. Below the polynomial comparison-width threshold, parameter covers and the smaller likelihood scale have polynomial sample cost. The theorem's shared calibration exponent can be chosen before the final sample exponent, without a circular dependence.

The later all-depth and output-envelope removal also check. The moment map is globally contractive up to its explicit directional covariance gain, so one-step fourth-power concentration yields polynomial finite width without a local envelope assumption. Exact same-sign attenuation cannot worsen squared loss outside the bounded label range; inside that range the cubic response estimate gives a fixed readout factor independent of teacher width. High-gain comparisons and the AM–GM spatial cover handle all smaller depths, including the direct one-hidden-layer feature-readout case. The deterministic sample-count cap is a likelihood calibration, not a prior or mixture over architectures.

The marginal sub-Gaussian-label extension checks. One common empirical label-tail/moment event controls all response comparisons, including the continuum of prefactors. Evidence and prior risk moments use the actual unmodified labels. Finite-dictionary Bernstein and one fixed teacher minimizer preserve the depth and teacher-width exponents. No conditional noise assumption or clipping of the actual observations is introduced.

The final theorem matches these proofs: all hidden depths, every learner width at least the comparator width, all prefactors in (0,1], fixed input dimension and coefficient cap, and a leading-one shallow comparison oracle with the stated calibrated raw Gaussian likelihood. No claim is made for arbitrary-depth comparison networks at the same width exponent.
