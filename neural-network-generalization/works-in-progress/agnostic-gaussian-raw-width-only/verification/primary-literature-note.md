# Focused primary-literature check for the shallow Gaussian midpoint results

18 November 2026. Primary article/proceedings pages and PDFs were inspected through the web connector. Direct local PDF downloads were blocked by the network proxy; the primary PDFs were readable through that connector. This is a focused check, not an exhaustive novelty search.

The closest references are Bach's finite atomic representer theorem and Tinsi–Dalalyan's Gaussian-prior shallow-network aggregation analysis. The new manuscripts should credit the compression, balancing, entropy inequalities and squared-loss identity as established tools. A defensible description of the present result is their use to control the actual final raw Gaussian posterior on random-design test risk, uniformly in the stated finite learner widths. No first-ever or globally optimal learning-rate claim follows from this search.

## Verified references and exact comparisons

1. **Francis Bach (2017), “Breaking the Curse of Dimensionality with Convex Neural Networks.” Journal of Machine Learning Research 18(19):1–53.** [Primary page](https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/14-546.html), [primary PDF](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume18/14-546/14-546.pdf). Section 2.2, printed page 6, explicitly gives at most n variation-norm atoms on n observed inputs by conic Carathéodory. This is the direct antecedent of the empirical compression lemma. Its statistical learner is variation-norm constrained convex optimization, not the finite Gaussian posterior in the new result. The explicit Borel selection and use of compressed midpoint centers in a posterior-dependent comparison law are supplied by the new proof. Bach itself attributes the underlying convex-network framework to earlier work, so it should be cited as a clear presentation rather than declared its origin.

2. **Laura Tinsi and Arnak Dalalyan (2022), “Risk bounds for aggregated shallow neural networks using Gaussian priors.” Proceedings of the 35th Conference on Learning Theory, PMLR 178:227–253.** [Primary page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/tinsi22a.html), [primary PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/tinsi22a/tinsi22a.pdf). Theorem 2 starts from a PAC-Bayesian inequality for an aggregate and bounds the Gaussian approximation/entropy contribution. Section 4.1 justifies final exponential weighting for fixed design; Section 4.2 uses mirror averaging for random design. Section 6 balances learner width against an estimation term proportional to that width. This is close prior art, but does not directly supply the claimed high-probability random-design guarantee for the ordinary final raw posterior uniformly over every sufficiently large prescribed learner width. Avoid suggesting that Gaussian-prior neural aggregation or its oracle analysis is new.

3. **Arnak Dalalyan and Alexandre B. Tsybakov (2008), “Aggregation by exponential weighting, sharp PAC-Bayesian bounds and sparsity.” Machine Learning 72:39–61.** [Primary author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.2839), [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.2839). The abstract and Section 1 explicitly use deterministic design. This is an appropriate sharp exponential-weighting precedent; its fixed-design loss should not be substituted for independent random-design test risk. The similarly titled 2007 COLT paper is a separate reference, so retain the complete title/year when citing.

4. **Pedro Savarese, Itay Evron, Daniel Soudry and Nathan Srebro (2019), “How do infinite width bounded norm networks look in function space?” COLT, PMLR 99:2667–2690.** [Primary page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v99/savarese19a.html), [primary PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v99/savarese19a/savarese19a.pdf). The univariate characterization links minimum network norm to second-derivative variation and spline fits. Its norm excludes bias penalties; the paper explicitly warns that regularizing hidden biases changes its exact result. It supplies important function-space context, but its exact norm identity is not the present Gaussian energy identity, which includes the hidden biases. The positive-homogeneous balancing principle is also standard and is credited there to Neyshabur and coauthors.

5. **Pietro Caputo and Daniel Parisi (2021), “Block Factorization of the Relative Entropy via Spatial Mixing.” Communications in Mathematical Physics 388:793–818.** [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-021-04237-1), [primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.10574). Lemma 4.2 explicitly gives a product-measure relative-entropy version of Shearer's inequality. Its spin-system formulation is not a Gaussian-neuron theorem. The new tanh proof includes the short general chain-rule argument needed for Gaussian packs, so this reference should credit the classical entropy principle rather than be invoked as an unexplained continuous-state theorem.

## Suggested short text

“The finite atomic compression used below is the variation-norm representer principle: on n observed inputs, an optimum can be represented using at most n signed atoms; see Bach (2017, Section 2.2). Our proof applies this principle to a posterior draw's midpoint with a comparison network and combines its Gaussian neighborhood cost with entropy coercivity. The linear program is a comparison device; it does not replace the Bayesian learning rule.”

“Tinsi and Dalalyan (2022) analyze shallow networks with product Gaussian priors by assuming a PAC-Bayesian aggregation inequality and bounding its approximation and entropy terms. Their random-design example uses mirror averaging, whereas their final exponential-weighting example concerns fixed design. Our one-dimensional argument directly controls the ordinary final raw Gaussian posterior under random design, uniformly over the stated learner widths. Sharp fixed-design exponential weighting goes back at least to Dalalyan and Tsybakov (2008).”

For the tanh appendix: “The random-subset entropy estimate is the chain-rule form of the classical product-measure Shearer inequality; see, for example, Caputo and Parisi (2021, Lemma 4.2). We include its continuous-state proof. Its role here is to preserve part of the posterior entropy penalty while the squared-loss midpoint identity supplies a disagreement term.”

## Limits of the comparison

These results do not show that the new rates are optimal among all estimators of one-dimensional functions. Classical spline/variation regularization and Gaussian-prior aggregation study related classes with different estimators, norms, width choices and risk conventions. I found no directly applicable stronger theorem for the exact finite-width, mean-field-or-stronger Gaussian prior and final raw posterior studied here in this focused check. That is a search outcome, not a novelty theorem. A Bayesian-lasso citation should not be used as attribution for the exact Gaussian polar pushforward unless that identity is checked in the cited source.
