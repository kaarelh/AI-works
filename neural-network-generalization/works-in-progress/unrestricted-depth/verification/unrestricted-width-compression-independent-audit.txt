Verdict: PASS, including width-free posterior sufficiency corollary.

1. Adjacent-pair setup.
The current incoming feature norm is <=SR. Changing the preactivation
immediately after V propagates with Lipschitz constant <=P_*/(SA), also
when V is itself a hidden matrix (its tanh contributes a factor <=1).
Current normalized row thresholds use S and cancel it in every width bound.

2. Large and small rows.
There are <=S^2||W||_F^2/B^2 large rows; bounded tanh and the suffix norm
give output change <=P_*/B. For small rows, cubic linearization error
has Euclidean norm <=tau^2 S R^3||W||_F/3. Propagation gives exactly
P_*tau^2R^3/3. The nuclear norm of the resulting linear branch is bounded
by sum a_i b_i. These operations do not require tanh homogeneity.

3. Middle sampling and concentration.
For p_i=b_i/(a_i Z), a sampled outgoing column has norm a_i Z/m=a_i/c.
Consequently incoming/outgoing squared Frobenius norms are exactly
c Ahat and Ahat/c, with E Ahat=A_m. The ratio of extreme middle row
squares is Q, giving Z max a_i^2<=A_m Q<=A Q. Hence the sampled vector
at each x has norm <=A Q S R. Its mean-error expectation is <=that/sqrt m;
the Euclidean norm of the error changes by <=2AQSR/m per sampled index.
McDiarmid therefore supplies a dimension-free concentration bound. The
stated m constant is more than sufficient for its union with the scalar
Hoeffding event over all finite X. No ambient output dimension is hidden.

4. Linear branch.
Rank-k truncation has operator error <=||B0||_*/(k+1)<=A/(k+1).
Balanced SVD factors U,V0 have squared Frobenius norms nu. K copies with
incoming sqrt(c/K)V0 and outgoing U/sqrt(cK) have exact total squared
norms c nu and nu/c. Their summed linear map is U V0. Cubic tanh error
is <=c nu^2(SR)^3/(3K), using ||U||op,||V0||op<=sqrt nu. Propagation
uses nu<=A, giving <=P_* c nu S^2 R^3/(3K).
In the sampled case Z>=A_m S^2/B^2 and A_m>=Delta A imply
c nu S^2<=mB^2/Delta; in the deleted-middle case it equals one.
The chosen K therefore works independently of the old widths and scales.

5. Exact norm bookkeeping.
Concatenating the sampled and linear channels gives new pair product
Ahat+nu, not merely a bound on a hypothetical linearized network.
It is <=A_m+sum_small a_i b_i+eta A <=(1+eta)A by Cauchy--Schwarz.
The construction continues to use ordinary tanh after every layer.
There is no hidden conversion from freely rescaled activations.

6. Sequential steps.
Only the selected hidden dimension and its two adjacent matrices change.
Compressing the next dimension preserves the preceding width bound.
At most six error allocations per layer sum to 6H e<epsilon, and
(1+1/(4H))^H<exp(1/4)<2 bounds the total product growth. Parameters m,k,K
are fixed polynomials of the displayed scale-invariant quantities and
log(2|X|). Uniform zero padding preserves the network because tanh(0)=0.
Minor presentation clarification sent to the author: if both branches
vanish at an intermediate step, stop and use the zero network; already
accumulated approximation estimates justify this and no later positive
prefix normalization is then needed.

7. Posterior corollary.
On the Boolean cube, log|X|=d log2 and R=sqrt(d+1). Compress to error1/4
and double the readout: new width is polynomial, signed margin>=3/2,
and product norm<=4P0. The prior polynomial-product target theorem then
has T polynomial in d,D0,P0, so its logarithmic depth overhead is bounded
by c log(2+d+D0+P0). Its smaller-width branch continues to use actual
C_L(f), and gamma>=1+zeta is retained. The original width and individual
weight magnitudes do not enter. This does not claim the same property at
gamma=1 or at the original depth without the stated additional layers.
