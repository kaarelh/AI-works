# Bayesian neural networks compete with small networks in fixed input dimension

[Paper (PDF)](paper.pdf) · [LaTeX source](source/paper.tex) · [Verification record](verification/README.md)

A finite Bayesian neural network can compete with the best smaller, bounded-weight network even when the data are not generated by a neural network. The bound applies to the ordinary posterior mean of the **raw real output**, under squared loss. One probability event covers all learner widths above the teacher width and all Gaussian prior exponents at or above mean field.

The activation may be any fixed globally Lipschitz function, with no requirement that its value at zero vanish. This includes tanh, ReLU, GELU, sigmoid, and softplus. The actual learner has a single dense Gaussian prior, and the result imposes no robustness restriction on its support.

## The bounds

Let $d$ be input dimension and $L$ the number of affine maps, including the linear readout. For fixed teacher resources, dimension, depth, and confidence, the sufficient excess-risk rates are:

| Prediction and activation | Excess-risk rate, up to logarithms |
| --- | --- |
| Raw posterior mean, globally Lipschitz activation | $n^{-2/[L(d+2)]}$ |
| Raw posterior mean, tanh | $n^{-1/\max(d+2,\,1+dL/2)}$ |
| Mean of individually clipped outputs, globally Lipschitz activation | $n^{-1/\max(d+2,\,dL/2)}$ |

The last row concerns a different prediction convention: average the clipped outputs of posterior networks. It is generally different from clipping the ordinary posterior mean.

More generally, the leading coefficient of the comparison risk is one. The bounds therefore compete with the population-risk minimizer of a specified bounded-weight class. With both comparison width and weight cap at most $n^\alpha$, the sufficient ranges are:

- Globally Lipschitz activation: $\alpha<1/[2L(d+2)]$.
- Tanh: $\alpha<1/(4+2dL)$, using the theorem's two-parameter calibration.

A fixed weight cap doubles these sufficient width exponents. The growing-class claims here concern raw prediction; no corresponding growing-class improvement is claimed for the clipped-output row.

## Model and scope

The data are IID, with inputs in the cube and labels in $[-1,1]$. Labels need not match any teacher or any Gaussian noise model. For hidden learner width $N$ and exponent $\gamma\ge1$, the independent Gaussian variances are $N^{1-\gamma}/d$ in the first layer, $N^{-\gamma}$ in internal layers, and $N^{-\gamma-1}$ in the readout. The likelihood variance is $n\lambda N^{-\gamma}$, with the stated calibration of $\lambda$. Internal layers have no biases; a constant input is included in the first layer. Ordinary internally biased comparison networks are also covered: for any fixed activation that is not identically zero, one extra constant neuron per hidden layer represents every bias, with only a fixed activation-dependent factor in the teacher weight cap. A width-$m$ biased teacher therefore requires learner width at least $m+1$. For ReLU the weight-cap factor can be one, and for tanh it can be two. This representation does not change the learner prior.

The proof uses Gaussian operator-norm tails and an elementary finite-cover Bernstein inequality. Covering all bounded Lipschitz functions on the input cube incurs a dimensional cost. These are **fixed-input-dimension bounds**: their constants are not claimed to be polynomial in $d$, and they do not give a polynomial dependence jointly on teacher width and depth.

The raw-output theorems also extend to labels with a known marginal sub-Gaussian tail, including Gaussian noise around a bounded regression function. The prior remains unchanged. A proof-only truncation of labels, together with an analysis-only normalization of the prediction observable, changes the calibration by logarithmic factors; the sample exponents and growing-class ranges remain the same. The finite-sample corollary explicitly accounts for the population label-tail error. It does not extend the fixed $[-1,1]$ prediction-clipping convention to unbounded labels.
