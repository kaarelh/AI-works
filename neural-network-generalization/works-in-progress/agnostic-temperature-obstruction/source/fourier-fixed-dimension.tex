\section{Sharper exponents on a fixed-dimensional sphere}
\label{sec:fourier-fixed}
The two-dimensional example uses a staircase whose first-layer energy is quadratic in the sample count. A Fourier grid in a larger, but still fixed, dimension reduces that cost. In particular, every strict coefficient-growth obstruction obtainable from a logarithmic-dimensional Boolean grid also has a counterexample in a fixed dimension chosen from the exponent slack.

\begin{lemma}[A tanh convolution for Fourier characters]
\label{lem:fourier-convolution}
For $0\le v\le1$, define $\Phi_v(t)=\E\tanh(t+\sqrt vG)$, with $G$ standard Gaussian, and put $h_0=\pi/2$. There is $\kappa_v\in[2e^{-1/2},4]$ such that
\[
 \cos t=\kappa_v^{-1}\int_{\R}\cos u\,
       \{\Phi_v(t-u+h_0)-\Phi_v(t-u-h_0)\}\,du.
\]
If $|t|\le T$ and $U\ge T+h_0+1$, restricting the integral to $[-U,U]$ changes its value by at most $C_0e^{-2(U-T)}$, where $C_0=2e^{\pi+2}$. Moreover
\[
 \left|\frac1{4U}\sum_{s\in\{-1,1\}}\int_{-U}^U
        \E\tanh^2(t-u+sh_0+\sqrt vG)\,du
              -(1-1/U)\right|
 \le(C_0/U)e^{-2(U-T)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $c_0=\int_{\R}\cos u\,\operatorname{sech}^2u\,du$. Since
$\cos u\ge1-u^2/2$, $\int\operatorname{sech}^2u\,du=2$, and
\[
 \int_{\R}u^2\operatorname{sech}^2u\,du
 \le\int_{\R}4u^2e^{-2|u|}\,du=2,
\]
we have $1\le c_0\le2$. Gaussian convolution multiplies this cosine integral by $e^{-v/2}$. The even integrable kernel
$K_v(s)=\Phi_v(s+h_0)-\Phi_v(s-h_0)$ is the integral of $\Phi_v'$ over an interval of length $2h_0$. Fubini therefore gives
\[
 \int_{\R}\cos s\,K_v(s)\,ds=2\sin(h_0)e^{-v/2}c_0=:\kappa_v.
\]
Its sine integral vanishes by evenness. Changing variables proves the character identity.

The inequalities $1-\tanh u\le2e^{-2u}$ and $\E e^{2\sqrt vG}=e^{2v}$ imply
$0\le K_v(s)\le2e^{\pi+2}e^{-2|s|}=C_0e^{-2|s|}$.
The two omitted tails integrate to at most $C_0e^{-2(U-T)}$; dividing by $\kappa_v>1$ preserves this bound.
For the last assertion, integrate $1-\tanh^2=\operatorname{sech}^2$. Its full-line integral is two, also after Gaussian convolution and translation. The missing tails are at most $4e^2e^{-2(U-T-h_0)}$ for each sign, using $\operatorname{sech}^2 u\le4e^{-2|u|}$. Averaging gives the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite Fourier memorization with unchanged covariance]
\label{lem:fourier-native}
Let $\mathcal X$ be a finite subset of the unit sphere in $\R^d$, with $d\ge2$. Suppose $r:\mathcal X\to[-1,1]$ has a real Fourier representation
\[
 r(x)=\sum_j c_j\cos(\omega_j\cdot x+\phi_j),\qquad
 c_j\ge0,\quad \sum_jc_j\le C,\quad C\ge1,
\]
where $|\phi_j|\le\pi$, $\|\omega_j\|_2\le K_\omega$, and
$|\omega_j\cdot x+\phi_j|\le T$ on $\mathcal X$. Fix $0<t\le1/4$. For a number $U\ge\max\{2,T+\pi/2+1\}$, set
\[
 A=4UC,\quad R^2\ge\max\{4,A^{2/H},768/t\},\quad
 \eta=\frac{t}{36H^2R^{H-1}}.
\]
Suppose $CC_0e^{-2(U-T)}\le\eta/4$, where $C_0$ is from Lemma~\ref{lem:fourier-convolution}. At every width
\begin{align*}
 N\ge\max\left\{\frac8{t^2},\ 
 \frac{32A^2}{\eta^2}\log(8|\mathcal X|),\ 
 \frac{16A^2}{\eta^2}\log\frac{288HR^2}{t^2}\right\},
\end{align*}
and every scale $N\sigma^2\le1$, a Gaussian mean shift $w_0+\xi$, with $\xi$ having the original prior law, satisfies
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal X}\left(\E|z_{w_0+\xi}(x)-r(x)|^2\right)^{1/2}\le t,
\]
with mean energy
\[
 Q_N(w_0)\le d\{K_\omega^2+(U+\pi+\pi/2)^2\}+(4H+1)R^2.
\]
Its relative entropy is exactly $Q_N(w_0)/(2\sigma^2)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first-layer Gaussian noise variance is the same at every $x\in\mathcal X$: because $\|(1,x)\|_2^2=2$, it equals $v=2N\sigma^2/d\le1$. Write $C'=\sum_jc_j$. If $C'=0$, the zero shift suffices as before. Otherwise sample an index $j$ with probability $c_j/C'$, sample $u$ uniformly from $[-U,U]$, and sample a fair sign $s$. Associate the first-layer mean row
\[
 a=(\phi_j-u+sh_0,\omega_j)
\]
and the scalar second-layer coefficient
\[
 b=\frac{4UC'}{\kappa_v}s\cos u.
\]
Then $|b|\le A$ and $\|a\|_2^2\le K_\omega^2+(U+\pi+h_0)^2$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:fourier-convolution}, the expected weighted first output
$\E_{a,b,G}b\tanh(a\cdot(1,x)+\sqrt vG)$ differs from $r(x)$ by at most $\eta/4$. The expected squared first output differs from $b_1:=1-1/U$ by at most $\eta/4$, uniformly on $\mathcal X$.

Take $N$ independent pairs $(a_i,b_i)$. Hoeffding and a union bound show that some fixed realization simultaneously approximates both these expectations to within $\eta/4$ at every point of $\mathcal X$. Indeed the failure probability is at most
$4|\mathcal X|\exp[-N\eta^2/(32A^2)]\le1/2$.
Use these fixed rows as the first-layer means. Under the actual independent Gaussian row noises, Hoeffding now shows, at each fixed input, that
\[
 \left|\frac1N\sum_i b_i h_i^1-r(x)\right|\le\eta,
 \qquad
 \left|\frac1N\sum_i(h_i^1)^2-b_1\right|\le\eta
\]
except on probability at most $4e^{-N\eta^2/(16A^2)}$.

Continue the variance recursion from Lemma~\ref{lem:memorizer}, now starting at $b_1=1-1/U\in[0,1]$. Use layer-two mean weights
$b_i/(Na_2R^{H-1})$, followed by the same later-layer and readout means. All derivative and concentration estimates in that lemma apply verbatim: they used only a deterministic initial variance in $[0,1]$, initial mean error at most $\eta$, and coefficient-square sum at most $A^2/N$. They prove the stated pointwise RMS bound. The second-layer energy is at most $4R^2$, and the conservative total displayed above follows. The Gaussian shift retains the covariance, giving its exact relative entropy.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every strict tanh obstruction has a fixed-dimensional example]
\label{thm:fixed-sharp-range}
Fix $H\ge2$, put $L=H+1$, and let $a>0$, $b\ge0$ satisfy
\[
 Ha+Lb>\frac L{2H}.
\]
There is a fixed integer $D\ge1$, depending only on these exponents and $H$, with the following property. Along sample counts $n=J^D$ with even integers $J\to\infty$, a law on the unit sphere in the fixed dimension $d=D+2$ has conditional mean exactly a depth-$L$ tanh network of width at most $n^a$ and coefficient cap $n^b$. The covariance is diagonal with eigenvalues bounded above and below by positive constants depending only on $D$. It has the individual and mixture excess-risk conclusions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:mixtures}, at learner widths
\[
 N\ge O_{D,H}\left(n^a+n^{(1+2/D)(2-1/H)}\log n\right).
\]
Thus every allowed raw posterior mean has excess at least $1/100$, and every arbitrary mixture of raw or individually clipped raw means has excess at least $1/200000$, with the stated respective high-probability events.

There is also a width-two version: for every $b>1/(2H)$, a fixed dimension $d$ suffices for the same assertions with teacher coefficient cap $n^b$ and no $n^a$ term in the width threshold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $D$ large enough that
\begin{equation}
 Ha+Lb>\frac L2\rho_D,
 \qquad
 \rho_D=\max\left\{\frac2D,\frac{1+2/D}{H}\right\}.
 \label{eq:fixed-slack}
\end{equation}
Such a fixed choice exists because $\rho_D\to1/H$. Put $r_D=1/\sqrt{2D}$ and let each noise coordinate range over the $J$ midpoint grid values
\[
 z_j=r_D(2j+1-J)/J,\qquad 0\le j<J.
\]
There are $n=J^D$ grid points $z\in[-r_D,r_D]^D$. Embed each noise input as
\[
 X=(0,z,\sqrt{1-\|z\|_2^2})\in\R^{D+2}.
\]
It receives probability $p/n$ and an independent fair sign label. The four signal inputs are
\[
 X=(T,0,\sqrt{1-T^2}),\qquad T\in\{-1,-\delta,\delta,1\},
\]
each of probability $q/4$ and label $g(T)$ from the two-chain teacher. All inputs have norm one.

For the growing-width teacher, use $m=\lfloor n^a\rfloor$, $B=n^b$, $r=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$, and $G=rB$. The construction of Lemma~\ref{lem:teacher} extends by replacing each chain neuron with $r$ copies, using weight $B$ on every edge within its group. The internal scalar gain becomes $G$, the readout is $rc(u_H-v_H)$, and
\[
 \delta=4/(BG^H),\qquad B/2\le c\le B,
 \qquad |g(1)|\le2G e^{-2G\tanh(1/2)}.
\]
The same mean-value calculation proves these statements. Its width is at most $m$ and all coefficients are at most $B$. Its conditional mean on the constructed law is exactly $g(T)$, with risk $p$.

Symmetry makes all cross-covariances vanish. The $T$ variance is at least $q/2$. Each noise coordinate has variance
$p r_D^2(1-J^{-2})/3\ge p/(8D)$. The final sphere coordinate has variance at least $pq/4$: noise values are at least $1/\sqrt2$, while the two outer signal values are zero and have total probability $q/2$. All variances are at most one. Thus the input covariance is nondegenerate independently of $n$.

It remains to construct the comparison Gaussian. Given the sample, prescribe the empirical noise means on the grid and zero at all four signal inputs. The discrete Fourier interpolant has coefficient absolute sum at most $\sqrt n$ by Parseval and Cauchy--Schwarz. Taking its real part preserves the exact real grid values. Multiply it by
\[
 m(z)=1-\prod_{\ell=1}^D\cos^2\left(\frac{\pi Jz_\ell}{2r_D}\right).
\]
This mask equals one at every midpoint grid point and zero at $z=0$. Its Fourier coefficient absolute sum is at most two. Thus the resulting real interpolant has a representation by cosines with nonnegative coefficients summing to at most $2\sqrt n$, after absorbing signs and phases. Every frequency has coordinates of magnitude at most $2\pi J/r_D$, hence norm $O_D(J)$ and phase magnitude on the evaluation set at most $T_0=O_D(J)$.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:fourier-native} with fixed $t=1/1000$, $C=2\sqrt n$, and
$U=T_0+K_{D,H}\log(2n)$, for a sufficiently large constant $K_{D,H}$. Its truncation condition holds for all sufficiently large $n$: $\eta^{-1}$ and $C$ grow at most polynomially in $n$, whereas the added window gives an arbitrarily high inverse polynomial tail. Since $D$ is fixed, $U=O_{D,H}(n^{1/D})$. The resulting comparison mean energy and width obey
\[
 K_n=O_{D,H}\left(n^{2/D}+n^{(1+2/D)/H}\right)=O_{D,H}(n^{\rho_D}),
\]
\[
 N_0=O_{D,H}\left(n^{(1+2/D)(2-1/H)}\log n\right).
\]
The comparison output has pointwise RMS error at most $t$ on the full support.

The occupancy event and the small-temperature proof are unchanged, splitting at $\tau_n=1/(100K_n)$. In the large-temperature branch the inner pair differs only in the $T$ coordinate, so Lemma~\ref{lem:sensitivity} gives half-difference
\[
 O_{D,H}(\delta K_n^{L/2})
 =O_{D,H}\left(n^{-Ha-Lb+\rho_D L/2}\right)=o(1)
\]
by \eqref{eq:fixed-slack}. This proves the individual lower bounds. The four signal atoms are fixed repeated atoms, so the sector and occupancy proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:mixtures} applies without change.

For the width-two conclusion set $r=1$, $B=n^b$, and choose $D$ so that $b>\rho_D/2$. Such a choice exists precisely under the asserted strict condition $b>1/(2H)$. The same proof applies, with $\delta=4B^{-L}$ and teacher width two.
\end{proof}

The input dimension in Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-sharp-range} is selected once from the fixed exponent gap and then remains fixed as $n$ grows. The data law and teacher still vary with $n$. The theorem does not claim the endpoint exponent in one universal fixed dimension, and it does not exclude pointwise consistency on a fixed distribution.
