Abstract
We construct fixed regression problems on which very wide Gaussian Bayesian tanh networks
learn slowly even though the true conditional mean is the shallow one-neuron function tanh(x1 ).
For every hidden learner depth H ≥ 3 and every β > 2/H, a fixed finite-dimensional input
law and independent Gaussian observation noise force excess squared risk at least n−β along
a sample subsequence, almost surely. The conclusion holds simultaneously at every learner
width at least n2 and every mean-field-or-stronger Gaussian prior scale, for every likelihood
temperature and every data-dependent mixture of their ordinary raw posterior means. A broad
Gaussian affine output component does not remove the obstruction. The teacher also has an
exact bounded-coefficient implementation at the learner’s depth on the data support. An explicit
finite-depth version gives excess at least cH −6 after 4 · 256H observations in input dimension
4H + 2, at every learner width above eCH . Thus no sample bound polynomial jointly in learner
depth, input dimension, and inverse excess accuracy can hold uniformly for this family. The
proof combines inexpensive Fourier noise memorization, Gaussian-readout geometry, and a
fixed distribution of rare grid blocks. A simpler two-dimensional construction gives constant
excess for width-two teachers with growing coefficients; a larger fixed dimension recovers sharper
coefficient-growth thresholds. These are learning-rate and distribution-uniform obstructions, not
claims of inconsistency or computational hardness.

1

What the negative results say

An agnostic approximation guarantee compares a Bayesian learner’s population loss with the loss
of the best smaller network, allowing the data to have noise or to lie outside that smaller class.
Fixed-depth polynomial oracle bounds can provide such comparisons while remaining independent
of a much larger learner width. This paper asks how fast their remainder can vanish and how it can
depend on learner depth.
One conclusion concerns a fixed regression problem. The conditional mean is g∗ (x) = tanh(x1 ),
which has one hidden neuron and unit coefficients, and the labels have independent Gaussian noise
of variance 1/16. For any chosen learner depth H ≥ 3 and any exponent β > 2/H, we construct
a fixed input law on a unit sphere in a fixed finite dimension. Along a deterministic sequence of
sample counts, every learner of width N ≥ n2 in the specified Gaussian family has excess at least
n−β , almost surely. Arbitrary temperature selection and averaging do not repair the rate. The
result even allows independent broad Gaussian affine output features, including an output bias and
direct linear connections. The construction and its precise width requirements are in Theorem 9.2
and Corollaries 9.4–9.5.

A second conclusion tracks constants as learner depth grows: in dimension 4H +2, excess remains
at least cH −6 after 4 · 256H samples, at every learner width above eCH (Theorem 10.1). This is an
explicit exponential-depth obstruction at inverse-polynomial accuracy. Neither result contradicts a
polynomial sample bound at each fixed depth or establishes exponential depth dependence at one
fixed positive accuracy. The input law in the fixed-problem construction is countably supported; no
claim is made here for a smooth input density.
The underlying tradeoff is explicit. Small likelihood temperatures fit noisy singleton observations
using a comparatively inexpensive deep-network memorizer. Larger temperatures shrink the
nonlinear part of the prediction. Conditional Gaussian readout formulas put all posterior means in
one quadratic constraint set. This supplies a common linear witness, so averaging individually bad
predictors cannot evade the obstruction.
We first give a simpler constant-excess construction in two dimensions. Its teacher coefficients
grow with sample count, so it addresses width-only guarantees that ignore coefficient size. We then
give the Gaussian comparison and mixture tools, improve the exponent ranges in fixed dimension,
and construct the fixed-law slow-rate example. Every learner has one specified dense architecture;
there is no prior over network sizes.

Interpretation and scope

Two different obstructions. The two-dimensional and fixed-dimensional constant-excess constructions concern teacher classes whose coefficients or widths grow with sample count. They show
why an unrestricted width-only oracle comparison fails for this Gaussian family. The later rate
construction has a fixed shallow teacher, fixed coefficients, and one fixed data law. It gives a
vanishing but necessarily slow remainder along a subsequence.

26

Learner depth and target depth. The final teacher is tanh(x1 ) at every learner depth. Its
exact depth-H realization on the support has width two and a coefficient bound below four. The
slow-rate mechanism therefore does not require a deep or unstable true function. It comes from
the noise-fitting possibilities of the unrestricted deep learner. A restriction on the learner’s prior
support changes those possibilities and is outside these negative theorems.
Prediction conventions. The constant-excess mixture theorem covers ordinary raw posterior
means and clipping each such mean before mixing. The stronger fixed-law rate theorem, especially
with broad affine output components and Gaussian labels, concerns ordinary raw means only. It
does not silently identify these with means of clipped posterior draws or with a clipped mixture.
Bayesian and asymptotic scope. The Gaussian-noise corollary is a correctly specified regression
example: the true conditional mean is realizable on the data support and the likelihood family
contains the true observation variance. The theorem ranges over every positive variance, including
data-dependent mixtures. Its width range grows with the sample subsequence, so it does not
contradict fixed-architecture large-sample asymptotics. It neither proves inconsistency nor rules out
other priors, other likelihood constructions, or additional prior features that already represent the
true nonlinear regression function.

supplementary sanity checks and are not proof assumptions. The sign-feature and Gaussian-recursion
constructions extend methods from the companion manuscript Limits of temperature tuning for
Gaussian Bayesian neural networks; all ingredients needed for the present claims are proved here.
