\begin{abstract}
Consider fully connected tanh networks with the critical, or mean-field,
Gaussian prior: first-layer, internal-layer, and readout entry variances
are $1/d$, $1/N$, and $1/N^2$, respectively. We prove that there are
universal constants $A,K_0$ such that, at one common depth
$L_d\le A(d+1)^2$, every Boolean function on $d$ inputs has global-margin
large-deviation rate at most $K_0$. The depth is independent of the function.
The proof first bounds every eigenvalue of the initial tanh feature kernel
below by $\exp[-O(d)]$. Small rank-one covariance tilts then amplify an
arbitrary Boolean component at bounded total relative-entropy cost.
This disproves a polynomial bound on absolute-rate sublevel cardinalities
jointly in dimension and depth, even at a fixed rate threshold. It does
not prove a Bayesian sample-complexity lower bound for a small-circuit
target: differences between competing rates remain uncontrolled.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model, meaning of the result, and proof organization}

Let $\mathcal X=\{-1,1\}^d$, with $d\ge1$, and let $L\ge2$.
A width-$N$ network has $L-1$ hidden layers and computes
\[
 h^0=\bar x=(1,x),\qquad h^s=\tanh(W^s h^{s-1})\quad(1\le s<L),
 \qquad z_w(x)=v^T h^{L-1}.
\]
There are no biases after the augmented input. Under the raw prior
$\mu_{N,1}$, all weights are independent centered Gaussians, with entry
variances $1/d$ in $W^1$, $1/N$ in internal matrices, and $1/N^2$ in $v$.
For a Boolean target $f$, write
\[
 A_{N,f}=\{w:f(x)z_w(x)>1/2\text{ for every }x\in\mathcal X\}.
\]
At fixed $d,L$, the limit
\[
 J_L(f)=\lim_{N\to\infty}-\frac1N\log\mu_{N,1}(A_{N,f})
\]
exists. We reproduce its proof in Appendix~\ref{sec:critical-variational}.
In particular the limit is taken in width with dimension and depth fixed;
no uniform interchange of these limits is asserted.

For a feature law $\nu$ on $[-1,1]^{\mathcal X}$, define
$Q(\nu)=\mathbb E_\nu hh^T$. Let $\mathsf M_Q$ be the law of
coordinatewise tanh applied to a centered Gaussian with covariance $Q$,
and set $Q_0(x,y)=(1+x\cdot y)/d$. The rate has the variational formula
\[
 \begin{split}
 J_L(f)=\inf\biggl\{&D(\nu_1\Vert\mathsf M_{Q_0})+
  \sum_{s=2}^{L-1}D(\nu_s\Vert\mathsf M_{Q(\nu_{s-1})})
  +\tfrac12 z^TQ(\nu_{L-1})^+z:\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{40mm}f(x)z(x)\ge1/2\text{ for every }x\biggr\}.
 \end{split}
\]
The final term is infinite when $z$ is outside the covariance range.
All covariance matrices here are uncentered feature second moments.
The constructed feature laws happen to have zero mean, so those two
notions agree for the main construction.

Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-constant-rate} proves that universal finite
$A,K_0$ exist such that, for each $d$, one common integer
$L_d\le A(d+1)^2$ satisfies $J_{L_d}(f)\le K_0$ for every Boolean $f$.
Thus
\[
 \log\#\{f:J_{L_d}(f)\le K_0\}=2^d\log2.
\]
No polynomial in $d,L$ can bound this logarithmic cardinality at the
fixed threshold $K_0$. Equivalently, for every fixed $d$ and $\eta>0$,
all functions have raw probability at least
$\exp[-N(K_0+\eta)]$ once $N$ is sufficiently large. The width threshold
in this equivalent asymptotic statement is not quantified here.

We first lower-bound the initial feature spectrum. We then track a
rank-one component $vff^T$ through three stages: free variance reduction,
amplification of its fraction of the covariance, and amplification of its
absolute size. The total Gaussian relative entropy stays bounded, and a
final readout realizes $f$ with unit margin at bounded cost.
The proof has received independent AI-agent mathematical reviews; it has
not been formalized in Lean. Constants are deliberately nonoptimized.

\section{What the capacity obstruction does not establish}

The result is about absolute rates. Under the prior conditioned on global
output margin and on a fixed noiseless training sample $S$, the exponential
posterior rate of a consistent function is
\[
 J_L(f)-\min_{g\text{ consistent with }S}J_L(g).
\]
This follows from the finite sum of global-margin function probabilities,
as detailed in the appendix. A universal upper bound on all the $J_L(f)$
does not control these differences. A positive rate gap, however small,
is multiplied by $N$ and can suppress a competitor in the width limit.
Nor does the theorem assert that all Boolean functions have small circuits.
Consequently it is neither a simple-target learning lower bound nor a
claim that the conditioned prior is uniform over Boolean functions.

The restriction to the critical Gaussian scaling also matters. This
construction exploits the relative-entropy cost of changing a whole
hidden feature law at the same speed $N$ as the output event. It does
not identify the corresponding rate or posterior at exponents
$\gamma>1$ or $\gamma<1$.
