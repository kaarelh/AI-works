\section*{Excess Gaussian energy: standalone supplement}
This supplement contains the scalar energy baseline and the excess-energy
estimates from \emph{Depth dependence, internal robustness, and Bayesian
neural-network learning} (17 October 2026). Depth counts all weighted
layers, including the linear readout. The global output margin is $1/2$.
The calculations concern deterministic parameter energy. The cited
simple-target learning result is a separate statistical conclusion of
that report, taken at fixed dimension, width and depth before the
Gaussian variance tends to zero.
\section{Model and scalar transmission baseline}
For input dimension $d\ge1$ and width $N\ge1$, write
\[
 h^0=(1,x),\qquad h^s=\tanh(W^sh^{s-1})\ (1\le s<L),
 \qquad z_w(x)=v^Th^{L-1},\qquad x\in\{-1,1\}^d.
\]
There are no later biases. The Gaussian energy is
\[
 \kappa_N(w)=\frac dN\|W^1\|_F^2+
 \sum_{s=2}^{L-1}\|W^s\|_F^2+N\|v\|_2^2.
\]
A hidden perturbation $\eta$ has normalized RMS size
$\|\eta\|_2/\sqrt N$. A constant-channel network has repeated
first-layer bias $a$, internal entries $b_s/N$, and readout entries
$c/N$. Its amplitudes and energy obey the scalar optimization below,
which is also meaningful for a real first-layer cost coefficient $d>0$.
