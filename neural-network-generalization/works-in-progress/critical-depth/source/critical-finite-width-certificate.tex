\section{Finite-width entropy certificates on input witnesses}
\label{sec:critical-finite-witness}

The finite-width argument uses forward conditional entropy and a finite
set of input probes. It does not replace a continuous empirical measure
by an absolutely continuous law of the same moments. Instead, a short
revealed prefix of an exchangeable layer supplies a conditional one-row
law whose moments approximate the empirical moments. This permits an
additive action error, with a polynomial width requirement.

\begin{lemma}[Exchangeable entropy decrement]
\label{lem:critical-entropy-decrement}
Let $P_N\ll\mu^{\otimes N}$ be exchangeable, with
$B=D(P_N\Vert\mu^{\otimes N})<\infty$, and let
$1\le K\le N-2$. Independently choose $k$ uniformly from
$\{0,\ldots,K-1\}$ and put
$\nu=P_N(X_{k+1}\in\cdot\mid X_1,\ldots,X_k)$.
Then
\[
 \mathbb E D(\nu\Vert\mu)\le\frac B{N-K}.
\]
For every measurable $\phi:E\to[-1,1]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-entropy-decrement-moment}
 \mathbb E\left|\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\phi(X_i)-\nu(\phi)\right|^2
 \le8(K/N)^2+\frac2{N-K}
                 +2\sqrt{\frac{2B}{K(N-K)}}.
\end{equation}
The same assertions hold conditionally on a past variable, and may
then be averaged over that variable.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write
$a_j=\mathbb E D(P_N(X_{j+1}\in\cdot\mid X_1,\ldots,X_j)
\Vert\mu)$. The entropy chain rule gives $\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_j=B$.
Exchangeability and the conditional entropy chain rule give
\[
 a_{j+1}-a_j
 =I(X_{j+2};X_{j+1}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_j)\ge0.
\]
Consequently $a_j\le B/(N-j)$ and
\[
 \frac1K\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}
 I(X_{j+2};X_{j+1}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_j)
 \le\frac B{K(N-K)}.
\]
Given the realized prefix, denote the relative entropy of the
conditional pair law to the product of its marginals by $I_k$.
The unrevealed empirical average has conditional mean $\nu(\phi)$
and conditional variance at most
$1/(N-k)+\sqrt{2I_k}$: the diagonal terms are at most one, and
Pinsker's inequality bounds each off-diagonal covariance by
$\sqrt{2I_k}$. Replacing this average by the full empirical average
changes it by at most $2K/N$. Squaring, averaging and using concavity
of the square root proves
\eqref{eq:critical-entropy-decrement-moment}.
\end{proof}

Let $\mu_N$ denote the original, unconditioned critical Gaussian
network prior. A \emph{function event} means an event determined by
the network's real-valued input--output function. In the following
lemma it is enough that the event be invariant under hidden-neuron
permutations. Covariances are uncentered second moments throughout.

\begin{lemma}[An approximate path from a finite-width event]
\label{lem:critical-finite-witness-extraction}
Fix a nonempty set $Z$ of $m$ cube inputs. Let $E$ be a function event
on which every output at $Z$ has absolute value at least $1/2$, and
suppose $\mu_N(E)\ge e^{-NA}>0$. Let $H=L-1\ge1$,
$0<\Delta\le1$ and $0<\tau\le1$.
There is a universal polynomial $p$ such that, whenever
\[
 N\ge p(m,H,A+1,\Delta^{-1},\tau^{-1}),
\]
there is a realization in $E$, with empirical hidden kernels
$Q_0,\ldots,Q_H$ on $Z$ and output $z$, and controlled laws
$\rho_j\ll\mathcal N(0,Q_{j-1})$, for which
\[
 \left\|\mathbb E_{\rho_j}[\tanh(G)\tanh(G)^{\mathsf T}]-Q_j
                         \right\|_{\max}\le\Delta,
\]
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^H D(\rho_j\Vert\mathcal N(0,Q_{j-1}))
          +\frac12z^{\mathsf T}Q_H^\dagger z\le A+\tau.
\]
Here $Q_0$ is the original input kernel, $Q_0(x,x)\le2$,
$Q_j(x,x)\le1$ for $j\ge1$, and $z\in\operatorname{range}(Q_H)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $P=\mu_N(\cdot\mid E)$, so $D(P\Vert\mu_N)\le NA$.
Disintegrate $P$ in forward order by hidden weight blocks and then
the readout. Let $B_j$ and $B_{\rm out}$ be the resulting conditional
relative entropies. Thus
\[
 \sum_j\mathbb E B_j+\mathbb E B_{\rm out}\le NA.
\]
Given all preceding weight blocks, the current block is exchangeable
in its rows under $P$. Indeed, permuting these rows and the corresponding
columns of the next block preserves the prior and $E$; all future
blocks have been integrated out. Its reference law is the original
product Gaussian row law. On $Z$ a reference row has preactivation
law exactly $\mathcal N(0,Q_{j-1})$.

At each hidden layer apply Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-entropy-decrement},
using an independently randomized prefix length. Push the resulting
conditional row law to its preactivations on $Z$, calling it $\rho_j$,
and put $D_j=D(\rho_j\Vert\mathcal N(0,Q_{j-1}))$ and
$\widetilde Q_j=\mathbb E_{\rho_j}\tanh(G)\tanh(G)^{\mathsf T}$.
Data processing and the lemma give
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_j D_j\le\frac{\sum_j\mathbb E B_j}{N-K}
\]
and, after summing the $m^2$ bounded covariance observables,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-finite-witness-moment-sum}
 \mathbb E\sum_j\|\widetilde Q_j-Q_j\|_F^2
 \le m^2\left\{8H(K/N)^2+\frac{2H}{N-K}
               +2\sqrt{\frac{2HNA}{K(N-K)}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The last term uses Cauchy--Schwarz across layers.

We retain the actual random readout. Given the hidden blocks, its
output under the reference prior is Gaussian with covariance $Q_H/N$.
For $r=\tfrac12z^{\mathsf T}Q_H^\dagger z$ and $0<s<1$,
Gaussian integration, including singular $Q_H$, gives
\[
 \log\mathbb E_{\mu_N}\left[e^{(1-s)Nr}\mid\text{hidden blocks}\right]
 =\frac{\operatorname{rank}(Q_H)}2\log(1/s)
 \le\frac m2\log(1/s).
\]
Entropy duality therefore implies
\[
 \mathbb E r\le
 \frac{\mathbb E B_{\rm out}/N+(m/(2N))\log(1/s)}{1-s}.
\]
In particular, if $K/N\le\eta$ and $s=\eta\le1/16$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-finite-witness-action-mean}
 \mathbb E\left(\sum_jD_j+r\right)
 \le\frac{A+(m/(2N))\log(1/\eta)}{1-\eta}.
\end{equation}
The projected readout has rank at most $m$, so its additive term is
$m\log(1/\eta)/(2N)$ before the displayed factor $1/(1-\eta)$.
It can therefore be made smaller than any prescribed action tolerance
by increasing width; no fixed positive readout penalty is introduced.

For explicit polynomial choices take
\[
 \eta=\min\{1/16,\tau/[32(A+1)]\},\qquad
 \kappa=\min\{\eta,\Delta\sqrt\eta/(32m\sqrt H)\},
 \qquad K=\lfloor\kappa N\rfloor.
\]
It suffices to require, for a large universal $C$,
\[
 N\ge C\left\{\kappa^{-1}
       +\frac{Hm^2}{\eta\Delta^2}
       +\frac{HA m^4}{\kappa\eta^2\Delta^4}
       +\frac{m\log(1/\eta)}\tau\right\}.
\]
Then $1\le K\le N-2$ and
\eqref{eq:critical-finite-witness-moment-sum} is at most
$\eta\Delta^2$. With probability at least $1-\eta$, all moment
errors are at most $\Delta$. Dividing the expectation in
\eqref{eq:critical-finite-witness-action-mean} by $1-\eta$ shows that
some realization in this good event has action at most $A+\tau$.
All preceding conditions are polynomial in the stated arguments.
The kernels and output of this realization have the remaining
properties by their empirical Gram representation. No conditioning
on future weight blocks was used in defining the layer costs.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-width representation on an input witness]
\label{thm:critical-finite-witness-compression}
There is a universal $C$ with the following property. Put
\[
 \epsilon=(A-V_*)_+,\qquad
 R=(d+L+A+2)^C\exp(C\sqrt{L\epsilon}).
\]
The universal constant can be chosen so that, for every set $Z$ of
$m$ cube inputs and every function event $E$ satisfying
$\mu_N(E)\ge e^{-NA}>0$ and $|z(x)|\ge1/2$ on $Z$,
\[
 N\ge R(m+1)^C
\]
implies that the sign restriction to $Z$ of some realization in $E$
is represented by an ordinary depth-$L$ tanh network of width at most
$R$. Its weights are arbitrary finite real numbers.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The empty witness is trivial. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-finite-witness-extraction} with
$\tau=1/(10L)$ and a moment tolerance $\Delta$ to be chosen.
Write $D_j$ for the extracted layer costs and $r$ for its terminal
readout cost. Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-approximate-entropy-budget}
gives
\[
 S:=\sum_j\sqrt{D_j}
 \le C\{\log(L+1)+\sqrt{L\epsilon+1}\},
\]
provided $\Delta\le c/L^4$.

For positive semidefinite $m$ by $m$ matrices whose entries differ by
at most $\Delta$, the square-root Gaussian coupling gives
\[
 \Delta_\infty(Q,\widetilde Q)
 \le\|Q^{1/2}-\widetilde Q^{1/2}\|_F
 \le\|Q-\widetilde Q\|_*^{1/2}
 \le m\sqrt\Delta.
\]
Here the middle inequality is the standard square-root trace inequality.
Starting from the original input kernel, repeatedly transfer the
extracted control law to the constructed preceding kernel using the
controlled Gaussian-coupling transfer theorem. This does not increase
its relative entropy. The constructed output kernels $Q'_j$ obey
\[
 e_j:=\Delta_\infty(Q_j,Q'_j)
 \le(1+\sqrt{2D_j})e_{j-1}+m\sqrt\Delta,\qquad e_0=0.
\]
Thus $e_H\le Hm\sqrt\Delta\,e^{\sqrt2 S}$.
The readout transfer has cost at most $r$ and changes each output by
at most $\sqrt{2r}\,e_H$. Choose $\Delta$ small enough that this is
at most $1/16$. Together with $\Delta\le c/L^4$, the choice can be
made with
\[
 \Delta^{-1}\le (m+1)^C(d+L+A+2)^C
                           e^{C\sqrt{L\epsilon}}.
\]
Consequently the polynomial width requirement in the extraction lemma
is bounded by $R(m+1)^C$, on enlarging $C$.

The resulting consistent controlled path has the same input kernel,
layer costs at most $D_j$, and outputs with the original signs and
margin at least $7/16$ on $Z$. Apply the anchored compression theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored-critical-compression}) to this path. Its
proof applies on any finite set, with $\log|Z|$ in place of the cube
union-bound factor $d$; since $|Z|\le2^d$, the same stated bound using
$d$ applies. The uniform compression error is at most $3/32$, so all
signs are preserved. Its width is at most
$\operatorname{poly}(d,L,A+2)e^{CS}$, which is bounded by $R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A finite-width exceptional-mass bound]
\label{cor:critical-finite-width-exceptional-mass}
There are universal $C,c>0$ such that, for $A\ge0$ and
\[
 R=(d+L+A+2)^C\exp(C\sqrt{L(A-V_*)_+}),
\]
one can choose a class $\mathcal G_A$ of Boolean functions with
$\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal G_A)\le R$ for which, at every
$N\ge R^C$,
\[
 \mu_N\{\text{global margin, sign}(z)\notin\mathcal G_A\}
 \le \exp(dR\log2-NA).
\]
The class is independent of the training sample and may be chosen
independent of $N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $M$ be the witness-compression width from
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-finite-witness-compression}, and let $v$ be
a universal upper bound for the VC dimension of depth-$L$, width-$M$
tanh networks on the cube. The standard polynomial VC bound for tanh
networks gives $v\le\operatorname{poly}(d,L,M)$.
Take $m=\lfloor v\rfloor+1$ and enlarge $R$ so that $m\le R$.
If $m>2^d$, use the class of all Boolean functions and the conclusion
is immediate.

Otherwise define $\mathcal G_A$ to contain those functions whose
restriction to every $m$-element input set is represented by a
width-$M$, depth-$L$ tanh network. If this class shattered an
$m$-element set, the representing networks would also shatter it,
contrary to their VC bound. Hence its VC dimension is less than $m$.
For each fixed $m$-element set $Z$, let $E_Z$ be the event of a
globally margined network whose sign restriction at $Z$ has no such
representation. The preceding theorem implies $\mu_N(E_Z)<e^{-NA}$
whenever $N\ge R^C$. A union bound over at most $(2^d)^m$ choices
of $Z$ gives the claimed exceptional-mass estimate. All constructions
depend on $A,d,L$, but not on the training sample or on $N$.
\end{proof}
