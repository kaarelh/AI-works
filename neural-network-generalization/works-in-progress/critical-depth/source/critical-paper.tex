\begin{abstract}
Can a Bayesian neural network learn with polynomial dependence on its
learner depth, without restricting the prior to internally stable networks?
We prove such bounds for the original critical Gaussian prior on tanh
networks, with the usual global output-margin condition and exact noiseless
training fit. The bounds hold at every width capable of implementing the
target. Every parity has polynomial sample complexity once learner depth
is a sufficiently large multiple of $\log(d+L+2)$, independently of parity
degree. More generally the bound is polynomial in Fourier variation.
A bounded-weight tanh teacher of depth $D$ gives $R^{O(D+1)}$ samples,
where $R$ includes dimension, teacher resources, and learner depth, when
$L\ge C(D+1)\log R$. Boolean threshold teachers need no bound on their
original coefficient magnitudes. The exponent still grows with teacher
depth: joint polynomial dependence on arbitrary teacher width and depth
remains open.

The proof gives an intrinsic theorem at every finite width $N$. If
$a_N$ is the target's normalized negative log-prior mass, the sample bound
is a universal polynomial in dimension, depth, $a_N$, and logarithmic
accuracy factors, times $\exp(C\sqrt{L(a_N-V_*)_+})$, where
$V_*\in(1,2)$ is a scalar baseline. Forward conditional entropy extracts
approximate covariance paths from finite-width events. Preserving a small
input-anchor covariance block then gives finite tanh representations and
VC bounds. A separate finite-width construction tracks the target signal
and residual variances to bound its actual prior mass. Ordinary VC bounds
cover the remaining small widths.

We also obtain bounds with only polynomial dependence on sequential length
for several sequential target classes, a quasipolynomial bound for general formulas, sharp excess-rate
estimates, and complementary parity lower bounds at smaller depths.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and meaning of the result}

Let $\mathcal X=\{-1,1\}^d$, $d\ge1$, and $L\ge2$. A width-$N$
network has $L-1$ hidden layers and computes
\[
 h^0(x)=\bar x=(1,x),\qquad
 h^s(x)=\tanh(W^s h^{s-1}(x))\quad(1\le s<L),\qquad
 z_w(x)=v^T h^{L-1}(x).
\]
There are no biases after the augmented input. Under the raw critical
Gaussian prior $\mu_{N,1}$, entries are independent centered Gaussians
with variances $1/d$ in $W^1$, $1/N$ in internal matrices, and $1/N^2$
in $v$. For a Boolean function $f$, write
\[
 A_{N,f}=\{w:f(x)z_w(x)>1/2\text{ for every }x\in\mathcal X\},
 \qquad
 J_L(f)=\lim_{N\to\infty}-\frac1N\log\mu_{N,1}(A_{N,f}).
\]
The limit exists at each fixed $d,L$. Its proof, including the strict
margin convention, appears in Appendix~\ref{sec:critical-variational}.

For a feature law $\nu$ on $[-1,1]^{\mathcal X}$, let
$Q(\nu)=\mathbb E_\nu hh^T$. Let $\mathsf M_Q$ be the law of
coordinatewise tanh applied to a centered Gaussian with covariance $Q$,
and put $Q_0(x,y)=(1+x\cdot y)/d$. The rate is
\[
\begin{split}
 J_L(f)=\inf\biggl\{&D(\nu_1\Vert\mathsf M_{Q_0})+
  \sum_{s=2}^{L-1}D(\nu_s\Vert\mathsf M_{Q(\nu_{s-1})})
  +\tfrac12 z^TQ(\nu_{L-1})^+z:\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{35mm}f(x)z(x)\ge1/2\quad(x\in\mathcal X)\biggr\}.
\end{split}
\]
The last term is infinite outside the covariance range. The matrices
$Q(\nu)$ are uncentered second moments, not necessarily centered
covariances.

\subsection{Learning at every realizable width}

A width is \emph{realizable} if a depth-$L$, width-$N$ tanh network
of the specified architecture implements the target's signs on the cube
with no zero output. Rescaling its readout gives strict output margin $1/2$; continuity then
makes $A_{N,f}$ have positive Gaussian probability. Our target-based
learning theorems hold at every such width, without an additional width
threshold. In particular, they hold at every width above the minimum
implementing width at the specified learner depth.

Training inputs are independent draws from any distribution
$\mathcal D$ on the cube and have noiseless labels $f(x)$. The learner
conditions the original Gaussian prior on $|z_w(x)|>1/2$ at every input
and on exact training signs. No internal stability event is added.
Every sample bound below ensures, with training probability at least
$1-\delta$, that the correct-label posterior probability exceeds $2/3$
outside a set of $\mathcal D$-mass at most $\varepsilon$.
The conclusions apply to the full posterior, including mixtures.

For Fourier variation
$A_F(f)=\sum_{S\subseteq[d]}|\widehat f(S)|$, put
$R_F=(d+L+2)A_F(f)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-all-width-fourier-learning}
gives the sufficient sample bound
\[
 n\ge\frac C\varepsilon\left\{
 R_F^c(1+\log(1/\varepsilon))^c+\log(1/\delta)\right\}
 \qquad\text{if }L\ge C_0\log R_F.
\]
Every signed parity has $A_F=1$. This therefore gives polynomial
samples for all parities, including full parity, at every realizable
width and at sufficiently large logarithmic learner depth.

For a depth-$D$, width-$C_T$ tanh teacher with parameter magnitudes
bounded by $B\ge1$ and global signed output margin at least $1/2$, put
$R=d+C_T+B+D+L+2$. Corollary~\ref{cor:critical-all-width-analog-teacher-learning}
gives
\[
 n\ge\frac C\varepsilon\left\{
 R^{c(D+1)}(1+\log(1/\varepsilon))^c+\log(1/\delta)\right\}
 \qquad\text{if }L\ge C_0(D+1)\log R.
\]
The constants $c,C,C_0$ are universal. Thus learner depth enters
polynomially for each fixed teacher depth. The exponent still depends
on teacher depth, and joint polynomial dependence on arbitrary teacher
width and depth remains open.

For Boolean threshold teachers, the corresponding bound does not depend
on the magnitudes or precision of their real coefficients. A teacher of
width $C_T$ and depth $h$ gives $R^{O(h)}$ samples with
$R=d+C_T+h+L+2$, at $L\ge Ch\log R$; see
Corollary~\ref{cor:critical-all-width-real-threshold-learning}.
In particular every Boolean halfspace has a polynomial sample bound
at every width $N\ge1$ under the logarithmic learner-depth condition.

Several target classes permit milder dependence on their original
sequential depth. Decision trees and two-state branching programs have
polynomial Fourier variation. Programs over fixed solvable finite groups,
including permutation programs of width at most four, have sample bounds
polynomial in their sequential length, with an exponent depending only on
the group. A fixed-rank first-layer bottleneck gives
polynomial dependence on teacher product norm even when the remaining
teacher depth is arbitrary. For general formulas the bound is quasipolynomial in dimension and
formula size at polynomial learner depth.
The precise resource and depth conditions, all at every realizable width,
appear in Section~\ref{sec:critical-all-width-target-learning}.

\subsection{An intrinsic bound and its limiting-rate counterpart}

There is a universal scalar baseline $V_*\in(1,2)$, defined below
as the limiting scalar cost of producing the required output margin
from a vanishing Gaussian state. At a realizable width put
\[
 a_N(f)=-\frac1N\log\mu_{N,1}(A_{N,f}).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-intrinsic-finite-width-learning} proves the
following sufficient sample bound at that exact width:
\[
 \frac C\varepsilon\left\{
 \big[(d+L+a_N(f)+2)(1+\log(1/\varepsilon))\big]^c
 \exp\!\left(C\sqrt{L(a_N(f)-V_*)_+}\right)
 +\log(1/\delta)\right\}.
\]
It depends on width only through the actual raw target cost.
The finite-width constructions in
Sections~\ref{sec:critical-finite-width-channel} and
\ref{sec:critical-finite-width-circuit-mass} bound this cost explicitly
for the preceding target classes once width exceeds a quantified
threshold. Ordinary network VC bounds handle all smaller realizable
widths. This is how the target-based conclusions remove that threshold.

A limiting-rate version is also useful. If $J_L(f)\le a$, then
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-excess-learning} gives, at all sufficiently
large widths,
\[
 n\ge\frac C\varepsilon\left\{
 [(d+1)(L+1)(a+2)]^c
 \exp\!\left(C\sqrt{L(a-V_*)_+}\right)+\log(1/\delta)\right\}.
\]
This rate-based statement alone does not quantify its width threshold.
The finite-width constructions bound the cost of explicit target
representations; they do not assert convergence within $1/L$ to the
unknown optimal rate $J_L(f)$ for every target.

\subsection{Why the finite representation is possible}

For a path with layer entropies $D_s$ and action at most
$V_*+\eta$, Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-sqrt-entropy-budget} gives
\[
 S:=\sum_s\sqrt{D_s}
 \le C[\log(L+1)+\sqrt{L\eta}].
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored-critical-compression} constructs a
same-depth finite tanh network with the same Boolean function and
width at most $\operatorname{poly}(d,L,A_0)e^{CS}$, where
$A_0=\max\{1,\text{total action}\}$.
A greedy procedure selects inputs whose evaluations account for
all significant conditional entropies and for the readout. Their
covariance block is then preserved exactly at every layer.
Leverage-based importance sampling and a positive definite correction
approximate each remaining input jointly with these anchors.
The correction is absorbed into the next incoming matrix, so it
adds no nonlinear layer.

This construction avoids requiring a low-rank approximation in the
full Gaussian-coupling metric. Such an approximation can be impossible
for a high-rank covariance, even when only one final Boolean readout
matters. The compressed network's weights may be very large; the
classical polynomial VC bound for sigmoid networks still controls
its number of Boolean sign patterns. Applying a finite-class
consistency bound to all functions of rate at most $J_L(f)$ then
gives the rate-based posterior theorem.

For finite width, forward conditional entropy and exchangeability
extract approximate covariance paths on a finite set of input probes.
A projected readout entropy bound preserves the scalar baseline, and
the entropy estimate is stable under the resulting moment errors.
Local finite-network representations then give a sample-independent
class of controlled VC dimension and a quantitative bound on its
exceptional prior mass. Ordinary network VC bounds cover the remaining
smaller widths.

\subsection{Rate estimates and complementary lower bounds}

The rate estimates used in the learning theorem are proved in this
paper. Universal flattening gives, for every Boolean function,
\[
 V_*-\frac C{L-1}\le J_L(f)\le
 V_*+C\frac{(d+1)^2+[\log(2L)]^2}{L}
 \qquad(L\ge C(d+1)).
\]
A bound polynomial in normalized excess alone would be insufficient:
at sufficiently large polynomial depth that quantity is polynomial
for every truth table, whereas arbitrary truth tables require
exponentially many observations. The exponential of its square root
in our capacity bound is consistent with this obstruction.

Controlled Fourier features give
\[
 J_L(f)\le V_*+
 C\frac{[\log(d+L+2)+\log A_F(f)]^2}{L}
\]
under the stated depth condition. A layerwise circuit simulation and
finite-precision teacher compilation give
$J_L(f)\le V_*+C(D+1)^2\log^2R/L$ for the teachers above.
These constructions supply the two learning corollaries.
Every sample of $n$ consistently labeled points also has an
extension with excess rate $O(\log^2(d+n+L)/L)$ at sufficient depth.
This bounds the posterior's rate without asserting that all such
extensions are accurate.

The excess-rate scale is sharp for constants, dictators, and all
parities: it is $\Theta(\log^2d/L)$ between sufficiently large
logarithmic depth and a sufficiently small fixed power of dimension.
The lower bound holds for every Boolean function. A separate
comparison proves that, for all sufficiently large $d$ depending on
$\theta$, full parity still has limiting Gibbs error $1/2$ with high
training probability when
\[
 2\le L\le c\theta\frac{\log d}{\log\log d},
 \qquad n\le d^{(1-\theta)/(L-1)},\qquad 0<\theta<1.
\]
Its confidence-error lower limit is at least $1/4$, including
mixtures of minimizers. This polynomial lower bound at smaller
depths is consistent with the positive logarithmic-depth result.

Neither rate flattening nor our learning proof makes the function
prior uniform. For a sample $S$, a consistent function's exponential
posterior rate is
\[
 J_L(g)-\min_{h\text{ consistent with }S}J_L(h).
\]
Any positive gap is multiplied by width $N$. In the rate-based
statements, dimension, depth, and sample size are fixed before width
tends to infinity. The intrinsic theorem and target-based every-width theorems instead
apply at each specified realizable width.
The analytic proofs have received independent AI-agent mathematical
review; they have not been formalized in Lean. Constants are not
optimized, and no efficient posterior-sampling algorithm is claimed.
