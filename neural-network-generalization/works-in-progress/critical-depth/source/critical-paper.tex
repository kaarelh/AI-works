\begin{abstract}
For fully connected tanh networks under the critical Gaussian prior, we
study the exponential probability cost of realizing a Boolean function
with a fixed output margin on the entire input cube. We prove that this
cost converges, with increasing depth, to the same universal constant
$V_*$ for every Boolean function. The constant satisfies $1<V_*<2$ and
is characterized by a one-dimensional relative-entropy control problem.
Quantitative bounds are uniform over all functions on $d$ inputs.
For structured targets, controlled feature laws give sharper bounds:
Fourier norm and circuit depth enter through small target components
that can be amplified at low entropy cost. In particular, every parity
attains a bounded rate at depth $\Theta((\log d)^2)$, the same scale
required even by constants and dictators. A layerwise construction also
gives polynomial teacher-depth dependence in the rate upper bound for
arbitrary bounded-weight, globally margined tanh teachers on the cube.

Every consistently labeled sample of $n$ points has a globally margined
extension with rate at most
$V_*+O(\log^2(d+n+L)/L)$ at sufficient depth. The actual width-first
posterior consequently concentrates below this ceiling. These results
do not establish polynomial-depth learning: small differences inside
that interval can determine which extensions the posterior prefers.
A separate comparison does give a sample lower bound for full parity:
for depths up to a small constant times $\eta\log d/\log\log d$,
$n\le d^{(1-\eta)/(L-1)}$ examples leave
limiting Gibbs error $1/2$ with high sample probability, for every
fixed $\eta>0$. This is a polynomial lower bound, not an exponential
bound in parity degree. We also bound the accumulated square-root
layer entropy by $O(\log L+\sqrt{L\epsilon})$ for a path of cost at
most $V_*+\epsilon$. This yields matching excess-rate bounds
$J_L(f)-V_*=\Theta(\log^2 d/L)$ for constants, dictators, and parities
over a specified depth range. The proofs include the finite-domain
large-deviation foundation and exact feature constructions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and meaning of the result}

Let $\mathcal X=\{-1,1\}^d$, $d\ge1$, and $L\ge2$. A width-$N$
network has $L-1$ hidden layers and computes
\[
 h^0(x)=\bar x=(1,x),\qquad
 h^s(x)=\tanh(W^s h^{s-1}(x))\quad(1\le s<L),\qquad
 z_w(x)=v^T h^{L-1}(x).
\]
There are no biases after the augmented input. Under the raw critical
Gaussian prior $\mu_{N,1}$, entries are independent centered Gaussians
with variances $1/d$ in $W^1$, $1/N$ in internal matrices, and $1/N^2$
in $v$. For a Boolean function $f$, write
\[
 A_{N,f}=\{w:f(x)z_w(x)>1/2\text{ for every }x\in\mathcal X\},
 \qquad
 J_L(f)=\lim_{N\to\infty}-\frac1N\log\mu_{N,1}(A_{N,f}).
\]
The limit exists at each fixed $d,L$. Its proof, including the strict
margin convention, appears in Appendix~\ref{sec:critical-variational}.

For a feature law $\nu$ on $[-1,1]^{\mathcal X}$, let
$Q(\nu)=\mathbb E_\nu hh^T$. Let $\mathsf M_Q$ be the law of
coordinatewise tanh applied to a centered Gaussian with covariance $Q$,
and put $Q_0(x,y)=(1+x\cdot y)/d$. The rate is
\[
\begin{split}
 J_L(f)=\inf\biggl\{&D(\nu_1\Vert\mathsf M_{Q_0})+
  \sum_{s=2}^{L-1}D(\nu_s\Vert\mathsf M_{Q(\nu_{s-1})})
  +\tfrac12 z^TQ(\nu_{L-1})^+z:\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{35mm}f(x)z(x)\ge1/2\quad(x\in\mathcal X)\biggr\}.
\end{split}
\]
The last term is infinite outside the covariance range. The matrices
$Q(\nu)$ are uncentered second moments, not necessarily centered
covariances.

The main result, Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-rate-flattening}, identifies
a universal constant $V_*$ such that, with $D=d+1$,
\[
 V_*-\frac C{L-1}\le J_L(f)\le
 V_*+C\frac{D^2+[\log(2L)]^2}{L}
 \qquad(L\ge CD).
\]
The same bound holds for every Boolean function at the same specified
depth. In particular, an absolute rate budget cannot provide a
polynomial bound on the logarithm of the number of possible functions:
at sufficiently large polynomial depth, a fixed budget already contains
all $2^{2^d}$ functions.

There are sharper upper bounds for small implementations.
Corollary~\ref{cor:analog-teacher-critical-rate} applies to any
depth-$D$, width-$C$ tanh teacher with weights bounded by $B$ and
signed output at least $1/2$ on the cube. Put
$R=d+C+B+D+L+2$. At learner depth $L\ge A(D+1)\log R$, it gives
\[
 J_L(f)\le V_*+A\frac{(D+1)^2\log^2 R}{L}.
\]
The constant $A$ is universal. Thus exponential teacher-depth
dependence can be removed from this critical prior-rate upper bound
without restricting the prior's support. The proof compiles the
teacher at finite precision, propagates exact Boolean gate vectors
through low-entropy mixture laws, and then amplifies the target
component. Theorem~\ref{thm:controlled-fourier-seed} supplies another
bound through the Fourier $\ell^1$ norm. In particular, all parities
attain a bounded rate at depth of order $(\log d)^2$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:near-baseline-posterior-ceiling} gives a different,
sample-dependent comparison: every consistent sample of at most $n$
points has an extension with rate at most
$V_*+A\log^2(d+n+L+2)/L$ when $L\ge A\log(d+n+L+2)$.
The actual width-first posterior concentrates below this ceiling.
This narrows the interval in which accurate and inaccurate extensions
must be compared; it does not supply that comparison.

Theorems~\ref{thm:critical-directional-budget}
and~\ref{thm:critical-excess-dimension-lower} describe the paths inside
this interval. For a path with hidden entropy costs $D_s$ and total
cost at most $V_*+\epsilon$, they give
\[
 \sum_s\sqrt{D_s}\le C\{\log(L+1)+\sqrt{L\epsilon}\},
 \qquad
 J_L(f)\ge V_*+c\frac{\log^2 d}{L}
 \quad(2\le L\le d^{c_0},\ d\ge d_0).
\]
The latter bound holds for every Boolean function. Together with the
Fourier construction it determines the excess-rate scale for constants,
dictators, and all parities when $C\log(d+2)\le L\le d^{c_0}$.
It does not determine their ordering within that scale.

Section~\ref{sec:critical-bures-transfer} turns the entropy estimate
into a covariance-stability theorem. Under a suitable transfer of
the controlled laws, one hidden transition amplifies Gaussian-coupling
distance by at most $1+\sqrt{2D_s}$. Thus the complete factor is
at most $C(L+1)^C\exp\{C\sqrt{L\epsilon}\}$. This is polynomial
in depth when $L\epsilon=O(\log^2 L)$. A corresponding readout
transfer bounds uniform output error without a factor depending on
the number of inputs. The theorem does not construct a small finite
network: generic high-rank covariances cannot be approximated by
low-rank ones in this distance.

A further decomposition separates changes to one selected input pair
from the remaining conditional entropy. When the latter vanishes, a
symmetry argument gives a shallow polynomial-product implementation.
An independent Fourier argument gives such an implementation whenever
the terminal kernel is invariant under Boolean translations. Neither
restriction is known for arbitrary optimizing paths. Small positive
conditional entropy alone is insufficient for depth-uniform stability;
Section~\ref{sec:critical-boolean-kernel-geometry} gives an explicit
example. Turning the square-root entropy estimate into a general
representation bound remains open.

This is not a claim that the conditioned prior is approximately uniform.
For a fixed training sample $S$, its exponential posterior rate at a
consistent function is
\[
 J_L(f)-\min_{g\text{ consistent with }S}J_L(g).
\]
Any positive difference, however small, is multiplied by width $N$.
The theorem neither orders these differences nor controls finite-width
probabilities to accuracy $1/N$. The general sample-complexity question
for small deep circuits remains open. All limits in width are taken
with dimension and depth fixed before studying the resulting function
$J_L$ as depth varies.

A complementary theorem describes the cost scale at every depth. Put
\[
 \Phi_L(d)=1+L\bigl(d^{1/(2L)}-1\bigr)^2.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-depth-profile} gives universal constants
$0<c<C$ such that every target has $J_L(f)\ge c\Phi_L(d)$, while
constants and dictators have $J_L(f)\le C\Phi_L(d)$.
Thus this expression characterizes the minimum absolute-rate scale
without asserting which function minimizes the exact rate at every
dimension and depth. Later sections give a target-relative separation
and a polynomial sample lower bound. The sharper comparison in
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-nearly-logarithmic-depth} covers growing
depths $L\le c\eta\log d/\log\log d$, with limiting Gibbs error
$1/2$ and confidence-error lower limit at least $1/4$. It concerns
the actual posterior, including mixtures of minimizing functions.

The proof first supplies a finite-width exponential potential. It then
uses a quantitative first-layer spectrum bound to seed an arbitrary
Boolean component, reduces the unused variance without entropy cost,
and amplifies that component through small covariance changes. A scalar
control problem describes the universal final cost. The arguments have
received independent AI-agent mathematical review; they have not been
formalized in Lean. Constants are not optimized.
