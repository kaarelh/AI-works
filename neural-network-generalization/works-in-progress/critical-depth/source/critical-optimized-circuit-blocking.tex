\section{Optimizing the circuit prefix and blocking Boolean layers}
\label{sec:critical-optimized-blocking}

The circuit construction can spend more than $1/L$ entropy in its
prefix. Optimizing that expenditure for the sample bound improves the
teacher-width and depth dependence. Blocking several Boolean layers
into a truth-table computation gives a further improvement. These
bounds still do not give polynomial sample complexity for general
small deep circuits.

\begin{proposition}[A prefix budget adapted to learning]
\label{prop:critical-optimized-prefix-budget}
Consider a layered Boolean threshold implementation of depth $q\ge1$.
Let $C_j$ be its state width at stage $j$. Suppose the exact feature
repair for every gate at that stage has entropy cost at most $P_j/r$
when the incoming covariance dominates $rHH^{\mathsf T}$, where
$P_j\ge1$. For $L\ge q$, put
\[
 T=1+\log d+\log(2L)+
          \sum_{j=1}^q\log\left(\frac{4C_jP_jL}{q}\right).
\]
There are universal constants $C_0,C_1$ such that
\[
 L\ge C_0T\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 J_L(f)\le V_*+C_1T^2/L.
\]
Consequently a sufficient sample size for the original critical
learner is
\[
 n\ge\frac{C}{\varepsilon}
       \{e^{CT}+\log(1/\delta)\}.
\]
The statistical conclusion has the same width-first quantifiers as
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-excess-learning}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:controlled-circuit-prefix}, replace
the entropy budget $1/(qL)$ at each stage by $\eta=q/L$.
Starting with $r_0=1/d$, use the rare-mixture weight
$\theta_j=\eta r_{j-1}/P_j$ and tracked component
\[
 r_j=\frac{\eta r_{j-1}}{4C_jP_j}.
\]
Inductively $r_j\le1$, so $0<\theta_j\le1$.
Entropy convexity gives cost at most $\eta$ per stage. The prefix
therefore costs at most $q^2/L$, has constant diagonal, and retains
the target component with
\[
 \log(1/r_q)=\log d+
       \sum_{j=1}^q\log\left(\frac{4C_jP_jL}{q}\right).
\]
In particular $T\ge q$. Increasing $C_0$ ensures
$L-q\ge L/2$ and the hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:steering-after-prefix}. That lemma gives
\[
 J_L(f)\le V_*+\frac{q^2}{L}
       +C\frac{(1+\log(1/r_q))^2+\log^2(2L)}{L-q}
 \le V_*+C_1T^2/L.
\]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-excess-learning}.
Its exponential term is $e^{CT}$; its polynomial prefactor is
absorbed in that expression because $T\ge\log(d+L+2)-C$.
The entropy and compression bounds allow arbitrary nonnegative
excess, so the prefix cost $q^2/L$ need not be bounded by a
universal constant.
\end{proof}

For a bounded-fan-in Boolean gate library, the repair constants $P_j$
are universal. A depth-$h$, width-$C$ implementation therefore has
the rate and learning bounds above with
\[
 T\le C_2\{\log(d+L+2)+h\log((C+2)(2+L/h))\}.
\]
This improves the earlier fixed-budget prefix factor $h\log(hL)$.

\begin{theorem}[Boolean layer blocking]
\label{thm:critical-boolean-blocking}
Suppose $f$ has a layered AND/OR/NOT circuit of fan-in at most two,
width $C$, and depth $h\ge1$, including constant rails. For an integer
$1\le b\le h$, put $k=\lceil h/b\rceil$ and
\[
 T_b=\log(d+L+2)+
       k\{\log(C+2)+2^b+\log(2+L/k)\}.
\]
There are universal constants $C_0,C_1,C_2$ such that, if
$L\ge C_0T_b$, then
\[
 J_L(f)\le V_*+C_1T_b^2/L,
 \qquad
 n\ge\frac{C_2}{\varepsilon}
            \{e^{C_2T_b}+\log(1/\delta)\}
\]
is sufficient for posterior confidence-error at most $\varepsilon$
with sample probability at least $1-\delta$, at every sufficiently
large learner width. The width threshold is independent of the
realized sample.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Divide the $h$ layers into $k$ blocks. Each output of a block depends
on at most $2^b$ signals at its entrance, so a full DNF uses at most
$2^{2^b}$ terms. Use one threshold layer for those conjunctions and
one for their disjunction. Constants are treated as constant gates.
This gives exactly $q=2k$ threshold stages after harmless identity
padding. Their widths are at most
$C_3(C+1)2^{C_3 2^b}$.

Normalize the integer threshold scores to have margin one.
Each conjunction has at most $2^b$ inputs and each disjunction at
most $2^{2^b}$ inputs. Their coefficient norms are polynomial in
those quantities. Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-feature-repair}, applied to
the gate's actual inputs and its constant rail, therefore gives
$P_j\le2^{C_4 2^b}$. Apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:critical-optimized-prefix-budget}.
Its parameter is at most $C_5T_b$. The stated depth hypothesis
ensures $L\ge q$ and all other required inequalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[An adaptive choice of block length]
\label{cor:critical-adaptive-blocking}
In the theorem put
\[
 a=\log(C+2)+\log(2+L/h),\qquad
 T_* =\log(d+L+2)+a\left(1+\frac{h}{\log(a+2)}\right).
\]
The same rate and sample bounds hold with $T_b$ replaced by $T_*$,
after increasing the universal constants, under $L\ge C_0T_*$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose $b=\min\{h,\max\{1,\lfloor\log_2(a+2)\rfloor\}\}$.
Then $2^b\le a+2$, $k\le1+h/b$, and
\[
 \log(2+L/k)\le\log(2+bL/h)
                    \le\log b+\log(2+L/h).
\]
Moreover $b$ is at least a universal constant times
$\min\{h,\log(a+2)\}$, and $\log b\le C a$.
Substitution gives $T_b\le C T_*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[General circuits and formulas]
\label{cor:critical-formula-blocking}
Let a bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit have depth $h$ and size at most
$s$, and put $S=\max\{16,d+s+2\}$. If
\[
 L\ge C\log(S+L)
                   \left(1+\frac{h}{\log\log(S+L)}\right),
\]
then a sufficient sample size is
\[
 \frac{C}{\varepsilon}
 \left\{(S+L)^{C(1+h/\log\log(S+L))}
                    +\log(1/\delta)\right\}.
\]
For a formula of size $s$, its original depth can be replaced by
$C\log S$ in these displays, after increasing constants.
At learner depths polynomial in $S$, this yields
$\exp\{O(\log^2S/\log\log S)\}$ samples, apart from the
statistical factors.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Layer a circuit by inserting identity gates along skipped wires;
the width is $O(S)$. In the preceding theorem choose
$b=\min\{h,\max\{1,\lfloor\log_2\log(S+L)\rfloor\}\}$.
The claimed parameter bound follows directly from the formula for
$T_b$.

A binary formula of size $m$ has a rooted subformula $G$ with size
between $m/3-O(1)$ and $2m/3+O(1)$. Replace that occurrence by
zero and one to obtain outer formulas $F_0,F_1$. The identity
\[
 F=(G\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}F_1)
       \mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}
       ((\mathop{\mathrm{NOT}}G)\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}F_0)
\]
permits parallel recursive construction of three formulas of size
at most $2m/3+O(1)$, followed by constant-depth logic. Simplify
unary gates and constants first. The resulting circuit has
polynomial size and depth $O(\log m)$ by the corresponding size
and depth recurrences. Apply the circuit result to this balanced
implementation.
\end{proof}

A fixed-width deterministic branching program of length $t$ likewise
has a polynomial-size bounded-fan-in circuit of depth $O(\log t)$:
multiply its constant-dimensional Boolean transition matrices in a
balanced tree. Each transition entry is an input literal or a constant,
and repeated queries remain explicit at the leaves. This gives the
same quasipolynomial sample bound. It does not justify treating the
program's work register as the full hidden state of a sequential
network; the queried input information cannot be discarded.

The bounds improve the teacher-depth dependence but remain
quasipolynomial for general polynomial-size logarithmic-depth circuits.
They do not establish a jointly polynomial theorem for arbitrary
small deep circuits, formulas, or constant-width branching programs.
