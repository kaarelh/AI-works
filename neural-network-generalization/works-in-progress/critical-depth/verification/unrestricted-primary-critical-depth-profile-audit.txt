1. Exact Jensen lower bound.
The projected-trace budget with k>=1 bounds d by
8r product_hidden(1+sqrt(2D_s))^2. The inequality
8r <= 4(1+sqrt(2r))^2 is valid. Applying concavity of
log(1+sqrt(2u)) to all L costs, including r, gives precisely
d <= 4(1+sqrt(2A/L))^(2L). Letting A decrease to J is legitimate.
The resulting positive-part inverse bound and J>=1/8 are correct.

2. Lower profile comparison.
For d>=16, u>=2v implies exp(v)-1 <= exp(u-v)-1, and exp(v)<=sqrt2
because L>=2. Thus the displayed factor sqrt2+1 is valid. For d<16,
the stated monotonicity in L follows from differentiation, and the
maximum at L=2 is at most 3 (strictly less when d<16). Therefore a
universal positive lower constant exists for all d>=1,L>=2.

3. Linear seed-depth threshold.
In the seed proof, delta=B1*S/L and p=B2*(S+log(2L))/L. For S>=1 and
L>=C*S, choose C sufficiently large after fixing B1,B2. Then both
S/L and log(2L)/L are uniformly small, so 0<delta<=p<=p0. Cooling
costs O(L/(B1*S)) layers; prescribed growth costs O(L/B1); purification
costs O(L/B2); the scalar path costs O(L/[B2*(S+log(2L))]) layers.
All ceiling errors fit after increasing C, and holding fills the
remaining exact number. Neither holding nor growth assumes bounded
total entropy; their possibly large cost O(S^2/L) is explicitly allowed.
Only the final scalar subpath must have bounded cost, which it does
independently of the previous stages. The purified residual gives the
same O(L^-4) lifting cost. Thus the unchanged excess upper bound holds
already for L>=C*S.

4. Upper profile comparison.
For L>=C1*log(d+1), the improved seed theorem gives
O(1+log(d+1)^2/L), since log(2L)^2/L is uniformly bounded. This is
bounded by a universal multiple of Phi. In the complementary range,
the exact mean-shift construction gives 5000L*d^(1/L). For d>=2,
t>=1/(4C1), so its ratio to L(exp(t)-1)^2 is universally bounded.
For d=1, the complementary range has bounded L. The final log-scale
equivalence uses t in [0,1/2] and handles d=1 through the additive 1.

5. Scope.
The theorem states comparability of the minimum absolute rate, not
that constants exactly minimize at all depths. It does not infer a
sample theorem or full target ordering. Those qualifications are correct.
