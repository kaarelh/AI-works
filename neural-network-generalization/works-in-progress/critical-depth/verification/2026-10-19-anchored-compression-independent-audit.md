**Verdict: the finite-width representation proof passes independent reconstruction.** This audit does not assert a product-norm bound or audit the subsequent VC/posterior-learning transfer.

1. The joint greedy algorithm is valid. Adding an entropy-deficient query increases the sum of marginal entropies by more than delta; adding a readout-deficient query increases the squared projection norm by more than t squared. Both quantities are monotone under enlargement. Rechecking all queries after every enlargement handles possible increases in an individual residual. The stated anchor bound follows from the combined potential.
2. The anchored query metric is the Euclidean distance between normalized regression coefficients and conditional standard deviations. Singular anchor blocks are handled by restricting to their covariance range. A joint feature coupling with the same anchor vector Gaussianizes to a valid query coupling, including nonzero feature means.
3. Rao–Blackwellization preserves the exact anchor marginal and all its second moments. One-query conditional Gaussian T2 gives the stated injected error; zero conditional variance is harmless because finite entropy forces equality on that deterministic conditional coordinate.
4. Propagation under a common anchor density is valid. The anchor density is normalized under both Gaussian references because their anchor covariance blocks are exactly equal. Under their common Gaussian query coupling, the scalar discrepancy has its RMS increased by at most 1+sqrt(2D). This follows either from Gaussian T2 in the covariance range or its one-dimensional projection. The extension can depend on the query without changing the actual candidate law, whose density is fixed on anchors.
5. The leverage sampling removes small-eigenvalue dependence. Each whitened sample coordinate is bounded by sqrt(s+1). Entrywise Hoeffding with errors eta/s for the anchor Gram and eta/sqrt(s) for cross vectors gives the displayed q^4 eta^-2 logarithmic width. The correction B is positive definite; direct calculation gives E^T B E=I and E^T B F_x=M^-1 vhat_x. Hence the exact anchor block and the claimed query bounds follow. The rank-zero case is also valid.
6. Candidate corrected diagonals can exceed one, but this causes no gap. The Rao–Blackwell injection is evaluated at the original bounded-diagonal covariance; the propagation bound is stated directly in the query metric and does not assume bounded candidate diagonal; the next quadrature samples raw tanh features, which are bounded by one before correction.
7. The covariance factor is absorbed into incoming linear weights and the final readout. This adds no activation layer. Leverage weights and inverse anchor eigenvalues may be large but are finite. No quantitative weight or product claim is being made.
8. At the terminal layer the original projected readout coefficient vector can be reused because the anchor block is exact; its error is bounded by sqrt(2r) times the query metric, without an l1 coefficient bound. The chosen delta and width estimate then give the claimed uniform approximation.
