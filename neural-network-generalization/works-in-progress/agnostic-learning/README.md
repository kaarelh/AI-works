[Problem brief](research-notes/problem-brief.md) · [Verification record](verification/README.md) · [Collection index](../../README.md)

The repository's main theorems assume clean labels: the data come from a small teacher network. This note asks what survives when nothing is assumed about the labels. The question is the following conjecture.

> After seeing $n$ data points, the test loss of NNBayes learning is at most the minimum test loss among all MLPs of width $\mathrm{poly}(n)$, where the polynomial may have a fractional exponent. The loss is squared error, and the Bayesian learner should condition to be close to the given outputs on the training inputs. We may pick whichever version of Bayesian learning is best.

If true, this would extend the repository's guarantees to data whose input–output map is complex but well approximated by small networks: the learner would do as well as the best small approximation, without the data being generated by one.

We read the conjecture as an *agnostic oracle inequality with polynomial sample complexity* that holds at every learner width (§1). The answer depends on how strongly the learner conditions on the training outputs, and the direction is the opposite of the heuristic in the conjecture.

1. **Close conditioning fails.** At depth $L\ge3$, the clean-label-scaled learner of the [smooth-likelihood note](../slt-likelihood/README.md) ($c=1$ below) and every learner with likelihood variance $c\sigma^2$ for $c$ up to about $n^{1-1/H}$ fail the conjecture as formalized in §1. On noisy labels they memorize the training noise, because a deep network can interpolate $n$ random labels with energy of order $d^2\log^2d+(dn)^{1/H}=o(n)$, where $H=L-1$ is the number of hidden layers. On the Boolean cube with fair random labels and $n\approx2^d$ samples, at learner widths above a polynomial in $n$, their test error exceeds that of the zero network by at least about $0.48$, a constant, so no polynomial sample bound that is uniform over input dimensions can hold. Hard tube conditioning fails too, for a different reason: its tube is empty or biased (§3).
2. **Tempered conditioning works.** With the likelihood variance multiplied by $c=n^{1-\theta}$ and $0<\theta<1/H$, the strong-prior learner satisfies the agnostic oracle inequality for clipped predictions at every learner width, with sample size polynomial in $d$, the competitor's width $m$ and weight bound $B$, $1/\varepsilon$ and $\log(1/\delta)$. In the conjecture's form, for depth $L\ge3$: at every learner width $N\ge n^\alpha$ the clipped test loss is at most the least test loss over depth-$L$ networks whose width and weights are at most $n^{\alpha}$, plus $O_L\bigl((d^2+\sqrt{d\log n}+\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)})\,n^{-\beta}\bigr)$, for every $\alpha<1/(4H)$; at depth two the bound decays only for $\alpha<1/8$ (§4).
3. **The transition is sharp.** At depth $L\ge3$ and for $\theta>1/H$ the learner memorizes again (on the noisy cube with $d=\log_2n$, at learner widths above a polynomial in $n$), so in the worst case over laws the critical tempering is $c\approx n^{1-1/H}$, up to polylogarithmic factors. Deeper networks need more tempering because memorization is cheaper for them (§4).
4. **The competitors' weights must be bounded.** A Gaussian prior caps the energy the posterior can afford at about $n/c$. With depth $L\ge3$, clipped predictions and a bound uniform over laws, some tempering works against width-one competitors with weights up to $n^\alpha$ if $\alpha<1/(2H)$, and none works if $\alpha>1/(2H)$; against competitors with width and weights up to $n^\alpha$, the threshold lies in $[1/(4H),\,L/(2H(2L-1))]$. Beyond it, no choice of $c(n)$ works. For a fixed law no weight bound is needed. Tempering trades robustness to noise against reach in weight size (§5).
5. **A fixed temperature works only at small learner width.** With a hot clipped likelihood ($\eta\le1/8$) and the finite-scale prior $\mu_{N,\gamma}$, $\gamma\ge1$, the progressive mixture of the posterior means of the clipped outputs (an average over sample prefixes) satisfies the oracle inequality, in expectation and, for a variant, with high probability, with sample size of order $A_LN^\gamma/(\eta\varepsilon)$; for the final posterior only a bound with an extra $4\eta$ is proved. The sample size must grow with the width: at fixed $n$ and any fixed $\eta$ the posterior means tend to $0$ as $N\to\infty$, and on a constant target that a width-one network fits exactly the excess tends to $1$; for the clipped predictors it is at least $1/2$ once $N^\gamma\ge22\eta n$ and $\eta n\ge1$ (for example $N\ge3n$ at $\gamma=1$, $\eta=1/8$, $n\ge8$; §6).
6. **Finite prior scale.** With the repository's width-tied prior scale $\sigma^2=N^{-\gamma}$, $\gamma\ge1$ (Gaussian weights), and no limit taken, close conditioning still fails and tempering still works, but the sharp transition is reproduced only for $\gamma\ge2-1/H$. At depth $L\ge3$, $c=1$ memorizes at every $\gamma\ge1$, mean field included, on the same noisy-cube laws and at learner widths above a polynomial in $n$. Tempering with $\theta<\theta_{L,\gamma}$ gives the oracle inequality for the posterior-averaged clipped test loss and for the posterior mean of the clipped outputs, at every learner width. Here $\theta_{L,\gamma}=1/H$ for $\gamma\ge2-1/H$; below that the proved range is smaller ($\theta<1/52$ instead of $1/2$ at depth $3$ and $\gamma=1$, where the proof relies on an imported mean-field cover), and the range up to $1/H$ is open. The positive result at finite scale does not cover unclipped predictions, and the conjecture's form of item 2 is not proved there: for $H\ge2$ and $\gamma\ge2$, the bound of the finite-scale proof tends to $0$ against competitors of width and weights $n^\alpha$ only when $\alpha<1/(8H)$ ([E, Remark 2.8](research-notes/E-finite-prior-scale.md); §7).
7. **Other ways to condition closely.** At learner width $\lceil n^\alpha\rceil$, $\alpha<1/4$, and input dimension $d\le n^\alpha$, the clipped predictions of the clean-label learner ($c=1$) are within $O(n^{2\alpha-1/2})$ of the best depth-$L$ network with width and weights at most $n^\alpha$: the failure of item 1 is a large-width phenomenon. Conditioning on a sup-norm tolerance above a known residual bound, or on the aggregate training error, also avoids memorization, at the price of an oracle hyperparameter. The sup-norm version assumes $|y-g(x)|\le\rho_0$ almost surely for a small network $g$, and it bounds the risk by $\rho^2+\varepsilon$, where $\rho>\rho_0$ is its tolerance, not by $L_D(g)+\varepsilon$. At depth two, where memorization is expensive, close conditioning still fails the polynomial sample bound for every $c\le\mathrm{polylog}(n)$: the proved lower bound on the excess is of order $(dc+1)^{-2}$, a constant when $c=O(1/\log n)$ but only polylogarithmically small at $c=1$; whether the excess itself tends to $0$ at $c=1$ is open (§8).

So the conjecture's heuristic is right for clean labels, which is the setting of the repository's theorems, and wrong in the agnostic setting for learners that are large compared with $n$. For the Gaussian-likelihood learners of items 1–3, at depth $L\ge3$ and large learner width, a guarantee uniform over input dimensions requires conditioning *less* on each training output, by a factor that grows polynomially with $n$. With the repository's priors, whose outputs shrink as the width grows, the tempering also has to be tied to the prior scale to stay width independent: at a fixed temperature the posterior means vanish at large width (§6; proved for the clipped likelihood).

## 1. The question made precise

**Networks.** As in the [continuous-domain paper](../../side-results/continuous-domains/): depth $L=H+1$, $H$ hidden tanh layers of width $N$, input $\bar x=(1,x)$, no internal biases, output $z_w(x)=v^\top h^H(x)$. The energy is

$$
\kappa_N(w)=\tfrac dN\lVert W^1\rVert_F^2+\sum_{\ell=2}^{H}\lVert W^\ell\rVert_F^2+N\lVert v\rVert_2^2 .
$$

**Learners.** All use the strong-prior Gaussian prior $\mu_\sigma\propto e^{-\kappa_N/(2\sigma^2)}$, or its finite-scale version $\sigma^2=N^{-\gamma}$.

- **SP$_c$.** Gaussian likelihood $y_i\sim\mathcal N(z_w(x_i),c\sigma^2)$. The posterior is $\propto\exp(-F_c/(2\sigma^2))$ with $F_c(w)=\kappa_N(w)+\frac1c\sum_i(z_w(x_i)-y_i)^2$. The limit $\sigma\to0$ is taken after the sample and the learner width are fixed. $c=1$ is the clean-label scaling of the [smooth-likelihood note](../slt-likelihood/README.md); $c$ may depend on $n$.
- **TUBE$_\rho$.** The prior conditioned on $|z_w(x_i)-y_i|<\rho$ for all $i$: the learner of the continuous-domain paper with noisy labels.
- **FIX$_\eta$.** Ordinary tempered Bayes: posterior $\propto\mu(dw)\exp(-\eta\sum_i(\operatorname{clip}z_w(x_i)-y_i)^2)$ with fixed $\eta$, at finite prior scale and learner width $N$.

Predictors are a posterior draw (Gibbs), the posterior mean of $z_w$ or of $\operatorname{clip}z_w$, and, for FIX$_\eta$, the progressive mixture. Here $\operatorname{clip}$ truncates to $[-1,1]$.

**The property.** Labels lie in $[-1,1]$, $X\subseteq[-1,1]^d$ is compact, and $L_D(f)=\mathbb E_D(f(x)-y)^2$. A learner has property (P$_L$) if there is a polynomial $p$ such that for every $d$, every law $D$ on $X\times[-1,1]$, every competitor $g$ of depth $L$, width $m$ and weights in $[-B,B]$, every learner width $N\ge m$ and every $n\ge p(d,m,B,1/\varepsilon,\log(1/\delta))$,

$$
\Pr\bigl[L_D(\text{learner})\le L_D(g)+\varepsilon\bigr]\ge1-\delta .
$$

Nothing is assumed about $D$: the best competitor may have large error, and the labels may be noisy or a complicated deterministic function of $x$. The weight bound $B$ matters (§5). Taking the competitor class to be all networks with width and weights at most $n^\alpha$ gives the conjecture's form, "test loss at most the minimum over width-$\mathrm{poly}(n)$ MLPs, plus $o(1)$". Uniformity over the learner width matches the repository's clean-label theorems, which hold at every width above the teacher's.

## 3. Close conditioning memorizes noise

**Theorem A** ([A, Theorem 4.4, Corollaries 4.5 and 4.5a, and Proposition 5.1](research-notes/A-tight-conditioning-fails.md)). Let $L\ge3$. Let $x$ be uniform on $\{-1,1\}^d$ and $y$ a fair random sign independent of $x$, so the zero network is Bayes optimal with $L_D(0)=1$. Let $n/2^d\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)$, and let $c=1$, or more generally $c\,(d^2\log^2d+(dn)^{1/H})=o(n)$. With probability at least $1-e^{-2^{d/2}}$, at every learner width $N$ above a polynomial in $n$, every near-minimizer of $F_c$, a Gibbs draw (with posterior probability tending to one as $\sigma\to0$) and the posterior mean, clipped or not, have

$$
L_D\ge1+\beta(\lambda)-o(1),\qquad \beta(\lambda)=\mathbb E\bigl[K^{-1};K\ge1\bigr],\ K\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda).
$$

$\beta(1)=0.4848$, and the maximum is $\beta=0.5174$ at $\lambda\approx1.503$.

**Mechanism.** At depth $\ge3$ a network can fit arbitrary values on $s$ cube points with energy $O(d^2\log^2d+(ds)^{1/H})$, by a sparse Fourier expansion in tanh features followed by a chain of layers that amplifies small signals (Lemma 2.1, refining the [noisy-interpolation note](../noisy-interpolation/README.md)). Fitting the noise removes squared error of order $n$, so whenever $c$ times the memorization energy is $o(n)$ the near-minimizers of $F_c$ fit each seen atom's label average. A test point that lands on an atom seen $K$ times is then predicted by the average of $K$ independent labels, which costs $1/K$ extra squared error. This is Cover's nearest-neighbour formula; the Poisson count of copies gives $\beta(\lambda)$. The exponent $1/H$ is optimal: memorizing most sign patterns on $s$ points needs energy at least of order $(s/d^2)^{1/H}$ (Proposition 8.2).

**Why this refutes (P$_L$).** The competitor $g\equiv0$ has width one and zero weights, and $n=2^d$ eventually exceeds every polynomial in $d$. So no polynomial sample bound holds for a Gibbs draw, the Gibbs risk, the posterior means (clipped or not) or any near-minimizer, hence the MAP estimate and every $\sigma\to0$ weak limit of the posterior ([A, Proposition 5.1 and Remark 5.1a](research-notes/A-tight-conditioning-fails.md)); the progressive-mixture predictor is not covered. The excess appears in the regime $n\asymp2^d$, where test inputs coincide with training inputs. It is proved to be $\Omega(1)$ only there: for $n\gg d2^d$ it is $O(2^d/n)$ at every width (Proposition 5.2), and for $n=o(2^d)$ nothing is claimed.

**What it does not show.**

- *It refutes particular learners, not the conjecture.* The conjecture lets us choose the Bayesian learner, and §4 gives one that works.
- *The input dimension must grow.* The counterexample has $d=\log_2n$, so it refutes sample bounds that are uniform over the input dimension, which is the repository's convention ("polynomial in input dimension"). At any fixed $d$ the same cube family is learned consistently at every width when $c=o(n)$, for example for fixed $c$: with probability at least $1-\delta'-Me^{-n/(8M)}$, where $M=2^d$ and $\delta'\in(0,1)$, every near-minimizer with slack $t$, at every width and depth, has excess at most $(8M/\delta'+4ct)/n$ ([A, Proposition 5.2](research-notes/A-tight-conditioning-fails.md)). A version of the conjecture whose $o(1)$ may depend on $d$ or on the law is not refuted.
- *Large learner widths only.* The failure is shown at learner widths above $N_0=\mathrm{poly}(n)$, at least about $2\cdot10^5n$ in the examples computed. No failure is proved below $N_0$, and at learner width $\lceil n^\alpha\rceil$ with $\alpha<1/4$ and $d\le n^\alpha$, the clipped predictions of SP$_1$ are within $O(n^{2\alpha-1/2})$ of the best depth-$L$ network with width and weights at most $n^\alpha$ (§8).
- *Near-atomic inputs.* The continuous-input version below has a density $2^{\Theta(d^2)}$ times the uniform density, so it is near-atomic at the learner's resolution. For input laws with bounded density ratio, such as uniform inputs on $[-1,1]^d$ with noisy labels, the question is open. For a density at most $K$ times uniform and $n=2^d$, Proposition 7.4 bounds the test mass within the proof's transfer radius of the training inputs by $K\,2^{3.05d-(L/(2H))d^2}$, which A reads (Remark 7.5, an interpretation rather than a theorem) as saying that the proof's Lipschitz transfer cannot reach such laws ([A, Proposition 7.4 and Remark 7.5](research-notes/A-tight-conditioning-fails.md)).

**Variants** ([A, §§6–7](research-notes/A-tight-conditioning-fails.md)).

- Sampling without replacement, at learner widths $N\ge N_0$: the excess is at least $(n/2^d)(1-o(1))$, and at $n=2^d$ the test error tends to $2$ (Proposition 6.1). This sampling scheme lies outside (P$_L$), which assumes i.i.d. data.
- TUBE$_\rho$ fails at every tolerance, tight or loose, for a different reason: on an input that carries both labels $\pm1$, the constraints $|z_w(x)\mp1|<\rho$ confine the output to the window $(1-\rho,\rho-1)$. This window is empty when $\rho\le1$, so the posterior is undefined, and when $\rho>1$ it is centred at the midrange $0$ of the labels rather than at their mean. With non-centred labels this violates (P$_L$) at every tolerance schedule, every depth $L\ge2$ and every learner width, already at a fixed input dimension and, for both repository priors, at every prior scale ([A, Propositions 6.6 and 6.6a](research-notes/A-tight-conditioning-fails.md)). With sampling without replacement and $\rho$ below the noise amplitude $b$, every posterior draw and the posterior mean have excess at least $(n/2^d)(b-\rho)^2$ at learner widths $N\ge N_0(d,n,\rho/2)$ (Proposition 6.5).
- **Continuous inputs and deterministic labels (Claim A′, Theorem 7.2).** Replace each cube vertex by a tiny cube of side $r\approx n^{-1-L/2}$ that carries a balanced $\pm1$ pattern at scale $r$. The inputs have a density, the labels are a deterministic function of $x$, and the Bayes risk is $0$; the learner's risk is still at least $1+\beta(\lambda)-o(1)$, worse than the zero network's risk $1$. Fine-scale structure that low-energy networks cannot resolve acts as noise for them.

## 4. Tempered conditioning

**Theorem B** ([B, Theorem 6.3](research-notes/B-tempered-likelihood.md)). Let $L=H+1\ge2$, $0<\theta<1/H$ and $c=n^{1-\theta}$. Fix $X$, $D$, a competitor $g$ of depth $L$, width $m$ and weights in $[-B,B]$, and $\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)$. For every $n\ge n_0$, with $n_0$ explicit and polynomial in $d,m,B,1/\varepsilon,\log(1/\delta)$, the following holds with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every learner width $N\ge m$: every near-minimizer of $F_c$ after clipping, the Gibbs predictor and the posterior mean of the clipped outputs have test loss at most $L_D(g)+\varepsilon$ in the limit $\sigma\to0$.

The excess is at most

$$
(E_*+1)\,n^{-\theta}+8(2/H)^{H/2}D_n\,n^{-(1-\theta H)/2}+\bigl(4+(U+1)^2\bigr)\sqrt{\ln(2/\delta)/(2n)},
$$

where $E_*=d(d+1)B^2+2(L-1)m^2B^2$ is the energy of the competitor's clone, $U$ bounds its output, and $D_n=\sqrt{2(d+1)\ln(n+1)}+\frac{H-1}2$. At $\theta=1/(H+2)$ this is $\tilde O(n^{-1/(H+2)})$. Unclipped outputs, including the posterior mean of $z_w$, are covered for $\theta<1/L$ (Theorem 6.4).

**Proof idea.** As $\sigma\to0$ the posterior concentrates on near-minimizers of $n^{-\theta}\kappa_N+\hat L_S$, which is energy-penalized least squares. Comparing with the competitor's clone bounds the near-minimizers' energy by $O(n^\theta)$ and their training error by $\hat L_S(g)+O(n^{-\theta})$. A width-free Rademacher bound for tanh networks of bounded energy, from the path-norm argument of the [noisy-interpolation note](../noisy-interpolation/README.md) §4, extended from the cube to arbitrary inputs, then gives uniform convergence at rate $(K/H)^{H/2}/\sqrt n$ for energy $K$; with $K\approx n^\theta$ this is $o(1)$ exactly when $\theta<1/H$. The repository's width-independent energy cover gives the same conclusion only for $\theta<1/(4(L-1))$ when $L\ge3$, and for $\theta<1/2$ at depth two ([B, Theorem 6.2](research-notes/B-tempered-likelihood.md)).

**Sharpness** ([B, Theorem 7.4](research-notes/B-tempered-likelihood.md)). For $H\ge2$ and every $\theta>1/H$, SP$_c$ fails on the noisy cube with $d=\log_2n$, with excess at least $1/e-o(1)$. The critical scaling is $c\approx n^{1-1/H}$ up to polylogarithmic factors, at learner widths above a polynomial in $n$, and in the worst case over laws.

**The conjecture's form** ([B, Corollary 8.1](research-notes/B-tempered-likelihood.md)). For $H\ge2$ and $\alpha<1/(4H)$, with $\theta=(1+8\alpha)/(H+2)$: with probability $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all learner widths $N\ge n^\alpha$, the tempered learner's clipped test loss (for the predictors of Theorem B) is at most the least test loss over depth-$L$ networks with width and weights at most $n^\alpha$, plus $O_L\bigl((d^2+\sqrt{d\log n}+\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)})\,n^{-\beta}\bigr)$ with $\beta=(1-4\alpha H)/(H+2)$. For example, at depth $3$ one may take $\alpha=1/16$ and $\beta=1/8$. At depth two the corollary's rate is useful only for $\alpha<1/8$.

**The constants are poor.** At $L=3$, $d=10$, $m=B=1$, $\varepsilon=0.1$, $\delta=0.05$ and $\theta=1/4$, the displayed bound first drops below $\varepsilon$ at about $10^{14}$ samples (the closed-form $n_0$ is about $10^{29}$). The exponents come from worst-case capacity bounds.

## 5. How large may the competitors' weights be?

The conjecture bounds only the competitors' width. For SP$_c$ and a guarantee uniform over laws, the Gaussian prior forces a bound on their weights as well, at a level tied to the tempering; for a fixed law no weight bound is needed (end of this section).

**A steep two-point law** ([B, Proposition 9.1](research-notes/B-tempered-likelihood.md)). Near-minimizers of $F_c$ have energy at most about $n/c$, and a network of energy $K$ is $(K/L)^{L/2}$-Lipschitz in one dimension. Put the two inputs $\pm\delta_0$, with labels $\pm1$, closer together than this allows: $\delta_0=\frac12\bigl(L/(n/c+1)\bigr)^{L/2}$. Then every near-minimizer and every limiting posterior predictor has test loss at least $1/4$, at every learner width and for every sample. When $n/c\ge2L$, a width-one network fits the law exactly, with weights at most $1.5(n/c+1)^{1/2}$ and energy at most $3.25(n/c+1)$. So, in the worst case over laws, the learner's reach in competitor energy is about $n/c$, which is $n^\theta$ under the tempering of §4.

**How large may the weights be?** ([B, Corollary 9.3](research-notes/B-tempered-likelihood.md)). Let $H\ge2$, and call a choice of $c(n)$ *working* for a competitor class if the polynomial sample bound of §1 holds against it for the clipped predictions, uniformly over laws and input dimensions, at every learner width at least the class's width bound.

- Width-one competitors with weights at most $n^\alpha$: some $c(n)$ works if $\alpha<1/(2H)$, and none works if $\alpha>1/(2H)$.
- Competitors with width and weights at most $n^\alpha$: some $c(n)$ works if $\alpha<1/(4H)$ (Corollary 8.1), and none works if $\alpha>L/(2H(2L-1))$. At depth $3$ the threshold lies in $[0.125,0.15]$.

The negative halves hold for every function $c(n)$. They use laws that change with $n$: the noisy cube in dimension $\log_2n$, at learner widths above $N_0(n)$, or the two-point law with $\delta_0\to0$. The trade-off is that a smaller $c$ raises the budget $n/c$, but makes the learner fit noise (§3).

**For a fixed law no weight bound is needed** ([B, Remark 9.4](research-notes/B-tempered-likelihood.md)). For each fixed $D$, tanh networks approximate $\mathbb E[y\mid x]$ in $L^2(D_X)$ to any accuracy, so Theorem B applied to an approximating network gives test loss at most the Bayes risk plus $\varepsilon$. The sample size and the learner-width threshold depend on $D$. The weight bound is the price of uniformity over laws.

## 6. Ordinary tempered Bayes at a fixed temperature

**Theorem C** ([C, Theorems C.1, C.2 and C.5](research-notes/C-fixed-temperature-bayes.md)).

- For every prior, every law and $0<\eta\le1/8$ (the exp-concavity threshold of squared loss on $[-1,1]$), the progressive mixture $\bar f_n=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{t=0}^n\hat f_t$, where $\hat f_t$ is the posterior mean of the clipped outputs after $t$ samples, satisfies $\mathbb E_S L_D(\bar f_n)\le\int L_D(\operatorname{clip}z_w)\,Q(dw)+\mathrm{KL}(Q\Vert\mu)/(\eta(n+1))$ for every probability measure $Q$ that does not depend on the sample.
- With the finite-scale prior $\mu_{N,\gamma}$, $\gamma\ge1$, and the repository's teacher-tube mass bound, this gives $\mathbb E_SL_D(\bar f_n)\le L_D(g)+4\rho+A_L(d,m,B,\rho)N^\gamma/(\eta(n+1))$ for every $\rho\in(0,1]$ and every depth-$L$ competitor of width $m\le N$ with weights in $[-B,B]$, $B\ge1$. At the single learner width $N=\lceil n^\alpha\rceil$ with $\alpha<1/(4+\gamma)$ and fixed $d$, this gives the conjecture's "$+o(1)$" form in expectation, but not uniformly over learner widths (next item): the expected test loss of $\bar f_n$ is at most the infimum of the test loss over networks with width and weights at most $n^\alpha$, plus $O_{L,\gamma,\nu}\bigl(d^2n^{(4+\gamma)\alpha-1}\log(dn)/\eta\bigr)$ ([C, Corollary C.2.2](research-notes/C-fixed-temperature-bayes.md)). With probability $1-2\delta$ the same holds for the average over $t<n$, with an additional term of order $\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/n}$ (Theorem C.6).
- Some width dependence is necessary. For every $\gamma>0$, every fixed $\eta>0$ and every sample, as $N\to\infty$ with $n$ fixed the posterior tends to the prior in total variation, the posterior means $\bar f_n$ and $\hat f_n$ tend to $0$ uniformly on $X$, and $\sup_x\int z_w(x)^2\,d\Pi_n\to0$. On the constant target $y\equiv1$, which a width-one network fits exactly, the excess of every predictor considered, clipped or not, tends to $1$; for the clipped predictors it is at least $1/2$ once $N^\gamma\ge4(4\eta n+\sqrt{2\eta n})$. For this target this locates the threshold $N^\gamma\asymp\eta n$ up to a factor $O(A_L)$; it does not show that the complexity term of the previous item has the best possible form.

This learner conditions only weakly: each observation changes the likelihood by a factor of at most $e^{4\eta}\le e^{1/2}$. Clipping is needed in the predictor, not in the likelihood. If $\hat f_t$ is replaced by the posterior mean of the unclipped outputs or its clip, the first statement fails for some two-point priors whose outputs lie far outside $[-1,1]$; for the network priors the unclipped predictors are open. For the final posterior at a fixed temperature only a bound with an additive $4\eta$ is proved (Theorem C.7).

## 7. Finite prior scale

The results of §§3–5 on SP$_c$ take the strong-prior limit $\sigma\to0$ after the sample and the learner width are fixed (the TUBE failure of §3 holds at every prior scale). The repository's finite-scale prior $\mu_{N,\gamma}$, here with Gaussian weights, instead ties the scale to the width, $\sigma^2=N^{-\gamma}$ with $\gamma\ge1$; $\gamma=1$ is the mean-field scale. With likelihood variance $cN^{-\gamma}$ ($c=1$ is the finite-scale learner of the [smooth-likelihood note](../slt-likelihood/README.md)) the posterior is proportional to $\exp(-N^\gamma F_c/2)$, a fixed probability measure, and no limit is taken ([E, Lemma 1.1](research-notes/E-finite-prior-scale.md)).

**Theorem E** ([E, Theorems 2.7 and 5.2](research-notes/E-finite-prior-scale.md)).

- *Positive, clipped predictions.* Let $c=n^{1-\theta}$ with $0<\theta<\theta_{L,\gamma}$. Once $n$ exceeds a polynomial in $d,m,B,1/\varepsilon,\log(1/\delta)$ that depends neither on the law nor on the learner width, with probability at least $1-\delta$ and simultaneously for every learner width $N\ge m$, the posterior-averaged test loss of the clipped outputs and the test loss of the posterior mean of the clipped outputs are at most $L_D(g)+\varepsilon$. At small learner widths only this average, not a single posterior draw, is controlled, and the polynomial depends on $L$, $\gamma$ and $\theta$. At depth two $\theta_{2,\gamma}=1$. For $L\ge3$, $\theta_{L,\gamma}=1/H$ when $\gamma\ge2-1/H$, as in the strong-prior limit; for $1<\gamma<2-1/H$ it is smaller, and for $\gamma$ slightly above $1$ it is even below its value at $\gamma=1$, which is $1/(4+16(L-2)3^{L-2})$ ($1/52$ at depth $3$). The case $\gamma=1$, $L\ge3$ uses the repository's mean-field cover, which was checked only at the level of its statement and formulas.
- *Negative, every $\gamma\ge1$.* At depth $L\ge3$, on the noisy cube with $n/2^d\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)$, for $c=1$ and for every $c$ with $c\,(d^4+(dn)^{1/H})=o(n)$, which includes $c=n^{1-\theta}$ with $\theta>1/H$: for all large $d$, at every learner width above a polynomial in $n$, with probability at least $1-e^{-2^{d/2}}$ over the sample, a posterior draw has test error at least $1+\beta(\lambda)-o(1)$ with posterior probability at least $1-2e^{-N^\gamma}$, and so do the expected Gibbs risk and the posterior means, clipped or not.

The negative direction needs a memorizer that survives the prior's noise. At finite scale the prior perturbs every weight, and the perturbation shrinks the slopes of the memorizing chain. The construction pre-compensates its centre for this loss, and averaging over the neurons of each layer removes the noise ([E, Proposition 4.4](research-notes/E-finite-prior-scale.md)).

So for $L\ge3$, $\gamma\ge2-1/H$ and clipped predictions, the finite-scale learner has the same critical exponent $\theta=1/H$ as the strong-prior limit, in the worst case over laws: the failure uses $d=\log_2n$ and learner widths above a polynomial in $n$. The polylogarithmic narrowing of the strong-prior limit is not established at finite scale. Open: the window $\theta\in[\theta_{L,\gamma},1/H)$ when $1\le\gamma<2-1/H$, the endpoint $\theta=1/H$, unclipped predictions, Claim A′ at finite scale, and, as in §3, any failure at a fixed input dimension or below the polynomial learner width.

## 8. Other ways to condition closely

The failure of §3 concerns SP$_c$ with $c=o(n^{1-1/H}d^{-1/H})$ at large learner widths, and TUBE$_\rho$, which fails at every tolerance on laws with non-centred $\pm1$ labels, including tolerances matched to the competitor's sup-norm residual ([A, Remark 6.6b](research-notes/A-tight-conditioning-fails.md)). Four other ways of conditioning closely were examined.

**Small learner width** ([small-learner-width.md](research-notes/small-learner-width.md), refereed in two rounds). Run the tightly conditioned learner SP$_1$ at learner width $N=\lceil n^\alpha\rceil$ with $0<\alpha<1/4$. For every law with $d\le n^\alpha$, with probability at least $1-\delta/2$, every clipped near-minimizer, and in the strong-prior limit the clipped Gibbs risk and the posterior mean of the clipped outputs, are within $O(n^{2\alpha-1/2})$ of the best depth-$L$ network with width and weights at most $n^\alpha$ (its Corollary 3(i) and Theorem 4). The proof is parameter counting: a network of width $n^\alpha$ has too few parameters to store $n$ labels. The guarantee holds at one learner width tied to $n$; it is not uniform over learner widths, and for $L\ge3$ it cannot be, by §3. So the memorization of §3 is a phenomenon of overparametrized learners. This repairs a proposition of the red-team pass whose stated rate was wrong.

**A tolerance above a known residual** (red-team Proposition 4.1, refereed: [logic](verification/RT-tube-logic.md), [computation](verification/RT-tube-computation.md)). Suppose a small network $g$ fits every label to within $\rho_0$: $|y-g(x)|\le\rho_0$ almost surely. Condition the strong prior on $|z_w(x_i)-y_i|<\rho$ for every $i$, with $\rho>\rho_0$. Then the clone of $g$ is feasible, so in the strong-prior limit the posterior stays at energy at most $E_*+2$ for every $n$, which rules out the memorization of §3 (at small $n$ it can still fit noise); with polynomially many samples the posterior-averaged clipped test loss is at most $\rho^2+\varepsilon$, at every learner width at least the width of $g$. This is a bounded-residual guarantee, not an oracle inequality: it compares with $\rho^2$, not with $L_D(g)$, and the learner must know $\rho_0$.

**Conditioning on the aggregate fit** (red-team Remark 6.2, refereed: [logic](verification/RT-aggregate-logic.md)). Condition the strong prior on $\frac1n\sum_i(z_w(x_i)-y_i)^2<\tau$. If $\tau\ge L_D(g)+(U+1)^2\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2n)}$, then with probability at least $1-2\delta$, at every learner width $N\ge m$, the clipped Gibbs risk and the risk of the posterior mean of the clipped outputs are at most $\tau+O\bigl(D_n((E_*+1)/H)^{H/2}n^{-1/2}\bigr)+O\bigl(\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/n}\bigr)$ in the strong-prior limit. The learner conditions on the aggregate fit, not on each output, and $\tau$ is an oracle hyperparameter. The referee noted that $\tau$ can be set from the data by a least-squares fit over the competitor class, which makes the learner partly non-Bayesian.

**Depth two** ([depth-two.md](research-notes/depth-two.md), refereed in two rounds and re-refereed twice during finalization; its last revision was checked but not refereed again). At depth two memorization is expensive: fitting $s$ arbitrary values costs energy of order $s$ up to factors polynomial in $d$, not $s^{1/H}$. Close conditioning still fails, but more weakly. On the noisy cube with $n\approx2^d$, at learner widths $N\ge N_0=O(n\log^4n)$, SP$_c$ has excess at least of order $(dc+1)^{-2}$ (Theorem 5.1, Corollary 5.2). This is a constant when $c\le\nu/\log_2n$ for a fixed $\nu>0$, and $\beta(\lambda)-o(1)$ when $c\log n\to0$. For every $c\le\mathrm{polylog}(n)$, $c=1$ included, the excess is at least polylogarithmically small, which defeats every polynomial sample bound, so SP$_c$ fails (P$_2$). Whether SP$_1$ satisfies the weaker "$+o(1)$" formulation of the conjecture at depth two is open: the lower bound tends to $0$, and the known upper bound does not. The tempered learner of §4 satisfies both formulations at depth two for clipped predictions (for example with $c=n^{1/2}$), so the conjecture itself is not refuted there either.

## 9. What this says about the motivating question

The motivation was that real input–output maps, though complex, are often well approximated by small networks, so a learner that competes with the best small network would inherit a test-error guarantee on such data. The results support one version of this and refute another.

- **What holds.** For clipped predictions, the tempered learner of §4 competes with every depth-$L$ network of width $m$ and weight bound $B$, at every learner width $N\ge m$, with a sample size polynomial in $d$, $m$, $B$, $1/\varepsilon$ and $\log(1/\delta)$, and the same holds at the repository's finite prior scale for $\theta<\theta_{L,\gamma}$, which equals $1/H$ when $\gamma\ge2-1/H$ (§7). Nothing is assumed about how the labels arise. So the repository's style of guarantee does extend from data generated by a small network to data that are merely approximated by one, provided the likelihood is tempered by a factor $c\approx n^{1-\theta}$ with $\theta<1/H$. For unclipped predictions, including the posterior mean of $z_w$, this is proved only for $\theta<1/L$, in the strong-prior limit.
- **What fails.** Conditioning each training output tightly fails at large learner width. Whatever the best small network does not capture, whether label noise or fine-scale structure that the learner cannot resolve at its energy level (Claim A′ in §3), acts as noise. On the laws of §3, a learner of large width that fits each output tightly memorizes this residual and pays for it at test time. Since real data are rarely produced by an exact small network, the residual is exactly what the conjecture is about. The repository's clean-label scaling $c=1$ is the wrong likelihood for this setting, unless the learner is small compared with $n$ (§8).
- **What remains uncertain.** The counterexamples are worst-case over laws: they need the input dimension to grow like $\log_2n$ and inputs that are near-atomic at the learner's resolution, and for inputs with bounded density, such as uniform inputs on $[-1,1]^d$ with noisy labels, the question is open. The positive results are proved in the strong-prior limit $\sigma\to0$ or at the repository's finite prior scale, for MLPs of fixed depth. Their constants are poor (about $10^{14}$ samples in the example of §4), and the exponent $1/H$ means that deeper networks need more tempering. The gaps between Bayesian learning and SGD, and between MLPs and other architectures, are not addressed.

## 10. Relation to other work

- **Tempered posteriors in classification.** Zhu and Srebro ([COLT 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v291/zhu25a.html)) and Zhu, Ohannessian and Srebro ([arXiv:2603.22644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22644)) study, for noisy binary classification, a PAC-Bayes/MDL rule that weights the KL divergence to the prior by $\lambda$. The Bayes choice $\lambda=1$ overfits with non-vanishing agnostic excess, and weights $\lambda$ with $1\ll\lambda\ll n/\log n$, for sample size $n$, are agnostically consistent. Our $c$ plays the role of $\lambda$. For deep tanh networks the required tempering is much stronger, $c\gg n^{1-1/H}$, because memorization costs energy only $n^{1/H}$.
- **Misspecified Bayes.** Grünwald and Langford (2007) and Grünwald and van Ommen's SafeBayes (2017) showed that Bayes can be inconsistent under misspecification and that tempering repairs it.
- **Cold posteriors.** SP$_c$ at scale $\sigma$ is the $\sigma=1$ posterior raised to the power $1/\sigma^2$, a cold posterior. Its $\sigma\to0$ limit is penalized least squares with penalty $c/n$.
- **Interpolation.** Along the joint scaling $n\asymp|X|$, where atoms repeat, Theorem A bounds the excess below by a constant: in the terms of Mallinar et al. (NeurIPS 2022), whose taxonomy concerns $n\to\infty$ at a fixed law, the overfitting along this scaling is not benign. Benign overfitting requires high effective dimension or estimators that are spiky at the data's resolution (Bartlett, Long, Lugosi and Tsigler 2020; Rakhlin and Zhai 2019; Haas et al. 2023). The open bounded-density case of §11 is a question of this kind.
- **Aggregation.** The fixed-temperature bound of §6 is the classical exp-concave progressive-mixture inequality (Vovk; Kivinen and Warmuth; Catoni; Audibert), combined with the repository's prior-mass bounds.

A fuller annotated bibliography is in [literature.md](research-notes/literature.md). Its references were checked against search listings; full texts were not available in the working environment.

## 11. Open problems

1. **Bounded-density inputs.** Does SP$_1$ fail (P$_L$) at large learner width on laws whose input density is at most $2^{o(d^2)}$ times uniform, for example uniform inputs on $[-1,1]^d$ with noisy labels? Proposition 7.4, as interpreted in A's Remark 7.5 (an interpretation, not a theorem), shows that the proof's Lipschitz transfer cannot reach them ([A, Proposition 7.4, Remark 7.5 and §10](research-notes/A-tight-conditioning-fails.md)).
2. **Intermediate learner widths.** For $c=1$, nothing is known between the small-width regime of §8 (learner width up to about $\sqrt{n/\log n}$) and the polynomial width $N_0$ of §3.
3. **Depth two at $c=1$.** Does SP$_1$ at depth two have the "$+o(1)$" formulation, or a constant excess? See §8 and [depth-two.md](research-notes/depth-two.md) for the open range of $c$.
4. **Competitor weights.** Close the window $\alpha\in[1/(4H),\,L/(2H(2L-1))]$ for competitors with width and weights at most $n^\alpha$ ([B, Corollary 9.3](research-notes/B-tempered-likelihood.md)), and decide whether a Bayesian learner other than SP$_c$ reaches further.
5. **The threshold itself.** The behaviour at $\theta=1/H$, where B narrows the gap to a polylogarithmic factor in $c$, and unclipped predictors for $n^{1-1/H}\lesssim c\lesssim n^{1-1/L}$.
6. **Finite scale.** The window $\theta\in[\theta_{L,\gamma},1/H)$ for $1\le\gamma<2-1/H$, which would follow from a better mean-field cover at $\gamma=1$, or from a way past the width threshold of spectral truncation for $\gamma>1$ (the obstructions in E's proof); unclipped predictions; Claim A′ at finite scale ([E, §6](research-notes/E-finite-prior-scale.md)).
7. **Fixed temperature.** A bound for the final posterior mean or Gibbs predictor at a fixed $\eta\le1/8$ without the additive $4\eta$; the unclipped predictors under network priors; and the behaviour of FIX$_\eta$ at large width under the standard fan-in prior or at $\gamma\le0$, where prior outputs do not shrink ([C, §C.9](research-notes/C-fixed-temperature-bayes.md)).
8. **Other predictors and learners.** The progressive-mixture predictor for SP$_c$; TUBE$_\rho$ on continuous i.i.d. inputs; a hyperprior over the tempering $c$ or the threshold $\tau$ of §8.
9. **Constants.** The proved sample sizes are astronomically large; the exponents come from worst-case capacity bounds.
