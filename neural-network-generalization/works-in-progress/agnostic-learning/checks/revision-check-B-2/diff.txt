> The learner is SP$_c$: a Gaussian strong-prior weight law with a Gaussian likelihood of variance $c\sigma^2$, where $c=c(n)=n^{1-\theta}$. The limit $\sigma\downarrow0$ is taken after the sample and the learner width $N$ are fixed. The depth is $L=H+1\ge2$. In this limit every predictor considered here is controlled through the near-minimizers of $F_c/n^\theta=n^{-\theta}\kappa_N+\hat L_S$, an energy-penalized least-squares objective (Lemma 3.1 and Corollary 3.2). The theorems below are therefore uniform-convergence bounds for that objective combined with a Laplace lemma, and posterior averaging plays no further role. This is the repository's convention for the strong-prior limit (`NNG/side-results/continuous-domains/source/paper.tex`, Theorem `strong`; this file is cited as `paper.tex` below). Nothing is proved here at a fixed prior scale (Claim E).
> 
> Labels lie in $[-1,1]$ throughout, as in the brief. This is used in (1.1), in Lemma 2.3(a) and in every loss range, so unbounded label noise, such as $y=f(x)+\mathcal N(0,s^2)$, is not covered. An extension that truncates the labels at $O(\sqrt{\log n})$ is plausible but has not been checked.

> **Tempering, not close conditioning.** The brief quotes the user's expectation that the learner "should condition to be pretty close to the given outputs on train inputs". For $H\ge2$ and a learner required to work at every large width, this expectation fails in the agnostic setting. SP$_c$ with any fixed $c>0$ (Section 1), or with $c=n^{1-\theta}$ for $\theta>1/H$ (Theorem 7.4(b)), fits pure label noise on the noisy cube: with probability $\ge1-e^{-n^{1/3}/2}$, its excess is $\ge1/e-o(1)$ at large widths. Claim A (`A-tight-conditioning-fails.md`) treats SP$_1$ and the tube-conditioned learner. The learner of Theorem 6.3 is tempered instead: $F_c=\kappa_N+n^\theta\hat L_S$ weights the training error by $n^\theta$ rather than by $n$ as SP$_1$ does, and the proof requires its near-minimizers to fit the training labels only to within $\hat L_S(g)+(E_*+1)n^{-\theta}$ (Lemma 2.3(c)). Its likelihood scale must depend on $n$ (Section 1).
