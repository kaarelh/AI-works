> - (1) **Positive.** The finite-scale proof of `NNG/works-in-progress/slt-likelihood/README.md` §2 adapts to agnostic data with $c=n^{1-\theta}$, with polynomial sample complexity at every width $N\ge m$, for clipped predictors: the expected Gibbs risk of $\operatorname{clip}z_w$ and the posterior mean of $\operatorname{clip}z_w$. For $L\ge3$ and $1\le\gamma<2-1/H$ the range proved here is smaller. For $1<\gamma<2-1/H$ the reason is that the width threshold of spectral truncation must be bridged (§2.7). At $\gamma=1$ the range is set by the size of the imported mean-field cover (I3): the bound on its log-cardinality has degree $D_H$ in the energy level, and the proof's condition (C5) then requires $\theta<1/D_H$. Bridging that cover's width threshold, of degree $D_T$, needs only $\theta<1/(D_T+1)$, a weaker condition. The true range is open in both cases (§6, open problems 1 and 6).

< 1. **The window $\theta\in[\theta_{L,\gamma},\frac1H)$ for $1\le\gamma<2-\frac1H$.** I conjecture that $\theta<1/H$ suffices at every $\gamma\ge1$. The obstruction in the proof is Lemma 2.4, step 6, which bounds the effect of the random bulk $(W^\ell)_{\le b}$ by its operator norm times the worst-case amplification. A random bulk acting on a fixed vector loses a further factor $\sqrt N$ when it is seen through a matrix of bounded Frobenius norm. Consistently, `../checks/E/check_lemmaT.py` shows deviations decaying like $N^{(1-\gamma)/2}\cdot N^{-1/2}$ rather than $N^{(1-\gamma)/2}$. The posterior can align the bulk with the data, but only at a translation cost (G2), and that cost turns the aligned part into energy. Making this rigorous would need a width-free capacity bound for "bounded energy plus random bulk" networks under the posterior. I do not have one.
