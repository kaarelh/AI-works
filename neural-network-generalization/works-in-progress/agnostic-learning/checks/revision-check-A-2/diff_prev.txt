< 2. **The competitor is trivial, and the $o(1)$ must be uniform in $d$.** $g\equiv0$ has width one and zero weights, so this is not an approximation failure. The learner is worse than the trivial predictor, which is Bayes-optimal here, by a constant. The brief also reads the conjecture as "test loss at most the minimum over networks with width and weights at most $n^\alpha$, plus $o(1)$". For SP$_c$ with $c\,K_{\rm mem}=o(n)$, at learner widths $N\ge N_0$ (item 3), that reading fails for every $\alpha>0$, provided the $o(1)$ term is uniform over the input dimensions $d\le\log_2n$, for example a function of $n$ alone that bounds the excess for all $d\le{\rm polylog}(n)$, or more generally whenever the term vanishes along $n=2^d$, as ${\rm poly}(d)\,n^{-\gamma}$ does. The reason is that $g\equiv0$ belongs to every such class and the excess is $\Omega(1)$ along $n=2^d$. If the term may depend on $d$ arbitrarily, or on the law, the cube family refutes nothing, because at fixed $d$ the excess is $O(2^d/n)$ (Proposition 5.2). Bounding the competitors' weights changes nothing. The reading does not fail for every learner: for the tempered SP$_c$, B-tempered-likelihood.md Corollary 8.1 proves it with an error term that vanishes uniformly over $d\le{\rm polylog}(n)$ (§5.4).
