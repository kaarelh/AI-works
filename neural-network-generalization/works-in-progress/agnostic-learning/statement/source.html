The tempered-likelihood learner:
definition and agnostic guarantee

Claude Opus 5.5, Kaarel Hänni, and Dmitry Vaintrob*
11 November 2026
This is a self-contained statement, without proofs, of the main positive result of the agnostic-learning note (neural-network-generalization/works-in-progress/agnostic-learning/). Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 are Theorem 6.3 and Corollary 8.1 of research-notes/B-tempered-likelihood.md, cited below as B. Section 4 also summarizes results of B and of E-finite-prior-scale.md (E) and A-tight-conditioning-fails.md (A) in the same folder. Their proofs are paper proofs with numerical checks, refereed by independent AI agents; they are not Lean-verified. The remark “One tempering for a range of class sizes” in Section 3 is derived here from Theorem 1 and has not been refereed.

1. Setting

Data. Fix \(d\ge1\) and a nonempty compact set \(X\subseteq[-1,1]^d\). A data law \(D\) is any Borel probability measure on \(X\times[-1,1]\). Nothing else is assumed: within \([-1,1]\), the labels may be noisy, or any deterministic or random function of the input. Unbounded label noise, such as Gaussian noise added to a target, is not covered. The sample \(S=((x_i,y_i))_{i=1}^n\) consists of \(n\) independent draws from \(D\). For \(f:X\to\mathbb R\), the test loss and the training loss are \[ L_D(f)=\mathbb E_{(x,y)\sim D}\,(f(x)-y)^2,\qquad \hat L_S(f)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl(f(x_i)-y_i\bigr)^2, \] and \(\operatorname{clip}(u)=\max\{-1,\min\{1,u\}\}\) truncates a real number to the label range.

Networks. The depth is \(L=H+1\ge2\), with \(H\) hidden layers, and the width is \(N\). A parameter is \(w=(W^1,\dots,W^H,v)\), with \(W^1\in\mathbb R^{N\times(d+1)}\), \(W^\ell\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}\) for \(2\le\ell\le H\), and \(v\in\mathbb R^N\). With \(\bar x=(1,x)\) and \(\tanh\) applied entrywise, the network computes \[ h^0(x)=\bar x,\qquad h^\ell(x)=\tanh\bigl(W^\ell h^{\ell-1}(x)\bigr)\ \ (1\le\ell\le H),\qquad z_w(x)=v^{\top}h^H(x). \] There are no biases other than the constant coordinate of \(\bar x\). The energy of \(w\) is \[ \kappa_N(w)=\frac dN\,\lVert W^1\rVert_F^2+\sum_{\ell=2}^{H}\lVert W^\ell\rVert_F^2+N\,\lVert v\rVert_2^2 . \]

Definition (the tempered-likelihood learner). Fix the depth \(L\), a learner width \(N\), a scale \(\sigma>0\) and an exponent \(\theta\in(0,1)\), and put \(c=n^{1-\theta}\).
  • Prior. \(\mu_\sigma(dw)\propto e^{-\kappa_N(w)/(2\sigma^2)}\,dw\): independent centred Gaussian weights with variances \(N\sigma^2/d\) (first layer), \(\sigma^2\) (internal layers) and \(\sigma^2/N\) (readout).
  • Likelihood. \(y_i\mid x_i,w\sim\mathrm N\bigl(z_w(x_i),\,c\,\sigma^2\bigr)\): Gaussian observation noise whose variance is the prior scale \(\sigma^2\) times the tempering factor \(c\).
  • Posterior. \[ \Pi_\sigma(dw)\ \propto\ e^{-F_{c,S}(w)/(2\sigma^2)}\,dw,\qquad F_{c,S}(w)=\kappa_N(w)+\frac1c\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl(z_w(x_i)-y_i\bigr)^2=\kappa_N(w)+n^{\theta}\,\hat L_S(z_w). \]
  • Strong-prior limit. The sample size \(n\), the sample \(S\) and the width \(N\) are fixed first; then \(\sigma\downarrow0\).
The learner's choices are the depth \(L\), the width \(N\) and the exponent \(\theta\); none of them depends on the data law, and the guarantees below hold at every width at least the competitor's. With \(c=1\), that is without tempering, this is the plain strong-prior Bayesian posterior with a Gaussian likelihood. The tempering \(c=n^{1-\theta}\) raises its likelihood to the power \(n^{\theta-1}\), so the learner is a power posterior whose temperature depends on \(n\), not one Bayesian model updated as data arrive.

Predictions. As \(\sigma\downarrow0\) the posterior concentrates on the near-minimizers \[ \mathcal N_1=\bigl\{w:\ F_{c,S}(w)\le \min F_{c,S}+1\bigr\} \] (the minimum exists). Equivalently, \(\mathcal N_1\) is the set where the energy-penalized least-squares objective \(\hat L_S(z_w)+n^{-\theta}\kappa_N(w)=n^{-\theta}F_{c,S}(w)\) is within \(n^{-\theta}\) of its minimum. The guarantee covers three ways of predicting, all with outputs clipped to \([-1,1]\):

  1. a near-minimizer: \(\operatorname{clip}z_w\) for any \(w\in\mathcal N_1\);
  2. a posterior draw, through its expected test loss \(\mathbb E_{w\sim\Pi_\sigma}L_D(\operatorname{clip}z_w)\) (the clipped Gibbs risk);
  3. the posterior mean of the clipped outputs, \(\bar f_\sigma(x)=\mathbb E_{w\sim\Pi_\sigma}\operatorname{clip}z_w(x)\).

The likelihood uses the unclipped output \(z_w\); clipping is applied only when predicting. Since every label lies in \([-1,1]\), clipping never increases a squared error.

Competitors. A competitor is any network \(g=z_{w^g}\) of the same architecture, with depth \(L\), width \(m\) and all weights in \([-B,B]\), \(B>0\), and we put \[ E_*=d(d+1)B^2+2(L-1)\,m^2B^2,\qquad U=mB,\qquad \Gamma_n=\sqrt{2(d+1)\ln(n+1)}+\frac{H-1}2 \] (\(\Gamma_n\) is written \(D_n\) in B). For every width \(N\ge m\), some width-\(N\) network computing exactly \(g\) (copy each hidden unit of \(g\) onto a group of \(\lfloor N/m\rfloor\) or \(\lceil N/m\rceil\) units and divide its outgoing weights by the group size) has energy at most \(E_*\), and \(U\ge\sup_x|g(x)|\).

2. The guarantee

Theorem 1 (agnostic oracle inequality at every learner width; B, Theorem 6.3). Let \(L=H+1\ge2\), \(\theta\in(0,1/H)\) and \(c=n^{1-\theta}\). Let \(n\ge1\), \(X\), the data law \(D\), the competitor \(g\) (width \(m\), weights in \([-B,B]\)) and \(\delta\in(0,1)\) be arbitrary, and put \[ \mathrm{Exc}_n=(E_*+1)\,n^{-\theta}+8\Bigl(\frac2H\Bigr)^{H/2}\Gamma_n\,n^{-(1-\theta H)/2}+\bigl(4+(U+1)^2\bigr)\sqrt{\frac{\ln(2/\delta)}{2n}} . \] There is an event of probability at least \(1-\delta\) over the sample, which depends on \(g\) but not on the learner width or on \(\sigma\), on which, for every learner width \(N\ge m\):
  1. every near-minimizer \(w\in\mathcal N_1\) has \(L_D(\operatorname{clip}z_w)\le L_D(g)+\mathrm{Exc}_n\);
  2. \(\Pi_\sigma\bigl\{w:\ L_D(\operatorname{clip}z_w)\gt L_D(g)+\mathrm{Exc}_n\bigr\}\to0\) as \(\sigma\downarrow0\);
  3. \(\limsup_{\sigma\downarrow0}\ \mathbb E_{w\sim\Pi_\sigma}L_D(\operatorname{clip}z_w)\le L_D(g)+\mathrm{Exc}_n\);
  4. \(\limsup_{\sigma\downarrow0}\ L_D(\bar f_\sigma)\le L_D(g)+\mathrm{Exc}_n\).
Moreover, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \(\mathrm{Exc}_n\le\varepsilon\) whenever \(n\ge n_0\), where \[ \begin{aligned} n_0:=\max\Bigl\{&\Bigl(\frac{3(E_*+1)}\varepsilon\Bigr)^{\frac1\theta},\ \Bigl(\frac{48(2/H)^{H/2}}\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{4(d+1)}{1-\theta H}}\Bigr)^{\frac4{1-\theta H}},\\ &\Bigl(\frac{24(2/H)^{H/2}(H-1)}\varepsilon\Bigr)^{\frac2{1-\theta H}},\ \frac{9\bigl(4+(U+1)^2\bigr)^2\ln(2/\delta)}{2\varepsilon^2}\Bigr\}, \end{aligned} \] and the third entry is omitted when \(H=1\). So the sample size is polynomial in \(d\), \(m\), \(B\), \(1/\varepsilon\) and \(\log(1/\delta)\) at fixed \(L\) and \(\theta\).

In words: whatever the data law, after \(n\) samples, with probability at least \(1-\delta\) and in the limit \(\sigma\downarrow0\), the tempered posterior at every width at least the competitor's predicts (after clipping) at least as well as the fixed network \(g\), up to an error \(\mathrm{Exc}_n\). The error tends to \(0\) as \(n\to\infty\), grows with the competitor's width and weight bound, and does not depend on the learner width. For example, \(\theta=1/(H+2)\) gives \[ \mathrm{Exc}_n\le\bigl[E_*+1+8(2/H)^{H/2}\Gamma_n\bigr]\,n^{-1/(H+2)}+\bigl(4+(U+1)^2\bigr)\sqrt{\frac{\ln(2/\delta)}{2n}}=\tilde O\bigl(\mathrm{poly}(d,m,B)\,n^{-1/(H+2)}\bigr). \] The constants are poor: at \(L=3\), \(d=10\), \(m=B=1\), \(\varepsilon=0.1\), \(\delta=0.05\) and \(\theta=1/4\), \(\mathrm{Exc}_n\) first drops below \(\varepsilon\) at \(n\approx10^{14.1}\), and the closed-form \(n_0\) is about \(10^{29.2}\).

3. Against networks whose width and weights are at most \(n^\alpha\)

Corollary 2 (B, Corollary 8.1). Fix \(L=H+1\ge2\) and \(\alpha\in\bigl(0,\frac1{4H}\bigr)\). Put \(\theta=\frac{1+8\alpha}{H+2}\in(0,\frac1H)\) and \(c=n^{1-\theta}\). Let \(\mathcal G^{(\alpha)}_n\) be the networks of the architecture above with depth \(L\), width at most \(\lfloor n^\alpha\rfloor\) and all weights in \([-n^\alpha,n^\alpha]\), and put \[ \beta=\frac{1-4\alpha H}{H+2}\ \ (H\ge2),\qquad \beta=\min\Bigl\{\frac{1-4\alpha}3,\ \frac12-4\alpha\Bigr\}\ \ (H=1), \] \[ \Xi_n=d(d+1)+2L+8\Bigl(\frac2H\Bigr)^{H/2}\Gamma_n+4\sqrt{2\ln(2/\delta)} . \] Then for every \(d\), every compact \(X\subseteq[-1,1]^d\), every data law \(D\) on \(X\times[-1,1]\), every \(n\ge1\) and every \(\delta\in(0,1)\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\), simultaneously for all learner widths \(N\ge n^\alpha\), \[ \limsup_{\sigma\downarrow0}\ \mathbb E_{w\sim\Pi_\sigma}L_D(\operatorname{clip}z_w)\ \le\ \min_{f\in\mathcal G^{(\alpha)}_n}L_D(f)+\Xi_n\,n^{-\beta}, \] and the same bound holds for every near-minimizer and for the posterior mean of the clipped outputs \(\bar f_\sigma\); the ordinary posterior mean \(\mathbb E_{\Pi_\sigma}z_w\) is not covered. When \(H=1\), \(\beta>0\) only for \(\alpha\lt1/8\).

For \(H\ge2\) this has the form of the motivating conjecture (test loss at most the least test loss over networks of size \(\mathrm{poly}(n)\), plus a vanishing term), restricted to small exponents and bounded weights: the clipped test loss is at most the least test loss over depth-\(L\) networks of the architecture above (tanh, no biases beyond the constant input) whose width and weights are at most \(n^\alpha\), plus \(O_L\bigl((d^2+\sqrt{d\log n}+\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)})\,n^{-\beta}\bigr)\), for every \(\alpha\lt1/(4H)\); at depth \(3\) one may take \(\alpha=1/16\), \(\beta=1/8\). It does not cover all polynomial sizes: the weights must be bounded as well as the width, and Section 4 reports (B, Corollary 9.3) that, once \(\alpha\gt L/(2H(2L-1))\), no tempering \(c(n)\) gives a polynomial sample bound for the clipped predictions that is uniform over laws and input dimensions and holds at every learner width \(N\ge n^\alpha\).

One tempering for a range of class sizes (derived here from Theorem 1, by the arithmetic of the proof of B's Corollary 8.1; not stated or refereed in the notes). Let \(H\ge2\) and fix any \(\theta\in(0,1/H)\), without reference to \(\alpha\). For \(\alpha\in(0,\theta/4)\), apply Theorem 1 with \(m=\lfloor n^\alpha\rfloor\) and \(B=n^\alpha\) to a minimizer \(g\) of \(L_D\) over \(\mathcal G^{(\alpha)}_n\); it exists because the parameter set is compact once narrower networks are padded with zeros, and it depends only on \(D\), \(n\) and \(\alpha\), not on the sample. Since \(E_*+1\le(d(d+1)+2L)\,n^{4\alpha}\) and \(4+(U+1)^2\le8n^{4\alpha}\), there is an event of probability at least \(1-\delta\), which depends on \(\alpha\), on which statements (a)–(d) of Theorem 1 hold at every learner width \(N\ge n^\alpha\), with \(L_D(g)\) replaced by \(\min_{f\in\mathcal G^{(\alpha)}_n}L_D(f)\) and \(\mathrm{Exc}_n\) replaced by \[ (d(d+1)+2L)\,n^{-(\theta-4\alpha)}+8\Bigl(\frac2H\Bigr)^{H/2}\Gamma_n\,n^{-(1-\theta H)/2}+4\sqrt{2\ln(2/\delta)}\;n^{-(1/2-4\alpha)} . \] This is \(\tilde O\bigl(n^{-\min\{\theta-4\alpha,\,(1-\theta H)/2\}}\bigr)\), since the third exponent never binds when \(H\ge2\). So one \(\theta\), chosen without knowing \(\alpha\), gives a vanishing bound for each fixed \(\alpha\in(0,\theta/4)\), and \(\theta\) close to \(1/H\) covers every \(\alpha\) up to nearly \(1/(4H)\). The rate is never faster than the rate \(n^{-\beta}\) of Corollary 2 and equals it only at \(\alpha=((H+2)\theta-1)/8\). No claim is made simultaneously over \(\alpha\).

4. What else is known (scope)

  • Unclipped predictions (B, Theorem 6.4). The same conclusions hold for \(z_w\), for the expected test loss \(\mathbb E_{\Pi_\sigma}L_D(z_w)\) of a posterior draw, and for the ordinary posterior mean \(\bar z_\sigma=\mathbb E_{\Pi_\sigma}z_w\) and its clip, provided \(\theta\in(0,1/L)\), with a larger error term. For \(\theta\in[1/L,1/H)\) the unclipped predictors are open.
  • Sharpness (B, Theorem 7.4). For \(H\ge2\) and \(\theta\gt1/H\), the learner memorizes label noise. Take inputs uniform on \(\{-1,1\}^d\), fair random labels independent of the input, and \(n=2^d\) samples; the zero network has test loss \(1\), the Bayes risk. With probability at least \(1-e^{-n^{1/3}/2}\), at every learner width \(N\ge N_0(n)=\lceil2(d+1)^3n^{1+\theta}\ln2\rceil+1\), every near-minimizer has test loss at least \(1+1/e-o(1)\), clipped or not, and so do the limits inferior as \(\sigma\downarrow0\) of the expected test loss of a posterior draw (clipped or not) and of the test losses of the posterior means \(\bar f_\sigma\), \(\bar z_\sigma\) and \(\operatorname{clip}\bar z_\sigma\). The exponent \(1/H\) is therefore sharp in the worst case over data laws. The bound is asymptotic (for \(H=2\) and \(\theta=0.55\) it is \(0\) at \(d=50\), \(100\) and \(200\)), the counterexample needs the input dimension \(d=\log_2n\) to grow, and at a fixed \(d\), or at learner widths below \(N_0(n)\), nothing is shown.
  • Competitor weights (B, Section 9). For this learner, with any tempering \(c(n)\) in place of \(n^{1-\theta}\), bounded competitor weights are needed for a guarantee uniform over laws. For \(H\ge2\) and the classes \(\mathcal G^{(\alpha)}_n\), once \(\alpha\gt L/(2H(2L-1))\) no \(c(n)\) gives a polynomial sample bound for the clipped predictions that is uniform over laws and input dimensions and holds at every learner width \(N\ge n^\alpha\); the failing laws change with \(n\) (the noisy cube in dimension \(\log_2n\) at large learner widths, or a two-point law in dimension \(1\) with inputs \(\pm\delta_0\to0\)). For a fixed data law and \(\theta\in(0,1/H)\) no weight bound is needed: by the universal approximation theorem, with probability at least \(1-\delta\), the clipped test loss comes within any \(\varepsilon>0\) of the Bayes risk once \(n\) and the learner width exceed thresholds that depend on the law (B, Remark 9.4).
  • Other competitors (B, Remark 8.2). A depth-\(L\) tanh network with biases, width \(m\), and weights and biases in \([-B,B]\) equals a network of the architecture above with width \(m+1\) and weights in \([-B',B']\), \(B'=\max\{B,1\}/\tanh1\); Theorem 1 applies to it with these values. Competitors of smaller depth or with other activations (such as ReLU) are not covered.
  • The prior scale. Everything above is stated in the strong-prior limit \(\sigma\downarrow0\). At the finite prior scale \(\sigma^2=N^{-\gamma}\), \(\gamma\ge1\), with no limit taken, the analogue of Theorem 1(c),(d) holds when \(\theta\lt\theta_{L,\gamma}\) (E, Theorem 2.7): for competitors with \(B\ge1\) and \(n\) above a polynomial in \(d,m,B,1/\varepsilon,\log(1/\delta)\) (not the explicit \(n_0\) above), with probability at least \(1-\delta\) and at every width \(N\ge m\), the expected clipped test loss of a posterior draw and the test loss of the posterior mean of the clipped outputs are at most \(L_D(g)+\varepsilon\). Here \(\theta_{L,\gamma}=1/H\) when \(L=2\) or \(\gamma\ge2-1/H\); \(\theta_{L,\gamma}=\max\bigl\{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma H-1},\frac{\gamma-1}{H(H-1)(2-\gamma)}\bigr\}\) when \(1\lt\gamma\lt2-1/H\); and \(\theta_{L,1}=1/(4+16(L-2)3^{L-2})\) for \(L\ge3\), a case whose proof relies on an imported mean-field cover. E states no analogue of Corollary 2; for \(H\ge2\) and \(\gamma\ge2\) its explicit rate decays against \(\mathcal G^{(\alpha)}_n\) only for \(\alpha\lt1/(8H)\) (E, Remark 2.8, in a comparison added after refereeing).
  • Close conditioning (A, Theorem 4.4 and Corollary 4.5). With no or weak tempering, that is \(c=1\) or any \(c\) with \(c\,\bigl(d^2\log^2(d+1)+(dn)^{1/H}\bigr)=o(n)\), the learner fails at depth \(L\ge3\). On the noisy cube above with \(n/2^d\to1\), with probability at least \(1-e^{-2^{d/2}}\) and at learner widths above a polynomial in \(n\), every near-minimizer, a posterior draw (with posterior probability tending to \(1\) as \(\sigma\downarrow0\)) and the posterior means in the limit, clipped or not, have test loss at least \(1+\beta(1)-o(1)\) as \(d\to\infty\), where \(\beta(1)\approx0.4848\), against \(1\) for the zero network. At finite \(d\) the certified excess is smaller (about \(0.25\) at \(H=2\), \(d=20\), for \(c=1\) and \(n=2^d\) with probability \(0.99\); A, Table 4.1).

Idea of the proof. As \(\sigma\downarrow0\) the posterior concentrates on near-minimizers of \(F_{c,S}\). Comparing with a low-energy width-\(N\) copy of the competitor bounds their training loss by \(\hat L_S(g)+(E_*+1)\,n^{-\theta}\), and comparing with the zero network bounds their energy by \(n^\theta+1\). A Rademacher bound for tanh networks of bounded energy, valid at every width, then controls the gap between the training and test loss of the clipped outputs. It is a path-norm bound on the last \(H\) layers, with the first layer charged through the VC dimension \(d+1\) of halfspaces rather than through its energy, which is why \(H\) and not \(L\) appears: at energy \(K\approx n^{\theta}\) the gap is of order \(K^{H/2}\Gamma_n/\sqrt n\), which vanishes exactly when \(\theta\lt1/H\). Finally, Hoeffding's inequality for the fixed competitor gives \(\hat L_S(g)\le L_D(g)+(U+1)^2\sqrt{\ln(2/\delta)/(2n)}\); a union bound over the two events, each of probability at least \(1-\delta/2\), and Jensen's inequality for the posterior mean give Theorem 1.
