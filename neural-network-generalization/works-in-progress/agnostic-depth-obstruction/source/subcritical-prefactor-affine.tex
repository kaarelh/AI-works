\section{Affine skips throughout the subcritical-prefactor range}
\label{sec:subcritical-prefactor-affine}
The event-entropy argument also extends the affine-output theorem beyond the sufficiently small prefactors used in its initial proof.

\begin{corollary}[All prefactors bounded strictly below criticality]
\label{cor:subcritical-prefactor-affine}
Fix $\nu_{\max}<1$. The constant-accuracy Gaussian-noise obstruction of Theorem~\ref{thm:affine-skip-fixed-accuracy} holds simultaneously for all scales $0<Ns\le\nu_{\max}$, after allowing its support-growth constant, minimum depth, and width constant to depend on $\nu_{\max}$. It retains the excess-risk lower bound $2^{-34}$, arbitrary independent Gaussian affine-output covariances, every positive likelihood temperature, and arbitrary mixtures of the raw posterior means. The inputs can be taken on the unit sphere as in Section~\ref{sec:gaussian-feature-memorizer}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The earlier theorem already covers $0<Ns\le c_0$ for a fixed sufficiently small $c_0>0$. It is therefore enough to prove the result uniformly on $\nu=Ns\in[c_0,\nu_{\max}]$. Put $\kappa=\kappa(\nu)$ and choose a fixed small norm threshold $t>0$. The event floor from Corollary~\ref{cor:subcritical-indicator-floor} has coefficient
\[
 q=Q_{H,N,\nu}(t,n)=(1-o(1))\kappa H
\]
uniformly on this compact interval whenever $n=4\cdot2^{\lfloor\eta H\rfloor}$ and $N\ge e^{CH}$. The subcritical-prefactor comparison has normalized entropy at most $(1+\delta)\kappa H$ and any fixed prescribed RMS error, after shrinking $\eta>0$ and increasing $C,H_0$ uniformly on that interval. Hence its comparison cost $K$ satisfies $1-q/K\le2\delta$ for sufficiently large $H$.

Integrate the independent affine Gaussian block exactly as before. Its marginal neural loss is
\[
 \widehat R_B(z)=\|B^{1/2}(y-z(S))\|_n^2,
 \qquad 0\prec B\preceq I,
\]
and $P$, the projection off the full affine training span, obeys $PB=P$ and $P\preceq B$. For the event
\[
 A_B=\{\|B^{1/2}z(S)\|_n>t\},
\]
containment in $\{\max_i|z(X_i)|>t\}$ gives the uniform entropy floor
\[
 2s\operatorname{KL}(\rho\|\mu)\ge q\rho(A_B)-2s\log2.
\]
The zero comparison has free energy at most $\widehat R_B(0)+s\le1+s$ on the sample event used previously. The finite-function comparison approximates the empirical atom means after the same clipping to $[-8,8]$ used in the original affine proof, and contraction by $B^{1/2}$ cannot increase its error. Thus the segment lemma applies in the $B$ inner product, with the entropy-floor error $2\lambda s\log2$, the high-output energy $q$, and the comparison cost $K$.

For every fixed $\Lambda<\infty$, the error $2\lambda s\log2$ is arbitrarily small uniformly over $\lambda\le\Lambda/H$ at the prescribed widths. Choose $\delta,t$ and the comparison tolerance as small as in the original affine proof. The segment lemma then gives, for some $p\in[0,1]$,
\[
 \|Pm_z-pPb_S\|_n\le2^{-17}
 \qquad(0<\lambda\le\Lambda/H).
\]
All constants can be made uniform in $B$ and in the compact prefactor interval.

For the complementary temperatures use the same event floor directly. If $p_B=\Pi(A_B)$, Gibbs comparison with the prior gives
\[
 p_B\le\frac{1+s}{\lambda q}+\frac{2s\log2}{q},
 \qquad
 \E_\Pi\|B^{1/2}z(S)\|_n^2\le5.
\]
The second inequality follows from $\widehat R_B(0)\le1$, $B\preceq I$, and $s\le1/2$, exactly as in Lemma~\ref{lem:subcritical-high-temperature}. Therefore
\[
 \|Pm_z\|_n\le\|B^{1/2}m_z\|_n
                  \le t+\sqrt{5p_B}.
\]
Since $\inf_{\nu\in[c_0,\nu_{\max}]}\kappa(\nu)>0$, choose a fixed sufficiently large $\Lambda$ to make the right side at most $2^{-17}$ for every $\lambda\ge\Lambda/H$. Increase the width and depth thresholds to control the vanishing terms. This proves the required projected near-zero estimate without an operator-energy tail.

The sample witness, residual from affine functions, Gaussian clipping estimates, and passage to population risk in the original affine proof are unchanged. On the unit sphere use the whitened affine design $(1,T,2\sqrt D\,x_{\rm nuisance},S)$, which spans exactly the same affine functions and has the same fixed lower Gram bound. The witness is independent of $B$, $\nu$, width, and temperature, so it applies simultaneously and survives mixtures. Finally choose the smaller support-growth constant and larger thresholds from the compact-prefactor proof and the existing $\nu\le c_0$ proof. Their common sample event proves the full asserted range.
\end{proof}
