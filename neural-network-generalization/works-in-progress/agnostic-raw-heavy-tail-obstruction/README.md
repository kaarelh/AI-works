# Failures of unbounded Bayesian prediction: a tanh network and Gaussian-mixture priors

Publication slug: `agnostic-raw-heavy-tail-obstruction`.

This standalone paper gives a lower bound for the **actual raw posterior mean** of a single biased tanh neuron with independent Student weights of any fixed tail index greater than two. Its ordinary Gaussian likelihood has any fixed positive variance and uses all observations. The prior and architecture are fixed.

For a sequence of bounded, noiseless data distributions, the raw mean has population squared risk at least a constant times `n^(1/3)` with constant probability, while the fixed zero-network comparator has risk at most `n^(-1/3)`. The same divergence holds in expectation. Every finite-sample posterior moment used in the proof exists.

The adverse distributions depend on sample size and have two very close common inputs and a rare input of mass `1/n`. An extension places every input on the unit circle in dimension two, and fixed-sample continuity gives smooth atomless densities on that circle; their density bounds are not uniform in sample size. A further corollary gives divergence for deterministic polylogarithmically growing likelihood variances, and for temperature `n^(1/4)` when the Student tail index is greater than three. These statements do not cover data-dependent temperature selection. The theorem refutes a distribution-uniform vanishing approximation oracle for this prior; it does not prove inconsistency for one fixed distribution, impose fixed input regularity, or establish failure for every wider architecture or heavy-tailed neural prior.

The proof controls the full evidence, including nearly saturated neurons, and the signed prediction numerator. It is not a lower bound only on posterior-averaged risk. A separate fixed-Gaussian-feature positive result remains outside this package because it has a different prior and does not address a hierarchical mixture of hidden features.

The zero comparator also has a comparison distribution with `KL=O(log n)` and risk at most its zero-network risk plus `n^(-2)`: condition only the readout on `|v|≤1/n`. The example therefore directly rules out a naive extension of a bounded-prediction PAC–Bayesian posterior-mean oracle to this unbounded prior.

A self-contained appendix gives a separate generic obstruction. One fixed prior on smooth functions on `[0,1]`, with a uniform sub-Gaussian supremum and centered rank-one conditional Gaussian kernels whose diagonals are at most one, fails at every fixed likelihood temperature: its raw posterior mean has risk growing linearly along a sample-size sequence. The zero function has prior mass one half and risk one half. Thus even a fixed multiplicative agnostic factor cannot follow from those generic tail and covariance assumptions alone. This example is **not a fixed finite neural architecture**, and it does not refute the specific normalized Gaussian-readout neural prior. Its adverse laws vary with sample size and may depend on temperature; a single law sequence defeating all temperatures is not claimed. It is compatible with fixed-temperature realizable bounds because its cheap comparator has nonzero approximation error.

A final generic extension replaces the Gaussian amplitude by any symmetric full-support law with finite first moment, while preserving its native supremum-tail bounds. This includes `asinh(Gaussian)` amplitudes, whose moments grow only logarithmically. One fixed function prior still fails at every fixed temperature, with possibly much slower divergence along a larger sample-size sequence. This remains a non-neural counterexample and is compatible with the positive neural theorem using a temperature proportional to `log² n`.
