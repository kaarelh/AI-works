\begin{abstract}
Can a wide Gaussian Bayesian neural network compete in test squared loss with the best smaller network, without assuming that the smaller network generates the data? For ReLU networks with $H\ge1$ hidden layers, we identify a sharp strict exponent threshold for comparator width $n^a$ and coefficient bound $n^b$. If $Ha+(H+1)b<1/2$, an explicit Gaussian likelihood calibration gives a coefficient-one oracle inequality with excess tending to zero at every learner width above comparator width, both in the strong-prior limit and for actual finite Gaussian posteriors at every scaling exponent $\gamma\ge2$. It covers ordinary raw-output posterior means. If $Ha+(H+1)b>1/2$ and $a>0$, one well-conditioned input law defeats every likelihood temperature, even when selected from the data. Its conditional mean is a globally bounded teacher in the stated class. Failure holds at every sufficiently large learner width, including actual canonical finite priors with $\gamma\ge2$. With one hidden layer, both finite-prior results cover every $\gamma\ge1$. The lower-bound dimension grows logarithmically with sample count; the threshold concerns guarantees uniform over laws and dimensions with polynomial dimension dependence. The equality case is open. Positive homogeneity, exact Boolean memorization, and a bounded narrow tent function give the two directions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Approximation guarantees and the threshold}
Data may be well approximated by a small network without being exactly generated by it. A natural guarantee for Bayesian neural-network learning is
\[
 \Risk(\widehat f_S)\le\inf_{g\in\mathcal G_n}\Risk(g)+o(1),
 \qquad \Risk(f)=\E(f(X)-Y)^2,
\]
where the comparator class $\mathcal G_n$ grows polynomially with the sample count $n$, while the learner may have much larger width. The additive remainder and the bound on comparator coefficients matter: even an unknown constant incurs estimation error, and width alone does not control how steep a real-valued network can be.

We study a fixed dense ReLU architecture, independent Gaussian weights, and a Gaussian squared-error likelihood. The likelihood variance is calibrated jointly with the prior. For $H$ hidden layers and total depth $L=H+1$, the dividing quantity is
\[
 s=Ha+Lb
\]
when comparator width and coefficient cap grow as $n^a$ and $n^b$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Comparator growth & A vanishing oracle bound exists & Every temperature fails\\
\midrule
Width $n^a$, weight cap $n^b$ & $Ha+Lb<1/2$ & $Ha+Lb>1/2$, $a>0$\\
Width $n^a$, fixed weight cap & $a<1/(2H)$ & $a>1/(2H)$\\
Width and weight cap $n^a$ & $a<1/(4H+2)$ & $a>1/(4H+2)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The positive guarantee has polynomial dependence on input dimension and is uniform over input and label laws. The negative construction uses dimension $\Theta(\log n)$. Thus these bounds identify the strict threshold for a dimension-uniform polynomial oracle guarantee; they do not establish the same negative boundary at one fixed dimension. Nothing is asserted on the equality line.

Both directions apply to actual finite Gaussian posteriors at every $\gamma\ge2$, and with one hidden layer at every $\gamma\ge1$. The strong-prior results give corresponding statements in the prior-strength limit.

The positive direction uses an exact symmetry of ReLU: rescaling layers without changing their product preserves the function. Balancing the layers lowers the energy of a comparator. The negative direction combines a cheap noisy component with a steep signal component in one law. Exact low-energy memorization makes weak regularization overfit the noise; stronger regularization misses the signal. The law and the failure event work for every temperature simultaneously.

These are statements about exact Bayesian inference. No efficient posterior algorithm, prior over network sizes, or sparse-architecture prior is introduced.

\section{Architecture, prior, and loss}
Let $d\ge1$, $H\ge1$, and $L=H+1$. All hidden layers have width $N$. Inputs lie in $[-1,1]^d$ and labels in $[-1,1]$. Their joint Borel law $D$ is arbitrary. Put $\bar x=(1,x)$ and
\[
 h^1=\relu(W^1\bar x),\qquad
 h^\ell=\relu(W^\ell h^{\ell-1})\ (2\le\ell\le H),
 \qquad z_w=v^\top h^H.
\]
There are no internal biases. The energy is
\begin{equation}
 Q_N(w)=\frac dN\|W^1\|_F^2+\sum_{\ell=2}^H\|W^\ell\|_F^2+N\|v\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
The prior $\mu_{N,\sigma}$ has density proportional to $e^{-Q_N/(2\sigma^2)}$. Its coordinate variances are $N\sigma^2/d$ in the first layer, $\sigma^2$ in internal layers, and $\sigma^2/N$ in the readout.

For an IID sample $S$ of size $n$, let $\empRisk_S(f)=n^{-1}\sum_i(f(X_i)-Y_i)^2$. A Gaussian observation likelihood with variance $n\lambda\sigma^2$ and raw network mean gives
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_{N,\sigma,S,\lambda}(dw)
 =Z^{-1}e^{-\empRisk_S(z_w)/(2\lambda\sigma^2)}\mu_{N,\sigma}(dw).
 \label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
The strong-prior limit takes $\sigma\downarrow0$ separately at fixed $n,S,N,\lambda$. It selects minimizers of
\begin{equation}
 \empRisk_S(z_w)+\lambda Q_N(w).
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
The main prediction convention is the ordinary posterior mean of $z_w$. We also control the stronger posterior-averaged raw risk. Clipping means projection to $[-1,1]$ and can only reduce squared loss against the labels under consideration.

\section{Balancing a comparator and bounding capacity}
\begin{lemma}[Exact comparator balancing]
\label{lem:teacher}
A depth-$L$, width-$m$ ReLU teacher $g$ with weights bounded by $B$ has, at every width $N\ge m$, an exact implementation of energy at most
\begin{equation}
 E=L[2^H d(d+1)]^{1/L}m^{2H/L}B^2.
 \label{eq:teacher-energy}
\end{equation}
It satisfies $|g(x)|\le U:=(d+1)m^H B^L$ on the input cube.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Partition each learner layer into $m$ groups of balanced sizes $k_j$, copy each teacher first-layer row, divide every edge leaving group $j$ by $k_j$, and divide its readout coefficient by $k_j$. This preserves every activation and output. The first-layer energy is at most $d(d+1)B^2$. Each internal energy and the readout energy are at most $2m^2B^2$, since $k_i/k_j\le2$ and $k_j\ge N/(2m)$.

Let these $L$ layer energies be $q_1,\ldots,q_L$. If one is zero, the function is zero. Otherwise multiply layer $\ell$ by
$(\prod_jq_j)^{1/(2L)}/\sqrt{q_\ell}$. These positive factors have product one. Positive homogeneity of ReLU preserves the output, while every layer energy becomes $(\prod_jq_j)^{1/L}$. Their sum gives~\eqref{eq:teacher-energy}. Finally, direct propagation of the coefficient bound through the original teacher gives amplitude at most $(d+1)m^HB^L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Teachers with biases in every layer]
The comparator may be an ordinary ReLU MLP with biases in all hidden layers and in the readout. If its width is $m$ and every weight and bias is bounded by $B\ge1$, add one constant neuron to each hidden layer. Its first-layer bias is one; subsequently a weight-one edge from the preceding constant neuron keeps its activation equal to one. Feed this neuron to each other neuron with the desired bias as its edge weight, and use the same construction for the readout bias. The resulting bias-free-internal architecture has width $m+1$, the same depth, and coefficient cap $B$, and represents the same function exactly. Thus every positive theorem applies with $m+1$ in place of $m$. The learner's dense Gaussian prior is unchanged.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Width-independent norm capacity]
\label{lem:capacity}
Put $p=L/2$ and $c_d=\sqrt{(d+1)/d}$. For every $K>0$, let $\mathcal Z_K$ contain the raw outputs of every width-$N$ network with $Q_N\le K$, taking the union over all $N\ge1$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{f\in\mathcal Z_K}\|f\|_\infty\le c_d(K/L)^p,
 \qquad \Rad_n(\mathcal Z_K)\le
       2^Hc_d(K/L)^p/\sqrt n.
 \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
Here $\Rad_n$ is the worst-case empirical Rademacher complexity on the input cube, with an absolute value inside the supremum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The product of the ordinary Frobenius norms of all layers is
\[
 P(w)=\|v\|_2\prod_{\ell=1}^H\|W^\ell\|_F
   =d^{-1/2}\prod_{\ell=1}^L\sqrt{q_\ell}
   \le d^{-1/2}(Q_N/L)^p.
\]
Since $\|\bar x\|_2\le\sqrt{d+1}$, the amplitude bound follows.

For the capacity bound, positive homogeneity normalizes every hidden Frobenius norm to at most one, leaving the readout norm at most $P$. Zero layers again give the zero function. Consider first $P=1$. Optimizing the readout on a fixed sample replaces the signed sum by the Euclidean norm of the vector of signed sums of the final hidden neurons. Write each last-hidden row as its norm times a unit row. Homogeneity and the sum-of-squares Frobenius constraint bound that Euclidean norm by the supremum of a single unit-row signed sum. The absolute Rademacher contraction inequality for ReLU bounds its expectation by twice that of the corresponding class with a linear final readout and one fewer hidden layer. All earlier parameter choices remain inside the supremum. Repeating this $H$ times gives a factor $2^H$ and leaves the unit-norm linear class on $\bar x$.

The linear class has empirical complexity at most
$n^{-1}(\sum_i\|\bar X_i\|_2^2)^{1/2}\le\sqrt{(d+1)/n}$, by Cauchy--Schwarz. Multiplying by $P$ proves~\eqref{eq:capacity}. This argument permits the supremum over all widths at every step, so the bound holds for their union. Symmetry under readout sign reversal makes the absolute and ordinary conventions coincide for the output class. Measurability follows from a countable union of rational parameter approximations, using continuity of ReLU and the energy bound.
\end{proof}

\section{A raw-output oracle inequality}
We use the standard smooth-loss theorem of Srebro, Sridharan and Tewari~\cite{sst}. In the form needed here, if a nonnegative loss is $s$-smooth and bounded by $b$ on a class $\mathcal F$, then with probability at least $1-\eta$, simultaneously for $f\in\mathcal F$,
\begin{align}
 R(f)-\empRisk_S(f)\le C_0\biggl[
 \sqrt{\empRisk_S(f)}\left(\sqrt{s}\log^{3/2}(2n)\Rad_n(\mathcal F)
                   +\sqrt{\frac{b\log(1/\eta)}n}\right)\notag\\
 {}+s\log^3(2n)\Rad_n(\mathcal F)^2
                   +\frac{b\log(1/\eta)}n\biggr].
 \label{eq:sst}
\end{align}
One may take the same conservative universal constant $C_0=10^5$ used in the preceding agnostic Gaussian analysis~\cite{sharper}. Square loss is $2$-smooth. No realizability assumption, convexity of the class, or Bernstein condition relative to the comparator is needed.

For $n\ge2$, $0<\delta<1$, define
\begin{gather}
 p=L/2,\quad v=c_d/L^p,\quad \kappa=2^Hv,\quad V=1+v,
 \quad J=\lceil\log_2n\rceil,\quad
 u_n=\frac1n\log\frac{2(J+1)}\delta,\notag\\
 A_n=C_0\left[\frac{\sqrt2\kappa\log^{3/2}(2n)}{\sqrt n}
                    +V\sqrt{u_n}\right],\quad
 a_n=2^pA_n,\quad r_n=A_n+A_n^2,\notag\\
 \lambda_n=\max\{1/n,(4a_n)^{1/p}\}.
 \label{eq:calibration}
\end{gather}
For sufficiently large $n$, $\lambda_n\le1$. Constants depend on fixed depth; the exponents below do not suppress a learner-width factor.

\begin{theorem}[Agnostic raw-risk bound]
\label{thm:upper}
Let $g$ be a fixed width-$m$, coefficient-$B$ teacher, and let $E,U$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:teacher}. Suppose $\lambda_n\le1$. With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every learner width $N\ge m$, every global minimizer of~\eqref{eq:objective} at $\lambda=\lambda_n$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 \Risk(z_w)\le\Risk(g)+\lambda_nE+r_n
 &+(U+1)\sqrt{\frac{2\Risk(g)\log(2/\delta)}n}\notag\\
 &+\frac{2(U+1)^2\log(2/\delta)}{3n}.
 \label{eq:oracle}
\end{align}
On the same event, for each fixed such $N$, the right side also bounds
\[
 \limsup_{\sigma\downarrow0}\int\Risk(z_w)\,d\Pi,
 \qquad
 \limsup_{\sigma\downarrow0}\Risk\left(\int z_w\,d\Pi\right).
\]
The clipped prediction conventions satisfy the same bound. The prior-scale convergence is pointwise in learner width.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Comparison with the zero network gives empirical risk at most one and energy $q=Q_N\le1/\lambda_n\le n$ for every minimizer. Apply~\eqref{eq:sst} to the deterministic classes $\mathcal Z_{2^j}$, $0\le j\le J$, with failure probabilities $\delta/[2(J+1)]$. On $\mathcal Z_K$ with $K\ge1$, square loss is bounded by $V^2K^{2p}$. Substituting Lemma~\ref{lem:capacity} in~\eqref{eq:sst}, then using the minimizer's empirical risk bound, gives deviation at most
\[
 A_nK^p+A_n^2K^{2p}.
\]
For the quadratic term, $C_0\ge1$ ensures that the square of the sum defining $A_n$ dominates $C_0$ times the sum of its two squared components.

If $q\le1$, the deviation is at most $r_n$. Otherwise use a dyadic radius $K\le2q$. Its deviation is at most $a_nq^p+a_n^2q^{2p}$. Since $p\ge1$, $q\le1/\lambda_n$, and $\lambda_n^p\ge4a_n$,
\[
 a_nq^p\le\lambda_nq/4,\qquad
 a_n^2q^{2p}\le\lambda_nq/16.
\]
Thus every minimizer, at every width, obeys
\[
 \Risk(z_w)\le\empRisk_S(z_w)+\lambda_nQ_N(w)+r_n
             \le\empRisk_S(g)+\lambda_nE+r_n.
\]
The teacher loss lies in $[0,(U+1)^2]$ and its variance is at most $(U+1)^2\Risk(g)$. Bernstein's inequality, with failure probability $\delta/2$, gives~\eqref{eq:oracle}.

For the posterior limit, the objective is continuous and coercive. At fixed $n,S,N,\lambda_n$, it has a compact nonempty minimizer set, and the posterior concentrates there. More precisely, with $J(w)=Q_N+\lambda_n^{-1}\empRisk_S(z_w)$ and $J_* =\min J$, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 \int(1+Q_N^L)\Ind_{\{J>J_*+\varepsilon\}}\,d\Pi\longrightarrow0.
\]
A positive-volume ball around a minimizer lower-bounds the normalizer. On the tail, split $J$ into a strictly larger constant and a positive fraction of $Q_N$; the remaining weighted Gaussian integral is polynomial in $\sigma$, whereas the gap contributes an exponentially vanishing factor. This is the usual Laplace estimate, now with the polynomial weight $1+Q_N^L$.

On the input cube, Lemma~\ref{lem:capacity} gives
$\Risk(z_w)\le2v^2Q_N^L+2$. This controls the tail; continuity and dominated convergence give continuity of risk on compact parameter sets. Hence the limiting posterior-averaged risk is at most its maximum on the minimizer set. Jensen gives the raw posterior-mean bound. Clipping then gives the remaining conventions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Growing comparator classes]
\label{cor:growing}
Fix $H,d,\delta$, and $a,b\ge0$ with $s=Ha+Lb<1/2$. Let $\mathcal G_n$ contain depth-$L$ ReLU networks of width at most $\max(1,\lfloor n^a\rfloor)$ and weights at most $n^b$. With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously at all learner widths at least that cap,
\[
 \limsup_{\sigma\downarrow0}\int\Risk(z_w)\,d\Pi
 \le\min_{g\in\mathcal G_n}\Risk(g)
       +\widetilde O_{H,d,\delta}\left(n^{-(1-2s)/L}\right).
\]
The same holds for ordinary raw posterior means and the clipped conventions. Dimension dependence in the explicit theorem is polynomial.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The parameter class is compact, its population risk is continuous, and it contains zero. Select a population minimizer before drawing the sample; its risk is at most one. Its energy bound has order $n^{2s/L}$ and amplitude bound order $n^s$. The calibration has order $\widetilde O(n^{-1/L})$. Teacher concentration contributes powers $n^{s-1/2}$ and $n^{2s-1}$, no larger than the displayed remainder because $L\ge2$. Narrower teachers embed by zero padding.
\end{proof}

\section{An exact low-energy ReLU memorizer}
\begin{lemma}[Exact Boolean memorization]
\label{lem:memorizer}
Let $D\ge1$, $d\ge D$, and $r:\{-1,1\}^D\to[-1,1]$ satisfy $\sum_xr(x)^2\le s_0$. Put $M_0=(D+1)2^D$. At every width $N\ge M_0$, a depth-$L$ ReLU network depending only on those $D$ coordinates represents $r$ exactly on the cube and has energy at most
\begin{equation}
 K_{\mathrm{mem}}=
 L[96\,2^{H-1}dD^3(D+1)s_0]^{1/L}.
 \label{eq:memorizer-energy}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A parity on $j\ge1$ coordinates is a function of their sum $t$, taking alternating values $y_0,\ldots,y_j\in\{-1,1\}$ at $t_i=-j+2i$. The piecewise-linear interpolant has first slope $s_0'=(y_1-y_0)/2\in\{-1,1\}$ and successive slope changes of magnitude two. It is
\[
 y_0\relu(1)+s_0'\relu(t+j)+
 \sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(s_i'-s_{i-1}')\relu(t-t_i).
\]
Here $t+j\ge0$ on the cube, so the leftmost hinge agrees with the required linear term. This uses at most $j+1$ features, each with augmented row norm at most $2D$. Their coefficient squares sum to at most $1+1+4(j-1)\le4D$. The constant parity uses one constant feature.

Normalized Fourier expansion and Parseval therefore represent $r$ by at most $M_0$ such features, with coefficient squared sum
\[
 T\le4D\sum_S\widehat r(S)^2
       \le4Ds_0/2^D.
\]
Zero and duplicate features may be retained. Clone this list into width $N$ using groups of sizes at least $N/(2M_0)$. The first-layer energy is at most $4dD^2$.

If $H=1$, divide each coefficient among its copies. The readout energy is at most $2M_0T\le8D(D+1)s_0$.

If $H\ge2$, split the next layer into two almost equal groups. One computes the sum of positive-coefficient features and the other the sum of negative-coefficient magnitudes. All features and these two sums are nonnegative, so ReLU leaves the sums unchanged. Dividing each incoming coefficient among its first-layer copies gives second-layer energy at most $2M_0T$. Relay the two values through each later hidden layer, placing weight $1/r_\pm$ on each edge inside a group of size $r_\pm$. Each such layer has energy two. Readout weights $+1/r_+$ and $-1/r_-$ give energy at most six.

The product of all layer energies is consequently at most
$96\,2^{H-1}dD^3(D+1)s_0$. This also bounds the $H=1$ case conservatively. If $r=0$, use the zero network; otherwise positively balance the layers as in Lemma~\ref{lem:teacher}. Their sum becomes at most~\eqref{eq:memorizer-energy}, without changing the function.
\end{proof}

\section{A bounded steep teacher}
\begin{lemma}[A four-group tent]
\label{lem:tent}
Let $m\ge4$, $B\ge1$, $r=\lfloor m/4\rfloor$, and suppose
$\delta=2/(r^HB^L)\le1/2$. A width-at-most-$m$, depth-$L$ ReLU network with weights bounded by $B$ realizes an odd function $g(t)$ of its first coordinate such that
\[
 |g(t)|\le1\quad(t\in\R),\qquad
 g(0)=g(1)=g(-1)=0,\qquad g(\delta)=1,\quad g(-\delta)=-1.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use four groups of $r$ neurons. First-layer features are
$\relu(B(t+2\delta))$, $\relu(B(t+\delta))$,
$\relu(B(t-\delta))$, and $\relu(B(t-2\delta))$.
The biases have magnitude at most $2B\delta\le B$. Each internal within-group edge is $B$, and all other edges vanish. These nonnegative channels are multiplied by $(rB)^{H-1}$ through the internal layers. The readout coefficients per group are $-B/2,B,-B,B/2$.

The output is $g(t)=S\psi_\delta(t)$, where $S=r^HB^L/2=1/\delta$ and
\[
 \psi_\delta(t)=-\relu(t+2\delta)+2\relu(t+\delta)
                 -2\relu(t-\delta)+\relu(t-2\delta).
\]
This function equals $t$ on $[-\delta,\delta]$, is odd, vanishes outside $[-2\delta,2\delta]$, and has absolute value at most $\delta$. The assertions follow. Unused neurons are zero.
\end{proof}

\section{The lower bound for every temperature}
\begin{theorem}[A single law defeats all temperatures]
\label{thm:lower}
Fix $H\ge1$, $a>0$, $b\ge0$, and suppose $s=Ha+Lb>1/2$. For $n=2^k$ put $d=k+2$, $m=\lfloor n^a\rfloor$, and $B=n^b$. For every sufficiently large $n$, there is a discrete law $D_n$ on $[-1,1]^d\times[-1,1]$ whose conditional mean is a globally bounded depth-$L$, width-at-most-$m$, coefficient-$B$ ReLU teacher $g_n$, with the following properties.

The Bayes risk is $p=9/10$. Every coordinate lies in $[-1,1]$ and takes both endpoints with positive probability; the covariance matrix is diagonal with eigenvalues in $[1/20,1]$.

There is a threshold $N_*(n)=O(n^a+n\log n)$ such that with probability at least $1-2e^{-n^{1/3}/2}$, simultaneously for every $N\ge N_*$ and every $\lambda>0$, every global minimizer of~\eqref{eq:objective} has raw and clipped risk at least $p+1/100$.

On the same event, for each fixed $N\ge N_*$, some $\sigma_0(n,S,N)>0$ works for every $0<\sigma\le\sigma_0$ and every $\lambda>0$: posterior-averaged raw and clipped risks, the ordinary raw-mean risk, its clipping, and the risk of the mean of clipped outputs are all at least $p+1/100$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take the teacher $g$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:tent}. Write $X=(T,A,Z)$, with $Z$ uniform on $\{-1,1\}^k$, independent of the other variables. With probability $p=9/10$, set $T=0$, $A=+1$, and let $Y$ be an independent fair sign. With probability $q=1/10$, set $A=-1$, take $T$ uniformly from $\{-1,-\delta,\delta,1\}$, and set $Y=g(T)$.

The conditional mean is $g(T)$ and its risk is $p$. The covariance matrix is
\[
 \operatorname{diag}(q(1+\delta^2)/2,4pq,1,\ldots,1),
\]
which gives the stated conditioning.

There are $n$ noisy atoms $(0,+1,z)$. Let $S_1$ count those observed once and let $n_s$ count steep observations. The expectation of $S_1$ is
$np(1-p/n)^{n-1}\ge npe^{-p}$. Changing one observation changes $S_1$ by at most two. McDiarmid and Hoeffding give the common event
\begin{equation}
 S_1/n\ge pe^{-p}-n^{-1/3},\qquad
 n_s/n\le q+n^{-1/3}
 \label{eq:occupancy}
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-2e^{-n^{1/3}/2}$. Work on this event.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:memorizer} to the $D=k+1$ Boolean coordinates $(A,Z)$. Its target is the empirical noisy label mean at an observed positive-tag atom, and zero elsewhere, including every negative-tag point. Its squared counting norm is at most $n$. Thus an exact memorizer exists at every $N\ge M_0=(D+1)2^D$, with
\begin{equation}
 K_{\mathrm{mem}}=O_H(n^{1/L}d^{5/L}).
 \label{eq:mem-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
It ignores $T$, and is zero throughout the steep component. Set $N_*=\max(m,M_0)$.

For noisy counts $k_j$, means $r_j$, and within-atom residual $W$, write
\[
 \empRisk_S(f)=W/n+n^{-1}\sum_jk_j(f_j-r_j)^2
                   +n^{-1}\sum_{i\text{ steep}}(f(X_i)-Y_i)^2.
\]
Comparing a minimizer with the exact memorizer gives
\begin{equation}
 n^{-1}\sum_jk_j(f_j-r_j)^2\le\lambda K_{\mathrm{mem}}+n_s/n.
 \label{eq:noise-residual}
\end{equation}
Clipping decreases this residual.

Put $\tau_n=n^{-1/L}d^{-4}$. If $\lambda\le\tau_n$, then
$\tau_nK_{\mathrm{mem}}=O_H(d^{5/L-4})=o(1)$ because $L\ge2$. The residual bound tends to $q$. On observed noisy atoms, the empirical-mean vector has squared norm at least $S_1$; count weights are at least one. The reverse triangle inequality gives
\[
 n^{-1}\sum_z f(0,+1,z)^2\ge
 \left[\sqrt{pe^{-p}-n^{-1/3}}
           -\sqrt{\tau_nK_{\mathrm{mem}}+q+n^{-1/3}}\right]_+^2.
\]
The Bayes excess equals
$p\E_Z f(0,+1,Z)^2+q\E[(f(X)-g(T))^2\mid A=-1]$.
It therefore has lower limit at least
$p(\sqrt{pe^{-p}}-\sqrt q)^2=0.075002\ldots$.

If $\lambda>\tau_n$, comparison with zero gives $Q_N\le1/\lambda$. ReLU is $1$-Lipschitz, so the input-coordinate sensitivity is at most
$d^{-1/2}(Q_N/L)^{L/2}$. Thus the inner-pair half-difference is at most
\[
 \eta_n=\delta d^{-1/2}(L\tau_n)^{-L/2}
  =O_H\bigl(n^{1/2-Ha-Lb}d^{2L-1/2}\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
\]
It also bounds clipped predictions. The inner steep points have total mass $q/2$ and labels $\pm1$, giving excess at least $(q/2)(1-\eta_n)^2\to0.05$. This proves the minimizer claim.

We prove the uniform finite-posterior assertion next. Its estimates apply on the same event and for each fixed $n,S,N$. No temperature-dependent exceptional sample event is introduced.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite posteriors and ordinary means}
For $\lambda\le\tau_n$, choose a fixed small ball around the exact memorizer on which $Q_N\le K_{\mathrm{mem}}+\varepsilon$ and empirical risk is at most its memorizer value plus $\varepsilon$. Let $\rho$ be the uniform law on that ball. Direct Gaussian-density calculation gives
\[
 \KL(\rho\|\mu_{N,\sigma})
    \le (K_{\mathrm{mem}}+\varepsilon)/(2\sigma^2)+C
 \quad(0<\sigma\le1),
\]
where $C\ge0$ is finite and independent of $\sigma,\lambda$; the $P\log\sigma$ normalization term can be dropped from this upper bound. The Gibbs variational identity implies
\[
 \int\empRisk_S\,d\Pi
 \le\empRisk_S(z_{w_{\mathrm{mem}}})+\varepsilon
              +\lambda(K_{\mathrm{mem}}+\varepsilon)+2\lambda\sigma^2C.
\]
For sufficiently large $n$, the occupancy event gives $S_1/n\ge1/3$. First choose $\varepsilon$ small and then $\sigma$ small; the expected noisy residual is at most $1/5$, uniformly over $\lambda\le\tau_n$.

Jensen passes this residual bound to ordinary raw means and to the clipped conventions. The reverse triangle inequality then gives excess at least
\[
 p(\sqrt{1/3}-\sqrt{1/5})^2>1/100.
\]
For Gibbs risks, use the reverse triangle inequality in the product Hilbert space of posterior parameter and observed-atom index. This uses expected residuals directly, rather than incorrectly averaging lower bounds on individual risks to infer a statement about a mean.

For $\lambda\ge\tau_n$, the prior readout is centered. ReLU homogeneity and the finite Gaussian moments imply
$\E_{\mu_{N,\sigma}}z_w(X_i)^2=O_{N,d,L}(\sigma^{2L})$ uniformly on the cube. Thus, for sufficiently small $\sigma$ at fixed $N$, prior expected empirical loss is at most two. Jensen lower-bounds the likelihood normalizer by $e^{-1/(\lambda\sigma^2)}$. Since $Q_N/\sigma^2$ is chi-square with parameter dimension $P$, and the likelihood is at most one,
\[
 \int e^{Q_N/(4\sigma^2)}\,d\Pi
   \le2^{P/2}e^{1/(\tau_n\sigma^2)}.
\]
For $K_0=8/\tau_n$, Markov's inequality and integration of this exponential tail give
\begin{equation}
 \int(1+Q_N^L)\Ind_{\{Q_N>K_0\}}\,d\Pi
     \longrightarrow0
 \label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
uniformly over $\lambda\ge\tau_n$. To see this directly, integrate $L u^{L-1}e^{-u/(4\sigma^2)}$ from $K_0$ to infinity. The result is $e^{-K_0/(4\sigma^2)}$ times a polynomial in $K_0,\sigma^2$; multiplication by the moment bound leaves the vanishing factor $e^{-1/(\tau_n\sigma^2)}$.

On $Q_N\le K_0$, the inner half-difference is at most $8^{L/2}\eta_n\to0$. For large $n$ make it at most $1/4$. The amplitude bound $|z_w|\le vQ_N^{L/2}$ and~\eqref{eq:tail} make the posterior tail's half-difference contribution at most $1/4$ for sufficiently small $\sigma$, uniformly in temperature. The raw mean and its clipped conventions then have excess at least $q/8=1/80>1/100$. Gibbs risks have the same constant lower bound by making the sublevel mass at least $3/4$, since each predictor there has excess at least $(q/2)(3/4)^2$.

Taking the smaller of the small- and large-temperature prior-scale thresholds completes the finite-posterior part of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}. It allows a temperature depending on the data, validation set, prior scale, or data law. The prior-scale threshold remains pointwise in width. A mixture over temperatures followed by raw averaging is a different predictor and is not covered by the individual-temperature theorem.

\begin{corollary}[Width alone does not control the oracle guarantee]
\label{cor:fixed-width}
Fix the depth as above and let $b>1/(2L)$. The same constant-excess conclusion holds with a teacher of fixed width 4 and coefficient bound $B=n^b$, with learner-width threshold $O_H(n\log n)$. It includes the canonical finite priors at every $\gamma\ge2$, and every $\gamma\ge1$ when $H=1$, as well as the strong-prior conclusion. Consequently a distribution-uniform, vanishing-excess guarantee against all teachers of width polynomial in $n$, without any restriction on coefficient size, is false for this learner family even when the comparator class only needs to contain width-4 teachers.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use one neuron per group in the teacher construction, so $r=1$ and $\delta=2B^{-L}$. All teacher estimates hold for sufficiently large $n$ because $B\to\infty$. The memorizer and sample event are unchanged. In the large-temperature branch the inner-pair half-difference is at most a fixed-depth constant times $n^{1/2-Lb}d^{2L-1/2}$, which tends to zero under the stated condition. The small-temperature branch is unaffected. The finite-posterior and canonical-scale proofs therefore apply without change.
\end{proof}
This is a dimension-uniform obstruction: the input dimension remains $\Theta(\log n)$ and the data law varies with $n$. It does not establish failure on one fixed distribution or at one fixed dimension. The growing coefficient bound, rather than the fixed teacher width, supplies the increasing input sensitivity.


\section{Consequences and limits of the threshold}
The positive and negative regions meet at $Ha+Lb=1/2$. The upper result is a sufficient calibrated learner, and the lower result defeats every positive calibration on one sample event, including data-dependent calibration. Equality remains open.

\begin{corollary}[No universal joint polynomial in depth and width]
For this unrestricted Gaussian-prior, Gaussian-likelihood learner family, there is no distribution-uniform sample bound polynomial jointly in input dimension, teacher width, and teacher depth, independent of arbitrarily large learner width, even with unit-bounded teacher coefficients and data-dependent temperature choice, either in the strong-prior setting or at each fixed canonical finite exponent $\gamma\ge2$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a proposed universal polynomial has teacher-width degree at most $D_0$. Choose a fixed $H$ with $2H>D_0+1$. Choose $r$ strictly between $D_0$ and $2H$, set $a=1/r$, and use the lower theorem with $b=0$. Its sample count has order $m^r$, exceeding the proposed polynomial for large $m$, since the depth is fixed and dimension grows only logarithmically. Yet the constant excess lower bound remains. This contradicts any fixed positive accuracy/confidence guarantee from that polynomial.
\end{proof}

This corollary does not assert an exponential-in-depth lower bound at one fixed teacher width: the sufficiently large thresholds in the construction may depend on depth. It is an unconditional obstruction to one joint polynomial in all three resources for this noisy-regression learner family.

The noise level can be made arbitrarily small in absolute squared-risk units by multiplying all labels and teacher readouts by any fixed $\rho\in(0,1]$. The Bayes risk becomes $0.9\rho^2$ and the lower-bound excess $\rho^2/100$. The input conditioning and exponent threshold are unchanged. Signal and noise are scaled together; this is not a small label-flip-probability result or a fixed unit-signal lower bound with vanishing noise.

Restrictions on prior support or on comparator stability change the question and may allow better rates. Neither result here addresses efficient posterior computation. The lower-bound law is discrete and varies with sample size; consistency at one fixed law is not refuted. The finite theorems cover the canonical regimes stated above. For multiple hidden layers, the interval $1\le\gamma<2$ is not settled here; the strong-prior and sufficiently-small-scale statements remain separate results.
