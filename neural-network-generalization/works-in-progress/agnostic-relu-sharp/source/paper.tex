\begin{abstract}
We identify a sharp strict exponent threshold for a dense Gaussian Bayesian ReLU network to compete in squared loss with a smaller network. For $H\ge1$ hidden layers, let comparator width and coefficient bound grow as $n^a$ and $n^b$. If $Ha+(H+1)b<1/2$, an explicit Gaussian likelihood calibration gives a coefficient-one oracle inequality with vanishing excess at every learner width above comparator width. This positive result covers ordinary raw-output posterior means in the strong-prior limit and at actual finite Gaussian scaling exponents $\gamma\ge2$; one hidden layer permits every $\gamma\ge1$. Above the threshold, when $a>0$, one well-conditioned input law defeats every data-dependent mixture of likelihood temperatures, prior scales, and sufficiently large learner widths. The negative result holds at every actual finite scale at or stronger than mean field, including all $\gamma\ge1$, with learner-width threshold $O(n^a+n\log n)$. Its regression function is a globally bounded network in the comparison class. A Gaussian-readout identity converts empirical separation into one linear population witness shared by all component predictors. A sparse two-channel memorizer and selective covariance narrowing give the all-scale comparison. The lower-bound dimension grows logarithmically with sample count. Equality, and the sharp positive boundary at multiple hidden layers for $1\le\gamma<2$, remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Approximation guarantees and the threshold}
Data may be well approximated by a small network without being exactly generated by it. A natural guarantee for Bayesian neural-network learning is
\[
 \Risk(\widehat f_S)\le\inf_{g\in\mathcal G_n}\Risk(g)+o(1),
 \qquad \Risk(f)=\E(f(X)-Y)^2,
\]
where the comparator class $\mathcal G_n$ grows polynomially with the sample count $n$, while the learner may have much larger width. The additive remainder and the bound on comparator coefficients matter: even an unknown constant incurs estimation error, and width alone does not control how steep a real-valued network can be.

We study a fixed dense ReLU architecture, independent Gaussian weights, and a Gaussian squared-error likelihood. The likelihood variance is calibrated jointly with the prior. For $H$ hidden layers and total depth $L=H+1$, the dividing quantity is
\[
 s=Ha+Lb
\]
when comparator width and coefficient cap grow as $n^a$ and $n^b$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Comparator growth & A vanishing oracle bound exists & All mixtures fail\\
\midrule
Width $n^a$, weight cap $n^b$ & $Ha+Lb<1/2$ & $Ha+Lb>1/2$, $a>0$\\
Width $n^a$, fixed weight cap & $a<1/(2H)$ & $a>1/(2H)$\\
Width and weight cap $n^a$ & $a<1/(4H+2)$ & $a>1/(4H+2)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The positive guarantee has polynomial dependence on input dimension and is uniform over input and label laws. The negative construction uses dimension $\Theta(\log n)$. Thus these bounds identify the strict threshold for a dimension-uniform polynomial oracle guarantee; they do not establish the same negative boundary at one fixed dimension. Nothing is asserted on the equality line.

The positive result applies to actual finite Gaussian posteriors at every $\gamma\ge2$, and with one hidden layer at every $\gamma\ge1$. The negative result covers every finite mean-field-or-stronger prior scale at all hidden depths, already at learner width $O(n^a+n\log n)$. It survives arbitrary data-dependent mixtures of the allowed posteriors, including a hyperprior over temperatures. The sharp positive boundary for multiple hidden layers at $1\le\gamma<2$ remains open. The strong-prior results give corresponding statements for the prior-strength limit and global minimizers.

The positive direction uses an exact symmetry of ReLU: rescaling layers without changing their product preserves the function. Balancing the layers lowers the energy of a comparator. The negative direction combines a cheap noisy component with a steep signal component in one law. Exact low-energy memorization makes weak regularization overfit the noise; stronger regularization misses the signal. The law and the failure event work for every temperature and prior scale simultaneously. An additional Gaussian-readout identity prevents averaging these predictors from repairing their errors.

These are statements about exact Bayesian inference. No efficient posterior algorithm, prior over network sizes, or sparse-architecture prior is introduced.

\section{Architecture, prior, and loss}
Let $d\ge1$, $H\ge1$, and $L=H+1$. All hidden layers have width $N$. Inputs lie in $[-1,1]^d$ and labels in $[-1,1]$. Their joint Borel law $D$ is arbitrary. Put $\bar x=(1,x)$ and
\[
 h^1=\relu(W^1\bar x),\qquad
 h^\ell=\relu(W^\ell h^{\ell-1})\ (2\le\ell\le H),
 \qquad z_w=v^\top h^H.
\]
There are no internal biases. The energy is
\begin{equation}
 Q_N(w)=\frac dN\|W^1\|_F^2+\sum_{\ell=2}^H\|W^\ell\|_F^2+N\|v\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
The prior $\mu_{N,\sigma}$ has density proportional to $e^{-Q_N/(2\sigma^2)}$. Its coordinate variances are $N\sigma^2/d$ in the first layer, $\sigma^2$ in internal layers, and $\sigma^2/N$ in the readout.

For an IID sample $S$ of size $n$, let $\empRisk_S(f)=n^{-1}\sum_i(f(X_i)-Y_i)^2$. A Gaussian observation likelihood with variance $n\lambda\sigma^2$ and raw network mean gives
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_{N,\sigma,S,\lambda}(dw)
 =Z^{-1}e^{-\empRisk_S(z_w)/(2\lambda\sigma^2)}\mu_{N,\sigma}(dw).
 \label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
The strong-prior limit takes $\sigma\downarrow0$ separately at fixed $n,S,N,\lambda$. It selects minimizers of
\begin{equation}
 \empRisk_S(z_w)+\lambda Q_N(w).
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
The main prediction convention is the ordinary posterior mean of $z_w$. We also control the stronger posterior-averaged raw risk. Clipping means projection to $[-1,1]$ and can only reduce squared loss against the labels under consideration.

\section{Balancing a comparator and bounding capacity}
\begin{lemma}[Exact comparator balancing]
\label{lem:teacher}
A depth-$L$, width-$m$ ReLU teacher $g$ with weights bounded by $B$ has, at every width $N\ge m$, an exact implementation of energy at most
\begin{equation}
 E=L[d(d+1)]^{1/L}m^{2H/L}B^2.
 \label{eq:teacher-energy}
\end{equation}
It satisfies $|g(x)|\le U:=(d+1)m^H B^L$ on the input cube.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put the teacher into the first $m$ coordinates of every hidden layer, with all other mean coordinates zero. Multiply the first-layer matrix by $\sqrt N$ and the readout by $1/\sqrt N$. ReLU homogeneity preserves the output. The weighted layer energies are now
\[
 d\|A_1\|_F^2,\quad \|A_2\|_F^2,\ldots,\|A_H\|_F^2,
 \quad\|c\|_2^2,
\]
whose product is at most $d(d+1)m^{2H}B^{2L}$.
If any layer energy is zero, the function is zero. Otherwise multiply layer $\ell$ by the geometric mean of the square roots of these energies, divided by that layer's square root. These positive factors have product one, so the function is preserved, and all $L$ layer energies become their geometric mean. This gives~\eqref{eq:teacher-energy}. Direct propagation of the coefficient cap through the original teacher gives $|g|\le(d+1)m^HB^L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Teachers with biases in every layer]
The comparator may be an ordinary ReLU MLP with biases in all hidden layers and in the readout. If its width is $m$ and every weight and bias is bounded by $B\ge1$, add one constant neuron to each hidden layer. Its first-layer bias is one; subsequently a weight-one edge from the preceding constant neuron keeps its activation equal to one. Feed this neuron to each other neuron with the desired bias as its edge weight, and use the same construction for the readout bias. The resulting bias-free-internal architecture has width $m+1$, the same depth, and coefficient cap $B$, and represents the same function exactly. Thus every positive theorem applies with $m+1$ in place of $m$. The learner's dense Gaussian prior is unchanged.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Width-independent norm capacity]
\label{lem:capacity}
Put $p=L/2$ and $c_d=\sqrt{(d+1)/d}$. For every $K>0$, let $\mathcal Z_K$ contain the raw outputs of every width-$N$ network with $Q_N\le K$, taking the union over all $N\ge1$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{f\in\mathcal Z_K}\|f\|_\infty\le c_d(K/L)^p,
 \qquad \Rad_n(\mathcal Z_K)\le
       2^Hc_d(K/L)^p/\sqrt n.
 \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
Here $\Rad_n$ is the worst-case empirical Rademacher complexity on the input cube, with an absolute value inside the supremum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The product of the ordinary Frobenius norms of all layers is
\[
 P(w)=\|v\|_2\prod_{\ell=1}^H\|W^\ell\|_F
   =d^{-1/2}\prod_{\ell=1}^L\sqrt{q_\ell}
   \le d^{-1/2}(Q_N/L)^p.
\]
Since $\|\bar x\|_2\le\sqrt{d+1}$, the amplitude bound follows.

For the capacity bound, positive homogeneity normalizes every hidden Frobenius norm to at most one, leaving the readout norm at most $P$. Zero layers again give the zero function. Consider first $P=1$. Optimizing the readout on a fixed sample replaces the signed sum by the Euclidean norm of the vector of signed sums of the final hidden neurons. Write each last-hidden row as its norm times a unit row. Homogeneity and the sum-of-squares Frobenius constraint bound that Euclidean norm by the supremum of a single unit-row signed sum. The absolute Rademacher contraction inequality for ReLU bounds its expectation by twice that of the corresponding class with a linear final readout and one fewer hidden layer. All earlier parameter choices remain inside the supremum. Repeating this $H$ times gives a factor $2^H$ and leaves the unit-norm linear class on $\bar x$.

The linear class has empirical complexity at most
$n^{-1}(\sum_i\|\bar X_i\|_2^2)^{1/2}\le\sqrt{(d+1)/n}$, by Cauchy--Schwarz. Multiplying by $P$ proves~\eqref{eq:capacity}. This argument permits the supremum over all widths at every step, so the bound holds for their union. Symmetry under readout sign reversal makes the absolute and ordinary conventions coincide for the output class. Measurability follows from a countable union of rational parameter approximations, using continuity of ReLU and the energy bound.
\end{proof}

\section{A raw-output oracle inequality}
We use the standard smooth-loss theorem of Srebro, Sridharan and Tewari~\cite{sst}. In the form needed here, if a nonnegative loss is $s$-smooth and bounded by $b$ on a class $\mathcal F$, then with probability at least $1-\eta$, simultaneously for $f\in\mathcal F$,
\begin{align}
 R(f)-\empRisk_S(f)\le C_0\biggl[
 \sqrt{\empRisk_S(f)}\left(\sqrt{s}\log^{3/2}(2n)\Rad_n(\mathcal F)
                   +\sqrt{\frac{b\log(1/\eta)}n}\right)\notag\\
 {}+s\log^3(2n)\Rad_n(\mathcal F)^2
                   +\frac{b\log(1/\eta)}n\biggr].
 \label{eq:sst}
\end{align}
Let $C_0\ge1$ be a valid universal constant for this inequality. Square loss is $2$-smooth. No realizability assumption, convexity of the class, or Bernstein condition relative to the comparator is needed.

For $n\ge2$, $0<\delta<1$, define
\begin{gather}
 p=L/2,\quad v=c_d/L^p,\quad \kappa=2^Hv,\quad V=1+v,
 \quad J=\lceil\log_2n\rceil,\quad
 u_n=\frac1n\log\frac{2(J+1)}\delta,\notag\\
 A_n=C_0\left[\frac{\sqrt2\kappa\log^{3/2}(2n)}{\sqrt n}
                    +V\sqrt{u_n}\right],\quad
 a_n=2^pA_n,\quad r_n=A_n+A_n^2,\notag\\
 \lambda_n=\max\{1/n,(4a_n)^{1/p}\}.
 \label{eq:calibration}
\end{gather}
For sufficiently large $n$, $\lambda_n\le1$. Constants depend on fixed depth; the exponents below do not suppress a learner-width factor.

\begin{theorem}[Agnostic raw-risk bound]
\label{thm:upper}
Let $g$ be a fixed width-$m$, coefficient-$B$ teacher, and let $E,U$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:teacher}. Suppose $\lambda_n\le1$. With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every learner width $N\ge m$, every global minimizer of~\eqref{eq:objective} at $\lambda=\lambda_n$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 \Risk(z_w)\le\Risk(g)+\lambda_nE+r_n
 &+(U+1)\sqrt{\frac{2\Risk(g)\log(2/\delta)}n}\notag\\
 &+\frac{2(U+1)^2\log(2/\delta)}{3n}.
 \label{eq:oracle}
\end{align}
On the same event, for each fixed such $N$, the right side also bounds
\[
 \limsup_{\sigma\downarrow0}\int\Risk(z_w)\,d\Pi,
 \qquad
 \limsup_{\sigma\downarrow0}\Risk\left(\int z_w\,d\Pi\right).
\]
The clipped prediction conventions satisfy the same bound. The prior-scale convergence is pointwise in learner width.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Comparison with the zero network gives empirical risk at most one and energy $q=Q_N\le1/\lambda_n\le n$ for every minimizer. Apply~\eqref{eq:sst} to the deterministic classes $\mathcal Z_{2^j}$, $0\le j\le J$, with failure probabilities $\delta/[2(J+1)]$. On $\mathcal Z_K$ with $K\ge1$, square loss is bounded by $V^2K^{2p}$. Substituting Lemma~\ref{lem:capacity} in~\eqref{eq:sst}, then using the minimizer's empirical risk bound, gives deviation at most
\[
 A_nK^p+A_n^2K^{2p}.
\]
For the quadratic term, $C_0\ge1$ ensures that the square of the sum defining $A_n$ dominates $C_0$ times the sum of its two squared components.

If $q\le1$, the deviation is at most $r_n$. Otherwise use a dyadic radius $K\le2q$. Its deviation is at most $a_nq^p+a_n^2q^{2p}$. Since $p\ge1$, $q\le1/\lambda_n$, and $\lambda_n^p\ge4a_n$,
\[
 a_nq^p\le\lambda_nq/4,\qquad
 a_n^2q^{2p}\le\lambda_nq/16.
\]
Thus every minimizer, at every width, obeys
\[
 \Risk(z_w)\le\empRisk_S(z_w)+\lambda_nQ_N(w)+r_n
             \le\empRisk_S(g)+\lambda_nE+r_n.
\]
The teacher loss lies in $[0,(U+1)^2]$ and its variance is at most $(U+1)^2\Risk(g)$. Bernstein's inequality, with failure probability $\delta/2$, gives~\eqref{eq:oracle}.

For the posterior limit, the objective is continuous and coercive. At fixed $n,S,N,\lambda_n$, it has a compact nonempty minimizer set, and the posterior concentrates there. More precisely, with $J(w)=Q_N+\lambda_n^{-1}\empRisk_S(z_w)$ and $J_* =\min J$, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 \int(1+Q_N^L)\Ind_{\{J>J_*+\varepsilon\}}\,d\Pi\longrightarrow0.
\]
A positive-volume ball around a minimizer lower-bounds the normalizer. On the tail, split $J$ into a strictly larger constant and a positive fraction of $Q_N$; the remaining weighted Gaussian integral is polynomial in $\sigma$, whereas the gap contributes an exponentially vanishing factor. This is the usual Laplace estimate, now with the polynomial weight $1+Q_N^L$.

On the input cube, Lemma~\ref{lem:capacity} gives
$\Risk(z_w)\le2v^2Q_N^L+2$. This controls the tail; continuity and dominated convergence give continuity of risk on compact parameter sets. Hence the limiting posterior-averaged risk is at most its maximum on the minimizer set. Jensen gives the raw posterior-mean bound. Clipping then gives the remaining conventions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Growing comparator classes]
\label{cor:growing}
Fix $H,d,\delta$, and $a,b\ge0$ with $s=Ha+Lb<1/2$. Let $\mathcal G_n$ contain depth-$L$ ReLU networks of width at most $\max(1,\lfloor n^a\rfloor)$ and weights at most $n^b$. With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously at all learner widths at least that cap,
\[
 \limsup_{\sigma\downarrow0}\int\Risk(z_w)\,d\Pi
 \le\min_{g\in\mathcal G_n}\Risk(g)
       +\widetilde O_{H,d,\delta}\left(n^{-(1-2s)/L}\right).
\]
The same holds for ordinary raw posterior means and the clipped conventions. Dimension dependence in the explicit theorem is polynomial.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The parameter class is compact, its population risk is continuous, and it contains zero. Select a population minimizer before drawing the sample; its risk is at most one. Its energy bound has order $n^{2s/L}$ and amplitude bound order $n^s$. The calibration has order $\widetilde O(n^{-1/L})$. Teacher concentration contributes powers $n^{s-1/2}$ and $n^{2s-1}$, no larger than the displayed remainder because $L\ge2$. Narrower teachers embed by zero padding.
\end{proof}
