# Partial results for unrestricted critical internal controls

These conventionally audited notes address parts of the original critical tanh depth problem. They do not prove a polynomial-depth learning bound or an arithmetic-formula compiler for every finite-entropy row law. They have not been formalized in Lean.

- [Signed-feature alignment](critical-nearbaseline-signed-alignment.md) applies to arbitrary finite-entropy internal controls. It proves quantitative strict decrease of the scalar Bellman value away from zero. A globally hard-margined path near the unrestricted baseline must have all label-signed query features close to one common direction whenever its variance exceeds a sufficiently large multiple of its excess action and bottleneck variance.
- [The first aligned crossing](critical-first-aligned-crossing.md) bounds the entropy of the **entire** prefix that reaches that variance scale. With excess parameter `E`, the crossing variance is `K E (1 + O_K(sqrt(E)))`, prefix entropy is `O_K(E)`, the post-bottleneck quadratic occupation and `sum q_previous sqrt(D)` are `O_K(E)`, and the weighted sum `sum q_previous D` is `O_K(E²)`.
- [Regular anchor-ridge references](small-entropy-regular-anchor-tilts.md) shows that every scalar moment-matching reference throughout that small-entropy prefix has a controlled normalized tilt parameter and a uniform quadratic Gaussian envelope. Its exact conditional relative-entropy decomposition uses the actual incoming states; it does not justify replacing the whole path at once.
- [Bounded-density finite-chaos formulas](bounded-density-finite-chaos-interface.md) gives a dimension-independent local scalar construction under an arbitrary bounded Gaussian density ratio. It factors the covariance of each fixed-degree polynomial feature through a partial-contraction algebra. A refinement retains the exact Gaussian tanh baseline and isolates a nonlinear density correction of size `C^k q sqrt(D)`, with a separate norm oracle. Its approximation error and degree-dependent entropy threshold are explicit; controlling them through a full path remains open.

The first three statements describe actual unrestricted critical paths or their exact moment-matching references. The finite-chaos construction uses an additional bounded-density hypothesis. Combining them requires further work: geometric alignment does not provide a computable representation of the residual, and a small entropy sum does not justify discarding innovations that later controls might amplify.
