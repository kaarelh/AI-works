# Partial results for unrestricted critical internal controls

These conventionally audited notes address parts of the original critical tanh depth problem. They do not prove a polynomial-depth learning bound or an arithmetic-formula compiler for every finite-entropy row law. They have not been formalized in Lean.

- [Signed-feature alignment](critical-nearbaseline-signed-alignment.md) applies to arbitrary finite-entropy internal controls. It proves quantitative strict decrease of the scalar Bellman value away from zero. A globally hard-margined path near the unrestricted baseline must have all label-signed query features close to one common direction whenever its variance exceeds a sufficiently large multiple of its excess action and bottleneck variance.
- [The first aligned crossing](critical-first-aligned-crossing.md) bounds the entropy of the **entire** prefix that reaches that variance scale. With excess parameter `E`, the crossing variance is `K E (1 + O_K(sqrt(E)))`, prefix entropy is `O_K(E)`, the post-bottleneck quadratic occupation and `sum q_previous sqrt(D)` are `O_K(E)`, and the weighted sum `sum q_previous D` is `O_K(E²)`.
- [Regular anchor-ridge references](small-entropy-regular-anchor-tilts.md) shows that every scalar moment-matching reference throughout that small-entropy prefix has a controlled normalized tilt parameter and a uniform quadratic Gaussian envelope. Its exact conditional relative-entropy decomposition uses the actual incoming states; it does not justify replacing the whole path at once.
- [Bounded-density finite-chaos formulas](bounded-density-finite-chaos-interface.md) gives a dimension-independent local scalar construction under an arbitrary bounded Gaussian density ratio. It factors the covariance of each fixed-degree polynomial feature through a partial-contraction algebra. A refinement retains the exact Gaussian tanh baseline and isolates a nonlinear density correction of size `C^k q sqrt(D)`, with a separate norm oracle. Its approximation error and degree-dependent entropy threshold are explicit; controlling them through a full path remains open.

The first three statements describe actual unrestricted critical paths or their exact moment-matching references. The finite-chaos construction uses an additional bounded-density hypothesis. Combining them requires further work: geometric alignment does not provide a computable representation of the residual, and a small entropy sum does not justify discarding innovations that later controls might amplify.

See the [audit guide](../../verification/critical-internal-controls/README.md) for exact checked snapshots. The scalar foundations and small-variance expansion are in the [critical-depth sources](../../../critical-depth/source/critical-rate-foundations.tex) and [expansion proof](../../../critical-depth/source/unrestricted-critical-scalar-expansion.tex). The local scalar-oracle and finite-bit interfaces are developed in the [non-Gaussian controls companion](../../non-gaussian-controls.pdf).

The [universal variance and diagonal-spread estimates](../universal-quadratic-variance-and-diagonal-occupation.md) supply the scalar drift used in the strengthened crossing budget. They hold for arbitrary entropy controls, but do not construct feature formulas.

A [local quadratic-tilt counterexample](small-entropy-quadratic-tilt-large-chi-square.md) shows why bounded density is an additional condition: exact entropy-minimizing covariance transitions can have vanishing KL but exponentially large density second moment. An equal-diagonal four-input augmented-cube variant is included. This is not a claim that the transition occurs in a globally optimal critical Boolean path.

Two further checked examples identify limitations of possible proof shortcuts. A [fixed bounded analytic density](bounded-density-wick-term-cancellation-obstruction.md), with matched Gaussian mean and variance and bounded derivatives of every fixed order, makes the fully separated Hermite/Wick expansion of the exact tanh kernel have divergent absolute sum. Consequently a one-term importance estimator has infinite second moment; aggregating the contractions can avoid this particular obstruction. The derivative bounds may depend on their order. A [canonical nearbaseline path](canonical-nearbaseline-small-component-amplification.md) suppresses a necessary dictator component below any prescribed inverse polynomial, then recovers it. Deleting that component makes learning the two opposite labels impossible. This path is quantitatively nearoptimal, but is not claimed to be an exact global optimizer. Neither example is a learning lower bound.

There is also a positive [exact scalar-profile factory](bounded-density-scalar-profile-factory.md): a square-integrable Gaussian multiplier paired with one scalar Gaussian profile admits a dimension-independent unbiased formula oracle. This gives exact variance corrections under bounded densities and scalar readouts of one final bounded-density layer. It does not supply simultaneous output-coordinate oracles with a bounded total second moment, which is the remaining feature-factorization requirement. The quadratic-tilt counterexample further shows that a rank-one costate with small covariance-normalized nuclear norm can still have exponentially large density second moment.

A supporting [scalar tilt-two alignment lemma](critical-scalar-tilt-two-alignment.md) shows that increasing scalar paths chosen within `p²` of their value infimum have a uniformly bounded sum of square roots of relative entropies against the canonical tilt-two laws. This describes selected nearoptimal scalar continuations; it does not identify the corresponding full multivariate row laws.

The audited [joint growth-and-purification construction](critical-joint-growth-purification.md) improves a critical variational target path. From a constant-diagonal state with target component `p` and residual at most `p`, it reaches a hard margin at cost `V_* + O(p)` in `O(1/p)` additional layers. Applied to the unmodified first-layer seed, this gives `J_(H+1)(f) <= V_* + C S_f²/H` for `H >= C S_f`, where `S_f = 1 + log(1/a_f)` is the stated first-kernel seed complexity. In particular the excess is `O_f(1/H)` for each fixed Boolean target and uniformly at most `C(d+1)²/H`. These are variational action bounds; finite-width target-mass estimates and actual learning bounds require separate analysis.

Finally, [capped variance occupation](critical-capped-occupation-sharp-bottleneck.md) sharpens the bottleneck for **arbitrary** nearbaseline row-control paths of excess action `epsilon`: the first half already contains variance at most `C/H + C sqrt(epsilon/H)`. The proof counts visits to dyadic variance bands using the last visit and a capped linear potential, without assuming monotonicity. Combined with the target-action construction, a qualifying path of excess `O(S_f²/H)` has a bottleneck of size `O((1+S_f)/H)`. This strengthens the variational geometry used by the crossing argument; it does not complete the missing feature compiler.
