1        Introduction
A sharp approximation oracle compares a learner’s risk with the risk of a small approximating network
with coefficient exactly one. This matters when approximation error is nonzero: a multiplicative loss
on that error can dominate the statistical remainder. We obtain such an oracle with high probability
for the final ordinary Bayesian posterior at one fixed architecture and one fixed likelihood variance.
    Our learner is a bounded linear combination of bounded hidden features. The prior conditions
the readout coefficients to have ℓ1 norm at most one, and the teacher obeys the same variation
bound. These are substantive restrictions. They ensure bounded predictions without clipping and
supply the counting argument that makes the result uniform in learner width. The prior retains
continuous coordinates and is dense almost surely inside this support. Incoming coefficients are
unrestricted under the prior. The learner has one hidden layer; no claim is made here for raw ReLU
features or an unrestricted multilayer clipped-output prior.

In the fast-rate regime N ≥ max{8C, n}, the width term is absorbed into the statistical remainder.
Writing R(g) = R0 + A(g), where R0 is the irreducible noise and A(g) the approximation error,
both terms retain coefficient one. The Gaussian observation variance is 1024(1 + σ 2 ); the noise
proxy is used to set it. The comparator, its width, and its coefficients are used only in the analysis.
    The key geometric fact is the squared-loss midpoint identity. Moving a function halfway toward
a teacher improves average loss by one quarter of their squared distance. To exploit that identity at
a fixed width, the prior must permit movement of an arbitrary posterior, rather than only put mass
near the teacher. We construct precisely this transfer. A readout with ℓ1 norm at most one has
many small coefficients. Randomly merging pairs preserves their conditional mean and incurs total
variance only O(C/N 2 ). After scaling the survivors, the freed slots hold a slightly shrunken teacher.
The inserted coefficients are distinguishable from zero, so the readout norm bounds how many
locations could have been used. Averaging over their locations cancels the apparent logarithmic
learner-width cost. A relative-loss exponential inequality then converts the empirical midpoint
improvement into a sharp population oracle.
    For tanh, Section 7 removes the incoming coefficient cap without adding neurons. On the
Boolean cube, inverse-tanh interpolation reduces each feature to an integer linear system. On a
continuous cube, slab anticoncentration permits capping a feature’s normalized slope because both
original and capped features are saturated outside a small slab. The output variation bound makes
the approximation errors add without a factor C. These refinements yield coefficient-free width-only
guarantees within the same restricted teacher class.
    The same posterior also has a slower guarantee at every realizable width N ≥ C, including
N = C. Every function with readout variation at most one lies p   in the absolute convex hull of single
tanh features. Its uniform relative-loss deviation is therefore O( (1 + σ 2 )(d + 1)/n), independently

of network width. Combined with direct prior localization, Theorem 8.2 gives a coefficient-one oracle
with this deviation term and the same logarithmic comparison cost divided by n. The learner-width
condition distinguishes the faster rate; it is not needed for a vanishing approximation oracle.

Related work. Sharp high-probability aggregation inequalities are established results; for example,
Lecué and Rigollet [1] prove them for Q-aggregation. Ordinary exponential weighting has different
behavior: Lecué and Mendelson [2] give probability lower bounds for general finite dictionaries, while
Høgsgaard, Rebeschini and Wegel [3] prove a sharp expectation bound for the final exponential-
weights aggregate at a sufficiently large fixed temperature. Our proof uses the additional approximate-
midpoint structure of this particular neural prior to obtain a high-probability statement for its
ordinary posterior. It is not a general upgrade of arbitrary-prior exponential weighting.
   The output-variation viewpoint is also established in convex neural-network theory; Bach [4]
studies output-norm regularization and generalization for single-hidden-layer models. Our all-width
Rademacher argument uses the familiar convex-hull reduction. The faster fixed-posterior result
additionally needs the prior-specific transfer.
   Dense heavy-tailed neural priors also have substantial precedent. Castillo and Egels [5] establish
posterior and variational contraction with independent heavy-tailed weights on deterministic dense

to zero as r ↓ 0. Full support follows by prescribing one jump near any desired value and requiring
the independent small-jump remainder to be small. Thus the incoming prior has full support and
continuous coordinates.
