\begin{abstract}
Can Bayesian neural-network regression compete with the best approximation
of width polynomial in the sample size, when the comparison network may
have arbitrarily large coefficients?  We give a positive result for one
continuous full-support prior on a prescribed dense tanh architecture.
Hidden outgoing weights have continuous gamma multipliers and Student
factors; the Gaussian readout has a Dirichlet allocation of its variance.
The likelihood uses raw squared errors and prediction is the ordinary raw
posterior mean after all observations.  For marginally sub-Gaussian labels,
a deterministic Gaussian observation variance proportional to $\sqrt n$
gives a coefficient-one expected-risk oracle.  At fixed input dimension
and comparison depth $h$, it competes with arbitrary-coefficient ReLU
networks of width $n^u$ for some $u>0$, with a vanishing polynomial
remainder, at every prescribed learner width $N\ge n$.
With only a finite second label moment, a data-calibrated observation
variance gives a slower vanishing oracle for a growing width class.
The input distribution may be arbitrary on the Boolean cube, or satisfy
slab anticoncentration on a continuous domain, including uniform spheres.
A binary-input compiler avoids a power-of-accuracy width cost; it uses
logarithmic extra depth for continuous inputs.  Margin-restoring tanh gates
make the comparison cost additive in extra learner depth.  For noisy
realizable conditional means, a separate theorem keeps one fixed likelihood
variance and gives an almost $1/n$ rate at fixed teacher resources.
For exactly Gaussian noise, a further theorem uses its true variance
without inflation, with a larger complexity polynomial.  For marginally
sub-Gaussian labels with bounded conditional mean, a separate full-support
readout with faster-decreasing tails gives a nearly inverse-sample agnostic
complexity penalty at observation variance proportional to $\log^2 n$.
Removing the conditional-mean bound adds an explicit $n^{-1+o(1)}$ term.
The learners use no output clipping, architecture mixture, or holdout
selection.
\end{abstract}

\section{Approximation quality rather than exact realizability}
A complicated regression function can still admit a useful narrow-network
approximation.  A learning guarantee should then compare test risk with
that approximation's risk, rather than require the target itself to have
small complexity.  Width alone is a demanding measure: a narrow network
may use arbitrarily large real coefficients, encode steep transitions, or
have large raw outputs.

This paper proves such a comparison for one fixed family of Bayesian tanh
networks.  The prior is specified in Section~\ref{sec:raw-model}.  It is
hierarchical in continuous weights and has full support on a prescribed
dense architecture.  It is not the independent Gaussian weight prior.
The observation model is ordinary Gaussian regression with a specified
sample-size-dependent variance.  Both its likelihood and final posterior
mean use unrestricted real outputs.

The main theorem bounds expected test risk by the best raw ReLU-network
risk plus an estimation remainder, with coefficient exactly one on the
comparison risk.  At fixed input dimension and comparison depth, its
resource is
\[
 \widetilde O\big(C^{2bh}+H\big),
\]
where $C$ is comparison width, $h$ is comparison hidden depth, $H$ is
learner hidden depth, and $b$ is a numerical constant from a Boolean
arithmetic compiler.  Its statistical penalty is this resource divided
by $\sqrt n$.  For example, taking
\[
 C_n=\lfloor n^{1/(8bh)}\rfloor,\qquad H\le n^{1/4},\qquad N\ge n
\]
gives a remainder $\widetilde O(n^{-1/4})$.  Boolean inputs require only
$H\ge O(h)$; continuous inputs require $H\ge c\log n$.  The latter
requirement is explicit and cannot be suppressed by calling learner
depth fixed.

Under only a finite second label moment, Theorem~\ref{thm:raw-finite-variance-width}
gives a slower vanishing comparison using a variance calibrated from the
observed labels.  Its output remains raw and its coefficient on comparison
risk is still one.

There are three ingredients.  A raw-posterior oracle charges a useful
comparison neighborhood by its entropy relative to the weight prior.
A coefficient-free Boolean compiler turns arbitrary real coefficients
into finite digital computation.  A tanh binary quantizer extends that
compiler to continuous inputs, while restoring digital margins prevents
padding to larger learner depth from repeatedly amplifying errors.
Clipping occurs only in this proof's comparison construction; sub-Gaussian
label tails make that comparison step inexpensive.  It never changes
the observed labels or the actual learner.

The main statistical conclusion is in expectation over the training
sample.  It is not a claim of efficient posterior computation, a
pointwise risk domination with no estimation term, or a guarantee at
all widths as small as the uncompiled teacher.  The explicit threshold
is the compiled width, and $N\ge n$ is a simple sufficient choice in
the growing-class corollary.

\paragraph{Relation to earlier results.}
Dense Bayesian neural networks with heavy-tailed weights and deterministic
architectures already have posterior-contraction guarantees for structured
smoothness classes\cite{castillo-egels}.  The present comparison target is
different: arbitrary-coefficient finite-depth ReLU approximations, a
coefficient-one agnostic risk inequality, every sufficiently large
prescribed width, and an ordinary raw posterior mean.  The prior and
sample-size-dependent observation variance are part of the theorem; the
result is not asserted for every reasonable prior.  The underlying
coefficient-reduction idea also has earlier precedents in neural
complexity theory\cite{maass,vardi-shamir}.  We use a precise finite-input
compiler\cite{booleancompiler} and standard threshold arithmetic
\cite{hab}, and prove the continuous quantization and Bayesian comparison
consequences needed here.
