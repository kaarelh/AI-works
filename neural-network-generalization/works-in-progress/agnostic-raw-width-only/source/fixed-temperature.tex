\section{A fixed likelihood for noisy realizable targets}
\label{sec:compiler-fixed-temperature}
When a narrow network represents the conditional mean exactly, the
Gaussian observation variance can stay fixed.  The posterior may still
be misspecified: the true noise need only be conditionally sub-Gaussian,
not Gaussian.

Suppose $Y=f_0(X)+\xi$, $\E[\xi\mid X]=0$, and
$\E[e^{t\xi}\mid X]\le e^{\sigma^2t^2/2}$.  Assume $|f_0|\le B_0$
almost surely and put $B=\max\{1,B_0\}$, $R_0=\E\xi^2$.
Retain the same continuous prior and raw posterior mean, but now use any
one fixed temperature
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compiler-fixed-T}
 T\ge\max\{8\sigma^2+1,49B^2\}.
\end{equation}
It does not depend on sample size, comparison width or depth.

\begin{theorem}[Coefficient-free learning of the conditional mean]
\label{thm:raw-realizable-compiler}
Assume one of the input conditions in Theorem~\ref{thm:raw-width-oracle}.
Suppose $f_0$ agrees almost surely with a member of
$\mathcal G_{C,h}$; its implementing coefficients are unrestricted.
Use the same compiled width and depth thresholds, and define $\mathcal D_n$
by the expression for $c\mathcal B_n$ with $M_n$ replaced by $B$, with
its numerical constant sufficiently large.  For every $N\ge m_*$ and
$H\ge H_*$, and all sufficiently large $n$,
\[
 \E_S\int R(z_w)\,\Pi_S(dw)
 \le R_0+\frac{c\mathcal D_n[T+\mathcal D_n+\log(n+2)+B^2]}n.
\]
The ordinary raw posterior mean has no larger risk.  Moreover, for every
$0<\delta<1/2$, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\[
 \int R(z_w)\,\Pi_S(dw)
 \le R_0+\frac{c[\mathcal D_n+\log(4/\delta)]}{n}
       \left[T+\mathcal D_n+\log\frac{n+2}{\delta}+B^2\right].
\]
At fixed input dimension, teacher depth and regularity, the expected
excess is
\[
 \widetilde O\left(\frac{(C^{2bh}+H)^2+T(C^{2bh}+H)}n\right).
\]
In particular the growing comparison width and learner-depth range of
Corollary~\ref{cor:raw-growing-width} give excess
$\widetilde O(n^{-1/2})$ for such realizable conditional means, at the
single fixed variance $T/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the globally bounded version $f_0=\operatorname{clip}_B g$ of the
regression function, where $g\in\mathcal G_{C,h}$ represents it almost
surely.  This changes neither the data law nor any test risk.
Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-compiler-resource}, at $\epsilon_n=(n+2)^{-8}$,
gives a comparison law $\rho_n$ with
\[
 \KL(\rho_n\|\mu_{N,H})\le\mathcal D_n,\qquad
 \rho_n\{\|z_w\|_\infty\le6B\}=1,\qquad
 \E_{\rho_n,X}(z_w-f_0)^2\le n^{-4}
\]
for all sufficiently large $n$.  Hence its pointwise mean squared
discrepancy $v(x)$ is at most $49B^2\le T$.

Here is the raw fixed-temperature argument, in the form needed for
population rather than uniform approximation.  Let
$\mathcal A(f)=\E_X(f-f_0)^2$, $c_T=1-2\sigma^2/T\ge3/4$, and define
\[
 D_T(f)=-T\log\E_X e^{-c_T(f-f_0)^2/T}.
\]
Conditional sub-Gaussianity, the entropy inequality and the Gibbs
variational identity give
\[
 \E_S\Pi_S D_T\le n^{-4}+T\mathcal D_n/n.
\]
For the confidence statement the same argument gives
$\Pi_SD_T\le cT[\mathcal D_n+\log(4/\delta)]/n$ outside a set of
probability $O(\delta)$.  To verify the comparison concentration used
here, $0\le v\le49B^2$ and $Pv\le n^{-4}$ imply
$P_nv\le2n^{-4}+cB^2\log(1/\delta)/n$ by Bernstein.  The conditional
noise term has variance proxy at most $4\sigma^2P_nv/n$ and is therefore
at most $cn^{-4}+cT\log(1/\delta)/n$ with the same confidence order.

A second use of the comparison law bounds the expected posterior
density relative to the prior by $\exp(\mathcal D_n+c)$.  Explicitly,
divide the likelihood by that at $f_0$, lower-bound its normalizer by
Jensen with $\rho_n$, and average the noise.  For $e=f-f_0$ and
$b(x)=\E_{\rho_n}(z_w-f_0)$, the exponent at one observation is bounded by
\[
 \frac{v-e^2}{T}+\frac{2\sigma^2(e-b)^2}{T^2}
                    \le\frac{3v}{2T}-\frac{e^2}{2T}
                    \le\frac{3v}{2T}.
\]
Because $v\le T$ and $Pv\le n^{-4}$, its input expectation is at most
$\exp(cn^{-4}/T)$.  Thus every nonnegative network functional $W$ obeys
$\E_S\Pi_SW\le e^{\mathcal D_n+c}\mu_{N,H}W$.

The native raw supremum has a Gaussian tail, so a cutoff with
$M^2=c[\mathcal D_n+\log(n+2)+B^2]$ makes the expected posterior
contribution of $(\|f\|_\infty+B)^2\mathbf1_{\{\|f\|_\infty>M\}}$
at most $n^{-2}$.  For confidence $1-\delta$, replace $\log(n+2)$ by
$\log((n+2)/\delta)$ and apply Markov's inequality to this tail term.
On the remaining functions the secant bound for the exponential gives
\[
 \mathcal A(f)\le
 \left(\frac1{c_T}+\frac{(M+B)^2}{T}\right)D_T(f).
\]
Combining the last three bounds proves the displayed inequalities.
The elementary entropy step and tail argument are also stated as the
fixed-temperature population-comparison theorem in~\cite{rawprior}.
Finally $R(f)=R_0+\mathcal A(f)$ and Jensen's inequality transfer the
result to the ordinary posterior mean.  Substitution of
$\mathcal D_n=\widetilde O(C^{2bh}+H)$ proves the last claims.
\end{proof}
