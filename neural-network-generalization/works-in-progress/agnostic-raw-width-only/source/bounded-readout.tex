\section{A readout with bounded absolute-weight sum}
\label{sec:bounded-readout-endpoint}
A readout restriction gives a faster fixed-likelihood alternative when the
regression function is bounded.  This is a different prior with bounded
readout support, not an improvement of the unrestricted-support prior in
the main theorem.  It requires no final output activation.

Keep the gamma-gated Student hidden layers.  At the readout use
\[
 P\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(\vartheta/N,\ldots,\vartheta/N),\qquad
 z_w(x)=b+\sum_{j=1}^N P_jU_jh_j^H(x),
\]
where $b,U_1,\ldots,U_N$ are independent uniform variables on $[-1,1]$,
independent of $P$ and the hidden layers.  Thus the readout coefficients
have absolute sum at most one, $|b|\le1$, and $|z_w(x)|\le2$ on every
input.  The weights are dense almost surely, but their support is
restricted.  The raw affine output is used directly in the Gaussian
likelihood and in its ordinary posterior mean.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-likelihood expected oracle under a readout restriction]
\label{prop:bounded-readout-width}
Assume $Y=f_0(X)+\xi$, with $|f_0|\le1$ and centered conditional
$\sigma^2$-sub-Gaussian noise.  Use any fixed temperature
$T\ge1024(1+\sigma^2)$, with physical Gaussian likelihood variance
$T/2$.  Under any input setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:raw-width-oracle},
for every eligible compiled width $N\ge m_*$ and depth $H\ge H_*$,
\[
 \E_S R(\widehat f_S)
 \le\inf_{g\in\mathcal G_{C,h}}R(g)
                +\frac{cT\mathcal B_n^{(1)}}{n+1}+c(n+1)^{-2},
\]
where $\mathcal B_n^{(1)}$ is the resource in the main theorem with its
proof range $M_n$ replaced by one.  In particular, at fixed $d,h$,
input regularity and noise bound, the remainder is
\[
 \widetilde O\left(\frac{(1+\sigma^2)(C^{2bh}+H)}n\right).
\]
For $C_n=\lfloor n^{1/(4bh)}\rfloor$, $H\le n^{1/2}$ and every
prescribed $N\ge n$, the remainder is $\widetilde O(n^{-1/2})$.
The previous lower depth thresholds still apply.  The prior family and
likelihood variance are fixed, and the returned predictor uses all
observations.  This is an expected-risk statement.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First modify only the compiler's final encoding.  A value $a\in[-1,1]$
can be rounded, with error at most $2^{1-t}$, to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-variation-digital-readout}
 q=\sum_{r=1}^t2^{-r}s_r-2^{-t},\qquad s_r\in\{-1,1\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed encode
$k=\min\{2^t-1,\lfloor2^{t-1}(a+1)\rfloor\}$ in $t$ binary digits,
and replace each binary digit by its signed version.  The raw affine
readout in \eqref{eq:unit-variation-digital-readout} has absolute
coefficient sum plus absolute bias exactly one.  The same expression
remains in $[-1,1]$ when all $s_r\in[-1,1]$.
For a rational clipped compiler output $a=A/Q$, the required rounding is
one constant-depth integer-division block.  Thus the width, depth and
precision bounds of the earlier compilers remain unchanged after
increasing their numerical constants.  Set $t$ large enough that the
additional error is at most the requested compiler accuracy.

Here is the readout comparison replacing the Gaussian-factor step in
Lemma~\ref{lem:raw-bit-comparison}.  Let $v_r=2^{-r}$ and
$L=\sum_rv_r=1-2^{-t}$.  Choose selected latent-gate centers
\[
 w_r=v_r+(1-L)/t,\qquad
 \sum_rw_r=1,\qquad w_r\ge2^{-t}/t,
\]
and uniform-factor centers $u_r=v_r/w_r$.  Then
$0\le u_r\le1-2^{-t}/t$.  Put each selected gate within relative
precision $\eta$ of $w_r$, its selected uniform factor within absolute
precision $\eta$ of $u_r$, and the bias within $\eta$ of $-2^{-t}$.
Take $\eta$ smaller than the desired functional precision and than
$c2^{-t}/t$.  Condition the total inactive latent-gate mass to be at
most $\eta$, reducing it further if needed for marker separation.
Its gamma small-ball cost is $O(\log(1/\eta)+t+\log(t+1))$.
The normalized selected coefficients then have total absolute error
$O(\eta)$, and the inactive contribution is deterministically $O(\eta)$
because $|U_jh_j^H|\le1$.

Each selected latent gate costs $\log N+O(\log(1/\eta))+
(\vartheta/N)\log(1/w_r)$.  Summing the last term costs at most
$c(t+\log(t+1))$, since $N\ge t$.  The standard disjoint location
mixture cancels the selected $\log N$ terms.  The uniform-factor and
bias intervals cost $O((t+1)\log(1/\eta))$.  All these costs are
polynomial in logarithmic precision, because
$\log(1/\eta)=O(t+\log(1/\delta)+\log(t+1))$.
The hidden prefix and restoring digital comparison are unchanged.
Consequently their comparison law has entropy at most
$c\mathcal B_n^{(1)}$ and population squared discrepancy
$O((n+2)^{-8})$ from $\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}g$.
It is automatically supported on raw outputs in $[-2,2]$.

Because $|f_0|\le1$, projection onto $[-1,1]$ cannot increase
$\E(g-f_0)^2$, hence cannot increase squared test risk.  The comparison
error changes risk by $O((n+1)^{-2})$, using
$R(f)-R(g)=\E[(f-f_0)^2-(g-f_0)^2]$ and the bounded comparison outputs.
The all-data exponential-weights expected-risk theorem for predictions
in $[-2,2]$, after rescaling its unit-range statement, gives
\[
 \E_S R(\widehat f_S)\le\rho R+
                   \frac{T\KL(\rho\|\mu)}{n+1}
 \quad\text{when }T\ge1024(1+\sigma^2).
\]
This is the bounded-prediction theorem used in~\cite{rawprior}; the bound
on predictions here comes from the readout prior, not an output link.
Substitute the comparison law and take population near-minimizers.
Finally $\mathcal B_n^{(1)}=\widetilde O(C^{2bh}+H)$ gives the stated
rates and growing-class substitution.
\end{proof}
