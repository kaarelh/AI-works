\section{One dense prior and raw Gaussian regression}
\label{sec:raw-model}
Let $X\in[-1,1]^d$ and let $Y$ be real valued.  The observations are iid,
and $R(f)=\E(f(X)-Y)^2$ denotes squared test risk.  Fix a hidden width $N$
and hidden depth $H$ before observing the sample.  All hidden activations
are tanh.  Fix $0<\vartheta\le1$ and a centered Student law of more than two
degrees of freedom.  The first-layer weights and all hidden biases have
this Student law.  For $2\le\ell\le H$, set
\[
 h_i^\ell(x)=\tanh\left(b_i^\ell+
             \sum_{j=1}^N S_{\ell-1,j}G_{\ell,ij}h_j^{\ell-1}(x)\right),
 \qquad S_{\ell-1,j}\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(\vartheta/N,1),
\]
where the $G_{\ell,ij}$ have the same Student law.  All these latent
variables are independent.  At the readout take a separate symmetric
Dirichlet vector and independent standard Gaussian factors:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-raw-prior}
 P\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(\vartheta/N,\ldots,\vartheta/N),\qquad
 z_w(x)=G_0+\sum_{j=1}^N\sqrt{P_j}\,G_jh_j^H(x).
\end{equation}
This is a continuous full-support prior on one dense architecture.  Hidden
outgoing weights share a continuous multiplier, and the readout shares a
variance allocation; its weight coordinates are not independent.  No
network size is sampled.  Its scalar distributions do not change with
sample size.  Their displayed fan-in dependence defines the prior family
as $N$ varies.

For a deterministic temperature $T_n$, the posterior and prediction are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw-posterior}
 \Pi_S(dw)=\frac{e^{-\sum_{i=1}^n(z_w(X_i)-Y_i)^2/T_n}}{Z_S}\mu_{N,H}(dw),
 \qquad \widehat f_S(x)=\int z_w(x)\,\Pi_S(dw).
\end{equation}
The physical Gaussian observation variance is $T_n/2$.  There is no
output clipping, output saturation, label truncation, holdout selection, or averaging
across sample sizes in this learner.  The posterior mean is the ordinary
mean after all $n$ observations.  Computing it efficiently is not part of
the guarantee.

We state explicitly the raw-network oracle used below.  It is proved in
the companion paper on raw priors\cite{rawprior}; the following explanation
also identifies its two quantitative inputs.  Write $L_q$ for the absolute
sum of the comparator's readout coefficients and $b_q$ for its output bias.
For a tanh comparator $q$ with the same depth $H$, width at most $m\le N$,
and hidden coefficient cap $B\ge1$, set
\begin{align}
 P_m&=(d+1)(m+1)+(H+1)(m+1)^2,\notag\\
 Q_m&=P_m+(H+1)(m+1)+H+1,\notag\\
 \mathcal K_n(q)&=Q_m\left[(H+2)\log\{c(d+m+2)(B+2)\}
       +\log\{c(H+2)(m+1)(B+2)(n+1)\}\right]\notag\\
 &\hspace{12mm}+L_q^2+b_q^2+1.\label{eq:raw-comparison-resource}
\end{align}
The count includes every position in the selected dense blocks, including
zero coefficients.  It is not a count only of nonzero edges.

\begin{proposition}[Raw network comparison oracle, imported]\label{prop:raw-network-oracle}
Suppose $\E\exp(Y^2/K_Y^2)\le2$ for a fixed finite $K_Y$.
There are constants $t_0,c>0$, depending only on the fixed prior laws and
$K_Y$, such that the single deterministic choice $T_n=t_0\sqrt n$ gives
\[
 \E_S R(\widehat f_S)\le R(q)+c n^{-1/2}\mathcal K_n(q)+c(n+1)^{-2}
\]
for every raw tanh comparator $q$ and every $N\ge m$.  The constants are
independent of learner depth, learner width, input dimension and comparator
resources.  No output bound on $q$ is required.
\end{proposition}

Two facts explain the role of this prior.  First, its raw supremum has a
width- and depth-independent Gaussian tail.  Indeed
$\sup_x|z_w(x)|\le|G_0|+\sum_j\sqrt{P_j}|G_j|$.
If $S_j\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(\vartheta/N,1)$ and $U=\sum_jS_j$, then
$U$ is independent of $P_j=S_j/U$, and
\[
 \sum_j\sqrt{S_j}|G_j|=\sqrt U\sum_j\sqrt{P_j}|G_j|.
\]
Each $\sqrt{S_j}G_j$ is a difference of two gamma variables.  The absolute
sum on the left is dominated by a gamma variable of fixed total shape;
its $p$th norm is $O(p)$, whereas $\|\sqrt U\|_p\ge c\sqrt p$.
Division proves $\|\sup_x|z_w(x)|\|_p\le c\sqrt p$.

Second, small comparator implementations have useful prior neighborhoods
at every larger width.  There is a comparison law $\rho_r$ satisfying
\[
 \E_{\rho_r}\|z_w-q\|_\infty^2\le r,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{KL}(\rho_r\|\mu_{N,H})
 \le cQ_m\log\frac{c(m+1)(B+2)W}{r}
          +c(L_q^2+b_q^2),
\]
where $W=(H+2)[c(d+m+2)(B+2)]^{H+2}$.
The comparison puts selected gates near nonzero values and suppresses the
sum of the others.  Its selected-gate entropy contains $\log N$ terms;
averaging placements among the $N$ neurons cancels those terms.  Student
coefficient neighborhoods cost logarithmically in $B$, while allocating
readout variance in proportion to coefficient magnitudes costs $O(L_q^2)$.
These are comparison measures, not changes to the learner or a prior over
architectures.

The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:raw-network-oracle} truncates the prior
only for analysis, applies the all-data bounded-prediction exponential-
weights oracle, and transfers back using the native Gaussian supremum
tail.  Balancing that tail against the posterior normalizer gives
$T_n\asymp\sqrt n$.  The final posterior and its mean in
\eqref{eq:raw-posterior} remain unrestricted.  The companion gives the
full latent-coordinate construction and tail-transfer proof.

We also use its supported-comparison form.  There is an analytical cutoff
$U_n=c_0 n^{1/4}$ such that every sample-independent law $\rho$ supported
on $\{\sup_x|z_w(x)|\le U_n\}$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw-supported-oracle}
 \E_S R(\widehat f_S)\le \int R(z_w)\,\rho(dw)
   +\frac{T_n\operatorname{KL}(\rho\|\mu_{N,H})}{n+1}
   +c(n+1)^{-2}.
\end{equation}
This follows by using $\rho$ directly in the proof-only bounded-prior
oracle: conditioning the prior to contain its support can only decrease
its KL cost.  The raw-prior transfer is unchanged.  This form permits a
comparison accurate in population $L^2$, rather than uniformly on the
whole input domain.  No such cutoff is imposed on the actual posterior.
