\section{Coefficient-free approximation oracles}
\label{sec:coefficient-free-oracle}
Let $\mathcal G_{C,h}$ be the class of real-valued ReLU networks with
hidden width at most $C$ and hidden depth $h$, arbitrary finite real
coefficients, and affine biases.  There is no norm or robustness restriction
on these comparison networks.  Their output is raw and can be unbounded
as their coefficients vary.  Only the input domain is compact.

We first make the accuracy and architecture requirements explicit.  For
$n\ge2$ set
\[
 M_n=\max\{1,K_Y\sqrt{20\log(n+2)}\},\qquad \epsilon_n=(n+2)^{-8}.
\]
On Boolean inputs use
\[
 k_B=4(d+1)(C+1)^h+\lceil\log_2(32/\epsilon_n)\rceil+2,
 \qquad m_B=\lceil c k_B^b\rceil,
 \qquad H_B=\lceil b(h+1)\rceil.
\]
For continuous inputs with density at most $\kappa$ relative to uniform
measure on the cube, put
\begin{align*}
 J&=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{c\max\{1,\kappa\}d(C+1)^h}{\epsilon_n}\right\rceil,\\
 k_D&=4(dJ+1)(C+1)^h+\lceil\log_2(32/\epsilon_n)\rceil+2,\\
 m_D&=\lceil c k_D^b\rceil,\qquad H_D=2J+\lceil b(h+1)\rceil+3.
\end{align*}
For the slab condition~\eqref{eq:digital-slab-assumption}, use instead
\[
 F=(hC+\lceil8/\epsilon_n\rceil+1)(C+1)^{dh},\qquad
 J_S=\left\lceil\log_2\max\left\{8,
 4\sqrt d(8\kappa F/\epsilon_n)^{1/\alpha}\right\}\right\rceil,
\]
and define $k_S,m_S,H_S$ by the density formulas with $J$ replaced by $J_S$.
Here $b,c$ are fixed numerical constants large enough for the compiler
lemmas.  Enlarging them is allowed once, before applying the theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Raw Bayesian regression against arbitrary coefficients]
\label{thm:raw-width-oracle}
Assume $\E\exp(Y^2/K_Y^2)\le2$.  Use the single prior
\eqref{eq:normalized-raw-prior}, posterior \eqref{eq:raw-posterior}, and
$T_n=t_0\sqrt n$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:raw-network-oracle}.
The following statements hold for every architecture fixed before the data.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $X\in\{-1,1\}^d$ has any distribution, take $m_*=m_B$ and
$H_*=H_B$.
\item If $X\in[-1,1]^d$ has density at most $\kappa$ relative to the
uniform probability measure, take $m_*=m_D$ and $H_*=H_D$.
\item If $P_X$ satisfies the slab condition~\eqref{eq:digital-slab-assumption},
take $m_*=m_S$ and $H_*=H_S$. This includes the sphere laws in
Corollary~\ref{cor:digital-cube-sphere}.
\end{enumerate}
For every $N\ge m_*$ and $H\ge H_*$, and all $n$ above a fixed
threshold depending only on the label envelope and prior parameters,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-free-raw-risk}
 \E_S R(\widehat f_S)\le
 \inf_{g\in\mathcal G_{C,h}} R(g)
                 +c\mathcal B_n n^{-1/2}+c(n+1)^{-2},
\end{equation}
where $t_n=\lceil\log_2(128/\epsilon_n)\rceil$ and
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal B_n={}&\left[(d+1)^2(J_*+2)^4
 +(d+1)(h+1)(m_*+1)^2+(H+1)(t_n+2)^2\right]\Lambda_n^2+M_n^2,\\
 \Lambda_n={}&c(1+\alpha^{-1})\left[J_*+
 \log\frac{(H+2)(m_*+2)(d+2)(\kappa+2)(M_n+2)}{\epsilon_n}\right].
\end{align*}
Use $J_*=0,\alpha=1,\kappa=1$ in the Boolean case;
$J_*=J,\alpha=1$ in the density case; and $J_*=J_S$ with the stated
slab constants in the third case.
The constants depend on the fixed scalar prior laws and $K_Y$; no
coefficient of a comparison network enters the bound.  The learner does
not use $C,h,\kappa$, the comparison network, or these resource bounds to
choose its temperature. The density and slab conditions are alternatives;
the former gives a smaller sufficient depth for cube-supported laws.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a comparison network $g$.  Pointwise projection onto $[-M_n,M_n]$
satisfies
\[
 (\operatorname{clip}_{M_n}g-Y)^2
 \le(g-Y)^2+(|Y|-M_n)_+^2.
\]
The label-tail assumption and the choice of $M_n$ make the expectation
of the last term at most $c(n+1)^{-4}$.  Apply the compiler to $g/M_n$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-compiler-resource}
with raw target $g$, range $M_n$, and precision $\epsilon_n$.
It gives a sample-independent law $\rho$ supported on raw outputs of
supremum at most $6M_n$, with
\[
 \E_\rho\|z_w-\operatorname{clip}_{M_n}g\|_{L^2(P_X)}^2
                       \le cM_n^2\epsilon_n,
 \qquad \operatorname{KL}(\rho\|\mu_{N,H})\le c\mathcal B_n.
\]
The label-tail bound and Cauchy--Schwarz therefore give
$\int R(z_w)\rho(dw)\le R(g)+c(n+1)^{-2}$.
For all sufficiently large $n$, $6M_n$ lies inside the analytical cutoff
$c_0n^{1/4}$ of \eqref{eq:raw-supported-oracle}.  That inequality proves
\eqref{eq:coefficient-free-raw-risk} for the fixed $g$.
Taking a deterministic sequence of population-risk near-minimizers proves
the infimum statement.  This last step is an
infimum of numerical upper bounds; no data-dependent selection of a
comparison network is required.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A growing-width comparison class]\label{cor:raw-growing-width}
Fix $d,h,K_Y$ and, in the continuous cases, the applicable regularity
constants $\kappa$ and $\alpha$.  Let
\[
 u=\frac1{8bh},\qquad C_n=\lfloor n^u\rfloor.
\]
For all sufficiently large $n$, every prescribed $N\ge n$, and every
prescribed learner depth in the ranges below, the same single posterior
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growing-class-raw}
 \E_S R(\widehat f_S)
 \le\inf_{g\in\mathcal G_{C_n,h}}R(g)
                     +\widetilde O(n^{-1/4}).
\end{equation}
On Boolean inputs the permitted range is
$b(h+1)\le H\le n^{1/4}$.  Under either continuous assumption it is
$c_{d,h,\kappa,\alpha}\log(n+2)\le H\le n^{1/4}$, with $\alpha=1$
for the density case.
The tilde suppresses powers of logarithms; constants may depend on the
fixed quantities above and the fixed prior laws.  The prior uses one
prescribed dense architecture, the likelihood has deterministic variance
$t_0\sqrt n/2$, and the predictor is the raw posterior mean on all data.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Both compiler widths satisfy $m_*=\widetilde O(n^{1/8})$, so $N\ge n$
is sufficient.  At fixed input dimension and teacher depth the resource is
$\mathcal B_n=\widetilde O(m_*^2+H)=\widetilde O(n^{1/4})$.
Division by $\sqrt n$ gives the result.  The lower bound on continuous
learner depth is exactly the logarithmic input-quantization overhead.
\end{proof}

The inequality compares the final raw Bayesian predictor with the best
approximation in a growing width class.  It does not assert that a learner
can match that class with no estimation remainder, or that the learner
width may be arbitrarily small.  Here the explicit sufficient threshold
is $N\ge m_*$, with the simpler $N\ge n$ in the corollary.  The constants
and powers are theoretical; no claim of practical sample counts is made.
