\section{Interpretation and limits}
\paragraph{The approximation class remains restricted.}
The oracle competes with teachers satisfying the same hidden gain bounds. In the general activation case the first-layer gain bound is also part of the class. An arbitrary small MLP need not have an equivalent implementation with these bounds. The theorem is information-theoretic and does not imply efficient posterior sampling or a gradient-descent guarantee.

\paragraph{Why the prior changes matter.}
The readout variance in~\eqref{eq:prior} is a substantive change. At $H\ge2$, its exponent is $\max(\gamma,2)+1$, rather than $\gamma+1$, and it includes the factor $1/H$. The likelihood is calibrated to the same precision. The theorem therefore does not assert that a hidden gain restriction alone fixes every unchanged scaling tuple. Its Gaussian coordinate prior still has a single dense architecture before the stated conditioning.

\paragraph{Depth and signal attenuation.}
Tanh with unit internal row sums attenuates hidden amplitudes: at hidden depth $H$, they are at most $\sqrt{3/(H+2)}$. At fixed readout cap, very deep teachers in this class may consequently have small output amplitude. The theorem does not hide this restriction. Hardtanh, also covered, passes every value in $[-1,1]$ unchanged and permits identity layers preserving a nonzero signal. ReLU likewise preserves positive signals through identity layers in the general case. Ordinary sigmoid has a different limitation: its input variation contracts by $4^{-(H-1)}$ under the unit row bounds, so its output oscillation is at most $CB\,4^{-(H-1)}$. Fixed-width, bounded-readout sigmoid teachers therefore become nearly constant at large depth.

\paragraph{What pays for hidden volume.}
An independent Gaussian row conditioned to lie in an $\ell^1$ ball need not have a useful unconditioned small-ball probability at large width. The affine comparison in Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} instead compares two distributions within exactly the same conditioned measure. Its normalizer cancels. The remaining volume term is dimension times $\log(1/\eta)$, which is absorbed by $\omega=HN^\kappa$. The independent readout still has a Gaussian entropy penalty at that speed, and it is this penalty that controls the function class.
