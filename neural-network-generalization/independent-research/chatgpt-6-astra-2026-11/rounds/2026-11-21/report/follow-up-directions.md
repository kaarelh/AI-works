# Most useful next questions

These are open directions, not additional results from this round. The new papers and exact checks narrow several of them substantially.

1. **Polynomial-width ReLU mistakes after short calibration.** Does there exist a family of Boolean targets in dimension O(k), with polynomial-width bounded-coefficient shallow ReLU teachers, for which a polynomial-width isotropic Gaussian learner makes exp(Ω(k)) confident observed-margin-only mistakes in its fixed-width strong-prior limit, following only polynomially many calibration observations? The control-rail theorem answers the fixed-width strong-prior question at exponential learner width and long calibration. The new standard-softplus theorem gives exponential confident mistakes with only one initial label at a polynomially small Gaussian scale, but its learner width remains exponential and it uses central-limit normalization. Its particular unique minimum-cost completions cannot solve the stronger question: their active-width and necessary-calibration lower bounds are both exponential. A new geometry is needed. The prior scale is allowed to become very small; a polynomial-scale teacher-box potential should not be confused with a scale-uniform bound.

2. **A fixed-scale lexicographic cosine theorem.** For every odd d and every positive common Gaussian scale, is the actual positive-margin Gaussian-limit posterior's next lexicographic prediction wrong in majority at every stage after the first? The current theorem proves all lexicographic *modes* wrong and posterior confidence in the cold limit. At arbitrary fixed scale it proves a mean-selected order with sampling mistake probability at least 1/e on every later round, and a stronger exponential tail of confident majority errors from the exact precision row sum. It does not prove fixed-scale lexicographic majority failure on every round. Two independent importance schemes support the stronger lexicographic statement in finite cases, but low-ESS histories are inconclusive. Regression coefficients have mixed signs, so replacing every observed output by its lower margin is not a valid shortcut.

3. **Does normalized spectral ordering survive depth?** For a standard bivariate Gaussian (Z1,Z2) of correlation c, define k_beta(c)=E[softplus_beta(Z1) softplus_beta(Z2)]/E[softplus_beta(Z1)^2]. On the d-cube start with c_0(x,y)=(1+x·y)/(d+1), and recursively set c_(l+1)=(1+k_beta(c_l))/2, corresponding to equal bias and normalized hidden-signal variance. After input centering and total-variance normalization, do all upper Fourier-degree tails increase with beta at every fixed depth? The one-layer random-feature and tangent-kernel results prove an exact shallow version, including arbitrary nonnegative layer learning-rate weights. The deeper recursion is a precise test case; a counterexample would also locate a useful boundary. No finite-network or trained-kernel monotonicity is assumed.

4. **Dimension dependence of constructive posterior sampling.** Can the exact finite-sector construction be implemented with controlled bit precision and useful dependence on dimension, rather than unrestricted fixed-dimension constants? The current theorem gives ideal-real-arithmetic expected O_d(N) cost and arbitrary fixed polynomial accuracy by retaining more sectors. The independent-proposal experiments expose substantial finite-width and importance-weight effects. A useful next result would quantify rejection constants and conditioning of the output disintegration, and either give a tractable regime with growing dimension or prove an obstruction.

The strongest completed connections to non-Bayesian learning are the normalized initialization tangent-kernel ordering and the all-but-first lexicographic mistake theorem for exact minimum-norm interpolation, which includes ReLU/softplus both-layer initialization tangent kernels. Their gradient-flow consequences freeze the kernel; the finite-Jacobian statement uses a zero-output linearization. The ReLU lower bound also applies to global minimum-norm and exact finite hinge-penalty solutions under its stated conditions, but a gradient-descent trajectory reaching those solutions remains open.

5. **The positive-margin bridge for degree-ordered kernels.** For every positive increasing radial covariance spectrum mu_0 <= ... <= mu_d and every lexicographic prefix H, is C(mu)[H,H]^-1 1 strictly positive entrywise? The new exact-interpolation theorem proves all next parity predictions nonpositive using arithmetic/harmonic spectral recursion, strictly negative when the final original degree drop is strict. The displayed KKT conjecture would make all positive-margin constraints active and extend those predictions to cold Gaussian posterior modes, including ReLU. An all-dimensional proof now covers prefix sizes 2^s, 2^s-2^t, and 2^s+2^t; the first two families only require a positive spectrum. Exact polynomial-coefficient checks additionally verify every ordered positive spectrum at all prefixes through dimension four, and a million higher-dimensional random-spectrum tests are supportive; no all-dimensional proof is claimed. The prefix restriction is essential: the strictly increasing spectrum (1,2,16,17) and observed set {0,1,3,6} in dimension three have a negative equality multiplier; the fourth constrained margin value is 143/141. Equality conditioning alone does not justify this step.

The proposed margin bridge also fails for general coordinatewise downsets: the seven-dimensional ball of radius five and spectrum (1,1,1,1,256,256,256,256) have origin multiplier -607406/5529169 under equality interpolation. The constrained origin value is 164275335/125401351 > 1. The kernel paper and finite checker include the exact positive KKT certificate. Thus a proof of the surviving conjecture must use more than downward closure.
