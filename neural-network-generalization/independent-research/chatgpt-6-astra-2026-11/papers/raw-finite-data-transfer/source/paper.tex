\begin{abstract}
Near-minimum-energy parity pretraining guarantees transfer after even
symmetrization of the hidden features, but an explicit optimal-energy
representation can fail on a particular nearby target with raw features.
We prove a complementary finite-width statement for the original raw
feature map. A family of $M$ orthonormal targets whose approximate
comparators have contamination in an $r$-dimensional space has average
squared approximation error at most $r/M$ plus its original squared
approximation deficit, with a controlled average coefficient norm.
For shallow ReLU parity features the contamination is linear in the input,
so $M=\binom r{2k}$ nearby tasks give an exceptional fraction of order
$r^{1-2k}$. Coordinate-permutation invariance converts this deterministic
average into a high-probability theorem for every fixed nearby target
under an actual finite-width, polynomial-data cold posterior.
The downstream learner uses ordinary raw features, with neither even
projection nor a linear input skip. The guarantee has an explicit
confidence--dimension condition; it is not simultaneous over all targets
in every representation. For each fixed $k\ge2$, the deterministic
$r/M$ error order is asymptotically sharp.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Relation to existing representation-learning work.}
Collins, Hassani, Soltanolkotabi, Mokhtari, and Shakkottai prove
multi-task feature extraction and downstream guarantees for a specified
gradient-trained ReLU model
(\href{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/collins24a.html}{ICML 2024}).
Van Meegen and Sompolinsky study learned coding schemes in Bayesian
networks under non-lazy scaling
(\href{https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-58276-6}{Nature Communications 2025}).
The question here is more specific: transfer from a single full-parity
source under a finite cold posterior. General representation learning,
Bayesian feature adaptation, and elementary Hilbert--Schmidt projection
identities are not claimed as new themes or general discoveries.

\section{A finite-rank contamination lemma}
Let $\mathcal H$ and $\mathcal C$ be finite-dimensional real Hilbert
spaces. Let $\Phi:\mathcal C\to\mathcal H$ be a feature synthesis map,
and let $J:\R^M\to\mathcal H$ be an isometry, whose columns are
orthonormal target functions. Suppose a coefficient map
$C:\R^M\to\mathcal C$ obeys
\[
                 \Phi C=J+L+E,
\]
where $\operatorname{rank}L\le d$. No orthogonality between the ranges
of these three maps is assumed. The Hilbert--Schmidt norm is denoted
$\|\cdot\|_{\rm HS}$.

\begin{lemma}[Remove a low-rank component]
Let $P$ be the orthogonal projection onto $\ker L$. Then
\[
 \|\Phi CP-J\|_{\rm HS}^2
  =\operatorname{rank}L+\|EP\|_{\rm HS}^2
  \le d+\|E\|_{\rm HS}^2,
 \qquad
 \|CP\|_{\rm HS}^2\le\|C\|_{\rm HS}^2.
 \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if each column of $E$ has norm at most $\Delta$ and
each column of $C$ has norm at most $B$, the $M$ functions
$g_t=\Phi CP e_t$ have
\[
 \frac1M\sum_t\|g_t-Je_t\|^2\le\frac dM+\Delta^2,
 \qquad \frac1M\sum_t\|CPe_t\|^2\le B^2.
 \tag{2}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because $LP=0$, the residual is $EP-J(I-P)$.
Its cross term in the Hilbert--Schmidt inner product vanishes:
\[
 \operatorname{tr}((I-P)J^*EP)
   =\operatorname{tr}(P(I-P)J^*E)=0.
\]
Also $J^*J=I$, so $\|J(I-P)\|_{\rm HS}^2
=\operatorname{tr}(I-P)=\operatorname{rank}L$.
Right composition by an orthogonal projection cannot increase a
Hilbert--Schmidt norm. This proves (1) and then (2).
The cancellation of the cross term avoids an unnecessary factor $M$
in the approximation deficit.
\end{proof}

\section{Application to actual raw ReLU features}
Fix even $r\ge6$, an integer $k\ge1$ with $j=r-2k\ge2$, and
$X=\{-1,1\}^r$ with uniform probability. Put
\[
 \chi(x)=\prod_{l=1}^r x_l,\quad D=r+1,\quad
 \rho=\frac{\binom{r-2}{(r-2)/2}}{2^{r-1}\sqrt r},\quad
 b_j=\rho(-1)^{(j-2)/2}
 \frac{\binom{(r-2)/2}{(j-2)/2}}{\binom{r-2}{j-2}}.
\]
Consider any finite network
\[
 F(x)=N^{-1}\sum_i a_i\relu(b_i+w_i\cdot x),\qquad
 E_0=(2N)^{-1}\sum_i(a_i^2+b_i^2+\|w_i\|^2).
\]
Suppose for fixed $s>0$ that
\[
 E_0\le(1+\epsilon)s/\rho,\qquad
 \E(s-\chi F)_+\le h,\qquad 0\le\epsilon\le1.
 \tag{3}
\]
Use the original raw kernel
$K(x,y)=N^{-1}\sum_i\relu(b_i+w_i\cdot x)\relu(b_i+w_i\cdot y)$.
Define
\begin{align*}
 M&=\binom r{2k},&C_k&=(r-1)^k,&\alpha&=b_r/(s b_j),\\
 u&=\sqrt{(s+E_0\sqrt D)(\epsilon s+2h)},&
 v&=\sqrt{2E_0(\epsilon s+h)/\rho},\\
 \Delta&=|\alpha|\{(1+C_k)v+C_ku\},&
 B^2&=2E_0\alpha^2,&R^2&=2E_0D.
\end{align*}
Here $b_r$ is the same formula at $j=r$, so $|b_r|=\rho$.
The constants can be replaced by common deterministic upper bounds
when (3) is a random event.

\begin{theorem}[Average raw-feature transfer at finite width]
There are actual raw-feature predictors $g_A\in\mathcal H(K)$ for
all sets $A\subseteq[r]$ of size $2k$, such that
\[
 \frac1M\sum_{|A|=2k}\|g_A-\chi_{[r]\setminus A}\|_2^2
       \le\frac rM+\Delta^2,
 \qquad
 \frac1M\sum_{|A|=2k}\|g_A\|_{\mathcal H(K)}^2\le B^2.
 \tag{4}
\]
The kernel radius is at most $R^2$. For fixed $k,s$, the scales are
\[
 \Delta=O_k(r^{2k+1}\sqrt{\epsilon+h/s}),\quad
 B^2=O_k(r^{2k+1}),\quad R^2=O(r^2).
 \tag{5}
\]
These statements are deterministic and hold at every finite width.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We recall precisely the geometric input from the companion
\emph{Polynomial-data feature transfer for cold Gaussian ReLU Bayes}.
Write $U_i=(b_i,w_i)$, $R_i=\|U_i\|$, and let $z_i$ be the vector of
coordinate signs of $w_i$, with a common fixed choice at zero.
Project $U_i$ to the zero-bias critical direction
$U_i'=R_i(0,z_i/\sqrt r)$. The sharp minimum-coordinate parity
inequality and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz give an $L^2(X)$ change at most
$v$ in the source network, and also after multiplying the readouts by
any signs. The source itself is within $u$ of $s\chi$.
For critical ridges the even Walsh coefficients are
$b_{|S|}\chi_S(z_i)$. Modulation by $\chi_A(z_i)$ therefore transports
even coefficients by an operator of norm at most $C_k$.

In particular the raw reweighted function
\[
 q_A(x)=\frac{\alpha}{N}\sum_i a_i\chi_A(z_i)
                                      \relu(U_i\cdot(1,x))
\]
can be written
\[
 q_A=\chi_{[r]\setminus A}+\ell_A+e_A,
 \qquad \|e_A\|_2\le\Delta,
 \tag{6}
\]
where the entire odd part of the projected critical network is linear:
\[
 \ell_A(x)=\frac{\alpha}{2N\sqrt r}
                         \sum_i a_iR_i\chi_A(z_i)z_i\cdot x.
\]
To check (6), the raw projection error contributes $|\alpha|v$.
The even part of the projected function differs from its target by at
most $|\alpha|C_k(u+v)$. No assertion that the original odd part is
linear is needed; its nonlinear projection error is included in $e_A$.

Take $\mathcal C=\R^N$, synthesis features
$\relu(U_i\cdot(1,x))/\sqrt N$, and coefficient columns
$C_{i,A}=\alpha a_i\chi_A(z_i)/\sqrt N$.
Their squared norms are at most $B^2$. The targets are orthonormal,
and the map with columns $\ell_A$ has rank at most $r$.
Apply (2). The coefficient norms bound the RKHS norms, even if the
feature synthesis map is not injective. Finally the raw feature radius
is at most $D N^{-1}\sum_i\|U_i\|^2\le R^2$.
The scales in (5) follow from $\rho=\Theta(r^{-1})$ and
$|b_{r-2k}|=\Theta_k(r^{-k-1})$.
\end{proof}

For a uniformly random nearby task, chosen independently of this fixed
representation, (4) gives for any $a,L>0$ an approximation-and-norm
failure probability at most
\[
                   \frac{r/M+\Delta^2}{a^2}+\frac{B^2}{L^2}.
 \tag{7}
\]
This is a guarantee for most tasks, not for all tasks. It is compatible
with an optimal-energy source representation having order-one error on
an explicitly chosen nearby task.

\section{Fixed tasks under a permutation-invariant posterior}
There is a useful distinction between averaging the task and averaging
the representation. Suppose a random pretrained parameter vector has a
law invariant under coordinate permutations, and let $\mathcal G$ be an
invariant event on which (3) holds with common constants. The construction
above is equivariant: permutations carry the family of deleted sets into
itself and permute the rows of the linear-contamination matrix. Its
orthogonal kernel projection is consequently equivariant as well.
The explicit coefficient norms and approximation errors are therefore
identically distributed over all $M$ tasks, conditional on $\mathcal G$.

\begin{corollary}[A fixed raw-feature task]
For every specified set $A$ of size $2k$, with probability at least
\[
 1-\Pr(\mathcal G^c)-\frac{r/M+\Delta^2}{a^2}-\frac{B^2}{L^2},
 \tag{8}
\]
the raw kernel contains a predictor within $a$ of
$\chi_{[r]\setminus A}$ in $L^2(X)$ and of RKHS norm at most $L$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Average the two pointwise bounds in (4) conditional on $\mathcal G$.
Equivariance makes each individual task's expectations equal to those
averages. Apply Markov's inequality twice and add the probability of
$\mathcal G^c$. If that event has probability zero, the assertion is
vacuous and no conditioning is required.
\end{proof}

The relevant Bayesian law has exactly this invariance. Draw uniform IID
inputs labeled by full parity, use an IID isotropic Gaussian parameter
prior, and condition on the observed margins only. A coordinate
permutation preserves the source labels, the sample law, the prior, and
the posterior normalization for the permuted sample. Thus the joint law
of the sample and a posterior draw is invariant. This argument uses the
joint random-sample law; a particular fixed sample need not be invariant.
The same observation applies to the exponential-power priors and the
narrower scalar hidden-bias priors in the power-prior companion.

\section{Polynomial-data raw transfer for a cold actual posterior}
The finite-posterior theorem of the Gaussian transfer companion supplies
(3), with $h\le s\epsilon$, on a joint event of probability at least
$1-\zeta_0-\beta^N$. Its sufficient width and pretraining size have orders
$N_0=O_s(r^3\epsilon^{-2})$ and
$m=O_s(r^3\epsilon^{-2}\log(2/\zeta_0))$, with an explicitly specified
inverse-polynomial upper ceiling on the prior standard deviation.
The good event may be taken to be (3) itself, which is invariant.
No conditioning on the entire cube is added.

\begin{theorem}[Finite Gaussian posterior, raw features, fixed nearby task]
Fix $k\ge1$, $s>0$, accuracy $0<\delta<1$, and confidence
$0<\zeta<1/4$. Assume
\[
       \frac r{\binom r{2k}}\le\frac{\zeta\delta^2}{128}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Choose $\epsilon=c_{k,s}\zeta\delta^2r^{-4k-2}$ with a sufficiently
small positive constant. At every width above the companion's finite
threshold, and at its stated cold Gaussian prior scale, draw one actual
posterior representation from uniform IID source data. Freeze its raw
features and minimize empirical hinge loss over the raw-feature RKHS
ball of squared radius $8B^2/\zeta$, where
$B^2=2(1+\epsilon)(s/\rho)\alpha^2$ is the deterministic energy-ceiling bound.
For every fixed target obtained by deleting $2k$ source coordinates,
polynomially many independent uniform target examples give test
classification error at most $\delta$, with probability at least
$1-\zeta$, for sufficient orders
\[
 \begin{split}
 N_0&=O_{k,s}(r^{8k+7}\delta^{-4}\zeta^{-2}),\\
 m&=O_{k,s}(r^{8k+7}\delta^{-4}\zeta^{-2}\log(1/\zeta)),\\
 n&=O_{k,s}(r^{2k+3}\delta^{-2}\zeta^{-1}\log(1/\zeta)).
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{10}
\]
Exact posterior sampling is assumed. The downstream procedure uses no
even feature transformation, input skip, or new hidden units.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $\zeta_0\le\zeta/8$ and choose $\beta$ so that
$\beta^N\le\zeta/8$. Make the constant in $\epsilon$ small enough
that $\Delta^2\le\zeta\delta^2/128$.
In (8) use $a=\delta/2$ and $L^2=8B^2/\zeta$.
By (9), the error-probability term is at most $\zeta/16$ and the
norm term is at most $\zeta/8$. Together with pretraining failure this
is less than $\zeta/2$.

The raw-feature hinge class has radius product $LR$ and loss bounded
by $1+LR$. Its comparator has population hinge loss at most $a$, since
$\E(1-\chi_Tg)_+\le\|g-\chi_T\|_2$.
The standard uniform Rademacher hinge bound, applied to the empirical
minimizer and comparator, adds at most
\[
 \frac{8LR}{\sqrt n}
       +2(1+LR)\sqrt{\frac{\log(4/\zeta)}{2n}}.
\]
Choose $n$ to make this at most $\delta/2$, with downstream failure
at most $\zeta/2$. Equations (5) give its order in (10); substituting
$\epsilon$ into the finite evidence and source-generalization thresholds
gives the other two orders.
\end{proof}

For fixed $k$, condition (9) is satisfied when
$r^{2k-1}\ge C_k/(\zeta\delta^2)$. It is material: this proof does
not give arbitrary confidence and accuracy at a fixed small dimension.
It gives a finite-width, polynomial-data theorem for each fixed nearby
task as dimension grows, without taking a width-first limit.
A union over all $M$ tasks would lose the gain in (9), and is not claimed.

The same rank argument applies to the power-cost projection in
\emph{Polynomial-data parity transfer under Gaussian and lighter tails}.
For every fixed $p\ge2$, replacing its deterministic error by
$O_{p,k,s}(r^{2k+1}(\epsilon+h/s)^{1/p})$ gives raw transfer with
$\epsilon=c_{p,k,s}\zeta^{p/2}\delta^p r^{-p(2k+1)}$ and
$N_0,m$ of order $r^{4pk+2p+3}\delta^{-2p}\zeta^{-p}$, with the
same logarithmic source-confidence factor. The downstream order in (10)
is unchanged. For its narrower continuous hidden-bias prior, the
Gaussian-order pretraining exponents in (10) apply for every fixed $p$.
The coordinate-permutation argument is unchanged in both cases.

\section{The average-error order is sharp for $k\ge2$}
Fix $k\ge2$, let $j=r-2k\ge2$, and choose a set $S$ of size $j+1$.
It is odd and at most $r-3$. Use all critical orientations
\[
                 Z=\{z\in\{-1,1\}^r:\chi_S(z)=1\}.
\]
There is one orientation from each antipodal pair. Give each incoming
vector $\sqrt{s/\rho}(0,z/\sqrt r)$ and each readout
$\sqrt{s/\rho}\operatorname{sgn}(b_r)\chi(z)$.
The resulting source network is exactly $s\chi$, at energy $s/\rho$.
Indeed the uniform law on $Z$ has density $1+\chi_S$ relative to the
cube. Its even character moments agree with the uniform law, and its
source linear moments vanish because $S$ is not a set of size $r-1$.

For every $l\in S$, the nearby target $T=S\setminus\{l\}$ satisfies
$\chi_T(z)=z_l$ on $Z$. Thus the functional
\[
             f\longmapsto\langle f,\chi_T-2\sqrt r\,b_j x_l\rangle
\]
annihilates every raw feature. Its squared norm is $1+4r b_j^2$.
Cauchy--Schwarz gives minimum squared approximation error at least
$1/(1+4r b_j^2)$. Equality holds: Fourier modulation produces
$\chi_T+x_l/(2\sqrt r b_j)$, whose best scalar multiple attains
that error. These are $j+1$ distinct nearby targets. Therefore
\[
 \frac1M\sum_{|A|=2k}\inf_{g\in\operatorname{span}K}
          \|g-\chi_{[r]\setminus A}\|_2^2
       \ge\frac{r-2k+1}{M(1+4r b_j^2)}.
 \tag{11}
\]
For fixed $k\ge2$, this is asymptotic to $r/M$, matching (4) when
$\Delta=0$. The construction uses exponentially many orientations;
it establishes sharpness of the unrestricted finite-width deterministic
statement. It is not a posterior-probability lower bound, nor does it
show that the rate is sharp for $k=1$.

\section*{Scope and provenance}
The critical-ridge geometry comes from the shared repository's
parity-energy work. The quantitative projection, Fourier modulation,
and finite-posterior evidence bounds are proved in this collection's
Gaussian and power-prior transfer companions. The present addition is
the low-rank cancellation and its fixed-task invariant-law consequence.
The accompanying Lean file constructs the finite orthogonal kernel
projection and proves the existential coefficient, error, and norm
conclusion of the finite matrix lemma. The worst-case raw obstruction, the earlier width-first typical-sector
result, and the finite-data statement here have different quantifiers.
All are compatible. Numerical checks and selected formal algebraic
lemmas document their stated finite scope; the full posterior theorem
is a conventional proof, not an independently reviewed or fully
formalized result. No adversarial-sequence mistake theorem follows from
these $L^2$ approximation estimates.
