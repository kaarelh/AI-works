This note strengthens the repository's observed-margin, shallow mean-field
tanh argument. It does not change its prior or likelihood. The intended
new points are a polynomial sufficient width, a bound uniform over all
representational widths by combining two regimes, and a ReLU extension
under faster-than-Gaussian parameter tails. No assertion of literature
novelty is made here. All constants below are deliberately conservative.

\section{A finite-domain theorem}
Let $X$ be a finite set, $M=|X|$, $f:X\to\{-1,1\}$, and
$\Phi:\Theta\to\R^X$ a measurable neuron output vector. Under a probability
law $P_0$, assume its cumulant $\Lambda(\xi)=\log\E_0e^{\xi\cdot\Phi}$ is
finite on all of $\R^X$, and the closed convex hull of the support of
$\Phi$ is $\R^X$. We assume $\E_0\Phi=0$. Let $I=\Lambda^*$.
For a history $S\subseteq X$, set
\[
 C_S=\{z:f(x)z_x\ge m\ (x\in S)\},\qquad m>0.
\]
The learner averages $N$ independent neurons and conditions on the
\emph{strict} observed margins $f(x)F_N(x)>m$. No condition is imposed
at unobserved inputs.

We use the following standard finite-dimensional convex facts, also
justifiable directly for the two priors below. The rate $I$ is finite,
strictly convex, smooth, and coercive; every prescribed mean has a unique
exponential tilt; each $C_S$ has a unique rate minimizer $z_S$; and its
tilt $Q_S$ is the entropy projection. One sufficient route is to note
that full convex support and an everywhere finite cumulant make
$\Lambda(\xi)-\xi\cdot z$ coercive for every $z$: for any radius $R$,
finitely many support neighborhoods have convex hull containing the
ball of radius $R$, and their positive probabilities give
$\Lambda(\xi)\ge R\|\xi\|-c_R$. Positive definite covariance and the
inverse function theorem then give the assertions. Coercivity of $I$
follows by restricting its defining supremum to a fixed dual sphere.

Assume there is a teacher law $Q_T$ satisfying
\[
 \KL(Q_T\|P_0)\le K,\qquad
 f(x)\E_T\Phi_x\ge m+\gamma\quad(x\in X),\qquad\gamma>0.
\]
Assume also, for some $1<q\le2$, $B>0$, and $\Psi\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 \KL(Q\|P_0)\le K+1&\implies \E_Q|\Phi_x|^q\le B,\label{eq:moment}\\
 \E_{Q_S}e^{|\Phi_x|/\Psi}&\le2,\qquad
 \E_{Q_T}e^{|\Phi_x|/\Psi}\le2.\label{eq:psi}
\end{align}
These hold uniformly over $x$ and $S$. We may decrease $\gamma$ to
$\min(\gamma,1)$, and do so henceforth. This ensures the small-deviation
range used below.

Fix a test margin $0\le t<m$. Put
\[
 h=\frac{m+t}{2},\quad\Delta=\frac{m-t}{2},\quad
 \kappa=\min\left\{1,
 \frac{\Delta^{q/(q-1)}}{2(2B)^{1/(q-1)}}\right\},\qquad
 \beta=\frac{\kappa}{16(K+1)}.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Explicit width]
If
\begin{align}
 N&\ge\frac{64\Psi^2}{\beta^2\gamma^2}
          \log\frac M\beta,\label{eq:width1}\\
 N&\ge\frac{16\log2}{\kappa},\label{eq:width2}
\end{align}
then at every history and every query with $f(x)(z_S)_x>h$,
\[
 \Pp\{f(x)F_N(x)\le t\mid f(y)F_N(y)>m\ (y\in S)\}
 \le e^{-N\kappa/2}.
\]
Along every adaptive input order, at most $\lfloor K/\kappa\rfloor$
queries have $f(x)(z_S)_x\le h$. In particular, if
$N\ge(2/\kappa)\log(M/\eta)$, fresh posterior predictions have expected
total failures of the test margin at most $K/\kappa+\eta$, and at most
$\lfloor K/\kappa\rfloor$ failures with probability at least $1-\eta$.
If additionally $e^{-N\kappa/2}<1-\theta$, the total number of queries
whose posterior test-margin success probability is at most $\theta$
is at most $\lfloor K/\kappa\rfloor$ deterministically.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Moment--entropy continuity]
If $1<q\le2$, $\E_P|Z|^q,\E_Q|Z|^q\le B$, then
\[
 |\E_PZ-\E_QZ|\le(2B)^{1/q}
             (2\KL(Q\|P))^{(q-1)/q}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use a common dominating measure, densities $p,q_1$, and
$\nu=p+q_1$, $r=|p-q_1|/(p+q_1)$. H\"older gives a first factor at
most $(2B)^{1/q}$. Its conjugate exponent $q/(q-1)$ is at least two,
and $r\le1$, so the other integral is bounded by
\[
 \int\frac{(p-q_1)^2}{p+q_1}
 \le2\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt {q_1})^2\le2\KL(Q\|P).
\]
The last inequality follows, for example, from Jensen applied to
$-\log\sqrt{p/q_1}$ and $-\log x\ge1-x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the theorem]
Write $I_S=I(z_S)=\KL(Q_S\|P_0)\le K$. For any law $Q$ with mean in
$C_S$, the exponential-family identity and first-order optimality imply
\[
 \KL(Q\|P_0)-I_S\ge\KL(Q\|Q_S).\tag{P}
\]
If $f(x)(z_S)_x\le h$, adding the observed margin moves this mean
coordinate by at least $\Delta$. Both successive projections have
entropy at most $K$, so (P) and the lemma charge at least $\kappa$ to
the entropy potential. The potential starts at zero and never exceeds
$K$, proving the bound on bad-mean queries.

For a good-mean query let
$C^-=C_S\cap\{z:f(x)z_x\le t\}$, and let $I^- =\min_{C^-}I$.
If $I^->K+1$, then $I^--I_S\ge1\ge\kappa$. Otherwise its minimizing
tilt and $Q_S$ both satisfy \eqref{eq:moment}; their indicated means
differ by more than $\Delta$. Again (P) gives
$I^-\ge I_S+\kappa$. At an unobserved query the inequalities defining
$C^-$ are strictly feasible, because $I$ is finite everywhere. Convex
duality for this polyhedron gives nonnegative constraint multipliers
with dual value $I^-$. Markov's inequality with those multipliers yields
the exact, finite-$N$ Chernoff estimate
\[
 P_0^N\{F_N\in C^-\}\le e^{-NI^-}
                        \le e^{-N(I_S+\kappa)}.\tag{U}
\]
There is no dimension-dependent prefactor. Equivalently, minimizing
$I$ over the polyhedron and maximizing the affine-constraint Chernoff
dual have the same value by Slater's condition.

For the denominator use $Q_\beta=(1-\beta)Q_S+\beta Q_T$. Its training
means have slack at least $\beta\gamma$, its entropy is at most
$I_S+\beta K$, and \eqref{eq:psi} is preserved under mixing. For a
random variable with $\E e^{|Z|/\Psi}\le2$,
$\E|Z-\E Z|^k\le2^{k+1}\Psi^k k!$. Summing the centered moment
series gives
\[
 \log\E e^{s(Z-\E Z)}\le16\Psi^2s^2
 \quad (|s|\le1/(4\Psi)).
\]
Thus a downward deviation $u\le8\Psi$ of an $N$-sample mean has
probability at most $\exp[-Nu^2/(64\Psi^2)]$. Union over at most $M$
training coordinates and \eqref{eq:width1} give $Q_\beta^N(H)\ge1-\beta$,
where $H$ is the strict training event. In particular $P_0^N(H)>0$.
Binary relative entropy, with $p=P_0^N(H)$ and $v=Q_\beta^N(H)$, gives
\[
 N\KL(Q_\beta\|P_0)\ge v\log(1/p)-\log2.
\]
Consequently
\begin{align*}
 -N^{-1}\log p-I_S
 &\le\frac{\beta(K+I_S)+\log2/N}{1-\beta}\\
 &\le\frac{\kappa/8+\kappa/16}{15/16}
 =\kappa/5<\kappa/2.
\end{align*}
Combining with (U) proves the conditional bound, even with a better
exponent than the stated one. The argument holds for every history
separately with the same constants, so no union over histories is
required. There are at most $M$ distinct queries; repeated observed
inputs have zero failure probability at test margin $t<m$.
Conditional expectations and the union bound apply to fresh posterior
draws under arbitrary adaptive input selection.
\end{proof}

\section{The dual multipliers are uniformly bounded}
The history tilt has the form
\[
 \frac{dQ_S}{dP_0}=
 \exp\left(\sum_{x\in S}\lambda_x f(x)\Phi_x-\Lambda(\xi_S)\right),
 \qquad \lambda_x\ge0,
\]
with complementary slackness. Gibbs' inequality with $Q_T$ gives
\[
 K\ge\E_T\log(dQ_S/dP_0)
 \ge I_S+\gamma\sum_x\lambda_x.
\]
Hence $\|\lambda\|_1\le K/\gamma$, without a factor $|S|$ or $M$.
This is the key to a polynomial uniform bound in \eqref{eq:psi}.

\section{Gaussian tanh, with the original normalization}
Take $D=d+1$, $\bar x=(1,x)$, $u\sim N(0,\tau^2I_D/d)$,
$a\sim N(0,\tau^2)$ independently, and $\Phi_x=a\tanh(u\cdot\bar x)$.
Assume a width-$C$ teacher $F_T=\sum_i v_i\tanh(u_i\cdot\bar x)$ has
signed margin greater than $1/2$, and every parameter is bounded by
$B_0>0$. The repository's Gaussian-mixture certificate uses
\[
 s=\min\{\tau/\sqrt d,1/(8CB_0\sqrt D)\},\quad q_0=ds^2/\tau^2,
\]
and has
\[
 K=\frac{dDB_0^2+4C^2B_0^2}{2\tau^2}
       +\frac D2(q_0-1-\log q_0),\qquad\gamma=1/4.
\]
Its mixture components have $u\sim N(u_i,s^2I_D)$ and
$a\sim N(2Cv_i,\tau^2)$ independently. Lipschitz continuity of tanh
makes its mean uniformly within $1/4$ of $2F_T$, hence the slack.

The Gaussian entropy inequality gives \eqref{eq:moment} with $q=2$ and
\[
 B=4\tau^2(K+1+\tfrac12\log2).
\]
Under $Q_S$, conditional on $u$, the readout is Gaussian with variance
$\tau^2$ and mean
\[
 \tau^2\sum_{x\in S}\lambda_x f(x)\tanh(u\cdot\bar x),
\]
whose magnitude is
at most $\tau^2K/\gamma$. The teacher's conditional readout mean, when
the mixture index is retained, has magnitude at most $2CB_0$. Put
\[
 A_0=\max\{\tau^2K/\gamma,2CB_0\},\qquad
 \Psi=4(1+A_0+\tau).
\]
For $a=\mu+\tau G$, $|\mu|\le A_0$, Young's inequality gives
\[
 \E e^{|a|/\Psi}
 \le e^{A_0/\Psi+\tau^2/\Psi^2}\E e^{G^2/4}
 \le e^{1/4+1/16}\sqrt2<2.
\]
As $|\Phi_x|\le|a|$, \eqref{eq:psi} follows. The tanh ridge vectors span the finite input set: choose a linear score
with distinct input values, a nonzero constant feature, and tanh thresholds
between consecutive scores. As their common gain grows, their evaluation
matrix tends to an invertible triangular step matrix. It is therefore
invertible at some finite gain. Gaussian incoming support contains
neighborhoods of those vectors, and arbitrarily large readouts of either
sign make the closed convex output support all of $\R^X$.
All hypotheses therefore
hold with polynomial constants. At fixed $B_0,\tau$, write
$K=O(d^2+C^2)$. With fixed margins and confidence levels the displayed
sufficient width is $O((1+K)^6[d+\log(2+K)+\log(1/\eta)])$, and the
mistake bound is $O(K^2)$. The first width exponent can be improved to
five using the following refined Bernstein estimate.

For either $Q_S,Q_T$, or their mixture, $\E a^2\le B_1=4\tau^2(K+\tfrac12\log2)$ and $a$ is a mixture of
$N(\mu,\tau^2)$ with $|\mu|\le A_0$. Set $L=\max(A_0,\tau)$.
For $k\ge2$, the Gaussian moment bound $\E|G|^k\le k!$ gives
\[
 \E|\Phi_x-\E\Phi_x|^k
 \le 8k!B_1(4L)^{k-2}.
\]
Therefore one may replace \eqref{eq:width1} by
\[
 N\ge\frac{32B_1+8L\beta\gamma}{\beta^2\gamma^2}
             \log\frac M\beta.
\]
Indeed the moment condition gives Bernstein variance proxy $16B_1$
and scale $4L$. This yields $O((1+K)^5[d+\log(2+K)])$ at fixed
$B_0,\tau$, in addition to the confidence/sampling width conditions.

\section{An elementary bound for the remaining widths}
This section is independent of entropy projections. If a prior gives
probability at least $e^{-J_N}$ to networks satisfying the correct
training margin at \emph{all} inputs, then any history has evidence
at least $e^{-J_N}$. Write $q_t$ for the conditional probability that
the next revealed example satisfies its training margin. Each round
adds $-\log q_t$ to minus log evidence, and their total is at most
$J_N$. A sign-error probability $e_t$ is at most $1-q_t$, so
\[
 \sum_t e_t\le\sum_t-\log q_t\le J_N.
\]
This holds pathwise for adaptive histories. Confidence mistakes with
success probability at most $\theta$ number at most
$J_N/(-\log\theta)$, since training success implies test success.
Fresh posterior sampling therefore has expected total sign errors
at most $J_N$. A high-probability bound follows from the usual
exponential supermartingale, for example
\[
 \Pp\{\text{errors}>(e-1)J_N+\log(1/\eta)\}\le\eta.
\]
Here $e$ is Euler's number: conditional on the past,
$\E e^{\mathbf1_{\rm error}}=1+(e-1)e_t\le e^{(e-1)e_t}$.

For the Gaussian tanh prior and every $N\ge C$, use centers
$a_i^*=2Nv_i$ for $i\le C$ and zero for other readouts. Constrain
all readouts within $r=1/8$ of these centers, and the first $C$ incoming
vectors coordinatewise within
$\delta=\min\{1,1/(16CB_0D)\}$ of their teacher vectors. Leave the
remaining incoming weights unrestricted. The output differs from
$2F_T$ by at most $r+2CB_0D\delta\le1/4$, so the full teacher margin
event holds. A valid entropy cost bound for this rectangular event is
\begin{align*}
 J_N={}&N\log^+\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\tau}{2r}
  +\frac{C(2NB_0+r)^2+(N-C)r^2}{2\tau^2}\\
 &+CD\left[\log^+\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\tau}{2\delta\sqrt d}
                 +\frac{d(B_0+\delta)^2}{2\tau^2}\right].
\end{align*}
This follows by lower-bounding the Gaussian density on each interval.
It is polynomial in $N,d,C,B_0,\tau,\tau^{-1}$, up to harmless
logarithms. Taking this bound for $C\le N<N_*$ and the preceding
width-independent bound for $N\ge N_*$ proves a polynomial total
mistake bound uniform over \emph{every} $N\ge C$. For a uniform
high-probability bound, choose $N_*$ using the requested $\eta$;
its polynomial dependence on $\log(1/\eta)$ is retained.

There is a better bound for the intermediate widths than the rectangular
event. Let $\Psi_T=4(1+2CB_0+\tau)$ and
\[
 N_T=\left\lceil\frac{64\Psi_T^2}{\gamma^2}\log(2M)\right\rceil.
\]
The teacher law's own concentration bound shows that for every
$N\ge N_T$, its product law assigns probability at least $1/2$ to
the full strict-margin event. Binary entropy therefore gives
\[
 J_N\le2NK+2\log2\qquad(N\ge N_T).
\]
Use the explicit box only for $C\le N<N_T$, this product-teacher
bound for $N_T\le N<N_*$, and the width-independent bound above $N_*$.
At fixed $B_0,\tau$ and fixed confidence/margin thresholds, with
$S=1+d^2+C^2$, the refined Bernstein estimate has
$N_*=O(S^5[d+\log S])$, while $N_T=O((1+C^2)d)$. Consequently
\begin{corollary}[A uniform bound over all representational widths]
For the unchanged observed-margin Gaussian tanh learner, every width
$N\ge C$ has total expected sampling mistakes and total fixed-confidence
mistakes bounded by
\[
 O_{B_0,\tau,\theta}\bigl(S^6[d+\log S]\bigr),
 \qquad S=1+d^2+C^2.
\]
The sharper $O(S^2)$ mistake bound holds at every
$N\ge O(S^5[d+\log S])$. High-probability versions retain polynomial
dependence on $\log(1/\eta)$.
\end{corollary}
The uniform polynomial is deliberately loose; its purpose is to remove
width as an uncontrolled asymptotic qualification. It does not give an
efficient algorithm for sampling the Bayesian posterior.

\section{ReLU with quartic and more general parameter tails}
Let all $D+1$ neuron coordinates independently have density
$Z_p^{-1}e^{-|w|^p}$, where fixed $p>2$ and
$Z_p=2\Gamma(1+1/p)<2$. Take $\Phi_x=a(u\cdot\bar x)_+$ and write
$E_\theta=|a|^p+\sum_i|u_i|^p$, $q_0=p/2$. H\"older and AM--GM give
\[
 |\Phi_x|^{q_0}\le\tfrac12D^{(p-1)/2}E_\theta,
 \qquad \E_0e^{E_\theta/2}=2^{(D+1)/p}.
\]
Consequently every entropy-$K+1$ law has
$q_0$-moment at most
$B_0'=D^{(p-1)/2}[K+1+(D+1)\log2/p]$. Use $q=\min(q_0,2)$ and
$B=(B_0')^{q/q_0}$. All linear output exponential moments exist,
and the ReLU ridge vectors span the finite cube (thresholds in a
strictly ordered linear score produce an invertible hinge matrix).
Arbitrarily large positive and negative readouts make the closed convex
support all of $\R^X$.

For a bounded teacher, use the uniform mixture over its $C$ neurons,
incoming coordinates uniform on $u_i+[-s,s]^D$, and readout uniform
on $2Cv_i+[-1,1]$, independently within each component, with
\[
 s=\min\{1,1/(16CB_0D)\}.
\]
Its mean differs from $2F_T$ by at most $1/8$, so take $\gamma=3/8$.
The explicit certificate
\[
 K=2^{p-1}\{D(B_0^p+s^p)+(2CB_0)^p+1\}+D\log(1/s)
\]
follows from $(x+y)^p\le2^{p-1}(x^p+y^p)$, the uniform densities,
and $Z_p<2$. Its support also gives the deterministic output bound
$T=(2CB_0+1)D(B_0+s)$.

Put $\alpha=2/p<1$ and $C_D=D^{1-1/p}/2^{2/p}$. Then
$|\Phi_x|\le C_D E_\theta^\alpha$. For every $b\ge0$,
\[
 b C_D E^\alpha\le E/2+c_p(bC_D)^{1/(1-\alpha)},\qquad
 c_p=(1-\alpha)(2\alpha)^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}.
\]
This is obtained by maximizing the left side minus $E/2$ over $E\ge0$.
Prior readout symmetry gives $\Lambda(\xi_S)\ge0$. The multiplier
bound therefore implies
\[
 \log\E_{Q_S}e^{|\Phi_x|}
 \le H:=\frac{D+1}{p}\log2+
 c_p\left[C_D(K/\gamma+1)\right]^{p/(p-2)}.
\]
By Jensen, \eqref{eq:psi} holds with
$\Psi=\max\{1,H/\log2,T/\log2\}$, also for $Q_T$. For every fixed
$p>2$ the theorem thus has polynomial width and mistake bounds in
$d,C,B_0$, and the prescribed confidence parameters. The clearest
example is $p=4$, for which $q=2$, $c_p=1/2$, and all powers are integers
apart from harmless dimension factors.

The all-width extension follows as before from a product box around
a padded teacher. One valid formula is obtained with
$\delta=[16D((2C+1)B_0+1)]^{-1}$, centers $a_i=2Nv_i,u_i=u_i^T$
for $i\le C$, and zero centers otherwise, constraining every coordinate
within $\delta$. The output error from $2F_T$ is at most
$\delta D[(2C+1)B_0+\delta]\le1/16$. Since $Z_p<2$, its negative
log probability is bounded by
\[
 N(D+1)\log(1/\delta)+C(2NB_0+\delta)^p
 +CD(B_0+\delta)^p+(N-C)(D+1)\delta^p.
\]
This makes the all-width polynomial claim explicit. As in the tanh case,
the product teacher law gives a better $O(NK)$ evidence bound once its
bounded-output concentration threshold is reached. No sharp polynomial
degree is claimed for this ReLU extension.
