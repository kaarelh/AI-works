\begin{abstract}
We study the width-first posterior of Gaussian neural networks with
hyperbolic-tangent hidden activations, conditioned on a fixed output
margin at every Boolean input and on noiseless training labels.
For every fixed subcritical width exponent $0<\gamma<1$, we prove an
$\exp\{c k\log(ed/k)\}$ eventual sample-complexity lower bound for
parity on $k$ coordinates when $k$ lies in a fixed polynomial sparsity
window and the depth grows as $L(d)=\log_2\log\log d+O(1)$.
The proof gives failure at explicitly specified sample sizes, for the
actual posterior and the criterion that the correct label must have
probability greater than $2/3$.
The new uniform ingredient is a coefficient estimate for iterated
arcsine kernels, obtained from an explicit B\"ottcher coordinate and a
uniform contour argument. It permits a depth-independent interpolation
budget and a linear parity obstruction on logarithmic active faces.
The theorem does not resolve the corresponding natural-scale lower
bound at a fixed depth.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and statement}
Let $\mathcal X=\{-1,1\}^d$ carry uniform probability measure.
Fix $U\subseteq[d]$ with $|U|=k$ and
$f(x)=\chi_U(x)=\prod_{j\in U}x_j$.
The learner is not given $U$. For depth $L=L(d)\ge2$ and hidden width
$N$, define
\[
 h^0(x)=(1,x),\qquad
 h^s(x)=\tanh(W^s h^{s-1}(x))\quad(1\le s<L),\qquad
 z_N(x)=W^L h^{L-1}(x).
\]
There are no additional biases. All weights are independent centered
Gaussians, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
 \Var W^1_{ij}=N^{1-\gamma}/d,\qquad
 \Var W^s_{ij}=N^{-\gamma}\quad(1<s<L),\qquad
 \Var W^L_i=N^{-\gamma-1}.
\end{equation}
Fix $a>0$ and $0<\gamma<1$. Condition the prior on
$|z_N(x)|>a$ for every $x\in\mathcal X$ and then on
$f(X_i)z_N(X_i)>0$ for independent uniform training inputs
$S=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$. Write
\begin{align*}
 q_{N,S}(x)&=\Pp\{f(x)z_N(x)>0\mid
        |z_N(y)|>a\ \forall y,\ f(X_i)z_N(X_i)>0\ \forall i\},\\
 F_N(S)&=\Pp_x\{q_{N,S}(x)\le2/3\}.
\end{align*}
The width limit is always taken first: $d,k,L,a,\gamma,S$ are fixed
while $N\to\infty$.

Put $D=d+1$, $\beta=2^{-(L-1)}$, $p=2-\beta$, and
$D_r=\sum_{j=0}^r\binom dj$. All logarithms other than $\log_2$ are
natural.

\begin{theorem}[Natural scale at slowly growing depth]\label{thm:main}
Fix $a>0$, $0<\eta_1<1-\eta_2<1$, and $0<b_-\le b_+<\infty$.
There are constants $\alpha,A,c,C>0$ and $d_0$ such that the following
holds. For $d\ge d_0$, suppose
\[
 d^{\eta_1}\le k\le d^{1-\eta_2},\qquad
 b_-\le\beta\log\log d\le b_+,
 \qquad \beta=2^{-(L(d)-1)}.
\]
Choose the largest odd integer $r\le\alpha k$, put
$\rho=r/(d+1)$, $H=\log(e/\rho)$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-size}
 n_r=\left\lceil A D_r rH\rho^{-1/400}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Then
\[
 c k\log(ed/k)\le\log n_r\le C k\log(ed/k),
\]
and for every target support $U$ of size $k$ and every fixed
$0<\gamma<1$,
\[
 \Pp_S\left\{\liminf_{N\to\infty}F_N(S)\ge\frac1{16}\right\}
 \ge\frac34.
\]
The conditioning events have positive probability for all sufficiently
large widths at every fixed dimension and sample.
\end{theorem}

The depth condition is equivalent to
$L(d)=\log_2\log\log d+O(1)$ with fixed bounds on the additive term.
The result concerns the particular sample sizes \eqref{eq:exact-size}.
It does not assert failure at every smaller sample size, and no
monotonicity of posterior risk is used. It implies the stated lower
bound for an eventual sample threshold: a guarantee that holds at
\emph{every} $n\ge m$ with sample success probability greater than
$1/4$ and width-limit confidence failure below $1/16$ must have
$m>n_r$. Width is taken first at each $d$ and $L(d)$; no minimum width
or interchange of limits is asserted.

\subsection{Variational formulation and proof organization}
Let $\Psi(t)=2\arcsin(t)/\pi$ and
\[
 K(x,y)=\Psi^{\circ(L-1)}\!\left(\frac{1+x\cdot y}{d+1}\right).
\]
All $L^p$ norms and Walsh coefficients below use the uniform cube
probability measure. Let $\lambda_j$ be the degree-$j$ eigenvalue of
the normalized covariance operator $2^{-d}K$. The corresponding energy
is
\[
 \|z\|_K^2=z^{\mathsf T}K^{-1}z
      =\sum_{V\subseteq[d]}\frac{\widehat z(V)^2}{\lambda_{|V|}}.
\]
Write $Q_r=K_{>r}$ and
$\tau_r=Q_r(x,x)=\sum_{j>r}\binom dj\lambda_j$.
The width-first posterior selects compatible sign functions minimizing
$\|z\|_K^2/2$ under
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obstacle}
 |z(x)|\ge a\quad(x\in\mathcal X),\qquad
 f(X_i)z(X_i)\ge a\quad(i\le n).
\end{equation}
We prove that every minimizer has test error at least $3/8$. The final
confidence conclusion is therefore independent of the posterior weights
among tied minimizers.

The main additional ingredient beyond a norm bound is transport of the
all-input margin. A minimizing field is split into a low-degree head,
a middle band, and a sufficiently high-degree tail. Near a sampled
nuisance input, the first two parts reduce to a frozen head and a common
scalar offset. A recurrence argument and angular concentration force
the radial tail profile to return to the head's sign. A center with an
incorrect head sign consequently produces a quantitative kernel-energy
witness. These witnesses control bounded, normalized wrong-side moments
on random active faces. A scalar $L^p$ Bernstein inequality and parity
influence then force the restricted head degree to be too large.

\section{Scope}
The theorem concerns the globally conditioned neural posterior itself,
including arbitrary mixtures among tied variational minimizers. It is
not a statement about posterior means or kernel ridge prediction.
The natural exponent is proved here with slowly increasing depth.
For any fixed depth, the same natural-scale lower bound remains open.
The lower polynomial bound on $k$ ensures that the relevant Fourier
levels exceed the uniform coefficient threshold $\exp(C/\beta)$ and
that the logarithmic active faces fit within the parity support.
