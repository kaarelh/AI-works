\section{Uniform linear calibration on logarithmic faces}
\label{sec:uniform-calibration}
\begin{lemma}[Linear face degree at small cusp exponent]
\label{lem:uniform-linear-calibration}
Assume the balanced-contact reduction of the preceding sections, with
uniform comparator and witness constants. Suppose $\beta\le1/4$,
$\beta\log m\le B_1$, and $\beta H\to\infty$. An accurate eligible
$m$-face with a polynomial truncation of degree $u\le a_1H$ must satisfy
\[
 u\ge c m,
\]
where $a_1>0$ is sufficiently small and $c>0$ depends only on $B_1$,
the fixed contact quantile, the comparator constant, and the fixed
accuracy advantage. The constants are independent of depth.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $p=2-\beta$ and $p_0=2-2\beta\in[3/2,2)$. In the H\"older step
of Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-contact-calibration}, the required distance
moment is
\[
 q=\frac{\beta p_0}{p-p_0}=p_0.
\]
The balanced contact distance has uniformly bounded $L^{p_0}$ norm
after division by $\sqrt m$. Thus the wrong-side $L^{p_0}$ norm is
bounded by $Cm^{\beta/(2p)}$, apart from the exceptional-set term.
This factor is bounded uniformly when $\beta\log m\le B_1$.

For the exceptional set of mass at most $C\rho^g$, hypercontractivity
gives the absorption factor
\[
 (p_0-1)^{-u/2}(C\rho^g)^{1/p_0-1/2}.
\]
Its logarithm is at most $-g\beta H/2+2\beta u+O(\beta)$ for small
$\beta$. Choosing $a_1\le g/8$ makes it tend to $-\infty$ because
$\beta H\to\infty$. The fixed quantile projection leakage can be
chosen below the accuracy tolerance uniformly, since $p_0\le2$.

The scalar Bernstein inequality used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-contact-calibration} has constants bounded for
$p_0\in[3/2,2]$. This follows directly from its complex-interpolation
proof: the imaginary-power prefactor is bounded on that compact
interval, and the angle derivative remains bounded at two.
Moreover $s_{p_0}=1+O(\beta)$. Consequently
$m^{s_{p_0}-1}\le C_{B_1}$ and, for $u\le m$,
$u^{s_{p_0}}/m\le C_{B_1}u/m$.
The positivity and influence inequalities in the same lemma now imply
$mc_0\le Cu^{s_{p_0}}$ after the fixed leakage is absorbed, with a
fixed $c_0>0$. Therefore $u\ge cm$.
\end{proof}
