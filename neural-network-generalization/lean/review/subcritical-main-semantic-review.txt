Final assembly checks
---------------------
The final common rate is min(cp,min(cn,cz)), hence positive and no greater
than any endpoint rate. A finite intersection of eventual dimension events
is taken before gamma is introduced. The small-sample regime
n<=exp(log(d+1)^3) and the complementary large-sample interval cover every n,
including the cutoff boundary and zero. Denominator positivity is supplied by
the actual finite-network theorem, independently of the sample-size event.

The positive-gamma branch quantifies over every tied global minimum-energy
feasible field. It does not assume a special minimizer or selection rule.
The sharp comparator uses beta=1/2^(depth+1), so its energy scale d^beta is
valid at every fixed depth. The actual squared-loss sampling and Gram events
are proved for the sampled cube inputs. Their intersection has probability
at least 7/8; intersecting the 15/16 comparator or conditioning-cost event
leaves 13/16, which suffices for the stated 3/4.

The adaptive head/tail convention is explicit: the head has degrees <=r and
the tail has degrees >=r+1. The corresponding kernel and trace identities
are proved in LargeSampleEvents rather than assumed. The scalar budget
lemma consumes actual uniform sampling, spectral, comparator, and cutoff
bounds; the final main theorem assumes none of these as unproved certificates.

The negative-gamma branch obtains the stronger Gaussian failure bound 3/8 at
confidence 13/20 and transfers it to finite-network failure at two thirds.
The strict buffer 13/20<2/3 is essential and is present in the proved bridge.
Thus a weak convergence statement is not incorrectly used at a discontinuous
unbuffered confidence threshold. The main conclusion weakens 3/8 to 1/16.

The gamma=0 branch normalizes the conditioning-cost energy budget by b^2.
Its large-d bounds give D/b^2<=C*n*W, with W=d^(2*beta)*r^(-beta), and W*T<=d/r
because 2*beta<=1. The auxiliary amplitude argument uses the same globally
conditioned Gaussian field; the independent auxiliary sample is integrated
out. Its scalar envelope tends to zero and yields amplitude exception <=1/32.
The eventual b^2>=10240 absorbs the remaining trace contributions. The final
Gaussian posterior failure bound also transfers through confidence 13/20,
retaining the strict two-thirds convention of the finite network.
