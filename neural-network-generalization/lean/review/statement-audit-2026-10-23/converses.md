# Statement audit: the two converse papers

Reviewed on 23 October 2026 against public commit `7c69c4abe5f93476d59c95b4d04d13b33ff01301`. This review independently read the manuscript theorem statements, endpoint declarations, and the definitions of the models and quantities in those declarations. Earlier correspondence records were consulted after that inspection. This is a semantic statement review, not a new compilation or a line-by-line review of every proof.

**Finding:** no substantive mismatch was found in either paper's advertised formalized main conclusions. The qualifications below matter when describing their scope. In particular, the circuit Bayesian converse concerns posterior sampling; the neural converse also covers majority prediction, but only under its specified support restriction and order of limits.

## Explicit circuit induction

The manuscript is [Few online mistakes imply a small Boolean circuit for explicit circuit-induction rules](../../../works-in-progress/online-mistakes/circuit-converse/source/paper.tex). Its main statements are the deterministic Occam theorem (source line 109), the geometric circuit-prior theorem (line 223), the exception-code theorem (line 322), and their infinite-IID corollary (line 379).

| Item checked | Formal source and finding |

| Input and circuit semantics | `BatchTwoCircuitConverseCircuits.lean:15–47`: inputs are `Fin d → Bool`; each new NAND gate addresses one of the `d+2+n` earlier wires; the initial wires include both constants. Empty circuits output false, and nonempty circuits output their last gate. Dead gates and repeated addresses are permitted. This is the manuscript's circuit model. |

The representation differences preserve the stated learning theorems. The formal overwrite uses padding to append exactly `3*d+3` gates; the manuscript's construction sometimes uses fewer, but every main conclusion uses the same upper bound. Prefix programs are an abstract syntax equivalent to a base circuit with an exception chain, with exact code lengths and a proved counting/Kraft bound. A concrete bitstring encoder/decoder and its bijection with that syntax are not included. Consequently this is a formalization of the mathematical description prior, not a verified serialization implementation.

The theorem does not provide a Bayesian majority-prediction converse. Its expectation refers to posterior sampling, not to majority errors or just a cumulative log-loss surrogate. The manuscript makes this distinction explicitly, and the formal types retain it.

## Polynomial-depth neural converse

The manuscript is [Polynomial-depth mistake converses for neural priors with controlled internal amplification](../../../works-in-progress/online-mistakes/depth-polynomial-converse/source/paper.tex). The main theorem starts at source line 182; the model and support are specified at lines 75–118.

The Lean depth parameter is `r`, with `H=r+1` hidden layers and total depth `L=r+2`. Under this translation:

- `Network.lean:23–60` encodes Boolean inputs and labels by `Bool`, mapped to real values `-1,+1` by `labelReal`. The appended constant input is placed first rather than last; this is only a coordinate permutation. The actual network has a tanh first layer, `r` tanh internal layers and a linear readout, without extra internal biases.
- `Network.lean:114` defines exactly the manuscript's weighted energy `d/N` times first-layer squared norm, plus internal squared norms, plus `N` times readout squared norm. `Model.lean:32–47` gives the actual independent Gaussian variances `N*sigma^2/d`, `sigma^2`, `sigma^2/N`.
- `BatchTwoNeuralConverseModel.lean:17–31` takes the supremum over **all** internal diagonal slopes in `[0,1]`, with the correct products from each hidden layer to the readout. Its compactness and continuity are proved. `gainSupport` (line 70) has both strict bounds `1/2 < |z(x)| < U` for every cube point and the exact gain inequality `gainSq < Lambda^2*(r+1)*readoutEnergy`.
- `restrictedPrior` and `posterior` (lines 97–105) are actual parameter-space conditioning, first on that support and then on the training signs. `functionPosterior_eq_parameter_posterior` in `BatchTwoNeuralConverseConditioning.lean:96` identifies the finite sign-function posterior with this conditioning. No unrelated prior on minimum-energy functions is silently substituted.

The endpoint `polynomial_depth_mistake_converse` in `BatchTwoNeuralConverseMain.lean:20` is written with the generic condition `SuccessHasMinimizer`. Unfolding that condition says that whenever the error flag is false, at least one current minimum-action function has the correct new label. This condition is **derived**, rather than additionally assumed, for the advertised prediction rules by the separate sampled, majority and confidence theorems in the same file. `IsMinimumMixture` in `BatchTwoNeuralConversePriorLimit.lean:147` requires nonnegative probabilities summing to one and support on consistent minimum-action functions; ties remain unrestricted.

The mistake notions agree with the paper: sampled and deterministic errors are literal wrong labels; majority permits either decision in an exact tie; confidence failure is the correct-label probability **at most** `theta`. Expected confidence bounds require `0 ≤ theta < 1`, covering the manuscript's `0 < theta < 1`. The pathwise confidence theorem is slightly more general. Expected-count theorems derive freshness of errors for each rule and prove the needed integrability. They do not replace expected mistakes by the expectation of their square.

The numerical bound also matches exactly. `singletonSlope`, `firstFeatureBudget`, `singletonBaseBudget`, `singletonReadoutBudget`, and `repairBudget` expand to the manuscript's `A_d`, `F_d`, `D_{d,H}`, `Q_H`, and `P_{d,H,U}`. `mistakeConverseWidth` substitutes `K=1+P*(m+1)^2` into `converseWidth` (`BatchTwoNeuralConverseReconstruction.lean:15`), whose formula is

```text
ceil(65536 * Lambda^2 * K * (1 + (r+1)*K) * (d + log(400*(r+2))))
```

The conclusion is `realizesAtComplexity Real.tanh f` at that actual width. Unfolding `Network.lean:135` produces a same-depth network with all weights bounded by the width and strict signed margin greater than `1/2`. This is stronger than merely asserting that the minimum such width is no larger. The reconstructed network need not satisfy the prior restriction, precisely as in the paper.

The limiting learner is handled separately by `iterated_limit_minimum_mixture` (`BatchTwoNeuralConversePriorLimit.lean:154`). `SmallPriorCluster` first takes positive `sigma` to zero at each fixed width; a subsequent diverging-width subsequence converges to the asserted minimum-action probability mixture. These are hypotheses identifying accumulation points, not assumptions that the posterior already concentrates on minimizers. The proved event-rate and width-convergence results establish that conclusion. The order of limits is not reversed. The main theorem does not claim a converse at every fixed width.

There is a harmless intermediate-domain qualification worth recording. The paper defines infeasible fixed-width energy as `+infinity`; Lean's `fixedSqrtAction` is a real-valued infimum and has the empty-set default when the support is empty. It is therefore **not** the square root of the extended-valued paper quantity at infeasible widths. The formal proof avoids using that identification there: `eventually_functionSupport_nonempty` is proved, fixed-width rate comparisons require nonemptiness, and the final width-limit theorem uses those statements only eventually. The all-width action is also proved nonempty for every Boolean target. This totalization does not change the main conclusion or make its minimizer hypothesis vacuous.

Finally, `BatchTwoNeuralConverseInfinite.lean:14,72,115` uses the actual infinite sum of 0/1 errors, proves errors vanish after coverage and total errors are bounded, and derives the expected bound for every full-support IID input law. The minimum-action support conditions are discharged by the prediction-specific wrappers; no uniform atom-mass bound is needed.

The optional paragraph after the main theorem replacing the gain restriction throughout by strict layerwise operator-norm bounds is not separately certified as another learner. It should continue to be excluded from the public formalization claim. Neither the manuscript nor the formal theorem asserts an unrestricted fixed-width neural converse or a cheap supported implementation of every small deep Boolean circuit.
