# Bayesian neural network generalization

This collection studies generalization under exact Bayesian inference over neural-network weights: how the prior, learner width, target complexity, and network depth affect what the learner can predict.

Papers and notes by ChatGPT-6 Astra, Claude (Fable 5, Opus 5, and Opus 5.5), Kaarel Hänni, and Dmitry Vaintrob. Authors are listed in alphabetical order; each paper names its own authors. [Author-credit review](verification/author-order-2026-11-06.md).

## Shared conventions

Here `d` is input dimension, `N` is learner width, `L` is depth (including the readout), and `gamma` controls how Gaussian weight variances scale with width; `gamma=1` is mean-field scaling.

In main results 1–4, the learner conditions the Gaussian parameter prior on a strict absolute output margin **at every input of the Boolean cube before observing training data**, then conditions on exact noiseless labels. Main result 5 uses the different, explicit robustness-support and training-margin conventions described in its statement. These are information-theoretic results about the exact posterior; efficient posterior computation is not asserted.

At fixed depth, $C_L(f)$ is the bounded-weight network complexity defined in the papers: the smallest eligible width $C$ with weights bounded by $C$. Circuit simulations are proved where used; arbitrary circuit size is not silently identified with fixed-depth network complexity.

For prediction, the success criterion is correct-label posterior probability **strictly greater than $`2/3`$**. The first papers phrase their main bounds in posterior average error; the confidence guarantee follows by rescaling the accuracy parameter by three. The later papers state the confidence convention explicitly.

## Main results

### 1. The strong-prior limit argument

[Paper (PDF)](main-results/strong-prior-generalization/paper.pdf) · [Source and build instructions](main-results/strong-prior-generalization/)

**Width-independent generalization in the strong-prior limit of Bayesian neural networks.** A writeup of the main proof thread in [Dmitry and Kaarel's slides](https://kaarelh.github.io/ai/nnbayes-generalization.pdf), including the compression and circuit arguments. At fixed depth, the sample bound is polynomial in input dimension, target complexity, and the accuracy parameters, independently of learner width $N\ge C_L(f)$.

This paper takes the prior variance to zero at each fixed width. It does not claim a variance threshold uniform over all widths. The [separate appendix audit](side-results/strong-prior-appendix-audit/paper.pdf) records the treatment of each slide appendix and the mistakes and repairs found there.

### 2. $\gamma>1$: every width above target complexity

[Paper (PDF)](main-results/gamma-above-one/paper.pdf) · [Source and build instructions](main-results/gamma-above-one/)

**Width-uniform Bayesian generalization above the mean-field scaling.** For every fixed depth and every fixed $\gamma>1$, proves polynomial sample complexity at the specified nonzero Gaussian variance for **every width $`N\ge C_L(f)`$**. The result allows bounded 1-Lipschitz hidden activations and arbitrary input distributions. The polynomial may depend on depth and $\gamma$; there is no uniform claim as $\gamma$ approaches one.

### 3. $\gamma=1$: every width above target complexity

[Paper (PDF)](main-results/meanfield-all-width/paper.pdf) · [Source and build instructions](main-results/meanfield-all-width/)

**Mean-field Bayesian circuit learning at every width above target complexity.** A standalone finite-width proof for fixed-depth tanh networks, with polynomial sample complexity for **every width $`N\ge C_L(f)`$** and arbitrary input distributions. No infinite-width limit or extra minimum-width hypothesis is needed. The earlier mean-field papers gave only a sufficiently-large-width guarantee.

### 4. $\gamma<1$: an exponential sample-complexity lower bound

[Paper (PDF)](main-results/subcritical-exponential/paper.pdf) · [Source and build instructions](main-results/subcritical-exponential/)

**Exponential parity sample complexity below the mean-field scaling.** For every fixed $\gamma<1$ and fixed depth, proves an $\exp(\Omega(k))$ sample lower bound for full $`k`$-bit parity under uniform IID training inputs. Here the ambient dimension is $d=k$.

More precisely, there are $c>0$ and $k_0$ such that, for every $k\ge k_0$ and each specified integer $0\le n\le e^{ck}$, with probability at least $3/4$ over the training sample, the limiting fraction of test inputs whose correct label has posterior probability at most $2/3$ is at least $1/16$ (in the liminf sense). Width tends to infinity first, with dimension, sample, margin, depth, and $\gamma$ fixed. The paper does not give a quantitative minimum width or a joint-limit theorem. The guarantee is for each specified sample size, not a simultaneous statement about all prefixes of one sample sequence.

This is the completed full-parity exponential theorem. The stronger fixed-depth sparse-parity target $\exp(\Omega(k\log(ed/k)))$ in the positive subcritical regime is still open; the partial results below do not replace that target with a solved claim.

### 5. Robust priors: polynomial dependence on depth

[Paper (PDF)](main-results/robust-polynomial-depth/paper.pdf) · [Source and build instructions](main-results/robust-polynomial-depth/) · [Robustness FAQ](TECHNICAL-FAQ.md#15-what-robustness-condition-gives-polynomial-depth-dependence)

**A polynomial-depth learning bound for NNBayes on robust networks.** Restrict the Gaussian prior to networks whose clipped predictions survive a calibrated ball of simultaneous internal activation and output perturbations. For layered Boolean-circuit targets of width $C$, one universal polynomial bounds sample complexity jointly in input dimension, depth, circuit width, robustness calibration, and statistical parameters, simultaneously at **every width $N\ge C$ and every $`\gamma\ge1`$**.

The learner conditions on signed training output above $1/2$ and controls posterior test-margin error at $1/4$, hence sign error and confidence failure. The main theorem needs no all-input clean-output margin; the manuscript also treats an optional extra conditioning variant, outside the original main-result-5 Lean statement and now covered by the second batch. The comparator is a layered Boolean circuit with constant wires and compilation overhead counted; this is not an unconditional polynomial-depth theorem for every small analog neural circuit.

The Lean circuit model counts the wires used by the implementation, including a bias wire when a NAND gate needs it. Padding does not force an extra constant output, so the proof covers the manuscript's small-width cases, including width-one literal, copy, and negation examples. The [current coverage inventory](lean/review/coverage-extension-2026-10-30.txt) gives this convention and its implementation; the [23 October statement audit](lean/review/statement-audit-2026-10-23/README.md) records the earlier mismatch.

A [supporting paper](works-in-progress/improved-polynomials/original-support-all-scales/paper.pdf) sharpens the sufficient bound to $O(C^6\log^{13}(2+C))$ at fixed remaining parameters under this **same restriction**, for every $\gamma\ge1$ and every $N\ge C$. It also improves the conservative joint polynomial envelope to degree 46. The exponents and the paper's printed numerical allocation are Lean verified; see the [literal-budget correspondence](lean/review/manuscript-literal-target-correspondence-2026-11-15.txt).

The [experiments](works-in-progress/depth-experiments/) include genuine trained-network certificates and counterexamples. They support studying the condition, but do not establish it for arbitrary gradient-descent-trained networks or validate Bayesian posterior sample complexity empirically.

## Conditional obstruction to polynomial circuit learning

[Paper (PDF)](works-in-progress/conditional-nonpolynomial-learning/paper.pdf) · [Source and verification scope](works-in-progress/conditional-nonpolynomial-learning/) · [Closed Lean proof](works-in-progress/conditional-nonpolynomial-learning/lean/)

**Assuming nonuniform P/poly differs from TC¹, unrestricted Gaussian tanh NNbayes has no universal sample bound polynomial jointly in input dimension, teacher width, and teacher depth, independent of learner width.** The result applies separately to every prescribed canonical scale schedule, including each fixed `gamma >= 1`, with noiseless exact-fit training and the original global-margin convention. Accuracy and confidence are already fixed constants. It rules out a universal polynomial guarantee; it does not establish an exponential-depth lower bound.

The paper presents this conditional negative result as its central theorem. A universal polynomial guarantee with the same target class, input-law and width quantifiers would require changing the prior; restricting its support is one possible change. Restrictions on targets or input laws also change the premises.

**The central theorem has a closed Lean proof.** The [entry point](works-in-progress/conditional-nonpolynomial-learning/lean/ClosedConditionalImpossibility.lean) proves the concrete statement with the complexity separation as its only conjectural hypothesis. The proof includes the actual Gaussian posterior ceiling, all three scale regimes, explicit circuit compilation, finite boosting and teacher compilation. The [verification record](works-in-progress/conditional-nonpolynomial-learning/verification/) distinguishes this theorem from stronger supplementary claims and records the clean source builds. The original five results and second batch keep their own numbering.

## Label-noise research

[Exact noisy interpolation in the strong-prior limit](works-in-progress/noisy-interpolation/README.md) is a supporting research note. For distinct uniformly sampled Boolean inputs, it proves a clean-error bound linear in the corrupted fraction, with a resource-dependent coefficient, for the original tanh/Gaussian exact-fit learner at every width of order at least $C+U^2d^2(s+1)$, where $U$ bounds the teacher's output; this threshold grows linearly with the number $s$ of corruptions. The proof covers arbitrary corruptions and posterior mixtures. For bounded-output Boolean-circuit implementations, the noise coefficient is independent of teacher width; polynomial dependence on dimension and depth remains. A noise bound uniform in dimension and target complexity is still open.

This note is not Lean verified and remains separate from the main results. The [label-noise FAQ](TECHNICAL-FAQ.md#33-do-the-good-generalization-results-survive-a-small-amount-of-label-noise) gives the conventions, width requirement, and remaining question.

For arbitrary bounded labels, the [finite-posterior oracle paper](works-in-progress/agnostic-finite-posteriors/) compares ordinary Bayesian tanh predictions with the best small bounded-weight network at every learner width and every `γ≥1`. A [joint Lipschitz-activation proof](works-in-progress/agnostic-lipschitz-oracles/) also covers ReLU, GELU, sigmoid, and softplus throughout `γ≥1`, with a dimensional cost. The [ReLU paper](works-in-progress/agnostic-relu-sharp/) gives matching strict comparator-growth exponents, while a [nonexpansive support restriction](works-in-progress/agnostic-nonexpansive/) gives a much better joint width–depth polynomial against restricted teachers. These use calibrated Gaussian likelihoods rather than exact interpolation. The [temperature obstruction](works-in-progress/agnostic-temperature-obstruction/) explains why tuning alone cannot remove all restrictions. See [FAQ 41](TECHNICAL-FAQ.md#41-can-nnbayes-compete-with-the-best-small-network-without-a-realizable-target) for the assumptions and comparison. These results have conventional proofs and independent AI-agent reviews; they are not Lean verified.

## Recent depth research

**Boolean depth: a lower bound for a natural mean-field prefactor, and barriers for the original prior.** For tanh with variances `t²/d`, `t²/N`, and `t²/N²`, any fixed `t>1` admits an actual finite-width `exp(Ω(depth))` confidence lower bound, even for a constant target. These are the same mean-field width powers with a different fixed variance prefactor. For coefficient-one tanh (`t=1`) and stronger canonical priors, the unconditional general Boolean-depth question remains open. The [conditional impossibility paper](works-in-progress/conditional-nonpolynomial-learning/) rules out a universal polynomial guarantee assuming `P/poly != TC¹`. The [lower-bound paper](works-in-progress/supercritical-depth-lower/) and [parallel-complexity paper](works-in-progress/boolean-depth-barriers/) give the precise distinction. The conditional impossibility theorem has a closed Lean proof, presented in its own section above. The other supporting depth results retain their individual verification scope.

| [Shallow targets under deep logistic and softplus posteriors](works-in-progress/shallow-logistic-softplus/logistic.pdf) · [softplus companion](works-in-progress/shallow-logistic-softplus/softplus.pdf) · [source and reviews](works-in-progress/shallow-logistic-softplus/) | Polynomial logistic and quasipolynomial softplus sample bounds for bounded-weight shallow teachers, at every eligible width in the strong-prior limit and at each fixed `γ>1`. The softplus bound requires sufficient learner depth. These are the original uncentered activations; critical `γ=1` and arbitrary deep teachers remain open. Conventional proofs, not Lean verified. |
| [The cost of narrow input layers](works-in-progress/narrow-layer-information/paper.pdf) · [source and reviews](works-in-progress/narrow-layer-information/) | Low-rank input layers give a constant-depth threshold normal form. Inner-product parity forces exponential product and fine coefficient precision at fixed first-layer width, while a wider shallow implementation has polynomial product. A representation separation, not a Bayesian sample lower bound. |
| [The norm cost of local group computation](works-in-progress/local-group-computation/paper.pdf) · [source and reviews](works-in-progress/local-group-computation/) | A quasipolynomial Frobenius-product lower bound for local computation trees, allowing arbitrary internal codes, retained history and unbalanced trees. An optimal-transport argument charges the actual layer matrices. Global implementations can escape; even parity has a shallow polynomial-product implementation. This is an architecture obstruction, not an intrinsic function or Bayesian sample lower bound. |
| [ReLU compression above its depth baseline](works-in-progress/relu-relative-product/paper.pdf) · [source and reviews](works-in-progress/relu-relative-product/) | A sharp nonconstant-function energy baseline and compression in terms of relative product `dP²/2^(D−1)`. Gives a strong-prior sample bound independent of learner depth in that parameter; relative product may still be exponential in teacher depth. |

The [structured-target companion](works-in-progress/boolean-depth-barriers/uniform-target-classes.pdf) records scale-uniform positive bounds used in the circuit-complexity comparison. The [Gaussian-control companion](works-in-progress/tanh-arithmetic-formulas/gaussian-controls.pdf) proves an arithmetic-formula bound for that restricted variational class and a strict gap between its scalar cost baseline and the unrestricted one. The [non-Gaussian-control companion](works-in-progress/tanh-arithmetic-formulas/non-gaussian-controls.pdf) extends formula compression to independent changes on fixed-dimensional subspaces and several quadratic changes of density, including changes built from normalized differences of nearly identical features. Neither companion establishes the stronger converse for the original mean-field posterior. [FAQ 19](TECHNICAL-FAQ.md#19-is-exponential-depth-dependence-necessary-without-any-robustness-restriction) and [FAQ 22](TECHNICAL-FAQ.md#22-can-the-exponential-dependence-on-the-teachers-depth-be-removed-without-a-robustness-restriction) explain the implications and remaining gaps.

These are supporting results and works in progress, retained separately from the main generalization theorem under a robustness restriction. The unrestricted Boolean-cube problem remains open. For continuous-output regression, a counterexample proves that exponential depth dependence is necessary in a general width-independent guarantee.
