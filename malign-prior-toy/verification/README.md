## Round 1

**Verdict.** The numbered theorems were correct, apart from an edge case in the machine-dependence theorem and small bookkeeping gaps. The errors were in what the prose said the theorems showed, and in the explanation of the computation. All findings were accepted. The reference numbers below are those of the report.

| Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|
| **B1.** The self-reproducing floor was summarised as 2^(−c) times a factor that is 1 without resource bounds. The theorem's factor is the share of explanations short enough to fit, with the wrapper, into one block; it is below 1 even without bounds, and 0 at most contexts when the block is shorter than the context's explanations. | The theorem now names this factor ρ°, and the text after it states the counting argument (a block determines its context, so at most 2^m sequences have a self-reproducing continuation at all). Abstract, Section 1, Section 7 and the README were corrected. |
| **B2.** Section 6 attributed both headline effects to the programs `+[:+]` and `+[.+]`. The mass comes from the three-command programs `+:]`, `-:]`, `+.]`, `-.]`, which use the restart rule for an unmatched `]`. Both examples have the same cause (a shorter program explains the context), so the split into "a floor" and "a capture" was unsupported. The alternating stream has six-command, not seven-command, shortest programs. | Confirmed by running the programs and by a brute-force count (`attribution` in `experiments/run_profiles.py`; 70% and 57% of the mass). Section 6 was rewritten: one mechanism, the right programs, the right lengths. BF8 has no wrappers, and the text now says so. |
| **B3.** The majority at bit 17 was computed for programs of at most 10 commands only, and weakens as the bound grows. | The experiment now repeats the computation for 7 to 11 commands (`length_dependence`), the paper has a table of it, and the abstract and README say that the effect weakens as longer programs are admitted. No claim is made beyond 11 commands. |
| **B4.** The remark "the searcher is the wrapper" covered only a hypothesis that draws one world. A hypothesis that keeps looking until it finds a handover gains a factor 1/H, with H the prior probability of a handover. Also, in the note's definition a handover is a function of the transcript, so the update "I am being consulted" that the original argument turns on cannot be expressed. | The remark was replaced by a theorem: for a world drawn from the prior conditioned on handing over, the expected share of any family of programs with a common prefix of length a is at most 2^(−a)/H, and the searcher attains it; the text discusses the bound H ≥ 2^(−|s|) and the time and input the search costs. A new remark treats handover rules under which only some worlds with a given transcript hand over and names the two quantities that then matter. "What the toy does not settle" and Section 7 were changed accordingly. |
| **B5.** The abstract stated the budget without the factor m. | Fixed. |
| **B6.** Several summary sentences claimed upper bounds or necessity that no theorem gives ("no more than the floor in general", "a majority needs one of three things", "the answer is no", "it is close to 1 if"). | Reworded to what is proved: "three results suggest that no general argument gives more (none is an upper bound at a given handover)", "three ways to a majority", a conditional statement in place of "the answer is no", and "the odds against the true continuation are large". |
| **B7.** Machine dependence: part (ii) fails at the empty context for k ≥ 2; "change every description length by at most k+1 bits" is false (they lower some); "whose prefix words are" should be "having as prefix words"; the self-reproduction form was only half covered. | Stated for nonempty contexts; "raise … by at most k+1 bits, and they lower some"; wording fixed; a sentence gives the machine for the self-reproduction form. |
| **B8.** Capture: part (b) is about N placements with the same record and different continuations, which the prose did not say; "a factor of about N·2^(−code length)" dropped the factor 2^(−c_X)/ξ(x); the matcher of part (c) has a long start-up, so the cap is unavailable under a tight schedule. | The reading of (b) is spelled out, the factor is given in full with an explanation of when it is of order 1, and (c) is stated as a cap under a generous schedule, with the time-bound section noting that the matcher is excluded by a tight one. |
| **B9.** The sentences after the calibration theorem dropped the conditioning on the world printing m more bits within the bounds, and "therefore malignity can only be defined relative to a population that differs from the prior" did not follow. | Rewritten: exchangeability on the stated population, the factor ξ(x)/Z_m(x) for worlds that stall, and the remark that the forms of the hypothesis hold on the prior population exactly to the extent of its unpredictability. |
| **B10.** The wrapper cannot print before step c, so the floor vanishes if T(1) < c; the contrarian prose was off by one (L+1 rounds); "a single flipped bit" should be a constant bit; an equality clause after the budget theorem was wrong; "ξ(z) is 0 when no program prints z" needs finite L. | All fixed in the places named. |
| **B11.** Proof gaps: bookkeeping of the payload in the self-reproducing floor; the empty first hit; Z_m > 0 in the obedient process; ξ(x) > 0 in the capture theorem. | A sentence on the bookkeeping was added; tests are now sets of nonempty strings; the two positivity conditions were added. |
| **B12.** "Under a tight schedule the hypotheses that can depart from the truth are …" was a classification, not a consequence of the definitions. | Now "among the hypotheses that remain … are those". |
| **C16.** The two forum posts that state the original argument were cited as if read; they could not be opened in the session. | Said once, in the first paragraph of the introduction. |
| **C18, C19, C20.** "Some hundreds of bits" was stated as fact; "about 1/ε handovers are needed" should be "suffice"; "persists" oversold a geometric number of generations with mean s/(1−s). | Marked as a guess; "suffice"; the mean is stated in the proposition and in Section 7. |
| **C21.** "Hypotheses that simulate the world can read the deployment off their own simulation" was covered by no result. | New part (d) of the capture theorem: a reader that follows the record at one location until a flag at another location goes up, and then prints a payload, costs the reader's length plus the code of the flag's location plus a constant. The text notes that this is a share of that one explanation's weight, not of the whole posterior. |
| **C23, C24, C25.** One-shot paragraph, the remark on MAP against sampling, and "remove the floor". | Rewritten: the constant 2^(−c) is independent of the data without bounds, the slack factor is not; the MAP remark now states the pigeonhole fact and the two real contrasts with sampling; "lowers the floor to about 2^(−K(n))". |
| **D3, D4.** The paper's description of BF8 left out that `]` jumps to just after the matching `[`, that a forward jump reads the commands it skips, and that one step can therefore read several commands; the theorems on capture and machine dependence do not apply to BF8 either. | Section 6 states all three, and lists which results apply to BF8. |
| **D6.** Wording in Section 6 ("largest after the first ten bits", "a tighter schedule shifts mass off the truth", "none"), the population curve's conditioning, exact zeros falling off the log axis, the budget table checking the finite form, a stale test comment. | "Selected positions"; "can shift", with the counterexample row named; "none" is defined to include mass cut by the command limit; the conditioning is stated; the left panel now uses an axis that is linear near 0; the caption says "finite form"; the comment in `test_bf8.py` refers to programs the test checks. |
| **E.** Two citations were worded beyond the sources (Cohen, Hutter, Bengio, Russell 2025; Wyeth and Hutter 2025); two bibliography items were never cited. | Reworded; the two items were removed. |
| **F.** Missing conventions; the count in the budget theorem can be m·ℓ; notation clashes; small wording. | Conventions added in Section 2; the count is now part of the theorem with its proof; locations are called i, the string in the fixed-point proof u, the matcher's constant c_match; the wording points were fixed. The letters N and k are still used for more than one thing, each time locally. |

The computation was reproduced by the referee without discrepancy: the tests pass, the rebuilt binary and the regenerated results and tables were identical to the committed ones, every number quoted in the paper matched the results file, and the enumerator agreed with brute force up to 7 commands at 48 bits.
