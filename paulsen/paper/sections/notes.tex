\section{Relation to the original manuscript and its formalisation}\label{app:notes}

The overall strategy---perturb by a Gaussian tangent of size $\sqrt\eps$, then
balance by a static scaling controlled in $\ell_\infty$; two seeds for
$d\gtrsim\log n$ and $n\gtrsim d^2$; Hamilton--Moitra for bounded $d$---and most
individual estimates are those of~\cite{Orig}. The differences are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \emph{Bias removal by a drift.} In~\cite{Orig} the filter bias $t^2b_*$ is
controlled through $\ip x{b_*}^2\le(2a/\rho)x^TLx$ and removed after the
perturbation by a second, local scaling (a minimisation on a mean-zero
$\ell_\infty$-ball, with a Hessian comparison under scaling). This requires the
two-sided comparison $L_{P_t}\asymp L+at^2I$ on all of $\one^\perp$, hence the
concentration of the full squared Gaussian graph, a matrix-series bound for the
cross term, and a re-verification of the dense core after the correction. Here
the identity $q(N_f)=\mathcal A(V^TF(I-2P)FU)$ (\cref{lem:commutator}) exhibits
$b_*=\mathcal Am_*$ with $\fro{m_*}^2\le2a/\rho$, and the drift $\frac t2m_*$
added to the noise cancels the bias at first order (\cref{lem:mean,lem:retraction}).
The only spectral input left is a gap on a bounded deterministic set of rows,
proved with second moments (\cref{lem:sample}\ref{S4}). This observation was
found by one of the review agents used in preparing this text.
\item \emph{Many-row seed for all small errors.} In~\cite{Orig} the many-row
theorem assumes $\eta\le c/d^2$, because the cubic remainder of the
normalisation identity is bounded termwise by $O(t^3d)$, and the operator norm of
the noise by $t^2\fro{Z}^2\le1$. \Cref{lem:remainder} uses the first-order
constraint $X^TZ+Z^TX=0$ a second time to centre the weights, giving $O(t^3)$,
and the net bound controls $\opn Z$. \Cref{thm:manyrow} therefore holds for
$\eta\le c_*$.
\item \emph{No block partition.} With 2., the global argument needs no random
partition into blocks of size $\lceil Ld^6\rceil$ (and no uniform-subset variance
identity): the regimes $d<D$, $n\ge Bd^2$, $n<Bd^2$ of \cref{prop:equalrow}
cover everything, since $A\log(2Bd^2)\le d$ for $d\ge D$.
\item \emph{Barrier constant.} The $\ell_\infty$ Poisson constant is replaced by
the half-set hitting time (\cref{def:barrier}), which is what the median barrier
actually uses (the two agree up to a factor $2$, \cref{rem:poisson}). With it the
maximum principle needs no connectivity argument, and the dense-core estimate
becomes one explicit barrier (\cref{lem:core}) instead of a Schur-complement
argument with an overlap condition. The balancing constant improves from $8$ to
$\frac{15}4$.
\item \emph{Presentation.} One toolbox section; both seeds routed through
\cref{def:seed,cor:seed}; a table of scales for the moderate seed; complete
proofs of all sampling estimates, including the variance bookkeeping behind
the dense core; explicit, acyclic constant choices.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Correctness of the original.} An independent section-by-section
check of~\cite{Orig} (by hand and numerically) found no false statement and no
substantive gap; the issues found were notational overloading and terse
justifications (e.g.\ the stated bound $\Lap(W^2)\preceq2p_{\max}L$, $W=P\circ P$,
is true, but the two-step inequality offered for it only gives $4p_{\max}L$; the
factor $2$ follows from a weighted-variance identity).

\paragraph{Formal status.} The Lean~4 development accompanying~\cite{Orig}
proves \cref{thm:main,thm:projection} by the original route. The arguments of
\cref{sec:manyrow,sec:moderate,sec:assembly}, the barrier formulation of
\cref{sec:toolbox} (\cref{def:barrier,lem:median,thm:balancing,lem:core}),
\cref{def:seed,cor:seed} and \cref{rem:optimal} given here are \emph{not}
formalised. \Cref{sec:bounded}, \cref{lem:align,lem:energy,lem:scaled,lem:potential,lem:scaling-ineq},
and \cref{app:gauss} (items 2--4 of the formalised Gaussian toolkit) follow the
formalised proof up to constants and presentation.
