\section{Formalisation}\label{app:formal}

\Cref{thm:main} is formalised in Lean~4 with Mathlib; the development
accompanies this paper (directory \texttt{lean/}). The formal statement is
\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.92\textwidth}
\small
\begin{verbatim}
def SharpPaulsenBound : Prop :=
  ∃ C : ℝ, 0 < C ∧
    ∀ (n d : ℕ), 0 < d → d ≤ n →
    ∀ (ε : ℝ) (U : Frame n d), 0 < ε → ε < 1 →
      IsNearlyEqualNormParseval ε U →
      ∃ W : Frame n d, IsEqualNormParseval W ∧
        sqDistance U W ≤ C * ε * (d : ℝ)
\end{verbatim}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}
where \texttt{Frame n d} is the type of real $n\times d$ matrices, Parsevalness
is $U^TU=1$, the near-Parseval condition is stated through the quadratic form
$x\mapsto\norm{Ux}^2$, the row-norm conditions refer to $d/n$ as a real
quotient, and \texttt{sqDistance} is the unnormalised squared Frobenius
distance. The projection form (\cref{thm:projection}) is derived for arbitrary
real symmetric idempotent matrices of rank $d$. Both statements are also
restated in plain Mathlib vocabulary (positive semidefiniteness, dot products,
traces, \texttt{Matrix.rank}), and the restatements are derived from them.

\paragraph{Structure.} The development has about $29{,}600$ lines. A library of
about $26{,}000$ lines contains frames, projections and alignment, the
squared-Gram Laplacian, diagonal scaling and the log-determinant potential,
complementation and normalisation, the Hamilton--Moitra argument
(\cref{sec:bounded}), Gaussian tools (quadratic forms, Lipschitz functions, nets,
covariance calculus), the constrained and row-independent noises and the
row-normalisation identity of \cref{sec:manyrow}, and the filtered covariance,
the mean formula and the sampling estimates of \cref{sec:moderate}. About
$3{,}600$ lines (directory \texttt{Paulsen/Linear}) implement the argument
specific to this paper:

\paragraph{Differences from the text.} The formal proof follows the argument
above, with these differences of detail.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item All constants are much larger (for instance $B=10^{40}$, $c_*=10^{-60}$ in
\cref{thm:manyrow}; $A=10^{130}$, $\eps_0=10^{-130}$, $D=10^{40}$ in
\cref{thm:moderate}); the balancing cost is $8\norm{q-p}_1$ rather than
$\frac{15}4\norm{q-p}_1$; the net bound is used with $\opn Z\le128$; and
\cref{lem:core}(ii) is used with $R=b/\lambda$.
\item In \cref{lem:sample}, the block estimate \ref{S4} is not proved from second
moments. Instead the formalisation uses two global events that are also
available from the library when $d\ge A\log 2n$: the concentration
$\opn{\Lap(Y)-\E\Lap(Y)}\le a/32$ of the squared Gaussian graph, and the bound
$|t\,x^T\mathcal C(Y)x|\le\frac1{32}(x^TLx+at^2\norm x^2)$ for all $x$. Together
with \cref{lem:expected-graph} they give a gap $\frac{at^2}{32}$ for $L_{P_t}$ on
$\one^\perp$, and subtracting the mean gives the gap on vectors supported on
$\mathfrak B$ that \cref{lem:seed-graph} needs. The rest of the moderate-row
argument is as in the text; in particular, nothing is corrected after the
drifted retraction.
\item In \cref{lem:rowmoments} the fourth moment of a centred Gaussian quadratic
form is bounded through its moment generating function, which gives a worse
constant. One functional, $a\sum_i(r_i^2-1)^2$, controls both $\fro{\Gamma Z}^2$ and
$\fro{\Lambda X}^2$.
\item \Cref{lem:mr-dense} is used in a slightly different form: the dense
reference graph has $\frac{17}{20}n$ heavy neighbours at every row, which
holds for every $d\ge1$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Checks.} The project builds with Lean~4.32.0 and Mathlib~v4.32.0.
\texttt{Audit.lean} checks that the final theorems have the target types; that
every theorem of the project depends only on the axioms \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}; and that the proof of the main
theorem uses the barrier toolbox, the centred remainder, the drift and the
partition-free assembly, and none of the Poisson-constant endpoints of the
library. Every module was also replayed in the kernel with
\texttt{leanchecker}.
