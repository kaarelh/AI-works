                 Solomonoff Induction is not the paragon of generalization
                                                               Kaarel Hänni
                                                               August 2025

                                                                  Abstract
          For any probability distribution over infinite bitstrings, a Solomonoff Inductor is roughly at least as good at pre-
      dicting next bits of the bitstring as any computable predictor P — more precisely, the total log loss of the Solomonoff
      Inductor cannot be worse than that of P by more than a constant cP (independent of the number of bits predicted and
      of the distribution on sequences). One might want to summarize this canonical result as ”you can’t make a predictive
      model that generalizes from data better than Solomonoff Induction”. In this paper, I argue that this is misguided: that
      while Solomonoff Induction indeed (roughly) beats every computable inductor at predicting raw data, it is nevertheless
      quite possible for a predictive model to generalize from data better than Solomonoff Induction, because generalization
      quality is often importantly different from the ability to predict raw data. While this point is perhaps fairly obvious
      once stated, I think it is easy to miss; in this paper, I spell out the point in detail. I also discuss some ideal predictive
      models that can generalize better than Solomonoff Induction.
