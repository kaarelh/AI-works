\section{The model--randomness split}\label{sec:split}

H\"anni has suggested that a steeper prior might give ``a preference for a simple sampler over a model where the sampled bits are filled in (solomonoff doesn't have a preference here, both get similar posterior)'' \citep{hanni2026request}. This section makes both halves precise. At $\lambda=1$ the sampler and the programs that fill in its sampled bits get similar posterior, up to explicit factors that do not depend on how much randomness was sampled (\cref{thm:split:lambda1,prop:split:si}). At $\lambda>1$ with private randomness the sampler wins in every countable product class (\cref{thm:split:private}). With shared randomness, or in SI, the universal mixture wins instead and no split is made (\cref{thm:split:shared}). Proofs are in \cref{app:split}.

\subsection{Setting}\label{sec:split:setting}

\begin{definition}[Samplers and fill-ins]\src{steeper \S3.1}\label{def:split:setting}
A \emph{sampler} is a computable conditional measure $\nu$ ($\nu(y\mid x)$ computable uniformly in $(x,y)$, $\sum_y\nu(y\mid x)=1$) with a program $\pi_\nu$ of length $k$ in the class considered. Distinct inputs $x_1,x_2,\dots$ are chosen by any rule, possibly adaptive, randomised or non-computable, and then $y_i\sim\nu(\cdot\mid x_i)$; $\Prob$ is the joint law and $D_n$ the first $n$ pairs. The \emph{sampled randomness} is $-\log\nu(D_n)$. \emph{Fill-ins} are deterministic programs consistent with $D_n$; in a product class their total weight is $\Wdet(D):=\sum\{2^{-\ell(\pi)}:\pi\text{ deterministic},\ \nu_\pi\supseteq D\}$.
\end{definition}

The first lemma bounds how much any program, or the whole mixture, can gain over the sampler on its own data.

\begin{lemma}[Ville deficiency]\status{proved; computed; Ville's inequality known}\src{steeper Lemma 3.1; check c4(A); referee q3(B)}\label{lem:split:ville}
Let $\xi$ be a shared hypothesis with $\xi(\emptyset)\le1$, for example the mixture $\xi_{\mathcal C}$ of a countable product class $\mathcal C$ with $\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal C}2^{-\ell(\pi)}\le1$ (for $\Hcl{\times}$ this is $\Mprod$). Let $\delta^*:=\sup_n\log\big(\xi(D_n)/\nu(D_n)\big)$. Then $\Prob[\delta^*\ge j]\le2^{-j}$ for all $j\ge0$. Consequently, for $\xi=\xi_{\mathcal C}$, every $\pi\in\mathcal C$ and every $n$,
\[
\nu_\pi(D_n)\le2^{\ell(\pi)+\delta^*}\nu(D_n).
\]
Adaptive inputs are allowed.
\end{lemma}

\emph{Proof idea.} $\xi(D_n)/\nu(D_n)$ is a nonnegative supermartingale with initial value at most $1$, by the one-point inequality; apply Ville's maximal inequality \citep{ville1939etude} (\cref{app:split:ville}).

The second lemma shows that fill-ins are cheap: a deterministic program can store a finite piece of each input's private random stream. This adapts the tape-length bound of \citet{claudeSFI2026fundamentals} to product classes and to every computable sampler.

\begin{lemma}[Fill-ins from stored stream prefixes]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Lemma 3.2$'$ (adapts foundations Prop 4.4); check c7(A)}\label{lem:split:stream}
Let the class be $\Hcl{\times}$, let $\nu$ be a sampler with a program $\pi_\nu$ of length $k$, and let $D$ be a data set with $n$ inputs, all of length at most $d$, with $\beta_D:=\min_{(x,y)\in D}\nu(y\mid x)>0$. Put $i_D:=\lceil\log(6n/\beta_D)\rceil$ and $t_D:=\lceil\log(2d+2+2\log(i_D+1))\rceil$. Then
\[
\Wdet(D)\ \ge\ 2^{-k-2\log(t_D+1)-\kappa}\,\nu(D).
\]
Since $\beta_D\ge\nu(D)$, $i_D\le\log(6n)+\log(1/\nu(D))+1$; so the cost $2\log(t_D+1)$ is doubly logarithmic in $d$ and triply logarithmic in $n$ and in the sampled randomness $-\log\nu(D)$.
\end{lemma}

\emph{Proof idea.} Give each input its own stream of tape positions and read a label from a stream prefix as soon as its dyadic interval certifiably lies inside one label's interval of $\nu(\cdot\mid x)$. Each stored tape $w$ of length $2^{2^t}$ gives one deterministic program; for uniform $w$ the streams are independent and certify the right labels with probability at least $\nu(D)/2$, and the only cost beyond $\pi_\nu$ is a code of $t$ (\cref{app:split:stream}). A direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) gives $2\log(t_D+1)=5.6$ bits at $d=20$, $n=10^3$, and $6.3$ bits at $d=100$, $n=10^6$. The lemma needs a computable \emph{measure}: for a semimeasure the label boundaries are not computable. For dyadic samplers a hashing argument gives a weaker bound (\cref{lem:split:hash}).

\subsection{$\lambda=1$: the sampler and its fill-ins get similar posterior}\label{sec:split:lambda1}

For a run of the data process put $d_n:=\max_{i\le n}|x_i|$, $\beta_n:=\min_{i\le n}\nu(y_i\mid x_i)$, $i_n:=\lceil\log(6n/\beta_n)\rceil$ and $t_n:=\lceil\log(2d_n+2+2\log(i_n+1))\rceil$. Let $\Pi_1(\det\mid D_n)$ be the posterior mass of all deterministic programs.

\begin{theorem}[SFI at $\lambda=1$]\status{proved}\src{steeper Thm 3}\label{thm:split:lambda1}
In the setting of \cref{def:split:setting} with the class $\Hcl{\times}$, for every sampler $\nu$ with a program of length $k$, simultaneously for all $n$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\Pi_1(\pi_\nu\mid D_n)\ge2^{-k-\delta^*}$;
\item $\Pi_1(\det\mid D_n)=\Wdet(D_n)/\xi_\times(D_n)\ge2^{-k-2\log(t_n+1)-\kappa-\delta^*}$;
\item the posterior odds of all fill-ins against the sampler program lie in $\big[2^{-2\log(t_n+1)-\kappa},\,2^{k+\delta^*}\big]$, whatever the sampled randomness $-\log\nu(D_n)$;
\item for a sampler whose values are multiples of $2^{-r}$, \cref{lem:split:hash} gives the alternative lower ends $2^{-k-2\log(n+1)-2\log(d_n+1)-2\log(r+1)-\kappa-\delta^*}$ in (ii) and $2^{-2\log(n+1)-2\log(d_n+1)-2\log(r+1)-\kappa}$ in (iii).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\Pi_1(\pi_\nu\mid D_n)=2^{-k}\nu(D_n)/\xi_\times(D_n)$ and $\Pi_1(\det\mid D_n)=\Wdet(D_n)/\xi_\times(D_n)$. Bound $\xi_\times(D_n)\le2^{\delta^*}\nu(D_n)$ by \cref{lem:split:ville}, $\Wdet(D_n)$ from below by \cref{lem:split:stream} (or \cref{lem:split:hash} for (iv)) and from above by $\xi_\times(D_n)$.
\end{proof}

The lower ends of (ii) and (iii) hold for every data set, not only for samples from $\nu$, and cover non-dyadic samplers: one that gives every label probability at least $1/3$, such as the Bern$(1/3)$ threshold hypotheses in Sam Eisenstat's coin example \citep{hanni2026request,claudeSFI2026fundamentals}, has $i_n\le\lceil\log(18n)\rceil$. The hashing bound of (iv) is weaker at realistic sizes (32 to 143 bits; \cref{app:split:hash}).

\begin{proposition}[SI at $\lambda=1$]\status{proved; (iii) known}\src{steeper Prop 3.3}\label{prop:split:si}
Let a string $z_{1:n}$ be drawn from a computable measure $\nu$ with a program $\pi_\nu\in\Hcl{\mathrm{SI}}$ of length $k$, and let $\delta^*:=\sup_n\log\big(M(z_{1:n})/\nu(z_{1:n})\big)$, where $M=\xi_1$ is the mixture of $\Hcl{\mathrm{SI}}$.
(i) $\Pi_1(\pi_\nu\mid z_{1:n})\ge2^{-k-\delta^*}$ for all $n$, and $\Prob[\delta^*\ge j]\le2^{-j}$. (ii) The deterministic hypotheses consistent with $z_{1:n}$ (point masses on computable infinite extensions) have total weight at least $2^{-k-K(n)-\kappa}\nu(z_{1:n})$. (iii) In the deterministic-program representation of $M$ every hypothesis is a fill-in; the sampler appears only as the programs ``decoder for $\nu$, then a code of $z$'', of total weight at least $2^{-k-\kappa}\nu(z)$.
\end{proposition}

For (ii) the point mass on $z_{1:n}0^\infty$ suffices; (iii) is the usual proof that $M\ge2^{-K(\nu)-O(1)}\nu$ \citep{li2008introduction} (\cref{app:split:si}). So H\"anni's ``both get similar posterior'' holds in SFI and in SI, up to factors $2^k$, $2^{2\log(t_n+1)+\kappa}$ (SFI) or $2^{O(k+K(n))}$ (SI), and the Ville deficiency, whatever the amount of sampled randomness.

\subsection{$\lambda>1$ with private randomness: the sampler wins}\label{sec:split:private}

Call a program $\pi$ \emph{$\nu$-equivalent on the run} if $\sum_n\Hel\big(\nu(\cdot\mid x_n),\nu_\pi(\cdot\mid x_n)\big)<\infty$, where $\Hel(P,Q):=1-\sum_y\sqrt{P(y)Q(y)}\in[0,1]$, also for a sub-probability $Q$.

\begin{theorem}[Private randomness, $\lambda>1$]\status{proved; computed; Kakutani-type argument known}\src{steeper Thm 4; check c4(C); referee r4, q3(C)}\label{thm:split:private}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a countable class of product programs with $\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal C}2^{-\ell(\pi)}\le1$ that contains $\pi_\nu$ (for example $\Hcl{\times}$, or a time-bounded or circuit subclass). Fix $\lambda>1$ and define $\delta^*$ from $\xi_{\mathcal C}$. Simultaneously for all $n$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{MAP.} Every $\lambda$-MAP $\hat\pi_n$ has $\ell(\hat\pi_n)\le(\lambda k+\delta^*)/(\lambda-1)=k+(k+\delta^*)/(\lambda-1)$ and $-\log\nu(D_n)-\ell(\hat\pi_n)-\delta^*\le-\log\nu_{\hat\pi_n}(D_n)\le-\log\nu(D_n)+\lambda k$.
\item \emph{Length tail.} $\Pi_\lambda(\{\pi:\ell(\pi)>L\}\mid D_n)\le2^{\lambda k+\delta^*-(\lambda-1)L}$ for every $L$.
\item \emph{Fill-ins.} Every deterministic program consistent with $D_n$ has $\ell\ge-\log\nu(D_n)-\delta^*$; $\Pi_\lambda(\det\mid D_n)\le2^{\lambda k+\lambda\delta^*-(\lambda-1)(-\log\nu(D_n))}$; and for the tempered posterior with $\eta=1/\lambda$, $\Pi^\eta(\det\mid D_n)\le2^{k+\delta^*-(1-\eta)(-\log\nu(D_n))}$.
\item \emph{Identification.} Suppose the input sequence is infinite. Then, almost surely, from some $n$ on every $\lambda$-MAP is $\nu$-equivalent on the run, and $\Pi_\lambda(\nu\text{-equivalent on the run}\mid D_n)\to1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof idea.} (i)--(iii) compare with the sampler program, $J_\lambda(\pi_\nu)=\lambda k-\log\nu(D_n)$, and bound every program's likelihood by \cref{lem:split:ville}. For (iv), the likelihood ratio $\Lambda_n:=\nu_\pi(D_n)/\nu(D_n)$ of a program that is not $\nu$-equivalent on the run tends to $0$ almost surely, by the martingale argument behind Kakutani's dichotomy \citep{kakutani1948equivalence}, which holds for adaptive inputs (\cref{app:split:private}).

\begin{remark}[What the theorem says, and what it does not]\status{remark}\src{steeper \S3.3}\label{rem:split:reading}
This is the split H\"anni hoped for, for every fixed $\lambda>1$, in a precise and limited sense. The selected model is a sampler, not a fill-in, but not necessarily $\nu$: it may carry up to $(k+\delta^*)/(\lambda-1)$ bits beyond the sampler, for example memorised noise. The fill-ins' posterior is exponentially small in the sampled randomness, but positive. ``$\nu$-equivalent on the run'' is not equality of predictive laws and says nothing about unseen inputs. On a finite domain such as $\{0,1\}^d$, (iv) is vacuous.
\end{remark}

\subsection{$\lambda>1$ with shared randomness: no split}\label{sec:split:shared}

\begin{theorem}[SI, or SFI with shared randomness, $\lambda>1$]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Thm 5; check c4(C); referee r4, q4(A)}\label{thm:split:shared}
Assume (Dom) and $\xi_1\le2^{\kappa_1}U$, let $\lambda>1$, and let $\pi_\nu$ be a program of length $k$ for a sampler in the class. For every observation $o$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item if $(\lambda-1)k>\lambda\ell_U+\kappa_0$, then $J_\lambda(\pi_U;o)<J_\lambda(\pi_\nu;o)$: the sampler program is never a $\lambda$-MAP, and every $\lambda$-MAP has length at most $\ell^*_\lambda<k$;
\item for every set $A$ of programs, $\Pi_\lambda(A\mid o)\le2^{-(\lambda-1)\min_{\pi\in A}\ell(\pi)+\lambda\ell_U+\kappa_1}$. In particular the power posterior puts at most $2^{-(\lambda-1)L+\lambda\ell_U+\kappa_1}$ on all programs of length at least $L$.
\end{enumerate}
For SI with adaptive or non-computable inputs, (Dom) in the needed form is supplied by $\Hcl{\mathrm{chr}}$ with $U=\MH$, not by $\Hcl{\mathrm{SI}}$; with i.i.d.\ computable inputs $\Hcl{\mathrm{SI}}$ with $U=M$ works.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof idea.} (i) compares $\pi_U$ with $\pi_\nu$ using (Dom); (ii) bounds the numerator by $2^{-(\lambda-1)\min_A\ell}\xi_1(o)$ and the normaliser by $2^{-\lambda\ell_U}U(o)$ (\cref{app:split:shared}).

Compare \cref{thm:split:private}. In a shared class the steeper posterior lives, up to mass $2^{-s}$, on programs of length at most $(\lambda\ell_U+\kappa_1+s)/(\lambda-1)$, \emph{whatever the data}; in the product class it concentrates on samplers $\nu$-equivalent on the run, whose length is unbounded over $\nu$. Predictively both converge to $\nu$'s predictions (in $\Hcl{F}$ by the merging theorem of \citealp{claudeSFI2026fundamentals}). The difference is \emph{what the selected hypothesis is}: a model plus private noise, or a short universal learner that contains no model of $\nu$.

\begin{example}[Noisy parity, full table: an illustration]\status{computed; illustration}\src{steeper Ex 3.4; check c4(C); referee q1(J)}\label{ex:split:noisyparity}
Let $d=12$ and let the data be the full table ($N=4096$ inputs) of a random parity with i.i.d.\ flips at rate $1/8$ ($509$ flips). A small product class contains the coin, parity-plus-noise samplers ($\ell=24.2$ bits) and the memoriser (parity plus flip set); a ``shared class'' adds the finite Bayes mixture $U$ over all parities and a grid of noise rates, with an \emph{assigned} length $\ell_U=12$. A direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) gives: the product-class MAP is the sampler at $\lambda=1,1.1,1.5,2,3,6$, and at $\lambda=1$ the memoriser is only $8.2$ bits worse, a tie up to $O(\log N)$; the shared-class MAP is the sampler for $\lambda<1.315$ and $U$ above, the threshold being $(-\log U(D)+\log\nu(D))/(k-\ell_U)$ (the rounded values $2234.0$, $2218.0$, $24.2$, $12$ give $1.311$). Since $\ell_U=12$ is assigned and this finite class does not satisfy (Dom), the example illustrates the mechanism of \cref{thm:split:shared}; it is not an instance of it.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[No split in SI's program representation]\status{proved}\src{steeper Rem 4.3}\label{rem:split:siprograms}
Solomonoff's $M$ is a mixture over deterministic programs. Tempering does not change deterministic hypotheses (\cref{lem:setup:basic}(iii)), and a power prior $2^{-\lambda|p|}$ charges sampled bits and model bits alike, so it is indifferent between ``decoder for $\nu$ plus a code of the randomness'' and a literal fill-in of the same length. The split needs a representation in which the randomness is not part of the program: a product hypothesis with private coins.
\end{remark}
