\section{The teacher's mistakes: the split at a positive rate}\label{sec:teacher}

This section gives the second reason for a complexity penalty steeper than the likelihood penalty: a learner that grasps what a teacher means, rather than predicting what the teacher would say, should not reproduce the teacher's systematic mistakes. It is the split of \cref{sec:kinks} at a positive rate: a bit of noise enters one likelihood term, and a fixed $\lambda>1$ discards it (\cref{sec:recovery}); a bit of an error rule enters the term of every input it governs, so $\lambda$ must grow linearly in $n$. We prove this for the instance of \cref{def:teacher:instance}, over all product hypotheses; \cref{sec:limits} shows where it stops. Proofs are in \cref{app:teacher}.

\subsection{From noise to errors}\label{sec:teacher:hanni}\label{sec:limits:schedule}

\begin{remark}[The three-hypothesis calculation]\status{computed}\src{steeper \S2.1; checks c1, T5 re-run}\label{rem:teacher:hanni}
(1) A simple model of $100$ bits predicts $100$ bits of labels per bit of description, and a systematic error model of $100$ further bits predicts $10$ bits per bit. The coin, the simple model and the simple model with its errors lie on a hull with slopes $100$ and $10$, so the simple model alone is selected exactly for $\lambda\in(10,100)$. (2) Per sample, let the errors cover a $2^{-10}$ fraction of the inputs and take $10{,}000$ bits. Of the good model (flip rate $p=2^{-10}$, $110$ bits), the memoriser ($10{,}100$ bits, risk $0$) and the coin, the good model minimises $c\,\ell+R$ exactly for $H(p)/9990<c<(1-H(p))/110$; a direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) gives the window $(1.1185\cdot10^{-6},8.989\cdot10^{-3})$, and plain MDL ($\lambda=1$) switches to the memoriser at $n\approx8.94\cdot10^5$. Other hypotheses might do better.
\end{remark}

The \emph{reuse factor} of a unit of randomness (one sampled label, or one cell $z\in\{0,1\}^m$ of an error rule) is the number of likelihood terms it determines. A label of noise, drawn per query or frozen per distinct input (PQ, FR of \cref{def:setup:data}), enters one term; a cell of the rule enters the term of every input in it, $2^{d-m}$ on the full table and about $n2^{-m}$ among $n$ samples. Repetition scales every threshold:

\begin{lemma}[Duplication]\status{proved; computed}\src{split/teacher Lemma 3.0; split/verify-teacher \S4; check t7(C)}\label{lem:teacher:reuse}
If every term of a data set $D$ is counted $r\ge1$ times, so that each program has fit $rL(\pi;D)$, then $J_\lambda=r\,J_{\lambda/r}(\cdot\,;D)$ and the $\lambda$-MAPs are the $(\lambda/r)$-MAPs of $D$. With every reference point $(\alpha_{\rm r},L_{\rm r})$ moved to $(\alpha_{\rm r},rL_{\rm r})$, every $\lambda$-threshold of $D$ (the hull's edge slopes and vertex $\lambda$-intervals, left steepness, the slopes of right lines and right secants) is multiplied by $r$, and so are the wedge margins and the offsets of right lines.
\end{lemma}

\emph{Proof.} $\lambda\ell+rL=r\big((\lambda/r)\ell+L\big)$, and the conditions of \cref{def:kink:kinks,lem:kink:main} are homogeneous in fits, offsets and slopes. \qed

Frozen noise counted once (FR) is like fresh noise: for fixed $\lambda>1$ every $\lambda$-MAP is at most $(k+\delta^*)/(\lambda-1)$ bits longer than the sampler's program, at every $n$. Counted per query (\emph{FQ}: a fixed table $F$ queried at i.i.d.\ inputs) it is reused at every repeat, like an error rule with $m=d$: if the class can memorise finitely many inputs, every $\lambda_n=o(n)$ eventually rejects every program for the sampler (\cref{prop:teacher:frozen}). Plain Bayes, MDL and SI learn the errors, as does every schedule with $(\lambda_n/n)(d+L_S)\to0$ along a sequence of instances (\cref{prop:limits:plain}).

\begin{proposition}[Which schedules keep the identification]\status{proved; computed}\src{split/teacher Prop 3.6, Prop 3.2(a); split/verify-teacher \S6; check v3}\label{prop:teacher:schedules}
In the instance of \cref{def:teacher:instance}, let the data be PQ with $\nu=\delta_{y^*}$ (FQ with $F=y^*$), the inputs uniform on $\{0,1\}^d$, and $\lambda_n>0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
\item If $\lambda_n/n\to0$, almost surely for all large $n$ every $\lambda_n$-MAP has $R=0$: it gives $y^*(x)$ probability $1$ at every $x\in\{0,1\}^d$, so $e=1$.
\item If $\lambda_n/n\to\infty$, almost surely for all large $n$ every $\lambda_n$-MAP has length at most $\ell_{\min}:=\min\{\ell(\pi):R(\pi)<\infty\}$. In $\Hcl{\times}$, $\ell_{\min}\le\kappa$, so once $d-4-\Lambda_1>\kappa$ these $\lambda_n$-MAPs have level at least $2$.
\item Let $B<\infty$ be an integer in $[\log(1/p),\infty)$, $c_{\rm hi}$ computed at this $B$, $2^{-m}<c_1\le c_2<c_{\rm hi}$, $\delta\in(0,1)$, $\epsilon_1:=\log(1/p)\sqrt{\ln(2/\delta)/(2n)}$, $\bar L:=(J_g(c_2)+\epsilon_1)/c_1$ and $\bar\epsilon_n:=\epsilon_1+B\sqrt{(\bar L\ln2+\ln(2/\delta))/(2n)}$. For each $n$, with these data or with $n\le2^d$ inputs drawn without replacement, and each $\lambda_n$ with $\lambda_n/n\in[c_1,c_2]$: with probability at least $1-\delta$, every $\lambda_n$-MAP satisfies \cref{thm:teacher:main}(ii) with $c=\lambda_n/n$ and $\epsilon=\bar\epsilon_n$, whenever $J_g(c)+\bar\epsilon_n<\Phi_2(c)$ on $[c_1,c_2]$, which holds for all large $n$. For PQ data with $\delta=n^{-2}$, almost surely this holds for all large $n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof idea.} (a) The memoriser has fit $0$, which leaves finitely many candidates, and the strong law eliminates those of positive risk. (b) One bit of length eventually outweighs the fit of a shortest finite-risk program. (c) is \cref{lem:teacher:finite} with a length budget uniform over $[c_1,c_2]$ (\cref{app:teacher:schedules}).

So a ratio $\lambda_n/n$ that stays in a compact subset of the window $(2^{-m},c_{\rm hi})$ of \cref{thm:teacher:main} keeps the identification; $\lambda=cn$ is the simplest such schedule.

\begin{remark}[Fixed $\lambda$ against $\lambda\propto n$]\status{remark (from proved results); known}\src{steeper end of \S2.5, \S4.4; split/teacher \S3.0, Remark 3.5}\label{rem:limits:schedule}
For noise a fixed $\lambda>1$ suffices (\cref{thm:recovery:main,thm:split:private}); for errors $\lambda/n$ must stay above about $2^{-m}$, the reuse factor per sample (\cref{thm:teacher:main}(iii),(iv)). With $\lambda=cn$ the learner discards every component whose rate is below $c$, valid or not (\cref{prop:limits:separable}): it is deliberately inconsistent for the teacher's label distribution, at the price of a batch excess: $-\log\xi_\lambda(D_n)\le n\,(c\,\ell(\pi)+\hat R(\pi))$ for every $\pi$, with $\hat R$ the empirical risk, so $c\,\ell(\pi)$ bits per sample over $\pi$'s own loss (not a sequential regret, \cref{rem:setup:persample}; literature: \cref{rem:related:mdl}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[$\lambda=cn$ and weight decay]\status{proved; computed; known}\src{imitation/decay notes-final \S\S1--4 (Props 2.1--2.3, 3.2, 4.1, 4.2, Example 4.3); checks d1, d2, d3; imitation/notes-final \S3}\label{rem:teacher:weightdecay}
With $\lambda=cn$ the length is charged once per sample: $2^{-cn\ell(\pi)}\nu_\pi(h_n)=\prod_{i\le n}2^{-c\ell(\pi)}\nu_\pi(y_i\mid x_i)$. A fixed $\lambda$ is Bayes' rule with the fixed prior $2^{-\lambda\ell}$, but no fixed prior with full support gives the $cn$-MAPs on every sample: the $cn$-MAPs depend on the sample only through its empirical distribution, whereas under such a prior a program that fits a sample worse than another stops being a MAP once the sample is repeated often enough, and in $\Hcl{\times}$, for every $c>0$, some samples have only such $cn$-MAPs. Per query, for $c$ above the truth's left steepness $c^*$ (\cref{prop:recovery:beyond}) the $cn$-MAPs end at shorter programs that predict worse, whereas the MAPs of every summable prior that charges $\mathcal E$ end in $\mathcal E$ (\cref{prop:teacher:nofixedprior}). The window of \cref{thm:teacher:main}, outside a thin band just above $2^{-m}$, is an instance: there the $cn$-MAPs eventually do not reproduce the teacher's labels, while every summable prior that charges a program reproducing them eventually selects such a program.

Weight decay \citep{krogh1992simple} has the same form. With the loss averaged over $n$ samples, gradient descent with weight-decay coefficient $\omega$ descends $\hat L_n(\theta)+\frac\omega2\|\theta\|^2$, and, for the log loss, $n$ times this is, up to a constant, the negative log posterior under a Gaussian prior \citep{mackay1992practical} with precision $n\omega$, which grows linearly in $n$ like the exponent of $2^{-cn\ell}$; a fixed prior needs $\omega\propto1/n$. In the limit, for a differentiable population loss $L$, the minimiser of $L(\theta)+\frac\omega2\|\theta\|^2$ minimises $L$ only if $\theta=0$ does, and it can classify worse (\cref{ex:teacher:weightdecay}). In both cases the penalty per sample does not vanish with the data. Discarding a component whose risk reduction per unit of complexity is small requires this (\cref{prop:limits:separable}): every $\lambda_n=o(n)$ eventually learns the errors (\cref{prop:teacher:schedules}(a)), and the same per-sample threshold keeps the near-copies of \cref{thm:imit:main}.

The analogy is one of objectives. Unlike a vertex of the hull, a nonzero minimiser of $L+\frac\omega2\|\theta\|^2$ with $L$ differentiable changes with every $\omega$, so the analogue of a window is a range of $\omega$ with one classifier, not with one hypothesis (\cref{app:teacher:weightdecay}). $\|\theta\|^2$ is not a description length: the information in a weight depends on its precision, which \citet{hinton1993keeping} control by adding Gaussian noise, and with normalisation layers the norm mainly sets the effective learning rate \citep{vanlaarhoven2017l2}. Stochastic gradient iterates are not minimisers \citep{mandt2017stochastic}; decoupled weight decay in adaptive optimisers is not an $L_2$ penalty \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled,xie2024implicit}; and the decay acts per step, so before convergence its effect depends on the learning rate and the number of steps. Grokking \citep{power2022grokking,liu2022towards} has been related to the weight norm and to weight decay \citep{liu2023omnigrok}, and analysed as memorisation followed by a cleanup that removes memorising components \citep{nanda2023progress}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The two kink bounds and the theorem}\label{sec:teacher:theorem}

The objective is the population objective $J_c$ of \cref{def:teacher:instance}, in the population reading of \cref{lem:kink:main}; samples are treated in \cref{sec:teacher:more}. Define
\begin{gather*}
t^*:=\tfrac{d-\Lambda_1}4,\qquad
\Phi_2(c):=\begin{cases}\min\{c(d-8-\Lambda_1)+G_1,\ G_{t^*-1}\}&t^*\ge2,\\ G_1&t^*<2,\end{cases}\qquad\Lambda_3:=4+2\log d+\kappa,\\
J_g(c):=c(d+\kappa_g)+H(p)\ge J_c(\pi_g),\qquad J_b(c):=c(d+\lceil L_S\rceil+\kappa_b)\ge J_c(\pi_b),\\
\Lambda_2:=2^mH(p)-B_S+4+\Lambda_1+\kappa_g\le\log(2^m+1)+\delta_S+4+\Lambda_1+\kappa_g,\\
\Delta(c):=\max\{0,\ c(4+\Lambda_1+\kappa_g)+H(p)-B_S/2^m\}.
\end{gather*}
For $c\ge2^{-m}$ and $\Lambda_2\ge0$, $\Delta(c)=\Lambda_2/2^m+(c-2^{-m})(4+\Lambda_1+\kappa_g)$. $J_g$ minus each piece of $\Phi_2$ is affine and increasing in $c$, so $\{c>0:J_g(c)\le\Phi_2(c)\}=(0,c_{\rm hi}]$ (empty if $c_{\rm hi}\le0$) with
\[
c_{\rm hi}:=\min\Big\{\frac{G_1-H(p)}{8+\Lambda_1+\kappa_g},\ \frac{G_{t^*-1}-H(p)}{d+\kappa_g}\Big\}\ \ (t^*\ge2),\qquad c_{\rm hi}:=\frac{G_1-H(p)}{d+\kappa_g}\ \ (t^*<2).
\]
$c_{\rm hi}>0$ requires $G_1>H(p)$; at $B=\infty$ this holds exactly for $p<p_0=0.05596\ldots$ (\cref{sec:parity:full}).

\begin{proposition}[The two kink bounds]\status{proved; computed}\src{split/teacher Prop 2.1(a),(b); checks t1(B), rB2(E)}\label{prop:teacher:kinks}
Let $B=\infty$ or let $B\ge\log(1/p)$ be an integer. Take the reference point $(d+\kappa_g,H(p))$, bounded by $\pi_g$, the fits $R\ge R_B$, the set $\mathcal N$ of programs of level at least $2$ ($\infty$ included) and the set $\mathcal M$ of programs of level $1$.
(a) \emph{Left.} Every $\pi\in\mathcal N$ has $J^B_c(\pi)\ge\Phi_2(c)$ for every $c>0$, so $\eta_{\mathcal N}(c)\ge\Phi_2(c)-J_g(c)$, which is nonnegative on $(0,c_{\rm hi}]$ and positive on $(0,c_{\rm hi})$. If $c_{\rm hi}>0$, every $\pi\in\mathcal N$ lies above the wedge with apex $(d+\kappa_g,H(p))$, steepness $c_{\rm hi}$ and margin $G_1-H(p)$: $R_B(\pi)\ge H(p)+\max\{c_{\rm hi}(d+\kappa_g-\ell(\pi)),\,G_1-H(p)\}$.
(b) \emph{Right.} Every $\pi\in\mathcal M$ has $\ell(\pi)\ge d-4-\Lambda_1$ and $R_B(\pi)\ge H(p)-\Lambda_2/2^m-2^{-m}(\ell(\pi)-d-\kappa_g)$: $\mathcal M$ has the right line of slope $2^{-m}$ and offset $\Lambda_2/2^m$, with length floor $d-4-\Lambda_1$. If $p<\frac14$, then $\Lambda_2\ge0$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof idea.} (a): by (D1) a program of level $t\ge2$ is at least $d-s(t)-\Lambda_1$ long or pays the risk floor $G_{t-1}$, and the concavity of $G$ leaves two cases. (b) is (D1) and (D2) at level $1$ (\cref{app:teacher:main}).

Not knowing the parity costs a positive amount per sample; knowing the rule buys only $2^{-m}$ of risk per bit. Between the two slopes lies the window:

\begin{theorem}[The teacher's-mistakes theorem]\status{proved}\src{steeper Thm 2; split/teacher Prop 2.1(c), Remark 2.6 (a1); split/verify-teacher \S8}\label{thm:teacher:main}
In the setting of \cref{def:teacher:instance}, with $B=\infty$ or an integer $B\ge\log(1/p)$, the following hold for every program $\pi$ of $\Hcl{\times}$, and of every product class covered by \cref{prop:related:computable}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),start=1,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item[(o)] \emph{Level bounds.} If $\lev(\pi)=1$, then $J^B_c(\pi)\ge c(d-4-\Lambda_1)+\max\{R_B(\pi),\,cB_S-(c2^m-1)R_B(\pi)\}$. If $\lev(\pi)\ge2$, including $\infty$, then $J^B_c(\pi)\ge\Phi_2(c)$.
\item \emph{Window.} If $c\,2^m>1$ and $c\le c_{\rm hi}$, then $\Phi(c)\ge\Phi^B(c)\ge\min\{c(d-4-\Lambda_1)+B_S/2^m,\ \Phi_2(c)\}$ and $J_c(\pi_g)\le\Phi(c)+\Delta(c)$, where $\Delta(c)\le c(4+\Lambda_1+\kappa_g)+(\log(2^m+1)+\delta_S)/2^m$.
\item \emph{Identification.} Let $c\,2^m>1$, $\epsilon\ge0$ and $J_g(c)+\epsilon<\Phi_2(c)$, and let $J^B_c(\pi)\le J_g(c)+\epsilon$; this includes every $\pi$ with $J_c(\pi)\le\Phi(c)+\epsilon$. Then $\lev(\pi)=1$. Put
\[
\Delta_\ell:=\kappa_g+\frac\epsilon c+\frac{\Lambda_2+\epsilon/c}{c2^m-1},\qquad R_{\max}:=J_g(c)+\epsilon-c\,(d-4-\Lambda_1)^+.
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=0pt,topsep=1pt]
\item \emph{It knows the simple model:} $u(\pi)\ge\max\{\frac34,\ 2^{-R_{\max}}-p-2^{-B}\}$.
\item \emph{It fits no better than noise:} $R_B(\pi)\ge H(p)-\dfrac{c\Lambda_2+\epsilon}{c2^m-1}$.
\item \emph{It is short:} $\ell(\pi)\le d+\Delta_\ell$.
\item \emph{It holds little information about the error rule:} $I(\pi)\le\Delta_\ell+\Lambda_3$, so $L_S-K(S\mid a,\pi)\le\delta_S+\Delta_\ell+\Lambda_3$.
\item \emph{It does not predict the errors:} for every integer $g\ge1$, $e(\pi)\le1-u(\pi)+\gamma_g+2^{-g}$, where $\gamma_g:=\sqrt{j_g\ln2/(2s_0)}$ and $j_g$ is the least integer $j\ge0$ with $j-2\log(j+1)>\delta_S+\Delta_\ell+\Lambda_3+1+2\log m+2\log s_0+g+2\log g+\kappa$.
\end{enumerate}
\item \emph{Memoriser regime.} Let $c\,2^m<1$, and let $J^B_c(\pi)\le J_b(c)+\epsilon$ with $J_b(c)+\epsilon<\Phi_2(c)$. Then $\lev(\pi)=1$ and $(1-c2^m)\min\{R_B(\pi),B_S/2^m\}\le\varrho:=c(\lceil L_S\rceil-L_S+\delta_S+4+\Lambda_1+\kappa_b)+\epsilon$. If $\varrho<(1-c2^m)B_S/2^m$, then $R_B(\pi)\le\varrho/(1-c2^m)$ and $e(\pi)\ge2^{-R_B(\pi)/p}-2^{-B}$.
\item \emph{The lower end is sharp.} If $c<H(p)/(\lceil L_S\rceil+\kappa_b+4+\Lambda_1)$, then $J_c(\pi_b)<J_c(\pi)$ for every $\pi$ with $\lev(\pi)=1$ and $R(\pi)\ge H(p)$. Since $L_S=2^mH(p)-O(m)$, this threshold is $2^{-m}\big(1+O((m+\log d+\kappa)/L_S)\big)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof idea.} (i), (ii)(b) and (ii)(c) are \cref{lem:kink:main}(i),(ii) with the bounds of \cref{prop:teacher:kinks} ($\tau=2^{-m}$, offset $\Lambda_2/2^m$, length floor $d-4-\Lambda_1$, $\eta_{\mathcal N}\ge\Phi_2-J_g$); the lemma's $\Delta(c)$ is $\Delta(c)$. The rest gives the position its meaning: (a) is the risk floor at $R_B\le R_{\max}$; (d) is list decoding and symmetry of information; in (e), much probability on the erroneous labels would put $S$ in a small set enumerable from the program \citep{hoeffding1963probability}, contradicting (d). (iii) is the side $c<2^{-m}$ of the same line, (iv) the memoriser's secant (\cref{app:teacher:main}).

So in the window $2^{-m}<c<c_{\rm hi}$, for error rules incompressible relative to the model, no product hypothesis beats the good model by more than $\Delta(c)$, and every near-optimal hypothesis knows the parity, is at most $\Delta_\ell$ bits longer than $d$, holds little information about the rule and gives the erroneous labels average probability at most $1-u+\gamma_g+2^{-g}$ (at most $0.2$ to $0.29$ at the example's sizes, against $1$ for the memoriser), when the rule is large compared with the information budget. Below $2^{-m}$, by a relative margin, near-optimal hypotheses reproduce the errors. The lower end $2^{-m}$ is the reuse factor: a bit of the rule serves about $n2^{-m}$ of $n$ samples, a bit of noise one.

\begin{remark}[What the theorem relies on]\status{remark}\src{steeper \S2.4 ``The hypotheses Theorem 2 relies on''; split/teacher Table 2.2}\label{rem:teacher:hypotheses}
Without one of the first four hypotheses a step of the proof fails as written; the fifth enters every bound. (1) \emph{A list-decodable model class} (\cref{lem:teacher:list}) gives the left side and the length floor of the right line; majority-like functions do not admit it. (2) \emph{The product form of $E$} gives the right slope $2^{-m}$ instead of $2^{-d}$ (\cref{lem:teacher:coding}). (3) \emph{Uniform inputs} make every slope a rate; the window scales with the input law (\cref{prop:limits:inputlaw}). (4) \emph{$G_1>H(p)$} is the margin of the left wedge; \cref{prop:teacher:rates} relaxes it to $p\le0.1233$, asymptotically. (5) \emph{A small deficiency $\delta_S$} enters the offset of the right line, as the Ville deficiency $\delta^*$ does for noise (\cref{thm:split:private}(i)), with the same tail $2^{-j}$; it is not an artefact of the proof (\cref{prop:limits:envelope}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What is selected]\status{remark; computed; a natural strengthening refuted}\src{split/teacher \S2.1 ``What is selected''; checks t5(A),(B), rA1(A), rB3(A)}\label{rem:teacher:selected}
\Cref{thm:teacher:main} concerns the near-optimal programs, not the minimiser. The natural strengthening that $\pi_g$ is a vertex of the population hull on the whole window fails in explicit rate codes: among the programs ``true parity with flip rate $q$'', a direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) at the example's sizes, with the machine constant set to $0$ or $30$ (an illustration), finds that the rate-$p$ program minimises $J_c$ only on the lower $67.7\%$ to $88.6\%$ of the window's logarithmic width; a coarser rate such as $2^{-9}$ takes over above, and satisfies every conclusion of \cref{thm:teacher:main}(ii). In $\Hcl{\times}$ nothing is claimed either way.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Full tables and samples}\label{sec:teacher:more}

On the full truth table the reuse factor is exact, and the window becomes one in $\lambda$. Let $N:=2^d$, and write $L_E,B_E,\delta_E$ for $L_S,B_S,\delta_S$ when $m=d$.

\begin{corollary}[Full truth tables: the window starts at the reuse factor]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Cor 2.5 (T5 Thm 3.5, Cor 3.5a); split/teacher Props 3.1(a),(b), 2.4(iii); checks c3, c7(H), t1(D), rB2(B)}\label{cor:teacher:fulltable}
Let $B=\infty$ and let the data be the whole truth table of $y^*$, so $n=N$; a $\lambda$-MAP minimises $\lambda\ell+NR=N\,J_{\lambda/N}$.
(i) For every $\lambda>2^{d-m}$ with $J_g(\lambda/N)<\Phi_2(\lambda/N)$, in particular every $\lambda\in(2^{d-m},Nc_{\rm hi})$, every $\lambda$-MAP $\pi$ satisfies \cref{thm:teacher:main}(ii) with $c=\lambda/N$ and $\epsilon=0$; so $\ell(\pi)\le d+\kappa_g+2^{d-m}\Lambda_2/(\lambda-2^{d-m})$, $NR(\pi)\ge NH(p)-\lambda2^{d-m}\Lambda_2/(\lambda-2^{d-m})$ and $I(\pi)\le\kappa_g+2^{d-m}\Lambda_2/(\lambda-2^{d-m})+\Lambda_3$.
(ii) Let $m=d$. If $G_1>H(p)$ and $N(G_1-H(p))>d+\kappa_g+\log(N+1)$, then at $\lambda=1$ the optimum of $\ell+NR$, and the values $\ell(\pi_g)+NR(\pi_g)$ and $\ell(\pi_b)+NR(\pi_b)$, lie within $O(\log N)+\delta_E$ of each other, so $\lambda=1$ does not decide whether the errors are memorised; lower-order terms and the reference machine do.
(iii) For $\lambda<NH(p)/(\lceil L_S\rceil+\kappa_b+4+\Lambda_1)$ the memoriser beats every program of level $1$ with $R\ge H(p)$.
\end{corollary}

\emph{Proof idea.} \Cref{thm:teacher:main}(ii) and (iv) at $c=\lambda/N$, with $c2^m-1=(\lambda-2^{d-m})/2^{d-m}$; (ii) compares the two programs with (D2) and the risk floor (\cref{app:teacher:fulltable}).

The threshold in (iii) is $2^{d-m}(1+O((m+\log d+\kappa)/L_S))$: the window in $\lambda$ starts, up to lower-order terms, at the reuse factor $2^{d-m}$, the slope of the right line of \cref{prop:teacher:kinks}(b) multiplied by $N$. For $m=d$ it is $1$, as for noise: the good model is $b_0$-sufficient, $b_0=\delta_E+O(\log N)$ (\cref{prop:parity:kink}), so at $\lambda=1$ its point lies on the sufficiency line up to $b_0$, like the sampler's in \cref{thm:split:lambda1}, and (ii) is the resulting tie. At $d=m=20$ and $p=2^{-10}$ a direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) gives the window $\lambda\in(1,1.194\cdot10^4)$ with the machine constant set to $0$ (an illustration) and $(1,6456)$ at $\kappa=30$.

Let $D$ be $n\le2^d$ inputs drawn uniformly without replacement, labelled by $y^*$. For $p<\frac14$, by (D1) the memoriser beats $\pi_g$ at every $\lambda$ below $F_g/(\lceil L_S\rceil+\kappa_b+4+\Lambda_1)\approx n2^{-m}$, with $F_g:=-\log\nu_{\pi_g}(D)\approx nH(p)$. Let $B\ge\log(1/p)$ be an integer, $\delta\in(0,1)$, $\epsilon_1$ as in \cref{prop:teacher:schedules}(c), $\hat L:=(J_c(\pi_g)+\epsilon_1)/c$ and $\epsilon_n:=\epsilon_1+B\sqrt{(\hat L\ln2+\ln(2/\delta))/(2n)}$.

\begin{corollary}[Finite samples]\status{proved; computed}\src{steeper Cor 2.7, Lemma 2.6; check c3}\label{cor:teacher:sampled}
With probability at least $1-\delta$, every $\lambda$-MAP with $\lambda=cn$ satisfies $J^B_c\le J_g(c)+\epsilon_n$, and hence the conclusions of \cref{thm:teacher:main}(ii) with $\epsilon=\epsilon_n$, whenever $c2^m>1$ and $J_g(c)+\epsilon_n<\Phi_2(c)$.
\end{corollary}

The proof is uniform convergence over all programs with Kraft weights (\cref{lem:teacher:finite}), which is pessimistic: at the example's sizes and $c=10^{-4}$, a direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) shows that near-population constants need about $10^{11}$ samples (machine constant $0$, an illustration; \cref{app:teacher:sampled}). The power posterior with $\lambda=cn$ also concentrates on near-optimal programs (\cref{prop:teacher:concentration}), and \cref{rem:teacher:hanni}(1) is realised in population form (\cref{ex:teacher:version1}).

\subsection{Flip rates below 0.1233}\label{sec:teacher:rates}

The bound $\Phi_2$ charges a program that does not know the parity only through the risk floor. Charging in its length the information it holds about the rule gives a continuous lower bound $\Phi_2^\sharp\ge\Phi_2$ on all programs of level at least $2$, and \cref{thm:teacher:main}(i),(ii) and \cref{prop:teacher:schedules}(c) hold with $\Phi_2^\sharp$ in place of $\Phi_2$ for every $c>2^{-m}$ with $J_g(c)+\epsilon<\Phi_2^\sharp(c)$ (\cref{prop:teacher:sharp} in \cref{app:teacher:rates}).

At $c\ge2^{-m}$ the charge $cI\ge p\,(I/s_0)$ is linear in the information per error cell, while the sharp floor of \cref{sec:teacher:lemmas} lets that information gain only about $2p\gamma_g(I)$, of order $\sqrt{I/s_0}$. Let $\psi_2(p,a):=\inf_{x\ge0}\big[apx-\log\big(\tfrac34-\tfrac p2+2p\sqrt{x\ln2/2}\big)\big]$ and $a^*(p):=\inf\{a\ge1:\psi_2(p,a)>H(p)\}$.

\begin{proposition}[A window for every flip rate below $p_1$, asymptotically]\status{proved; computed}\src{split/teacher Prop 5.3; split/verify-teacher \S9; checks t4(A), t6(A), rB7(A), v5(A),(B)}\label{prop:teacher:rates}
Fix a dyadic $p\in(0,\frac14)$, $\kappa$, $B=\infty$ and $a>a^*(p)$. Let $m\to\infty$ with $s_0=p2^m$, $d\ge m$, $d2^{-m}\to0$ and $\delta_S=o(2^m)$, and let $c_m\ge a2^{-m}$ satisfy $c_md\to0$. Then there is $\epsilon>0$ such that for all large $m$, $J_g(c)+\epsilon<\Phi_2^\sharp(c)$ for every $c\in[a2^{-m},c_m]$; so the identification of \cref{thm:teacher:main}(ii) holds there (\cref{prop:teacher:sharp}(iii)). Moreover $a^*(p)<\infty$ if and only if $1-\log(\frac32-p)>H(p)$, and $a^*(p)=1$ if and only if $\psi_2(p,1)\ge H(p)$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof idea.} At $c=a2^{-m}$ a budget $I=xs_0$ costs $apx$ per sample, and as $m\to\infty$ the level-$2$ floor tends to $-\log(\frac34-\frac p2+2p\sqrt{x\ln2/2})$; so the level-$2$ term $\Psi_2$ of $\Phi_2^\sharp$ (\cref{app:teacher:rates}) has $\liminf_m\Psi_2(a2^{-m})\ge\psi_2(p,a)>H(p)$, while the length terms vanish. As $a\to\infty$, $\psi_2(p,a)$ increases to $1-\log(\frac32-p)$ (\cref{app:teacher:rates}).

The first condition is that of \cref{prop:parity:range}(iii), which holds exactly for $p<p_1=0.1233072\ldots$ (\cref{sec:parity:full}). $\psi_2(p,1)-H(p)$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,\frac14]$, and a direct computation (code in the accompanying repository) puts its root at $p^*=0.0778658\ldots$ (\cref{app:teacher:rates}). So asymptotically a window reaching far above $2^{-m}$ exists for every dyadic $p\le0.1233$, reaching down to $a2^{-m}$ for every $a>1$ when $p\le0.0778$. At $d=100$ and $m=20$, with the machine constant set to $0$ (an illustration), windows exist at the flip rates $1/16$, $5/64$, $3/32$, $7/64$ and $15/128$, where \cref{thm:teacher:main} gives none, with $e\le0.197$ to $0.269$ at a value of $c$ inside each (\cref{tab:teacher:rates}).

\begin{question}[other models, flip rates, samples and schedules]\status{open}\src{steeper \S2.6; split/teacher \S5 ``What remains open'', Prop 3.3(c), Prop 3.6; split synthesis \S6 items 8--10, 12, 14}\label{q:teacher:open}
(a) For which classes of simple models other than parities is there a window as in \cref{thm:teacher:main}? We expect that majority-like functions give none. (b) For $p_1\le p<\frac14$ and $m<d$, with $p_1=0.1233072\ldots$ as in \cref{sec:parity:full}, is there a window on which every near-optimal hypothesis has level at most $2$? The present floors cannot give level $1$ there; the level-$3$ analogue of $\psi_2$ tends to $-\log(\frac58-\frac p4)>H(p)$, and what is missing is (D2) at level $2$. (c) Do near-population constants hold for sampled data far below $10^{11}$ samples? (d) On $n\gg2^m$ inputs drawn without replacement, with unequal multiplicities of the sampled cells, is the least $\lambda$ at which a parity with flip noise is a $\lambda$-MAP about the mean multiplicity of the sampled error cells, up to $O(\log)$ terms, and does the largest grow like $n$? (e) What do schedules do whose ratio $\lambda_n/n$ leaves the window infinitely often?
\end{question}
